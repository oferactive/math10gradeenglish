1. Slope: how steep a line is

The slope of a line tells us how much \(y\) changes when \(x\) grows by 1. It is the "rate of change" of the line, and it is the same everywhere on the line.

For two points \(A(x_1,y_1)\) and \(B(x_2,y_2)\) on the line:

\(m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\)
change in y divided by change in x
SlopeWhat the line looks like
\(m\gt 0\)Rises from left to right (increasing).
\(m\lt 0\)Falls from left to right (decreasing).
\(m=0\)Horizontal line \(y=k\).
not definedVertical line \(x=k\) (here \(\Delta x=0\)).
Example 1

Find the slope of the line through \(A(1,-2)\) and \(B(4,7)\).

Solution.

\(m=\dfrac{7-(-2)}{4-1}=\dfrac{9}{3}=3\)

Every step of 1 to the right raises the line by 3.

2. The equation of a line

A line with slope \(m\) that meets the y-axis at \((0,b)\) has the equation \(y=mx+b\). The number \(b\) is called the y-intercept.

Often we know a point \((x_1,y_1)\) and the slope, but not \(b\). Then we use the point-slope form:

\(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\)
line through \((x_1,y_1)\) with slope \(m\)

Line through two points: first find the slope from the two points, then use point-slope form with either point.

Example 2

Find the equation of the line through \(A(2,5)\) and \(B(-1,-4)\).

Solution.

Slope: \(m=\dfrac{-4-5}{-1-2}=\dfrac{-9}{-3}=3\)

Point-slope with \(A\): \(y-5=3(x-2)\)

\(y=3x-6+5\Rightarrow y=3x-1\)

Check with \(B\): \(3\cdot(-1)-1=-4\) ✓

Is every line a function? A function gives exactly one \(y\) for each \(x\). The vertical line \(x=k\) contains many points with the same \(x\), so it is not the graph of a function. Every other line is the graph of a linear function \(y=mx+b\).

Does a point lie on a line? Substitute its coordinates into the equation. If the equation is true, the point is on the line.

3. Explicit form and general form

The same line can be written in two ways:

Explicit form\(y=mx+b\)
General form\(Ax+By+C=0\)

The explicit form shows the slope and the y-intercept at once. The general form can also describe vertical lines (when \(B=0\)). To move from general form to explicit form, isolate \(y\):

Example 3

Write \(2x-4y+8=0\) in explicit form and find the slope.

Solution.

\(-4y=-2x-8\)

\(y=\tfrac12x+2\)

Slope \(m=\tfrac12\), y-intercept \(b=2\).

4. Parallel and perpendicular lines

Parallel lines\(m_1=m_2\)
Perpendicular lines\(m_1\cdot m_2=-1\)

Parallel lines have the same steepness, so they have equal slopes. Perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals: if one slope is \(\tfrac23\), the other is \(-\tfrac32\).

Special case: a horizontal line \(y=k\) is perpendicular to every vertical line \(x=c\).

Example 4

Find the line through \(P(4,1)\) that is perpendicular to \(y=2x-3\).

Solution.

The given slope is \(2\), so the perpendicular slope is \(-\tfrac12\) (because \(2\cdot(-\tfrac12)=-1\)).

\(y-1=-\tfrac12(x-4)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac12x+3\)

5. Midpoint and distance

Midpoint of AB\(M\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\ \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)\)
Distance AB\(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\)

The midpoint is the average of the coordinates. The distance formula is Pythagoras' theorem: \(\Delta x\) and \(\Delta y\) are the legs of a right triangle and \(AB\) is the hypotenuse.

Example 5

For \(A(1,1)\) and \(B(6,5)\), find the midpoint and the length of \(AB\).

Solution.

\(M\left(\dfrac{1+6}{2},\dfrac{1+5}{2}\right)=M(3.5,\ 3)\)

\(AB=\sqrt{5^2+4^2}=\sqrt{41}\approx 6.40\)

Example 6

\(M(2,-1)\) is the midpoint of \(AB\) and \(A(-3,4)\). Find \(B\).

Solution.

\(\dfrac{-3+x_B}{2}=2\Rightarrow x_B=7\)

\(\dfrac{4+y_B}{2}=-1\Rightarrow y_B=-6\)

\(B(7,-6)\)

6. Where two lines meet

The intersection point lies on both lines, so its coordinates satisfy both equations. We solve the two equations together. When both are in explicit form, set the right-hand sides equal.

To find where a line meets the axes: on the x-axis \(y=0\), and on the y-axis \(x=0\).

Example 7

Find the intersection of \(y=2x-1\) and \(y=-x+5\).

Solution.

\(2x-1=-x+5\Rightarrow 3x=6\Rightarrow x=2\)

\(y=2\cdot 2-1=3\)

The lines meet at \((2,3)\).

Example 8

The line \(y=-\tfrac12x+4\) meets the axes at \(A\) and \(B\). Find the area of triangle \(AOB\) (\(O\) is the origin).

Solution.

y-axis: \(x=0\Rightarrow y=4\), so \(B(0,4)\).

x-axis: \(0=-\tfrac12x+4\Rightarrow x=8\), so \(A(8,0)\).

The triangle has a right angle at \(O\): \(S=\dfrac{8\cdot 4}{2}=16\).

7. Common mistakes

  • Mixing the order in the slope formula: \(\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_1-x_2}\) gives the wrong sign. Subtract in the same order on top and bottom.
  • Writing the slope as \(\dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta y}\) instead of \(\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\).
  • For perpendicular lines, taking only the reciprocal (\(\tfrac12\) from \(2\)) and forgetting the minus sign: the correct slope is \(-\tfrac12\).
  • Reading the slope from the general form \(Ax+By+C=0\) as \(A\). Isolate \(y\) first: \(m=-\tfrac{A}{B}\).
  • In the distance formula, forgetting to square a negative difference: \((-3)^2=9\), not \(-9\).
  • Saying that \(x=3\) has slope 0. It is vertical, so its slope is not defined. The line with slope 0 is \(y=3\).

8. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Find the slope of the line through \((-2,3)\) and \((4,0)\).

    Show solution

    \(m=\dfrac{0-3}{4-(-2)}=\dfrac{-3}{6}=-\tfrac12\)

  2. Find the equation of the line with slope \(-3\) through \((1,4)\).

    Show solution

    \(y-4=-3(x-1)\Rightarrow y=-3x+7\)

  3. Does the point \((3,5)\) lie on the line \(y=2x-1\)?

    Show solution

    \(2\cdot 3-1=5\) ✓ Yes, it lies on the line.

  4. Write \(3x+2y-6=0\) in explicit form.

    Show solution

    \(2y=-3x+6\Rightarrow y=-1.5x+3\)

  5. Find the slope of a line perpendicular to \(y=\tfrac34x+2\).

    Show solution

    \(m=-\tfrac43\), because \(\tfrac34\cdot(-\tfrac43)=-1\).

  6. Find the midpoint of \(A(-4,2)\) and \(B(6,8)\).

    Show solution

    \(M\left(\dfrac{-4+6}{2},\dfrac{2+8}{2}\right)=M(1,5)\)

  7. Find the distance between \((2,-1)\) and \((5,3)\).

    Show solution

    \(d=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=\sqrt{25}=5\)

  8. Find the intersection of \(y=x+2\) and \(y=-2x+8\).

    Show solution

    \(x+2=-2x+8\Rightarrow x=2,\ y=4\)

    The point is \((2,4)\).
