Before you start: Draw a quick sketch for every question. It helps you see whether a slope should be positive or negative and whether your answer makes sense.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the slope of the line through the two points.

    1. \((1,3)\) and \((4,9)\)
    2. \((-1,5)\) and \((2,-1)\)
    3. \((2,4)\) and \((7,4)\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(m=\dfrac{9-3}{4-1}=2\)

    (b) \(m=\dfrac{-1-5}{2-(-1)}=-2\)

    (c) \(m=\dfrac{4-4}{7-2}=0\) (a horizontal line)

  2. Write down the slope and the y-intercept of each line.

    1. \(y=-4x+7\)
    2. \(y=\tfrac23x\)
    3. \(y=-5\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(m=-4,\ b=7\)

    (b) \(m=\tfrac23,\ b=0\)

    (c) \(m=0,\ b=-5\)

  3. Find the equation of the line with slope \(2\) through \((3,1)\).

    Show solution

    \(y-1=2(x-3)\Rightarrow y=2x-5\)

  4. Find the equation of the line through \((0,-3)\) and \((2,5)\).

    Show solution

    \(m=\dfrac{5-(-3)}{2-0}=4\)

    The line passes through \((0,-3)\), so \(b=-3\): \(y=4x-3\).

  5. Find the equation of the line through \((-2,1)\) and \((4,-2)\).

    Show solution

    \(m=\dfrac{-2-1}{4-(-2)}=-\tfrac12\)

    \(y-1=-\tfrac12(x+2)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac12x\)

  6. Which of the points \(A(1,1)\), \(B(2,4)\), \(C(-1,-5)\) lie on the line \(y=3x-2\)?

    Show solution

    \(A\): \(3-2=1\) ✓

    \(B\): \(6-2=4\) ✓

    \(C\): \(-3-2=-5\) ✓

    All three points lie on the line.

  7. Write each line in explicit form.

    1. \(2x+y-5=0\)
    2. \(x-3y+6=0\)
    3. \(4x-5y-10=0\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(y=-2x+5\)

    (b) \(3y=x+6\Rightarrow y=\tfrac13x+2\)

    (c) \(-5y=-4x+10\Rightarrow y=0.8x-2\)

  8. Find the midpoint of each segment.

    1. \(A(2,1),\ B(6,5)\)
    2. \(A(-3,4),\ B(1,-1)\)
    3. \(A(-5,2),\ B(5,-2)\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(M(4,3)\)

    (b) \(M(-1,1.5)\)

    (c) \(M(0,0)\)

  9. Find the length of each segment.

    1. \((0,0)\) to \((6,8)\)
    2. \((-2,1)\) to \((3,13)\)
    3. \((1,-1)\) to \((3,1)\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10\)

    (b) \(\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=13\)

    (c) \(\sqrt{2^2+2^2}=\sqrt8=2\sqrt2\)

  10. Find where the line \(y=2x-6\) meets the x-axis and the y-axis.

    Show solution

    x-axis: \(0=2x-6\Rightarrow x=3\), point \((3,0)\).

    y-axis: \(x=0\Rightarrow y=-6\), point \((0,-6)\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Find the line through \((2,-1)\) that is parallel to \(y=-3x+4\).

    Show solution

    Parallel, so \(m=-3\).

    \(y+1=-3(x-2)\Rightarrow y=-3x+5\)

  2. Find the line through \((6,2)\) that is perpendicular to \(y=3x-1\).

    Show solution

    \(m=-\tfrac13\)

    \(y-2=-\tfrac13(x-6)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac13x+4\)

  3. Find the line through \((1,2)\) that is perpendicular to \(2x+5y-1=0\).

    Show solution

    Given line: \(y=-\tfrac25x+\tfrac15\), slope \(-\tfrac25\).

    Perpendicular slope \(\tfrac52\).

    \(y-2=\tfrac52(x-1)\Rightarrow y=2.5x-0.5\)

  4. Are the lines perpendicular, parallel, or neither?

    1. \(y=\tfrac12x+3\) and \(y=-2x+1\)
    2. \(2x-y+1=0\) and \(4x-2y-6=0\)
    3. \(y=3x\) and \(y=\tfrac13x+2\)
    Show solution

    (a) slopes \(\tfrac12\) and \(-2\): product \(-1\), perpendicular.

    (b) \(y=2x+1\) and \(y=2x-3\): equal slopes, parallel.

    (c) slopes \(3\) and \(\tfrac13\): product \(1\), neither.

  5. Find the intersection point of the lines \(y=3x-4\) and \(2x+y-6=0\).

    Show solution

    Second line: \(y=-2x+6\).

    \(3x-4=-2x+6\Rightarrow 5x=10\Rightarrow x=2\)

    \(y=2\), the point is \((2,2)\).

  6. \(M(3,-2)\) is the midpoint of \(AB\), and \(A(-1,5)\). Find \(B\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{-1+x}{2}=3\Rightarrow x=7\)

    \(\dfrac{5+y}{2}=-2\Rightarrow y=-9\)

    \(B(7,-9)\)

  7. For which value of \(k\) does the point \((k,7)\) lie on the line \(y=2x-5\)?

    Show solution

    \(7=2k-5\Rightarrow k=6\)

  8. For which value of \(a\) is the line \(y=(a-1)x+3\) parallel to \(y=4x\)? For which value is it perpendicular to \(y=4x\)?

    Show solution

    Parallel: \(a-1=4\Rightarrow a=5\).

    Perpendicular: \(4(a-1)=-1\Rightarrow a-1=-\tfrac14\Rightarrow a=\tfrac34\).

  9. Show that the triangle with vertices \(A(1,1)\), \(B(4,5)\), \(C(8,2)\) is right-angled, and find its area.

    Show solution

    \(m_{AB}=\dfrac{4}{3}\), \(m_{BC}=\dfrac{-3}{4}\), and \(\tfrac43\cdot(-\tfrac34)=-1\), so \(AB\perp BC\): the right angle is at \(B\).

    \(AB=\sqrt{9+16}=5\), \(BC=\sqrt{16+9}=5\)

    \(S=\dfrac{5\cdot 5}{2}=12.5\)

  10. Find \(k\) so that the distance between \((1,k)\) and \((4,2)\) is 5.

    Show solution

    \(\sqrt{3^2+(2-k)^2}=5\Rightarrow (2-k)^2=16\)

    \(2-k=\pm 4\Rightarrow k=-2\) or \(k=6\).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. The vertices of triangle \(ABC\) are \(A(-2,1)\), \(B(4,3)\), \(C(2,-3)\).

    1. Find the equation of the median from \(A\).
    2. Find the equation of the altitude from \(A\) to \(BC\).
    3. Show that the triangle is isosceles.
    Show solution

    (a) \(D\) is the midpoint of \(BC\): \(D(3,0)\).

    \(m_{AD}=\dfrac{0-1}{3+2}=-\tfrac15\), \(AD:\ y-0=-\tfrac15(x-3)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac15x+\tfrac35\).

    (b) \(m_{BC}=\dfrac{-3-3}{2-4}=3\), so the altitude from \(A\) has slope \(-\tfrac13\): \(y-1=-\tfrac13(x+2)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac13x+\tfrac13\).

    (c) \(AB=\sqrt{36+4}=\sqrt{40}\), \(AC=\sqrt{16+16}=\sqrt{32}\), \(BC=\sqrt{4+36}=\sqrt{40}\). \(AB=BC\), so the triangle is isosceles with apex \(B\).

  2. The points \(A(1,2)\), \(B(5,4)\), \(C(7,8)\) are three vertices of parallelogram \(ABCD\). Find \(D\).

    Show solution

    In a parallelogram the diagonals bisect each other, so \(AC\) and \(BD\) have the same midpoint.

    Midpoint of \(AC\): \((4,5)\).

    \(\dfrac{5+x_D}{2}=4\Rightarrow x_D=3\), \(\dfrac{4+y_D}{2}=5\Rightarrow y_D=6\)

    \(D(3,6)\)

  3. Find the perpendicular bisector of the segment \(A(-1,3)\), \(B(5,-1)\). Then find the point on the x-axis that is equally far from \(A\) and \(B\).

    Show solution

    Midpoint \(M(2,1)\), \(m_{AB}=\dfrac{-4}{6}=-\tfrac23\), so the bisector has slope \(\tfrac32\).

    \(y-1=\tfrac32(x-2)\Rightarrow y=1.5x-2\)

    Every point on the perpendicular bisector is equally far from \(A\) and \(B\). On the x-axis \(y=0\): \(1.5x=2\Rightarrow x=\tfrac43\).

    The point is \(\left(\tfrac43,0\right)\).

  4. The line \(\ell\) passes through \((0,6)\) and \((3,0)\). The line \(k\) passes through the origin and is perpendicular to \(\ell\).

    1. Find the equation of \(\ell\).
    2. Find the equation of \(k\).
    3. Find the point \(H\) where the lines meet.
    4. Find the length \(OH\). What does it measure?
    Show solution

    (a) \(m_\ell=\dfrac{0-6}{3-0}=-2\), \(\ell:\ y=-2x+6\).

    (b) \(m_k=\tfrac12\), \(k:\ y=\tfrac12x\).

    (c) \(-2x+6=\tfrac12x\Rightarrow 6=2.5x\Rightarrow x=2.4,\ y=1.2\). \(H(2.4,\ 1.2)\).

    (d) \(OH=\sqrt{2.4^2+1.2^2}=\sqrt{7.2}\approx 2.68\). This is the distance from the origin to \(\ell\).

  5. A square \(ABCD\) has vertices \(A(1,1)\) and \(B(4,2)\), and \(C\) is above \(AB\). Find \(C\) and \(D\), and the area of the square.

    Show solution

    \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) moves \(3\) right and \(1\) up. A side perpendicular to it moves \(1\) left and \(3\) up (slopes \(\tfrac13\) and \(-3\)).

    \(C=B+(-1,3)=(3,5)\), \(D=A+(-1,3)=(0,4)\).

    Side \(AB=\sqrt{9+1}=\sqrt{10}\), area \(=10\).

  6. Find the area of the triangle formed by the lines \(y=x+1\), \(y=-x+5\) and the x-axis.

    Show solution

    \(y=x+1\) meets the x-axis at \((-1,0)\). \(y=-x+5\) meets it at \((5,0)\).

    The lines meet each other where \(x+1=-x+5\Rightarrow x=2,\ y=3\).

    Base \(=6\), height \(=3\), area \(=\dfrac{6\cdot 3}{2}=9\).

  7. Point \(P\) lies on the line \(y=2x\) and is equally far from \(A(0,4)\) and \(B(6,0)\). Find \(P\).

    Show solution

    Let \(P(t,2t)\). \(PA^2=PB^2\):

    \(t^2+(2t-4)^2=(t-6)^2+(2t)^2\)

    \(t^2+4t^2-16t+16=t^2-12t+36+4t^2\)

    \(-16t+16=-12t+36\Rightarrow -4t=20\Rightarrow t=-5\)

    \(P(-5,-10)\)

  8. The vertices of quadrilateral \(ABCD\) are \(A(0,0)\), \(B(4,2)\), \(C(6,6)\), \(D(2,4)\). Show that \(ABCD\) is a rhombus, and find its area.

    Show solution

    \(AB=\sqrt{16+4}=\sqrt{20}\), \(BC=\sqrt{4+16}=\sqrt{20}\), \(CD=\sqrt{16+4}=\sqrt{20}\), \(DA=\sqrt{4+16}=\sqrt{20}\).

    All four sides are equal, so it is a rhombus.

    Diagonals: \(AC=\sqrt{36+36}=6\sqrt2\), \(BD=\sqrt{4+4}=2\sqrt2\).

    Area \(=\dfrac{AC\cdot BD}{2}=\dfrac{6\sqrt2\cdot 2\sqrt2}{2}=12\)

  9. The line \(y=mx+2\) passes through \((0,2)\). For which values of \(m\) does it meet the segment between \(A(3,0)\) and \(B(3,8)\)?

    Show solution

    At \(x=3\) the line has \(y=3m+2\). The segment is all points with \(x=3\) and \(0\le y\le 8\).

    \(0\le 3m+2\le 8\Rightarrow -\tfrac23\le m\le 2\)

  10. \(A(2,0)\) and \(B(0,4)\). Find all points \(C\) on the line \(y=x\) for which triangle \(ABC\) has a right angle at \(C\).

    Show solution

    Let \(C(t,t)\). Right angle at \(C\) means \(CA\perp CB\), so \(CA^2+CB^2=AB^2\).

    \((t-2)^2+t^2+t^2+(t-4)^2=4+16\)

    \(4t^2-12t+20=20\Rightarrow 4t(t-3)=0\)

    \(t=0\) or \(t=3\): \(C(0,0)\) or \(C(3,3)\).
