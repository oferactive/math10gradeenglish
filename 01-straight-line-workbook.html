Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the slope of the line through the two points.

    1. \((1,3)\) and \((4,9)\)
    2. \((-1,5)\) and \((2,-1)\)
    3. \((2,4)\) and \((7,4)\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Slope \(m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\): subtract in the same order on top and bottom.

    (a) \(m=\dfrac{9-3}{4-1}=\dfrac63=2\). The line rises 2 units for every unit to the right.

    (b) \(m=\dfrac{-1-5}{2-(-1)}=\dfrac{-6}{3}=-2\). The denominator is \(2+1=3\) because subtracting \(-1\) adds 1.

    (c) \(m=\dfrac{4-4}{7-2}=\dfrac05=0\). Both points have \(y=4\), so the line is horizontal.

    Answer: (a) \(2\); (b) \(-2\); (c) \(0\).

  2. Write down the slope and the y-intercept of each line.

    1. \(y=-4x+7\)
    2. \(y=\tfrac23x\)
    3. \(y=-5\)
    Show solution

    Idea. In \(y=mx+b\), the number multiplying \(x\) is the slope \(m\) and the constant term is the y-intercept \(b\).

    (a) \(y=-4x+7\): \(m=-4\), \(b=7\).

    (b) \(y=\tfrac23x\) is \(y=\tfrac23x+0\): \(m=\tfrac23\), \(b=0\) (the line passes through the origin).

    (c) \(y=-5\) is \(y=0\cdot x-5\): \(m=0\), \(b=-5\) (a horizontal line).

    Answer: (a) \(m=-4,\ b=7\); (b) \(m=\tfrac23,\ b=0\); (c) \(m=0,\ b=-5\).

  3. Find the equation of the line with slope \(2\) through \((3,1)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. A point and a slope are known, so use \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\).

    \(y-1=2(x-3)\).

    Open the bracket: \(y-1=2x-6\). Add 1: \(y=2x-5\).

    Answer: \(y=2x-5\).

    Check: at \(x=3\), \(y=6-5=1\) ✓

  4. Find the equation of the line through \((0,-3)\) and \((2,5)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. First find the slope from the two points, then the equation. One point is on the y-axis, which gives \(b\) directly.

    \(m=\dfrac{5-(-3)}{2-0}=\dfrac82=4\).

    The point \((0,-3)\) is the y-intercept, so \(b=-3\).

    Answer: \(y=4x-3\).

    Check: at \(x=2\), \(y=8-3=5\) ✓

  5. Find the equation of the line through \((-2,1)\) and \((4,-2)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Slope from two points, then point-slope form with one of them.

    \(m=\dfrac{-2-1}{4-(-2)}=\dfrac{-3}{6}=-\tfrac12\).

    With \((-2,1)\): \(y-1=-\tfrac12(x-(-2))=-\tfrac12(x+2)\).

    \(y-1=-\tfrac12x-1\), so \(y=-\tfrac12x\).

    Answer: \(y=-\tfrac12x\) (it passes through the origin).

    Check with the other point: \(-\tfrac12\cdot4=-2\) ✓

  6. Which of the points \(A(1,1)\), \(B(2,4)\), \(C(-1,-5)\) lie on the line \(y=3x-2\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute each point's \(x\) into \(3x-2\) and compare with its \(y\).

    \(A(1,1)\): \(3\cdot1-2=1\) — equal to \(y=1\) ✓

    \(B(2,4)\): \(3\cdot2-2=4\) — equal to \(y=4\) ✓

    \(C(-1,-5)\): \(3\cdot(-1)-2=-5\) — equal to \(y=-5\) ✓

    Answer: all three points lie on the line.

  7. Write each line in explicit form.

    1. \(2x+y-5=0\)
    2. \(x-3y+6=0\)
    3. \(4x-5y-10=0\)
    Show solution

    Idea. To get explicit form, move everything except the \(y\)-term to the other side, then divide by the coefficient of \(y\).

    (a) \(2x+y-5=0\Rightarrow y=-2x+5\).

    (b) \(x-3y+6=0\Rightarrow -3y=-x-6\). Divide by \(-3\) (all signs change): \(y=\tfrac13x+2\).

    (c) \(4x-5y-10=0\Rightarrow -5y=-4x+10\). Divide by \(-5\): \(y=\tfrac45x-2=0.8x-2\).

    Answer: (a) \(y=-2x+5\); (b) \(y=\tfrac13x+2\); (c) \(y=0.8x-2\).

    Watch out: when you divide by a negative number, every term changes sign.

  8. Find the midpoint of each segment.

    1. \(A(2,1),\ B(6,5)\)
    2. \(A(-3,4),\ B(1,-1)\)
    3. \(A(-5,2),\ B(5,-2)\)
    Show solution

    Idea. The midpoint's coordinates are the averages of the endpoints' coordinates.

    (a) \(\left(\tfrac{2+6}2,\tfrac{1+5}2\right)=(4,3)\).

    (b) \(\left(\tfrac{-3+1}2,\tfrac{4+(-1)}2\right)=\left(\tfrac{-2}2,\tfrac32\right)=(-1,1.5)\).

    (c) \(\left(\tfrac{-5+5}2,\tfrac{2+(-2)}2\right)=(0,0)\): the two points are symmetric about the origin.

    Answer: (a) \((4,3)\); (b) \((-1,1.5)\); (c) \((0,0)\).

  9. Find the length of each segment.

    1. \((0,0)\) to \((6,8)\)
    2. \((-2,1)\) to \((3,13)\)
    3. \((1,-1)\) to \((3,1)\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Use \(d=\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2+(\Delta y)^2}\).

    (a) \(\Delta x=6\), \(\Delta y=8\): \(d=\sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{100}=10\).

    (b) \(\Delta x=3-(-2)=5\), \(\Delta y=13-1=12\): \(d=\sqrt{25+144}=\sqrt{169}=13\).

    (c) \(\Delta x=2\), \(\Delta y=1-(-1)=2\): \(d=\sqrt{4+4}=\sqrt8=\sqrt{4\cdot2}=2\sqrt2\approx2.83\).

    Answer: (a) 10; (b) 13; (c) \(2\sqrt2\).

  10. Find where the line \(y=2x-6\) meets the x-axis and the y-axis.

    Show solution

    Idea. On the x-axis every point has \(y=0\); on the y-axis every point has \(x=0\).

    x-axis: \(0=2x-6\Rightarrow 2x=6\Rightarrow x=3\). Point \((3,0)\).

    y-axis: \(y=2\cdot0-6=-6\). Point \((0,-6)\).

    Answer: \((3,0)\) and \((0,-6)\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Find the line through \((2,-1)\) that is parallel to \(y=-3x+4\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Parallel lines have equal slopes, so the new line has slope \(-3\); then use the given point.

    \(y-(-1)=-3(x-2)\), i.e. \(y+1=-3x+6\).

    Subtract 1: \(y=-3x+5\).

    Answer: \(y=-3x+5\).

    Check: at \(x=2\), \(-6+5=-1\) ✓, and the slope is \(-3\) like the given line ✓

  2. Find the line through \((6,2)\) that is perpendicular to \(y=3x-1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The given slope is 3. A perpendicular slope \(m\) satisfies \(3m=-1\), so \(m=-\tfrac13\).

    \(y-2=-\tfrac13(x-6)=-\tfrac13x+2\).

    Add 2: \(y=-\tfrac13x+4\).

    Answer: \(y=-\tfrac13x+4\).

    Check: at \(x=6\), \(-2+4=2\) ✓

  3. Find the line through \((1,2)\) that is perpendicular to \(2x+5y-1=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. First read the slope of the given line by writing it in explicit form; then take the negative reciprocal.

    \(2x+5y-1=0\Rightarrow 5y=-2x+1\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac25x+\tfrac15\). Its slope is \(-\tfrac25\).

    Perpendicular slope: the reciprocal of \(-\tfrac25\) is \(-\tfrac52\); change the sign: \(\tfrac52\).

    \(y-2=\tfrac52(x-1)=\tfrac52x-\tfrac52\), so \(y=\tfrac52x-\tfrac12\).

    Answer: \(y=2.5x-0.5\).

    Watch out: the slope of \(Ax+By+C=0\) is \(-\tfrac AB\), not \(A\).

  4. Are the lines perpendicular, parallel, or neither?

    1. \(y=\tfrac12x+3\) and \(y=-2x+1\)
    2. \(2x-y+1=0\) and \(4x-2y-6=0\)
    3. \(y=3x\) and \(y=\tfrac13x+2\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Compare the slopes: equal slopes mean parallel (or the same line); a product of \(-1\) means perpendicular.

    (a) Slopes \(\tfrac12\) and \(-2\). Product \(\tfrac12\cdot(-2)=-1\): perpendicular.

    (b) \(2x-y+1=0\Rightarrow y=2x+1\); \(4x-2y-6=0\Rightarrow 2y=4x-6\Rightarrow y=2x-3\). Same slope 2, different intercepts: parallel.

    (c) Slopes \(3\) and \(\tfrac13\). They are not equal, and the product is \(1\), not \(-1\): neither.

    Answer: (a) perpendicular; (b) parallel; (c) neither.

  5. Find the intersection point of the lines \(y=3x-4\) and \(2x+y-6=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Write both lines in explicit form, then set the right-hand sides equal.

    \(2x+y-6=0\Rightarrow y=-2x+6\).

    \(3x-4=-2x+6\). Add \(2x\) and 4 to both sides: \(5x=10\), so \(x=2\).

    \(y=3\cdot2-4=2\).

    Answer: \((2,2)\).

    Check: \(2\cdot2+2-6=0\) ✓

  6. \(M(3,-2)\) is the midpoint of \(AB\), and \(A(-1,5)\). Find \(B\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The midpoint's coordinates are averages. We know the average and one endpoint, so we solve for the other endpoint.

    x: \(\dfrac{-1+x_B}{2}=3\Rightarrow -1+x_B=6\Rightarrow x_B=7\).

    y: \(\dfrac{5+y_B}{2}=-2\Rightarrow 5+y_B=-4\Rightarrow y_B=-9\).

    Answer: \(B(7,-9)\).

    Check: \(\left(\tfrac{-1+7}2,\tfrac{5-9}2\right)=(3,-2)\) ✓

  7. For which value of \(k\) does the point \((k,7)\) lie on the line \(y=2x-5\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. The point is on the line if its coordinates satisfy the equation. Substitute and solve for \(k\).

    \(7=2k-5\). Add 5: \(12=2k\), so \(k=6\).

    Answer: \(k=6\).

    Check: \(2\cdot6-5=7\) ✓

  8. For which value of \(a\) is the line \(y=(a-1)x+3\) parallel to \(y=4x\)? For which value is it perpendicular to \(y=4x\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. The slope of \(y=(a-1)x+3\) is \(a-1\). Compare it with the slope 4 of \(y=4x\).

    Parallel: equal slopes, \(a-1=4\Rightarrow a=5\).

    Perpendicular: product of slopes \(-1\): \(4(a-1)=-1\Rightarrow a-1=-\tfrac14\Rightarrow a=\tfrac34\).

    Answer: parallel for \(a=5\); perpendicular for \(a=\tfrac34\).

  9. Show that the triangle with vertices \(A(1,1)\), \(B(4,5)\), \(C(8,2)\) is right-angled, and find its area.

    Show solution

    Idea. A right angle shows up as two sides with slopes whose product is \(-1\). Then the two perpendicular sides are the base and the height.

    \(m_{AB}=\dfrac{5-1}{4-1}=\dfrac43\). \(m_{BC}=\dfrac{2-5}{8-4}=-\dfrac34\).

    \(\tfrac43\cdot\left(-\tfrac34\right)=-1\), so \(AB\perp BC\): the right angle is at \(B\), where these sides meet.

    \(AB=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\), \(BC=\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5\).

    Area of a right triangle: half the product of the legs, \(\dfrac{5\cdot5}{2}=12.5\).

    Answer: right angle at \(B\); area \(12.5\).

  10. Find \(k\) so that the distance between \((1,k)\) and \((4,2)\) is 5.

    Show solution

    Idea. Write the distance formula with the unknown \(k\), set it equal to 5, and solve.

    \(\Delta x=4-1=3\), \(\Delta y=2-k\): \(\sqrt{9+(2-k)^2}=5\).

    Square both sides: \(9+(2-k)^2=25\Rightarrow (2-k)^2=16\).

    A number whose square is 16 is \(4\) or \(-4\): \(2-k=4\Rightarrow k=-2\), or \(2-k=-4\Rightarrow k=6\).

    Answer: \(k=-2\) or \(k=6\).

    Check: for \(k=6\), \(\sqrt{9+16}=5\) ✓; for \(k=-2\), \(\sqrt{9+16}=5\) ✓ (two points, one above and one below).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. The vertices of triangle \(ABC\) are \(A(-2,1)\), \(B(4,3)\), \(C(2,-3)\).

    1. Find the equation of the median from \(A\).
    2. Find the equation of the altitude from \(A\) to \(BC\).
    3. Show that the triangle is isosceles.
    Show solution

    Idea. A median goes from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side; an altitude goes from a vertex perpendicular to the opposite side; isosceles means two equal sides.

    (a) The midpoint of \(BC\): \(D\left(\tfrac{4+2}2,\tfrac{3-3}2\right)=(3,0)\).

    \(m_{AD}=\dfrac{0-1}{3-(-2)}=-\tfrac15\). Through \(D\): \(y=-\tfrac15(x-3)=-\tfrac15x+\tfrac35\).

    (b) \(m_{BC}=\dfrac{-3-3}{2-4}=\dfrac{-6}{-2}=3\). The altitude is perpendicular to \(BC\), so its slope is \(-\tfrac13\).

    Through \(A(-2,1)\): \(y-1=-\tfrac13(x+2)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac13x+\tfrac13\).

    (c) \(AB=\sqrt{6^2+2^2}=\sqrt{40}\), \(BC=\sqrt{2^2+6^2}=\sqrt{40}\), \(AC=\sqrt{4^2+4^2}=\sqrt{32}\).

    \(AB=BC\), so the triangle is isosceles; the equal sides meet at \(B\).

    Answer: (a) \(y=-\tfrac15x+\tfrac35\); (b) \(y=-\tfrac13x+\tfrac13\); (c) \(AB=BC=\sqrt{40}\).

  2. The points \(A(1,2)\), \(B(5,4)\), \(C(7,8)\) are three vertices of parallelogram \(ABCD\). Find \(D\).

    Show solution

    Idea. In a parallelogram the diagonals bisect each other, so the diagonals \(AC\) and \(BD\) have the same midpoint.

    Midpoint of \(AC\): \(\left(\tfrac{1+7}2,\tfrac{2+8}2\right)=(4,5)\).

    This is also the midpoint of \(BD\): \(\dfrac{5+x_D}2=4\Rightarrow x_D=3\); \(\dfrac{4+y_D}2=5\Rightarrow y_D=6\).

    Answer: \(D(3,6)\).

    Check: \(\overrightarrow{AB}=(4,2)\) and \(\overrightarrow{DC}=(7-3,8-6)=(4,2)\): opposite sides are equal and parallel ✓

    Watch out: the vertices go in order \(A,B,C,D\), so \(A\) and \(C\) are opposite, not neighbours.

  3. Find the perpendicular bisector of the segment \(A(-1,3)\), \(B(5,-1)\). Then find the point on the x-axis that is equally far from \(A\) and \(B\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The perpendicular bisector passes through the midpoint and is perpendicular to \(AB\). All its points are equally far from \(A\) and \(B\), so the required point is where it meets the x-axis.

    Midpoint \(M\left(\tfrac{-1+5}2,\tfrac{3-1}2\right)=(2,1)\).

    \(m_{AB}=\dfrac{-1-3}{5-(-1)}=-\tfrac46=-\tfrac23\); the perpendicular slope is \(\tfrac32\).

    \(y-1=\tfrac32(x-2)\Rightarrow y=1.5x-2\).

    On the x-axis \(y=0\): \(1.5x=2\Rightarrow x=\tfrac43\).

    Answer: bisector \(y=1.5x-2\); the point is \(\left(\tfrac43,0\right)\).

    Check: \(PA^2=\left(\tfrac73\right)^2+9=\tfrac{130}9\) and \(PB^2=\left(\tfrac{11}3\right)^2+1=\tfrac{130}9\) ✓

  4. The line \(\ell\) passes through \((0,6)\) and \((3,0)\). The line \(k\) passes through the origin and is perpendicular to \(\ell\).

    1. Find the equation of \(\ell\).
    2. Find the equation of \(k\).
    3. Find the point \(H\) where the lines meet.
    4. Find the length \(OH\). What does it measure?
    Show solution

    Idea. Find \(\ell\) from two points, \(k\) from its slope (perpendicular) and the origin, then solve them together. The perpendicular from a point to a line gives the shortest distance.

    (a) \(m_\ell=\dfrac{0-6}{3-0}=-2\); the y-intercept is 6: \(\ell:\ y=-2x+6\).

    (b) Perpendicular slope \(\tfrac12\), through the origin: \(k:\ y=\tfrac12x\).

    (c) \(-2x+6=\tfrac12x\Rightarrow 6=2.5x\Rightarrow x=2.4\), \(y=\tfrac12\cdot2.4=1.2\). \(H(2.4,1.2)\).

    (d) \(OH=\sqrt{2.4^2+1.2^2}=\sqrt{5.76+1.44}=\sqrt{7.2}\approx2.68\).

    Answer: (a) \(y=-2x+6\); (b) \(y=\tfrac12x\); (c) \(H(2.4,1.2)\); (d) \(\approx2.68\), the distance from the origin to the line \(\ell\) (the shortest distance).

  5. A square \(ABCD\) has vertices \(A(1,1)\) and \(B(4,2)\), and \(C\) is above \(AB\). Find \(C\) and \(D\), and the area of the square.

    Show solution

    Idea. In a square, each side is the previous side turned by 90°. Turning the step \((3,1)\) by 90° counter-clockwise gives \((-1,3)\).

    From \(A\) to \(B\): 3 right, 1 up, so \(\overrightarrow{AB}=(3,1)\).

    The perpendicular step of the same length: \((-1,3)\) (slopes \(\tfrac13\) and \(-3\) multiply to \(-1\)). It points upward, as required.

    \(C=B+(-1,3)=(3,5)\), \(D=A+(-1,3)=(0,4)\).

    Side: \(AB=\sqrt{9+1}=\sqrt{10}\); area \(=(\sqrt{10})^2=10\).

    Answer: \(C(3,5)\), \(D(0,4)\), area 10.

    Check: \(BC=\sqrt{1+9}=\sqrt{10}\) ✓

  6. Find the area of the triangle formed by the lines \(y=x+1\), \(y=-x+5\) and the x-axis.

    Show solution

    Idea. Find the three vertices (two on the x-axis, one where the lines cross). The base lies on the x-axis and the height is the y-coordinate of the top vertex.

    \(y=x+1\) meets the x-axis where \(x+1=0\): \((-1,0)\).

    \(y=-x+5\) meets it where \(-x+5=0\): \((5,0)\).

    The lines meet where \(x+1=-x+5\Rightarrow 2x=4\Rightarrow x=2\), \(y=3\): \((2,3)\).

    Base \(=5-(-1)=6\); height \(=3\).

    Answer: area \(=\dfrac{6\cdot3}2=9\).

  7. Point \(P\) lies on the line \(y=2x\) and is equally far from \(A(0,4)\) and \(B(6,0)\). Find \(P\).

    Show solution

    Idea. A point on \(y=2x\) has the form \((t,2t)\). "Equally far" means \(PA=PB\); comparing squares avoids roots.

    \(PA^2=(t-0)^2+(2t-4)^2=t^2+4t^2-16t+16\).

    \(PB^2=(t-6)^2+(2t-0)^2=t^2-12t+36+4t^2\).

    Set them equal; \(5t^2\) cancels: \(-16t+16=-12t+36\Rightarrow -4t=20\Rightarrow t=-5\).

    Answer: \(P(-5,-10)\).

    Check: \(PA^2=25+196=221\), \(PB^2=121+100=221\) ✓

  8. The vertices of quadrilateral \(ABCD\) are \(A(0,0)\), \(B(4,2)\), \(C(6,6)\), \(D(2,4)\). Show that \(ABCD\) is a rhombus, and find its area.

    Show solution

    Idea. A rhombus is a quadrilateral with four equal sides. Its area is half the product of the diagonals.

    \(AB=\sqrt{4^2+2^2}=\sqrt{20}\), \(BC=\sqrt{2^2+4^2}=\sqrt{20}\), \(CD=\sqrt{4^2+2^2}=\sqrt{20}\), \(DA=\sqrt{2^2+4^2}=\sqrt{20}\).

    All four sides are equal, so \(ABCD\) is a rhombus.

    Diagonals: \(AC=\sqrt{6^2+6^2}=6\sqrt2\), \(BD=\sqrt{2^2+2^2}=2\sqrt2\).

    Area \(=\dfrac{6\sqrt2\cdot2\sqrt2}2=\dfrac{24}2=12\).

    Answer: a rhombus with area 12.

    Check: the diagonals have slopes 1 and \(-1\): perpendicular, as in every rhombus ✓

  9. The line \(y=mx+2\) passes through \((0,2)\). For which values of \(m\) does it meet the segment between \(A(3,0)\) and \(B(3,8)\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. The segment \(AB\) is vertical (\(x=3\)), from \(y=0\) to \(y=8\). The line meets it if, at \(x=3\), its height is between 0 and 8.

    At \(x=3\): \(y=3m+2\).

    Condition: \(0\le3m+2\le8\).

    Subtract 2: \(-2\le3m\le6\). Divide by 3: \(-\tfrac23\le m\le2\).

    Answer: \(-\tfrac23\le m\le2\).

    Check: \(m=2\) gives \(y=8\) at \(x=3\), the point \(B\) ✓; \(m=-\tfrac23\) gives \(y=0\), the point \(A\) ✓

  10. \(A(2,0)\) and \(B(0,4)\). Find all points \(C\) on the line \(y=x\) for which triangle \(ABC\) has a right angle at \(C\).

    Show solution

    Idea. A right angle at \(C\) means Pythagoras' theorem holds with \(AB\) as hypotenuse: \(CA^2+CB^2=AB^2\). A point on \(y=x\) is \((t,t)\).

    \(CA^2=(t-2)^2+t^2\), \(CB^2=t^2+(t-4)^2\), \(AB^2=2^2+4^2=20\).

    \((t^2-4t+4)+t^2+t^2+(t^2-8t+16)=20\Rightarrow 4t^2-12t+20=20\).

    \(4t^2-12t=0\Rightarrow 4t(t-3)=0\Rightarrow t=0\) or \(t=3\).

    Answer: \(C(0,0)\) or \(C(3,3)\).

    Check: for \(C(3,3)\), \(m_{CA}=\tfrac{3-0}{3-2}=3\), \(m_{CB}=\tfrac{3-4}{3-0}=-\tfrac13\), product \(-1\) ✓
