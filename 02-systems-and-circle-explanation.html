Systems of Equations and the Circle

1. Systems of two linear equations

A solution of a system is a pair \((x,y)\) that makes both equations true. On a graph, each equation is a line, and the solution is the point where the lines meet.

Two ways to solve:

  • Substitution: isolate one variable in one equation and substitute it into the other.
  • Elimination: multiply the equations so that one variable has opposite coefficients, then add.
Example 1 (substitution)

Solve: \(2x+3y=12\) and \(x-y=1\).

Solution.

From the second equation: \(x=y+1\).

\(2(y+1)+3y=12\Rightarrow 5y=10\Rightarrow y=2\)

\(x=3\). Solution: \((3,2)\).

Example 2 (elimination)

Solve: \(3x+2y=16\) and \(5x-2y=0\).

Solution.

Add the equations: \(8x=16\Rightarrow x=2\).

\(3\cdot2+2y=16\Rightarrow y=5\). Solution: \((2,5)\).

How many solutions? Two lines can meet in one point, be parallel, or be the same line:

Slopes and interceptsLinesNumber of solutions
different slopesmeet at one pointexactly one
equal slopes, different interceptsparallelnone
equal slopes, equal interceptsthe same lineinfinitely many

2. Systems with a parameter

A parameter is a letter that stands for a number we do not know yet. We ask: for which values of the parameter does the system have one solution, no solution, or infinitely many?

Method: bring both equations to explicit form (or compare coefficients) and use the table above.

Example 3

For which values of \(a\) and \(b\) does the system \(x+ay=3\), \(2x+4y=b\) have one solution, no solution, or infinitely many?

Solution.

Multiply the first equation by 2: \(2x+2ay=6\). Compare with \(2x+4y=b\).

If \(2a\ne 4\), i.e. \(a\ne2\): the slopes differ, so there is one solution.

If \(a=2\) and \(b=6\): both equations are \(2x+4y=6\), the same line, so infinitely many.

If \(a=2\) and \(b\ne6\): parallel lines, no solution.

3. A line and a parabola

To find where the line \(y=mx+k\) meets the parabola \(y=ax^2+bx+c\), set the two expressions equal. We get a quadratic equation. Its discriminant \(\Delta\) tells us how many meeting points there are:

DiscriminantMeeting points
\(\Delta\gt 0\)two points: the line cuts the parabola
\(\Delta=0\)one point: the line is tangent to the parabola
\(\Delta\lt 0\)none
Example 4

Find the meeting points of \(y=x^2-2x-3\) and \(y=x+1\).

Solution.

\(x^2-2x-3=x+1\Rightarrow x^2-3x-4=0\Rightarrow (x-4)(x+1)=0\)

\(x=4\Rightarrow y=5\); \(x=-1\Rightarrow y=0\).

The points are \((4,5)\) and \((-1,0)\).

Example 5

For which \(k\) is the line \(y=2x+k\) tangent to \(y=x^2\)?

Solution.

\(x^2=2x+k\Rightarrow x^2-2x-k=0\)

Tangent means one meeting point: \(\Delta=4+4k=0\Rightarrow k=-1\).

Then \(x^2-2x+1=0\Rightarrow x=1\), and the point of tangency is \((1,1)\).

4. The circle centred at the origin

A circle is the set of all points at distance \(R\) from a centre. If the centre is the origin \(O(0,0)\), a point \((x,y)\) is on the circle when its distance from \(O\) is \(R\):

\(\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=R\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2+y^2=R^2\)
the circle with centre \(O(0,0)\) and radius \(R\)

To check where a point is, compare \(x^2+y^2\) with \(R^2\): equal means on the circle, smaller means inside, larger means outside.

The circle is not the graph of a function: for most values of \(x\) there are two points, one above and one below the x-axis.

Example 6

Find the equation of the circle centred at the origin that passes through \((-6,8)\).

Solution.

\(R^2=(-6)^2+8^2=100\)

\(x^2+y^2=100\), radius \(10\).

5. The shifted circle

Moving the circle \(x^2+y^2=R^2\) so that its centre is at \((a,b)\) replaces \(x\) by \(x-a\) and \(y\) by \(y-b\). This is the same shift rule we use for graphs of functions. It also follows from the distance formula.

\((x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=R^2\)
centre \((a,b)\), radius \(R\)
Example 7

Write the equation of the circle with centre \((2,-1)\) and radius 3. Does \((2,2)\) lie on it?

Solution.

\((x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=9\)

\((2-2)^2+(2+1)^2=9\) ✓ Yes.

Note: in this grade we always write the circle in this form. The expanded "general form" of the circle equation is not part of the syllabus.

6. A line and a circle

Substitute the line into the circle equation. Again a quadratic equation appears: two solutions mean the line cuts the circle, one solution means it is tangent, and none means they do not meet.

Example 8

Find where the line \(y=x+1\) meets the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\).

Solution.

\(x^2+(x+1)^2=25\Rightarrow 2x^2+2x-24=0\Rightarrow x^2+x-12=0\)

\((x+4)(x-3)=0\Rightarrow x=3\) or \(x=-4\)

The points are \((3,4)\) and \((-4,-3)\).

A useful fact: the tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency. So the tangent to \(x^2+y^2=R^2\) at \(P\) is perpendicular to \(OP\).

7. Common mistakes

  • Substituting back into the same equation you used to isolate a variable. Check your answer in both equations.
  • Saying "no solution" when the two equations describe the same line. Equal slopes and equal intercepts give infinitely many solutions.
  • Forgetting that \(x^2=4\) has two solutions, \(x=2\) and \(x=-2\).
  • Taking \(R^2\) as the radius: for \(x^2+y^2=16\) the radius is \(4\), not \(16\).
  • Sign errors in the shifted circle: \((x+3)^2+(y-1)^2=4\) has centre \((-3,1)\), not \((3,-1)\).
  • After finding \(x\), forgetting to find the matching \(y\). A meeting point needs both coordinates.

8. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Solve: \(x+y=7\), \(x-y=1\).

    Show solution

    Add: \(2x=8\Rightarrow x=4\), then \(y=3\). Solution \((4,3)\).

  2. How many solutions does the system \(y=2x+3\), \(4x-2y=1\) have?

    Show solution

    Second equation: \(y=2x-0.5\). Equal slopes, different intercepts: parallel lines, no solution.

  3. Find where \(y=x^2\) meets \(y=x+2\).

    Show solution

    \(x^2-x-2=0\Rightarrow x=2\) or \(x=-1\).

    Points \((2,4)\) and \((-1,1)\).

  4. Find the equation of the circle centred at the origin through \((-6,8)\).

    Show solution

    \(R^2=36+64=100\Rightarrow x^2+y^2=100\)

  5. Is \((4,-2)\) inside, on, or outside the circle \(x^2+y^2=16\)?

    Show solution

    \(16+4=20\gt 16\): outside.

  6. Find the centre and radius of \((x-3)^2+(y+5)^2=49\).

    Show solution

    Centre \((3,-5)\), radius \(7\).

  7. Find where the line \(y=2x\) meets the circle \(x^2+y^2=5\).

    Show solution

    \(x^2+4x^2=5\Rightarrow x=\pm1\)

    Points \((1,2)\) and \((-1,-2)\).

  8. For which values of \(k\) is the horizontal line \(y=k\) tangent to \(x^2+y^2=9\)?

    Show solution

    The circle reaches its highest point at \((0,3)\) and its lowest at \((0,-3)\). So \(k=3\) or \(k=-3\).
