Before you start: After every system, substitute your answer into the original equations. It takes ten seconds and catches most mistakes.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Solve by substitution: \(y=2x-1\), \(3x+y=9\).

    Show solution

    \(3x+2x-1=9\Rightarrow 5x=10\Rightarrow x=2\), \(y=3\). Solution \((2,3)\).

  2. Solve by elimination: \(2x+3y=7\), \(4x-3y=5\).

    Show solution

    Add: \(6x=12\Rightarrow x=2\). Then \(4+3y=7\Rightarrow y=1\). Solution \((2,1)\).

  3. Solve: \(x+2y=8\), \(3x-y=3\).

    Show solution

    \(x=8-2y\Rightarrow 3(8-2y)-y=3\Rightarrow 24-7y=3\Rightarrow y=3\), \(x=2\). Solution \((2,3)\).

  4. Without solving, decide how many solutions each system has.

    1. \(y=3x+1\), \(y=3x-2\)
    2. \(2x-y=4\), \(6x-3y=12\)
    3. \(y=x+1\), \(y=-x+3\)
    Show solution

    (a) Equal slopes 3, different intercepts: none.

    (b) \(2x-y=4\Rightarrow y=2x-4\) and \(6x-3y=12\Rightarrow y=2x-4\): the same line, infinitely many.

    (c) Slopes 1 and −1: exactly one.

  5. Write the equation of the circle centred at the origin.

    1. radius 4
    2. radius \(\sqrt7\)
    3. passing through \((5,-12)\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(x^2+y^2=16\)

    (b) \(x^2+y^2=7\)

    (c) \(R^2=25+144=169\Rightarrow x^2+y^2=169\)

  6. Find the radius of each circle.

    1. \(x^2+y^2=36\)
    2. \(2x^2+2y^2=50\)
    3. \(x^2+y^2=12\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(6\)

    (b) divide by 2: \(x^2+y^2=25\), radius \(5\)

    (c) \(\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3\)

  7. Decide whether each point is inside, on, or outside the circle \(x^2+y^2=20\).

    1. \((2,4)\)
    2. \((3,3)\)
    3. \((-4,3)\)
    4. \((1,-4)\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(4+16=20\): on.

    (b) \(9+9=18\): inside.

    (c) \(16+9=25\): outside.

    (d) \(1+16=17\): inside.

  8. Find where the parabola \(y=x^2\) meets the line \(y=4\).

    Show solution

    \(x^2=4\Rightarrow x=\pm2\). Points \((2,4)\) and \((-2,4)\).

  9. Find where the parabola \(y=x^2-1\) meets the line \(y=x+1\).

    Show solution

    \(x^2-1=x+1\Rightarrow x^2-x-2=0\Rightarrow x=2\) or \(x=-1\).

    Points \((2,3)\) and \((-1,0)\).

  10. Find the points on the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\) whose x-coordinate is 3.

    Show solution

    \(9+y^2=25\Rightarrow y=\pm4\). Points \((3,4)\) and \((3,-4)\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Solve: \(\dfrac{x}{2}+\dfrac{y}{3}=4\), \(x-y=3\).

    Show solution

    Multiply the first equation by 6: \(3x+2y=24\).

    \(x=y+3\Rightarrow 3y+9+2y=24\Rightarrow y=3\), \(x=6\). Solution \((6,3)\).

  2. Consider the system \(2x+ay=4\), \(x+3y=2\). For which values of \(a\) does it have exactly one solution? Infinitely many? No solution?

    Show solution

    Multiply the second equation by 2: \(2x+6y=4\).

    If \(a\ne6\): one solution.

    If \(a=6\): both equations are \(2x+6y=4\), infinitely many.

    There is no value of \(a\) with no solution, because the right-hand sides already match.

  3. Consider the system \(kx+y=3\), \(4x+ky=6\). Find the values of \(k\) for which the system has no solution, and those for which it has infinitely many.

    Show solution

    From the first equation \(y=3-kx\). Substitute: \(4x+k(3-kx)=6\Rightarrow (4-k^2)x=6-3k\).

    If \(k^2\ne4\): one solution.

    \(k=2\): \(0\cdot x=0\), true for every \(x\): infinitely many.

    \(k=-2\): \(0\cdot x=12\), impossible: no solution.

  4. Find the meeting points of \(y=x^2-4x+3\) and \(y=x-1\).

    Show solution

    \(x^2-5x+4=0\Rightarrow x=1\) or \(x=4\).

    Points \((1,0)\) and \((4,3)\).

  5. Find the meeting points of \(y=x^2+2x\) and \(y=-x-2\).

    Show solution

    \(x^2+3x+2=0\Rightarrow x=-1\) or \(x=-2\).

    Points \((-1,-1)\) and \((-2,0)\).

  6. For which value of \(k\) is the line \(y=2x+k\) tangent to the parabola \(y=x^2+1\)? Find the point of tangency.

    Show solution

    \(x^2+1=2x+k\Rightarrow x^2-2x+1-k=0\)

    \(\Delta=4-4(1-k)=4k=0\Rightarrow k=0\)

    \(x^2-2x+1=0\Rightarrow x=1\), point \((1,2)\).

  7. Find where the line \(y=x+2\) meets the circle \(x^2+y^2=10\).

    Show solution

    \(x^2+(x+2)^2=10\Rightarrow 2x^2+4x-6=0\Rightarrow x^2+2x-3=0\)

    \(x=1\) or \(x=-3\). Points \((1,3)\) and \((-3,-1)\).

  8. Write the equation of the circle with centre \((3,-2)\) and radius 5. Does the point \((6,2)\) lie on it?

    Show solution

    \((x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=25\)

    \((6-3)^2+(2+2)^2=9+16=25\) ✓ Yes.

  9. Find the centre and the radius of each circle.

    1. \((x+1)^2+(y-4)^2=12\)
    2. \(x^2+(y-5)^2=1\)
    3. \((x-2)^2+y^2=\tfrac14\)
    Show solution

    (a) centre \((-1,4)\), radius \(\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3\)

    (b) centre \((0,5)\), radius \(1\)

    (c) centre \((2,0)\), radius \(\tfrac12\)

  10. A cinema sold 12 tickets for 375 shekels. An adult ticket costs 40 shekels and a child ticket 25 shekels. How many of each were sold?

    Show solution

    \(a+c=12\), \(40a+25c=375\).

    \(40a+25(12-a)=375\Rightarrow 15a=75\Rightarrow a=5\), \(c=7\).

    Five adult tickets and seven child tickets.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. The line \(y=-x+7\) meets the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\) at \(A\) and \(B\).

    1. Find \(A\) and \(B\).
    2. Find the length of the chord \(AB\).
    3. Find the midpoint \(M\) of \(AB\) and show that \(OM\perp AB\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(x^2+(7-x)^2=25\Rightarrow 2x^2-14x+24=0\Rightarrow x^2-7x+12=0\Rightarrow x=3,4\). \(A(3,4)\), \(B(4,3)\).

    (b) \(AB=\sqrt{1+1}=\sqrt2\)

    (c) \(M(3.5,\ 3.5)\). \(m_{OM}=1\), \(m_{AB}=-1\), product \(-1\), so \(OM\perp AB\). (The line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.)

  2. For which values of \(m\) does the line \(y=mx+5\) not meet the circle \(x^2+y^2=5\)? For which values is it tangent?

    Show solution

    \(x^2+(mx+5)^2=5\Rightarrow (1+m^2)x^2+10mx+20=0\)

    \(\Delta=100m^2-80(1+m^2)=20m^2-80\)

    No meeting point: \(\Delta\lt 0\Rightarrow m^2\lt 4\Rightarrow -2\lt m\lt 2\).

    Tangent: \(\Delta=0\Rightarrow m=2\) or \(m=-2\).

  3. The parabola \(y=-x^2+6x-5\) and the line \(y=x-1\) meet at \(A\) and \(B\). \(V\) is the vertex of the parabola.

    1. Find \(A\) and \(B\).
    2. Find \(V\).
    3. Find the area of triangle \(ABV\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(-x^2+6x-5=x-1\Rightarrow x^2-5x+4=0\Rightarrow x=1,4\). \(A(1,0)\), \(B(4,3)\).

    (b) \(x_V=-\dfrac{6}{-2}=3\), \(y_V=-9+18-5=4\). \(V(3,4)\).

    (c) Draw the vertical line through \(V\); it meets \(AB\) at \((3,2)\). The segment from \((3,2)\) to \(V\) has length 2.

    Area \(=\dfrac12\cdot 2\cdot(4-1)=3\) (the base is the vertical segment, and the height is the horizontal distance from \(x=1\) to \(x=4\)).

  4. Consider the parabola \(y=x^2-6x+5\) and the horizontal line \(y=k\).

    1. For which \(k\) do they meet at two points? At one point? Not at all?
    2. For \(k=5\), find the meeting points and the distance between them.
    Show solution

    (a) The vertex is at \(x=3\), \(y=9-18+5=-4\).

    Two points: \(k\gt -4\). One point: \(k=-4\). None: \(k\lt -4\).

    (b) \(x^2-6x+5=5\Rightarrow x(x-6)=0\). Points \((0,5)\) and \((6,5)\), distance \(6\).

  5. The points \(A(-5,0)\), \(B(5,0)\) and \(C(3,4)\) lie on the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\).

    1. Check that \(C\) is on the circle.
    2. Show that angle \(ACB\) is a right angle.
    3. Find the area of triangle \(ABC\).
    4. What do you notice about \(AB\)?
    Show solution

    (a) \(3^2+4^2=25\) ✓

    (b) \(m_{CA}=\dfrac{4}{8}=\dfrac12\), \(m_{CB}=\dfrac{4}{-2}=-2\). Product \(-1\), so the angle at \(C\) is right.

    (c) Base \(AB=10\), height \(4\): area \(=20\).

    (d) \(AB\) is a diameter. An angle that stands on a diameter is a right angle (you will prove this in the unit on the circle).

  6. Find the equation of the tangent to the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\) at \(P(3,4)\), and find where it meets the x-axis.

    Show solution

    \(m_{OP}=\tfrac43\). The tangent is perpendicular to \(OP\), so its slope is \(-\tfrac34\).

    \(y-4=-\tfrac34(x-3)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac{25}{4}\)

    \(y=0\Rightarrow x=\tfrac{25}{3}\). Point \(\left(\tfrac{25}{3},0\right)\).

  7. Find where the line \(y=x+1\) meets the circle \((x-2)^2+(y-1)^2=10\).

    Show solution

    \(y-1=x\), so \((x-2)^2+x^2=10\Rightarrow 2x^2-4x-6=0\Rightarrow x^2-2x-3=0\)

    \(x=3\) or \(x=-1\). Points \((3,4)\) and \((-1,0)\).

    Check: \(1+9=10\) ✓ and \(9+1=10\) ✓.

  8. The perimeter of a rectangle is 34 cm and its diagonal is 13 cm. Find the sides.

    Show solution

    \(a+b=17\), \(a^2+b^2=169\).

    \(a^2+(17-a)^2=169\Rightarrow 2a^2-34a+120=0\Rightarrow a^2-17a+60=0\)

    \(a=12\) or \(a=5\). The sides are 12 cm and 5 cm.

  9. Consider the line \(x+y=4\) and the circle \(x^2+y^2=k\) (\(k\gt 0\)). For which \(k\) is the line tangent to the circle? For which \(k\) do they meet at two points?

    Show solution

    \(x^2+(4-x)^2=k\Rightarrow 2x^2-8x+16-k=0\)

    \(\Delta=64-8(16-k)=8k-64\)

    Tangent: \(k=8\) (point \((2,2)\), radius \(2\sqrt2\)).

    Two points: \(k\gt 8\). No meeting point: \(0\lt k\lt 8\).

  10. Find the points on the circle \(x^2+y^2=20\) whose y-coordinate is twice their x-coordinate. Show that the segment between them is a diameter.

    Show solution

    \(y=2x\Rightarrow x^2+4x^2=20\Rightarrow x=\pm2\)

    Points \((2,4)\) and \((-2,-4)\).

    The midpoint is \((0,0)\), the centre, so the segment passes through the centre: it is a diameter (length \(2\sqrt{20}=4\sqrt5\)).
