Before you start: After every system, substitute your answer into the original equations. It takes ten seconds and catches most mistakes.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Solve by substitution: \(y=2x-1\), \(3x+y=9\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(y\) is already isolated in the first equation, so substitute it into the second.

    \(3x+(2x-1)=9\Rightarrow 5x-1=9\Rightarrow 5x=10\Rightarrow x=2\).

    \(y=2\cdot2-1=3\).

    Answer: \((2,3)\).

    Check: \(3\cdot2+3=9\) ✓

  2. Solve by elimination: \(2x+3y=7\), \(4x-3y=5\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The coefficients of \(y\) are \(+3\) and \(-3\), so adding the equations eliminates \(y\).

    \((2x+3y)+(4x-3y)=7+5\Rightarrow 6x=12\Rightarrow x=2\).

    Substitute in the first: \(4+3y=7\Rightarrow 3y=3\Rightarrow y=1\).

    Answer: \((2,1)\).

    Check: \(4\cdot2-3\cdot1=5\) ✓

  3. Solve: \(x+2y=8\), \(3x-y=3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Isolate \(x\) in the first equation (its coefficient is 1) and substitute into the second.

    \(x=8-2y\).

    \(3(8-2y)-y=3\Rightarrow 24-6y-y=3\Rightarrow -7y=-21\Rightarrow y=3\).

    \(x=8-6=2\).

    Answer: \((2,3)\).

    Check: \(3\cdot2-3=3\) ✓

  4. Without solving, decide how many solutions each system has.

    1. \(y=3x+1\), \(y=3x-2\)
    2. \(2x-y=4\), \(6x-3y=12\)
    3. \(y=x+1\), \(y=-x+3\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Two lines meet once if their slopes differ; never if they are parallel (equal slopes, different intercepts); everywhere if they are the same line.

    (a) Both slopes are 3 and the intercepts are 1 and \(-2\): parallel lines.

    (b) \(2x-y=4\Rightarrow y=2x-4\). \(6x-3y=12\Rightarrow\) divide by 3: \(2x-y=4\), the same equation. The same line.

    (c) Slopes 1 and \(-1\) differ, so the lines cross once.

    Answer: (a) no solution; (b) infinitely many; (c) exactly one.

  5. Write the equation of the circle centred at the origin.

    1. radius 4
    2. radius \(\sqrt7\)
    3. passing through \((5,-12)\)
    Show solution

    Idea. A circle centred at the origin is \(x^2+y^2=R^2\): the right-hand side is the radius squared.

    (a) \(R=4\Rightarrow R^2=16\): \(x^2+y^2=16\).

    (b) \(R=\sqrt7\Rightarrow R^2=7\): \(x^2+y^2=7\).

    (c) The point is on the circle: \(R^2=5^2+(-12)^2=25+144=169\): \(x^2+y^2=169\) (radius 13).

    Answer: (a) \(x^2+y^2=16\); (b) \(x^2+y^2=7\); (c) \(x^2+y^2=169\).

  6. Find the radius of each circle.

    1. \(x^2+y^2=36\)
    2. \(2x^2+2y^2=50\)
    3. \(x^2+y^2=12\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Bring each equation to the form \(x^2+y^2=R^2\); the radius is the square root of the right-hand side.

    (a) \(R^2=36\Rightarrow R=6\).

    (b) Divide by 2: \(x^2+y^2=25\Rightarrow R=5\).

    (c) \(R=\sqrt{12}=\sqrt{4\cdot3}=2\sqrt3\approx3.46\).

    Answer: (a) 6; (b) 5; (c) \(2\sqrt3\).

    Watch out: the radius is \(\sqrt{R^2}\), not the number on the right.

  7. Decide whether each point is inside, on, or outside the circle \(x^2+y^2=20\).

    1. \((2,4)\)
    2. \((3,3)\)
    3. \((-4,3)\)
    4. \((1,-4)\)
    Show solution

    Idea. \(x^2+y^2\) is the squared distance from the centre. Compare it with \(R^2=20\): equal means on, smaller means inside, larger means outside.

    (a) \(2^2+4^2=20\): on the circle.

    (b) \(3^2+3^2=18\lt 20\): inside.

    (c) \((-4)^2+3^2=25\gt 20\): outside.

    (d) \(1^2+(-4)^2=17\lt 20\): inside.

    Answer: (a) on; (b) inside; (c) outside; (d) inside.

  8. Find where the parabola \(y=x^2\) meets the line \(y=4\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Meeting points have the same \(y\) on both graphs: \(x^2=4\).

    \(x^2=4\) has two solutions, \(x=2\) and \(x=-2\); in both cases \(y=4\).

    Answer: \((2,4)\) and \((-2,4)\).

  9. Find where the parabola \(y=x^2-1\) meets the line \(y=x+1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Set the two expressions for \(y\) equal and solve the quadratic equation.

    \(x^2-1=x+1\Rightarrow x^2-x-2=0\Rightarrow (x-2)(x+1)=0\).

    \(x=2\Rightarrow y=3\); \(x=-1\Rightarrow y=0\).

    Answer: \((2,3)\) and \((-1,0)\).

    Check: \(2^2-1=3\) ✓, \((-1)^2-1=0\) ✓

  10. Find the points on the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\) whose x-coordinate is 3.

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute \(x=3\) into the circle equation and solve for \(y\).

    \(9+y^2=25\Rightarrow y^2=16\Rightarrow y=4\) or \(y=-4\).

    Answer: \((3,4)\) and \((3,-4)\), one above and one below the x-axis.

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Solve: \(\dfrac{x}{2}+\dfrac{y}{3}=4\), \(x-y=3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Clear the fractions first by multiplying by the common denominator 6, then substitute.

    \(6\cdot\tfrac x2+6\cdot\tfrac y3=6\cdot4\Rightarrow 3x+2y=24\).

    From the second equation \(x=y+3\).

    \(3(y+3)+2y=24\Rightarrow 5y+9=24\Rightarrow y=3\); \(x=6\).

    Answer: \((6,3)\).

    Check: \(\tfrac62+\tfrac33=3+1=4\) ✓

  2. Consider the system \(2x+ay=4\), \(x+3y=2\). For which values of \(a\) does it have exactly one solution? Infinitely many? No solution?

    Show solution

    Idea. Make the \(x\)-coefficients equal and compare the rest of the two equations.

    Multiply the second equation by 2: \(2x+6y=4\). The first is \(2x+ay=4\).

    If \(a\ne6\), the coefficients of \(y\) differ, so the lines have different slopes: exactly one solution.

    If \(a=6\), both equations are \(2x+6y=4\): the same line, infinitely many solutions.

    The right-hand sides are already equal (both 4), so the lines can never be parallel and distinct.

    Answer: one solution for \(a\ne6\); infinitely many for \(a=6\); no value gives no solution.

  3. Consider the system \(kx+y=3\), \(4x+ky=6\). Find the values of \(k\) for which the system has no solution, and those for which it has infinitely many.

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute and look at the coefficient of \(x\). When it is 0, the equation becomes either always true or impossible.

    From the first equation \(y=3-kx\). Substitute: \(4x+k(3-kx)=6\Rightarrow 4x+3k-k^2x=6\Rightarrow(4-k^2)x=6-3k\).

    If \(4-k^2\ne0\) (\(k\ne\pm2\)), divide: one solution.

    \(k=2\): \(0\cdot x=6-6=0\), true for every \(x\): infinitely many solutions.

    \(k=-2\): \(0\cdot x=6+6=12\), impossible: no solution.

    Answer: no solution for \(k=-2\); infinitely many for \(k=2\).

    Check: \(k=2\) gives \(2x+y=3\) and \(4x+2y=6\), the same line ✓

  4. Find the meeting points of \(y=x^2-4x+3\) and \(y=x-1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Set the expressions equal and solve.

    \(x^2-4x+3=x-1\Rightarrow x^2-5x+4=0\Rightarrow(x-1)(x-4)=0\).

    \(x=1\Rightarrow y=0\); \(x=4\Rightarrow y=3\).

    Answer: \((1,0)\) and \((4,3)\).

  5. Find the meeting points of \(y=x^2+2x\) and \(y=-x-2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Set the expressions equal and solve.

    \(x^2+2x=-x-2\Rightarrow x^2+3x+2=0\Rightarrow(x+1)(x+2)=0\).

    \(x=-1\Rightarrow y=-(-1)-2=-1\); \(x=-2\Rightarrow y=2-2=0\).

    Answer: \((-1,-1)\) and \((-2,0)\).

  6. For which value of \(k\) is the line \(y=2x+k\) tangent to the parabola \(y=x^2+1\)? Find the point of tangency.

    Show solution

    Idea. A line is tangent to a parabola when they meet at exactly one point, that is, when the quadratic equation has discriminant \(\Delta=0\).

    \(x^2+1=2x+k\Rightarrow x^2-2x+(1-k)=0\).

    \(\Delta=(-2)^2-4\cdot1\cdot(1-k)=4-4+4k=4k\).

    \(\Delta=0\Rightarrow k=0\). Then \(x^2-2x+1=(x-1)^2=0\Rightarrow x=1\), \(y=2\).

    Answer: \(k=0\); point of tangency \((1,2)\).

  7. Find where the line \(y=x+2\) meets the circle \(x^2+y^2=10\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute the line into the circle to get a quadratic in \(x\).

    \(x^2+(x+2)^2=10\Rightarrow x^2+x^2+4x+4=10\Rightarrow 2x^2+4x-6=0\).

    Divide by 2: \(x^2+2x-3=0\Rightarrow(x+3)(x-1)=0\).

    \(x=1\Rightarrow y=3\); \(x=-3\Rightarrow y=-1\).

    Answer: \((1,3)\) and \((-3,-1)\).

    Check: \(1+9=10\) ✓, \(9+1=10\) ✓

  8. Write the equation of the circle with centre \((3,-2)\) and radius 5. Does the point \((6,2)\) lie on it?

    Show solution

    Idea. A circle with centre \((a,b)\) and radius \(R\) is \((x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=R^2\). Substitute the point to test it.

    \((x-3)^2+(y-(-2))^2=5^2\Rightarrow(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=25\).

    \((6-3)^2+(2+2)^2=9+16=25\).

    Answer: \((x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=25\); yes, \((6,2)\) is on it.

  9. Find the centre and the radius of each circle.

    1. \((x+1)^2+(y-4)^2=12\)
    2. \(x^2+(y-5)^2=1\)
    3. \((x-2)^2+y^2=\tfrac14\)
    Show solution

    Idea. In \((x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=R^2\), the centre is \((a,b)\) (opposite signs to the brackets) and the radius is \(\sqrt{R^2}\).

    (a) \(x+1=x-(-1)\): centre \((-1,4)\); \(R=\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3\).

    (b) \(x^2=(x-0)^2\): centre \((0,5)\); \(R=1\).

    (c) centre \((2,0)\); \(R=\sqrt{\tfrac14}=\tfrac12\).

    Answer: (a) \((-1,4)\), \(2\sqrt3\); (b) \((0,5)\), 1; (c) \((2,0)\), \(\tfrac12\).

  10. A cinema sold 12 tickets for 375 shekels. An adult ticket costs 40 shekels and a child ticket 25 shekels. How many of each were sold?

    Show solution

    Idea. Two unknowns (adult and child tickets) and two facts (number of tickets, total price) give a system of two equations.

    Let \(a\) = adult tickets, \(c\) = child tickets: \(a+c=12\) and \(40a+25c=375\).

    From the first \(c=12-a\). Substitute: \(40a+25(12-a)=375\Rightarrow 40a+300-25a=375\Rightarrow 15a=75\Rightarrow a=5\).

    \(c=12-5=7\).

    Answer: 5 adult tickets and 7 child tickets.

    Check: \(5\cdot40+7\cdot25=200+175=375\) ✓

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. The line \(y=-x+7\) meets the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\) at \(A\) and \(B\).

    1. Find \(A\) and \(B\).
    2. Find the length of the chord \(AB\).
    3. Find the midpoint \(M\) of \(AB\) and show that \(OM\perp AB\).
    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute the line into the circle to find \(A\) and \(B\); then use the distance and midpoint formulas, and compare slopes.

    (a) \(x^2+(7-x)^2=25\Rightarrow x^2+49-14x+x^2=25\Rightarrow 2x^2-14x+24=0\Rightarrow x^2-7x+12=0\Rightarrow(x-3)(x-4)=0\).

    \(x=3\Rightarrow y=4\); \(x=4\Rightarrow y=3\). \(A(3,4)\), \(B(4,3)\).

    (b) \(AB=\sqrt{(4-3)^2+(3-4)^2}=\sqrt2\).

    (c) \(M\left(\tfrac{3+4}2,\tfrac{4+3}2\right)=(3.5,3.5)\). \(m_{OM}=\tfrac{3.5}{3.5}=1\); \(m_{AB}=-1\) (the slope of \(y=-x+7\)).

    \(1\cdot(-1)=-1\), so \(OM\perp AB\).

    Answer: (a) \((3,4)\), \((4,3)\); (b) \(\sqrt2\); (c) \(M(3.5,3.5)\), \(OM\perp AB\). This is a general fact: the line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

  2. For which values of \(m\) does the line \(y=mx+5\) not meet the circle \(x^2+y^2=5\)? For which values is it tangent?

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute the line into the circle. The number of meeting points is decided by the discriminant: \(\Delta\lt 0\) none, \(\Delta=0\) one (tangent), \(\Delta\gt 0\) two.

    \(x^2+(mx+5)^2=5\Rightarrow x^2+m^2x^2+10mx+25=5\Rightarrow(1+m^2)x^2+10mx+20=0\).

    \(\Delta=(10m)^2-4(1+m^2)\cdot20=100m^2-80-80m^2=20m^2-80\).

    No meeting point: \(20m^2-80\lt 0\Rightarrow m^2\lt 4\Rightarrow -2\lt m\lt 2\).

    Tangent: \(20m^2-80=0\Rightarrow m^2=4\Rightarrow m=\pm2\).

    Answer: no meeting point for \(-2\lt m\lt 2\); tangent for \(m=2\) or \(m=-2\).

    Check: the line passes through \((0,5)\), outside the circle (radius \(\sqrt5\approx2.24\)), so steep lines meet it and flat lines miss it ✓

  3. The parabola \(y=-x^2+6x-5\) and the line \(y=x-1\) meet at \(A\) and \(B\). \(V\) is the vertex of the parabola.

    1. Find \(A\) and \(B\).
    2. Find \(V\).
    3. Find the area of triangle \(ABV\).
    Show solution

    Idea. Meeting points from an equation; the vertex from \(x=-\tfrac b{2a}\); the area by cutting the triangle with a vertical line through \(V\).

    (a) \(-x^2+6x-5=x-1\Rightarrow 0=x^2-5x+4=(x-1)(x-4)\). \(A(1,0)\), \(B(4,3)\).

    (b) \(x_V=-\dfrac{6}{2\cdot(-1)}=3\), \(y_V=-9+18-5=4\). \(V(3,4)\).

    (c) The vertical line \(x=3\) meets \(AB\) (the line \(y=x-1\)) at \((3,2)\). The segment from \((3,2)\) to \(V(3,4)\) has length 2.

    This segment splits triangle \(ABV\) into two triangles with this common base; their heights are the horizontal distances \(3-1=2\) and \(4-3=1\).

    Area \(=\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot2+\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot1=2+1=3\).

    Answer: (a) \(A(1,0)\), \(B(4,3)\); (b) \(V(3,4)\); (c) area 3.

  4. Consider the parabola \(y=x^2-6x+5\) and the horizontal line \(y=k\).

    1. For which \(k\) do they meet at two points? At one point? Not at all?
    2. For \(k=5\), find the meeting points and the distance between them.
    Show solution

    Idea. The parabola opens upward, so its lowest point is the vertex. A horizontal line above the vertex cuts it twice, through the vertex touches it once, below it misses it.

    (a) Vertex: \(x=-\tfrac{-6}{2}=3\), \(y=9-18+5=-4\).

    Two points for \(k\gt -4\); one point for \(k=-4\); none for \(k\lt -4\).

    (b) \(x^2-6x+5=5\Rightarrow x^2-6x=0\Rightarrow x(x-6)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=6\).

    The points are \((0,5)\) and \((6,5)\); they have the same height, so the distance is \(6-0=6\).

    Answer: (a) two for \(k\gt -4\), one for \(k=-4\), none for \(k\lt -4\); (b) \((0,5)\), \((6,5)\), distance 6.

  5. The points \(A(-5,0)\), \(B(5,0)\) and \(C(3,4)\) lie on the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\).

    1. Check that \(C\) is on the circle.
    2. Show that angle \(ACB\) is a right angle.
    3. Find the area of triangle \(ABC\).
    4. What do you notice about \(AB\)?
    Show solution

    Idea. Check by substitution; a right angle shows as perpendicular slopes; the area uses base \(AB\) on the x-axis and the height of \(C\).

    (a) \(3^2+4^2=9+16=25\) ✓

    (b) \(m_{CA}=\dfrac{4-0}{3-(-5)}=\dfrac48=\dfrac12\); \(m_{CB}=\dfrac{4-0}{3-5}=-2\). \(\tfrac12\cdot(-2)=-1\), so \(CA\perp CB\).

    (c) \(AB=10\) (from \(-5\) to 5) and the height is the y-coordinate of \(C\), 4. Area \(=\tfrac12\cdot10\cdot4=20\).

    (d) \(A\) and \(B\) are opposite ends of a line through the centre \(O\): \(AB\) is a diameter.

    Answer: (a) yes; (b) right angle at \(C\); (c) 20; (d) \(AB\) is a diameter. An angle that stands on a diameter is always right (Unit 19).

  6. Find the equation of the tangent to the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\) at \(P(3,4)\), and find where it meets the x-axis.

    Show solution

    Idea. The tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.

    Slope of the radius \(OP\): \(\tfrac{4-0}{3-0}=\tfrac43\). Tangent slope: \(-\tfrac34\).

    \(y-4=-\tfrac34(x-3)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac94+4=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac{25}4\).

    On the x-axis: \(0=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac{25}4\Rightarrow \tfrac34x=\tfrac{25}4\Rightarrow x=\tfrac{25}3\).

    Answer: \(y=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac{25}4\); it meets the x-axis at \(\left(\tfrac{25}3,0\right)\).

  7. Find where the line \(y=x+1\) meets the circle \((x-2)^2+(y-1)^2=10\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute the line into the circle. Here \(y-1=x\), which makes the substitution short.

    \((x-2)^2+x^2=10\Rightarrow x^2-4x+4+x^2=10\Rightarrow 2x^2-4x-6=0\Rightarrow x^2-2x-3=0\Rightarrow(x-3)(x+1)=0\).

    \(x=3\Rightarrow y=4\); \(x=-1\Rightarrow y=0\).

    Answer: \((3,4)\) and \((-1,0)\).

    Check: \((3-2)^2+(4-1)^2=1+9=10\) ✓; \((-1-2)^2+(0-1)^2=9+1=10\) ✓

  8. The perimeter of a rectangle is 34 cm and its diagonal is 13 cm. Find the sides.

    Show solution

    Idea. The perimeter gives \(a+b\); the diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs \(a,b\), so Pythagoras gives \(a^2+b^2\).

    \(2(a+b)=34\Rightarrow a+b=17\). \(a^2+b^2=13^2=169\).

    \(b=17-a\): \(a^2+(17-a)^2=169\Rightarrow 2a^2-34a+289=169\Rightarrow 2a^2-34a+120=0\Rightarrow a^2-17a+60=0\).

    \((a-5)(a-12)=0\Rightarrow a=5\) or \(a=12\) (and then \(b\) is the other one).

    Answer: the sides are 5 cm and 12 cm.

    Check: \(5^2+12^2=169\) ✓, \(2(5+12)=34\) ✓

  9. Consider the line \(x+y=4\) and the circle \(x^2+y^2=k\) (\(k\gt 0\)). For which \(k\) is the line tangent to the circle? For which \(k\) do they meet at two points?

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute the line into the circle and use the discriminant to count meeting points.

    \(y=4-x\): \(x^2+(4-x)^2=k\Rightarrow 2x^2-8x+16-k=0\).

    \(\Delta=64-4\cdot2\cdot(16-k)=64-128+8k=8k-64\).

    Tangent: \(\Delta=0\Rightarrow k=8\). Then \(2x^2-8x+8=0\Rightarrow x=2\), the point \((2,2)\); the radius is \(\sqrt8=2\sqrt2\).

    Two points: \(\Delta\gt 0\Rightarrow k\gt 8\). No point: \(0\lt k\lt 8\).

    Answer: tangent for \(k=8\); two meeting points for \(k\gt 8\).

    Check: the distance from \(O\) to the line is \(OH\) with \(H(2,2)\): \(\sqrt8\), equal to the radius when \(k=8\) ✓

  10. Find the points on the circle \(x^2+y^2=20\) whose y-coordinate is twice their x-coordinate. Show that the segment between them is a diameter.

    Show solution

    Idea. "y is twice x" is the line \(y=2x\). A chord is a diameter when it passes through the centre, i.e. its midpoint is the centre.

    \(x^2+(2x)^2=20\Rightarrow 5x^2=20\Rightarrow x^2=4\Rightarrow x=\pm2\).

    Points \((2,4)\) and \((-2,-4)\).

    Midpoint: \(\left(\tfrac{2-2}2,\tfrac{4-4}2\right)=(0,0)\), the centre. So the segment passes through the centre: it is a diameter.

    Its length: \(\sqrt{4^2+8^2}=\sqrt{80}=4\sqrt5\), which equals \(2R=2\sqrt{20}\) ✓

    Answer: \((2,4)\) and \((-2,-4)\); the segment is a diameter.
