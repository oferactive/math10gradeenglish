1. Linear and quadratic functions: a review

A linear function \(f(x)=mx+b\) has a straight-line graph. A quadratic function has a parabola as its graph. A quadratic can be written in three useful forms:

Standard form\(f(x)=ax^2+bx+c\)
Vertex form\(f(x)=a(x-p)^2+k\)
Factored form\(f(x)=a(x-x_1)(x-x_2)\)

In vertex form, the vertex is \((p,k)\). In factored form, the roots are \(x_1\) and \(x_2\). If \(a\gt 0\) the parabola opens upward (a minimum); if \(a\lt 0\) it opens downward (a maximum). The x-coordinate of the vertex is \(x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}\), halfway between the roots.

Example 1

For \(f(x)=x^2-6x+8\), find the roots, the vertex and the y-intercept.

Solution.

\(x^2-6x+8=(x-2)(x-4)\): roots \(2\) and \(4\).

Vertex at \(x=3\): \(f(3)=9-18+8=-1\), so \((3,-1)\).

y-intercept: \(f(0)=8\).

2. Power functions

A power function is \(f(x)=x^n\) with \(n\) a whole number. Linear (\(n=1\)) and quadratic (\(n=2\)) are the first two. The shape depends on whether \(n\) is even or odd:

Even \(n\) (\(x^2, x^4,\dots\))Odd \(n\) (\(x^3, x^5,\dots\))
Symmetryabout the y-axis: \(f(-x)=f(x)\)about the origin: \(f(-x)=-f(x)\)
Passes through\((0,0),\ (1,1),\ (-1,1)\)\((0,0),\ (1,1),\ (-1,-1)\)
Valuesnever negativenegative for \(x\lt 0\)
As \(x\to\pm\infty\)both ends go upleft end down, right end up

The higher the power, the flatter the graph between \(-1\) and \(1\), and the steeper outside that interval.

Families of graphs. Shifting and stretching \(x^n\) gives \(y=a(x-p)^n+k\). The point \((0,0)\) moves to \((p,k)\), and \(a\) stretches the graph (and flips it if \(a\lt 0\)). The next units study this in detail.

Even and odd functions in general. A function is even if \(f(-x)=f(x)\) for every \(x\), and odd if \(f(-x)=-f(x)\). For example \(x^4-3x^2\) is even, \(x^3+2x\) is odd, and \(x^3+1\) is neither.

3. Polynomials, roots and multiplicity

A polynomial is a sum of power functions with coefficients, for example \(f(x)=2x^3-x^2+5\). The highest power is the degree, and its coefficient is the leading coefficient.

When a polynomial is written as a product of factors, its roots can be read directly. A factor that appears \(k\) times gives a root of multiplicity \(k\):

Multiplicity of the rootWhat the graph does there
odd (1, 3, …)crosses the x-axis (the sign changes)
even (2, 4, …)touches the x-axis and turns back (the sign does not change)
Example 2

Describe the roots of \(f(x)=(x+1)(x-2)^2\).

Solution.

\(x=-1\): multiplicity 1, the graph crosses the axis.

\(x=2\): multiplicity 2, the graph touches the axis.

\(f(0)=1\cdot4=4\), so the graph meets the y-axis at \((0,4)\).

To factor, look for a common factor first, then use known patterns: \(x^3-4x=x(x-2)(x+2)\), \(x^3-6x^2+9x=x(x-3)^2\).

4. Where a polynomial is positive or negative

The roots split the x-axis into intervals. Inside each interval the sign of the polynomial does not change, so one test value is enough. A sign table organises this.

Example 3

Where is \(f(x)=x(x-2)(x+1)\) positive and where is it negative?

Solution.

Roots: \(-1,\ 0,\ 2\), each of multiplicity 1.

\(x\)\(x\lt -1\)\(-1\lt x\lt 0\)\(0\lt x\lt 2\)\(x\gt 2\)
test value\(-2\)\(-0.5\)\(1\)\(3\)
sign of \(f\)−+−+

\(f\gt 0\) for \(-1\lt x\lt 0\) or \(x\gt 2\); \(f\lt 0\) for \(x\lt -1\) or \(0\lt x\lt 2\).

Shortcut: start at the far right, where the sign is the sign of the leading coefficient. Moving left, the sign changes at every root of odd multiplicity and stays the same at every root of even multiplicity.

5. Behaviour at infinity and the number of turns

For very large \(|x|\), the term with the highest power is much bigger than all the others, so it decides the behaviour of the graph. For example, \(f(x)=-2x^3+100x^2\) behaves like \(-2x^3\): it goes down on the right and up on the left.

DegreeLeading coefficient \(\gt 0\)Leading coefficient \(\lt 0\)
evenboth ends up ↑ ↑both ends down ↓ ↓
oddleft down, right up ↓ ↑left up, right down ↑ ↓

A polynomial of degree \(n\) has at most \(n\) roots and at most \(n-1\) extreme points (turning points). A polynomial of odd degree always has at least one root, because its two ends go in opposite directions.

6. A first look at rate of change

The average rate of change of \(f\) between \(x=a\) and \(x=b\) is the slope of the line through \((a,f(a))\) and \((b,f(b))\):

\(\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\)
average rate of change on the interval \([a,b]\)

For a linear function the rate of change is the same everywhere. For other functions it changes from place to place. This idea leads to the derivative in Unit 07.

Example 4

Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=x^2\) on \([1,3]\) and on \([3,5]\).

Solution.

\([1,3]\): \(\dfrac{9-1}{2}=4\)

\([3,5]\): \(\dfrac{25-9}{2}=8\)

The graph becomes steeper as \(x\) grows.

7. Common mistakes

  • Reading the roots of \((x+3)(x-1)\) as \(3\) and \(-1\). The roots are \(-3\) and \(1\): set each factor equal to zero.
  • Assuming the graph crosses the axis at every root. At a root of even multiplicity it only touches.
  • Thinking \(-x^4\) is the same as \((-x)^4\). \((-x)^4=x^4\), but \(-x^4\) is never positive.
  • Deciding the end behaviour from the first term written instead of the term with the highest power.
  • Dividing both sides of \(x^3\gt x\) by \(x\). You lose solutions and may flip the inequality. Move everything to one side and factor.
  • Mixing up average rate of change \(\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\) with the change in \(f\), \(f(b)-f(a)\).

8. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Find the degree and the leading coefficient of \(-2x^3+x-7\).

    Show solution

    Degree 3, leading coefficient \(-2\).

  2. Find the roots of \(f(x)=(x-4)(x+1)^2\) and say whether the graph crosses or touches the x-axis at each.

    Show solution

    \(x=4\): multiplicity 1, crosses. \(x=-1\): multiplicity 2, touches.

  3. For which \(x\) is \(x(x-5)\) negative?

    Show solution

    Roots \(0\) and \(5\), parabola opening up: negative for \(0\lt x\lt 5\).

  4. Is \(f(x)=x^5\) even, odd, or neither?

    Show solution

    \(f(-x)=(-x)^5=-x^5=-f(x)\): odd.

  5. How does \(y=-x^6\) behave as \(x\to\infty\) and as \(x\to-\infty\)?

    Show solution

    Even degree, negative leading coefficient: both ends go down to \(-\infty\).

  6. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=x^3\) on \([1,3]\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{27-1}{3-1}=13\)

  7. How is \(y=(x+3)^2-4\) obtained from \(y=x^2\)? Where is its vertex?

    Show solution

    3 units to the left and 4 units down. Vertex \((-3,-4)\).

  8. What is the largest possible number of extreme points of a polynomial of degree 4?

    Show solution

    \(4-1=3\).
