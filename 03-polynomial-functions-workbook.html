Before you start: When a question gives a polynomial, factor it first whenever you can. The factored form shows the roots, their multiplicity and the sign of the function.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the degree and the leading coefficient.

    1. \(3x^4-2x+1\)
    2. \(-x^3+5x^2\)
    3. \((2x-1)(x+3)\)
    Show solution

    (a) degree 4, leading coefficient 3

    (b) degree 3, leading coefficient \(-1\)

    (c) \((2x-1)(x+3)=2x^2+5x-3\): degree 2, leading coefficient 2

  2. Find the roots of \(f(x)=x(x-3)(x+5)\).

    Show solution

    \(x=0,\ 3,\ -5\)

  3. Find the roots, the vertex and the y-intercept of \(y=x^2-6x+5\).

    Show solution

    \((x-1)(x-5)=0\Rightarrow x=1,5\)

    Vertex: \(x=3\), \(y=9-18+5=-4\): \((3,-4)\).

    y-intercept: \(5\).

  4. Compute \(f(-2)\) for \(f(x)=x^2\), \(x^3\) and \(x^4\). Which of these functions are symmetric about the y-axis?

    Show solution

    \(4,\ -8,\ 16\).

    The even powers \(x^2\) and \(x^4\) are symmetric about the y-axis.

  5. Describe what happens to \(y\) as \(x\to\infty\) and as \(x\to-\infty\).

    1. \(y=x^3\)
    2. \(y=-x^4\)
    3. \(y=-2x^5+x\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(+\infty\) on the right, \(-\infty\) on the left

    (b) \(-\infty\) on both sides

    (c) behaves like \(-2x^5\): \(-\infty\) on the right, \(+\infty\) on the left

  6. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=x^2\) on each interval.

    1. \([0,2]\)
    2. \([2,5]\)
    3. \([-3,1]\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(\dfrac{4-0}{2}=2\)

    (b) \(\dfrac{25-4}{3}=7\)

    (c) \(\dfrac{1-9}{4}=-2\)

  7. Factor completely.

    1. \(x^2-5x+6\)
    2. \(x^3-4x\)
    3. \(x^3-6x^2+9x\)
    Show solution

    (a) \((x-2)(x-3)\)

    (b) \(x(x-2)(x+2)\)

    (c) \(x(x^2-6x+9)=x(x-3)^2\)

  8. Find the roots of \(f(x)=(x-1)^2(x+4)^3\) and their multiplicity. Where does the graph cross the x-axis?

    Show solution

    \(x=1\): multiplicity 2, touches.

    \(x=-4\): multiplicity 3, crosses.

  9. Where is \(f(x)=(x-2)(x+3)\) positive? Where is it negative?

    Show solution

    Positive: \(x\lt -3\) or \(x\gt 2\). Negative: \(-3\lt x\lt 2\).

  10. Describe how the graph of \(y=(x-2)^3+1\) is obtained from \(y=x^3\). Where does the point \((0,0)\) move?

    Show solution

    Shift 2 units right and 1 unit up. \((0,0)\) moves to \((2,1)\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Make a sign table for \(f(x)=x(x-2)(x+1)\).

    Show solution

    Roots \(-1,0,2\). Degree 3, leading coefficient positive, so the far right is positive:

    \(x\lt -1\)\(-1\lt x\lt 0\)\(0\lt x\lt 2\)\(x\gt 2\)
    −+−+

  2. Where is \(f(x)=-(x-1)(x+2)^2\) positive?

    Show solution

    Roots: \(1\) (crosses) and \(-2\) (touches). For \(x\gt 1\), \(f\lt 0\) (leading coefficient \(-1\)).

    Crossing at \(1\): positive for \(-2\lt x\lt 1\). Touching at \(-2\): still positive for \(x\lt -2\).

    \(f\gt 0\) for \(x\lt 1,\ x\ne-2\).

  3. Find the polynomial of degree 3 with roots \(-1\), \(2\), \(4\) whose graph passes through \((0,16)\).

    Show solution

    \(f(x)=a(x+1)(x-2)(x-4)\)

    \(f(0)=a\cdot1\cdot(-2)\cdot(-4)=8a=16\Rightarrow a=2\)

    \(f(x)=2(x+1)(x-2)(x-4)\)

  4. Describe the graph of \(y=x^2(x-3)\): roots, sign and end behaviour.

    Show solution

    Roots: \(0\) (touches) and \(3\) (crosses).

    Negative for \(x\lt 0\) and for \(0\lt x\lt 3\); positive for \(x\gt 3\).

    Left end down, right end up (degree 3, leading coefficient 1).

  5. What is the largest possible number of extreme points for a polynomial of degree 5? Of degree 4? Explain why every polynomial of degree 3 has at least one root.

    Show solution

    Degree 5: at most 4. Degree 4: at most 3.

    A degree 3 polynomial goes to \(-\infty\) on one side and to \(+\infty\) on the other, so its unbroken graph must cross the x-axis at least once.

  6. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=x^3\) on \([0,1]\), \([1,2]\) and \([2,3]\). What does the result tell you about the graph?

    Show solution

    \(1,\ 7,\ 19\).

    The rate of change grows, so the graph gets steeper and steeper for \(x\gt 0\).

  7. For \(g(x)=2(x+1)^2-8\), find the vertex, the roots and the y-intercept.

    Show solution

    Vertex \((-1,-8)\).

    \(2(x+1)^2=8\Rightarrow (x+1)^2=4\Rightarrow x=1\) or \(x=-3\).

    \(g(0)=2-8=-6\).

  8. The parabola \(y=a(x-1)(x-5)\) passes through \((3,8)\). Find \(a\) and the vertex.

    Show solution

    \(a\cdot2\cdot(-2)=8\Rightarrow a=-2\)

    The vertex is halfway between the roots, at \(x=3\): vertex \((3,8)\).

  9. Solve the inequality \(x^3-x\gt 0\).

    Show solution

    \(x(x-1)(x+1)\gt 0\)

    Sign table with roots \(-1,0,1\): positive for \(-1\lt x\lt 0\) or \(x\gt 1\).

  10. Decide whether each function is even, odd or neither.

    1. \(f(x)=x^4-3x^2\)
    2. \(g(x)=x^3+2x\)
    3. \(h(x)=x^3+1\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(f(-x)=x^4-3x^2=f(x)\): even

    (b) \(g(-x)=-x^3-2x=-g(x)\): odd

    (c) \(h(-x)=-x^3+1\), which is neither \(h(x)\) nor \(-h(x)\): neither

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Let \(f(x)=x^3-4x^2+4x\).

    1. Factor \(f\).
    2. Find the roots and describe the graph at each.
    3. Make a sign table.
    4. Solve \(f(x)\le 0\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(f(x)=x(x^2-4x+4)=x(x-2)^2\)

    (b) \(0\): crosses. \(2\): touches.

    (c) \(f\lt 0\) for \(x\lt 0\); \(f\gt 0\) for \(0\lt x\lt 2\) and \(x\gt 2\).

    (d) \(f(x)\le0\) for \(x\le0\) or \(x=2\).

  2. A polynomial of degree 4 has a double root at \(-2\), simple roots at \(1\) and \(3\), and \(f(0)=-24\).

    1. Find \(f(x)\).
    2. Describe the end behaviour.
    3. Where is \(f\) positive?
    Show solution

    (a) \(f(x)=a(x+2)^2(x-1)(x-3)\), \(f(0)=a\cdot4\cdot3=12a=-24\Rightarrow a=-2\).

    (b) Degree 4 with \(a\lt 0\): both ends go down.

    (c) Far right negative; crossing at 3: positive on \((1,3)\); crossing at 1: negative on \((-2,1)\); touching at \(-2\): negative for \(x\lt -2\).

    \(f\gt 0\) only for \(1\lt x\lt 3\).

  3. A ball is thrown upward. Its height after \(t\) seconds is \(h(t)=20t-5t^2\) metres.

    1. Find the average speed between \(t=0\) and \(t=2\).
    2. Find the average rate of change between \(t=2\) and \(t=4\). What does its sign mean?
    3. When does the ball hit the ground?
    4. What is its greatest height?
    Show solution

    (a) \(h(0)=0\), \(h(2)=20\): \(\dfrac{20-0}{2}=10\) m/s.

    (b) \(h(4)=0\): \(\dfrac{0-20}{2}=-10\) m/s. The negative sign means the ball is coming down.

    (c) \(5t(4-t)=0\Rightarrow t=4\) s.

    (d) The vertex is at \(t=2\), so the greatest height is \(20\) m.

  4. From a square sheet of side 12 cm, squares of side \(x\) are cut from the corners and the sides are folded up to make an open box.

    1. Express the volume \(V(x)\).
    2. For which values of \(x\) does the box exist?
    3. Make a table of \(V\) for \(x=1,2,3,4,5\).
    4. Estimate the value of \(x\) that gives the largest volume.
    Show solution

    (a) The base is \((12-2x)\) by \((12-2x)\) and the height is \(x\): \(V(x)=x(12-2x)^2\).

    (b) \(0\lt x\lt 6\).

    (c) \(V(1)=100\), \(V(2)=128\), \(V(3)=108\), \(V(4)=64\), \(V(5)=20\).

    (d) The largest value in the table is near \(x=2\). In Unit 13 you will prove that the maximum is exactly \(V(2)=128\) cm³.

  5. Let \(p(x)=x^3-3x+1\). Show that \(p\) has three roots, one in each of the intervals \((-2,-1)\), \((0,1)\) and \((1,2)\).

    Show solution

    \(p(-2)=-1\), \(p(-1)=3\), \(p(0)=1\), \(p(1)=-1\), \(p(2)=3\).

    The sign changes on each of the three intervals. The graph is unbroken, so it crosses the x-axis in each interval.

    A cubic has at most 3 roots, so these are all of them.

  6. Find \(a\) and \(b\) so that \(f(x)=x^3+ax^2+bx\) has roots at \(2\) and \(-3\).

    Show solution

    \(0\) is also a root (no constant term), so \(f(x)=x(x-2)(x+3)=x(x^2+x-6)=x^3+x^2-6x\).

    \(a=1,\ b=-6\)

  7. For \(y=-(x+1)^4+16\), find the roots, the extreme point and the y-intercept.

    Show solution

    \((x+1)^4=16\Rightarrow x+1=\pm2\Rightarrow x=1\) or \(x=-3\).

    Maximum point \((-1,16)\) (the graph of \(-x^4\) moved 1 left and 16 up).

    y-intercept: \(-1+16=15\).

  8. The graph of a polynomial of degree 3 crosses the x-axis at \(-2\), touches it at \(1\), and passes through \((0,4)\). Find the polynomial.

    Show solution

    \(f(x)=a(x+2)(x-1)^2\)

    \(f(0)=a\cdot2\cdot1=2a=4\Rightarrow a=2\)

    \(f(x)=2(x+2)(x-1)^2\)

  9. For which values of \(x\) is it true that:

    1. \(x^3\gt x^2\)
    2. \(x^4\gt x^2\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(x^3-x^2=x^2(x-1)\gt 0\): \(x\gt 1\).

    (b) \(x^4-x^2=x^2(x-1)(x+1)\gt 0\): \(x\lt -1\) or \(x\gt 1\).

  10. Let \(f(x)=x^2\). Show that the average rate of change of \(f\) on \([a,a+h]\) equals \(2a+h\). What happens when \(h\) becomes very small?

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{(a+h)^2-a^2}{h}=\dfrac{2ah+h^2}{h}=2a+h\)

    As \(h\) shrinks, the average rate approaches \(2a\). This is the rate of change of \(x^2\) at the point \(x=a\): the derivative, which you will meet in Unit 07.
