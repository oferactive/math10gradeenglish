Before you start: When a question gives a polynomial, factor it first whenever you can. The factored form shows the roots, their multiplicity and the sign of the function.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the degree and the leading coefficient.

    1. \(3x^4-2x+1\)
    2. \(-x^3+5x^2\)
    3. \((2x-1)(x+3)\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Find the highest power of \(x\) and the number in front of it. When the polynomial is a product, multiply the leading terms.

    (a) Highest power \(x^4\) with coefficient 3: degree 4, leading coefficient 3.

    (b) Highest power \(x^3\) with coefficient \(-1\): degree 3, leading coefficient \(-1\).

    (c) \((2x-1)(x+3)=2x^2+6x-x-3=2x^2+5x-3\): degree 2, leading coefficient 2.

    Answer: (a) 4 and 3; (b) 3 and \(-1\); (c) 2 and 2.

  2. Find the roots of \(f(x)=x(x-3)(x+5)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. A product equals zero exactly when one of its factors equals zero.

    \(x=0\), or \(x-3=0\Rightarrow x=3\), or \(x+5=0\Rightarrow x=-5\).

    Answer: \(x=0,\ 3,\ -5\).

    Watch out: the root of \(x+5\) is \(-5\), not 5.

  3. Find the roots, the vertex and the y-intercept of \(y=x^2-6x+5\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Factor to find the roots; the vertex is halfway between the roots; the y-intercept is \(f(0)\).

    Two numbers with product 5 and sum \(-6\): \(-1\) and \(-5\). \(x^2-6x+5=(x-1)(x-5)\): roots 1 and 5.

    Vertex: \(x=\tfrac{1+5}2=3\) (or \(-\tfrac{b}{2a}=\tfrac62=3\)); \(y=9-18+5=-4\).

    y-intercept: \(y(0)=5\).

    Answer: roots 1 and 5; vertex \((3,-4)\); y-intercept \((0,5)\).

  4. Compute \(f(-2)\) for \(f(x)=x^2\), \(x^3\) and \(x^4\). Which of these functions are symmetric about the y-axis?

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute \(-2\). An even power removes the minus sign, an odd power keeps it.

    \((-2)^2=4\); \((-2)^3=-8\); \((-2)^4=16\).

    For \(x^2\) and \(x^4\), \(f(-2)=f(2)\): the values at \(x\) and \(-x\) are equal, so the graph is symmetric about the y-axis.

    For \(x^3\), \(f(-2)=-f(2)\): symmetric about the origin instead.

    Answer: \(4,\ -8,\ 16\); \(x^2\) and \(x^4\) are symmetric about the y-axis.

  5. Describe what happens to \(y\) as \(x\to\infty\) and as \(x\to-\infty\).

    1. \(y=x^3\)
    2. \(y=-x^4\)
    3. \(y=-2x^5+x\)
    Show solution

    Idea. For large \(|x|\) the highest-power term decides everything. Odd degree: the two ends go opposite ways; even degree: the same way. A negative leading coefficient flips the picture.

    (a) \(x^3\): odd, positive. Right end up (\(+\infty\)), left end down (\(-\infty\)).

    (b) \(-x^4\): even, negative. Both ends down (\(-\infty\)).

    (c) \(-2x^5+x\) behaves like \(-2x^5\): odd, negative. Right end down, left end up.

    Answer: (a) \(+\infty\) / \(-\infty\); (b) \(-\infty\) on both sides; (c) \(-\infty\) on the right, \(+\infty\) on the left.

  6. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=x^2\) on each interval.

    1. \([0,2]\)
    2. \([2,5]\)
    3. \([-3,1]\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Average rate of change \(=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\).

    (a) \(\dfrac{f(2)-f(0)}{2-0}=\dfrac{4-0}2=2\).

    (b) \(\dfrac{f(5)-f(2)}{5-2}=\dfrac{25-4}3=\dfrac{21}3=7\).

    (c) \(\dfrac{f(1)-f(-3)}{1-(-3)}=\dfrac{1-9}4=-2\).

    Answer: (a) 2; (b) 7; (c) \(-2\). On \([-3,1]\) the function decreases more than it increases, so the average is negative.

  7. Factor completely.

    1. \(x^2-5x+6\)
    2. \(x^3-4x\)
    3. \(x^3-6x^2+9x\)
    Show solution

    Idea. First take out a common factor, then use known patterns: two numbers with given sum and product, difference of squares \(a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)\), or a perfect square \(a^2-2ab+b^2=(a-b)^2\).

    (a) Product 6, sum \(-5\): \(-2\) and \(-3\). \((x-2)(x-3)\).

    (b) Common factor \(x\): \(x(x^2-4)\), then difference of squares: \(x(x-2)(x+2)\).

    (c) Common factor \(x\): \(x(x^2-6x+9)\); \(x^2-6x+9=(x-3)^2\) (perfect square). \(x(x-3)^2\).

    Answer: (a) \((x-2)(x-3)\); (b) \(x(x-2)(x+2)\); (c) \(x(x-3)^2\).

  8. Find the roots of \(f(x)=(x-1)^2(x+4)^3\) and their multiplicity. Where does the graph cross the x-axis?

    Show solution

    Idea. The power of each factor is the multiplicity of its root: odd crosses, even touches.

    \((x-1)^2\): root \(x=1\), multiplicity 2 (even), the graph touches the axis.

    \((x+4)^3\): root \(x=-4\), multiplicity 3 (odd), the graph crosses the axis.

    Answer: \(x=1\) (touches) and \(x=-4\) (crosses); the graph crosses only at \(x=-4\).

  9. Where is \(f(x)=(x-2)(x+3)\) positive? Where is it negative?

    Show solution

    Idea. This is a parabola that opens upward with roots \(-3\) and 2. Upward parabolas are positive outside the roots and negative between them.

    Test \(x=0\) (between the roots): \((0-2)(0+3)=-6\lt 0\).

    Test \(x=5\): \(3\cdot8\gt 0\); test \(x=-5\): \((-7)(-2)\gt 0\).

    Answer: positive for \(x\lt -3\) or \(x\gt 2\); negative for \(-3\lt x\lt 2\).

  10. Describe how the graph of \(y=(x-2)^3+1\) is obtained from \(y=x^3\). Where does the point \((0,0)\) move?

    Show solution

    Idea. \(y=(x-p)^3+k\) is \(y=x^3\) moved \(p\) to the right and \(k\) up. Every point \((x,y)\) moves to \((x+p,y+k)\).

    \(p=2\), \(k=1\): 2 units right and 1 unit up.

    \((0,0)\) moves to \((0+2,0+1)=(2,1)\). This is the centre of symmetry of the new graph.

    Answer: 2 right, 1 up; \((0,0)\to(2,1)\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Make a sign table for \(f(x)=x(x-2)(x+1)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The roots split the axis into intervals. On the far right the sign is the sign of the leading coefficient; moving left, the sign changes at each root of odd multiplicity.

    Roots: \(x=0\), \(x=2\), \(x=-1\), each of multiplicity 1.

    Degree 3 with leading coefficient \(+1\): positive for \(x\gt 2\). Then it changes at 2, at 0 and at \(-1\):

    \(x\lt -1\)\(-1\lt x\lt 0\)\(0\lt x\lt 2\)\(x\gt 2\)
    −+−+

    Check: \(x=1\): \(1\cdot(-1)\cdot2=-2\lt 0\) ✓; \(x=-2\): \((-2)(-4)(-1)=-8\lt 0\) ✓

    Answer: positive on \((-1,0)\) and \((2,\infty)\); negative on \((-\infty,-1)\) and \((0,2)\).

  2. Where is \(f(x)=-(x-1)(x+2)^2\) positive?

    Show solution

    Idea. Use the roots and their multiplicities, starting from the sign on the far right.

    Roots: \(x=1\) (multiplicity 1, crosses) and \(x=-2\) (multiplicity 2, touches).

    Far right, e.g. \(x=2\): \(-(1)(16)=-16\lt 0\).

    Crossing at 1: positive for \(-2\lt x\lt 1\) (check \(x=0\): \(-(-1)(4)=4\gt 0\) ✓).

    Touching at \(-2\): the sign stays positive for \(x\lt -2\) (check \(x=-3\): \(-(-4)(1)=4\gt 0\) ✓).

    At \(x=-2\) itself \(f=0\), which is not positive.

    Answer: \(f(x)\gt 0\) for \(x\lt 1\), except \(x=-2\).

  3. Find the polynomial of degree 3 with roots \(-1\), \(2\), \(4\) whose graph passes through \((0,16)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Roots \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) give the factored form \(a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)(x-r_3)\); the extra point fixes \(a\).

    \(f(x)=a(x+1)(x-2)(x-4)\).

    Substitute \((0,16)\): \(a\cdot(1)\cdot(-2)\cdot(-4)=8a=16\Rightarrow a=2\).

    Answer: \(f(x)=2(x+1)(x-2)(x-4)\).

    Check: \(f(0)=2\cdot1\cdot(-2)\cdot(-4)=16\) ✓

  4. Describe the graph of \(y=x^2(x-3)\): roots, sign and end behaviour.

    Show solution

    Idea. Read roots and multiplicities from the factors, then the signs, then the end behaviour from the leading term \(x^3\).

    \(x^2\): root 0, multiplicity 2, touches. \((x-3)\): root 3, multiplicity 1, crosses.

    Signs: \(x^2\ge0\) always, so the sign is the sign of \(x-3\): negative for \(x\lt 3\) (except \(x=0\) where it is 0), positive for \(x\gt 3\).

    Leading term \(x^3\): left end down, right end up.

    Answer: roots 0 (touches) and 3 (crosses); negative for \(x\lt 0\) and \(0\lt x\lt 3\), positive for \(x\gt 3\); left end down, right end up.

  5. What is the largest possible number of extreme points for a polynomial of degree 5? Of degree 4? Explain why every polynomial of degree 3 has at least one root.

    Show solution

    Idea. The number of turning points is at most the degree minus 1. An odd-degree polynomial has ends going in opposite directions.

    Degree 5: at most \(5-1=4\) extreme points. Degree 4: at most 3.

    A degree-3 polynomial goes to \(-\infty\) at one end and to \(+\infty\) at the other. Its graph has no breaks, so it must cross the x-axis on the way.

    Answer: at most 4 and at most 3; a cubic always has at least one root.

  6. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=x^3\) on \([0,1]\), \([1,2]\) and \([2,3]\). What does the result tell you about the graph?

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute \(\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\) on each interval and compare.

    \([0,1]\): \(\dfrac{1-0}1=1\). \([1,2]\): \(\dfrac{8-1}1=7\). \([2,3]\): \(\dfrac{27-8}1=19\).

    The rates grow: \(1\lt 7\lt 19\).

    Answer: 1, 7, 19. The function grows faster and faster: the graph becomes steeper as \(x\) increases.

  7. For \(g(x)=2(x+1)^2-8\), find the vertex, the roots and the y-intercept.

    Show solution

    Idea. In vertex form \(a(x-p)^2+k\), the vertex is \((p,k)\). Roots: set \(g(x)=0\). y-intercept: \(g(0)\).

    Vertex: \(p=-1\), \(k=-8\): \((-1,-8)\).

    \(2(x+1)^2-8=0\Rightarrow(x+1)^2=4\Rightarrow x+1=2\) or \(x+1=-2\Rightarrow x=1\) or \(x=-3\).

    \(g(0)=2\cdot1-8=-6\).

    Answer: vertex \((-1,-8)\); roots 1 and \(-3\); y-intercept \(-6\).

  8. The parabola \(y=a(x-1)(x-5)\) passes through \((3,8)\). Find \(a\) and the vertex.

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute the given point to find \(a\). The vertex of a parabola lies halfway between its roots.

    \(8=a(3-1)(3-5)=a\cdot2\cdot(-2)=-4a\Rightarrow a=-2\).

    Roots 1 and 5, so the vertex is at \(x=3\); its height is the given \(y=8\).

    Answer: \(a=-2\); vertex \((3,8)\) (a maximum, since \(a\lt 0\)).

  9. Solve the inequality \(x^3-x\gt 0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Never divide by \(x\) (its sign is unknown). Factor, find the roots, make a sign table.

    \(x^3-x=x(x^2-1)=x(x-1)(x+1)\). Roots \(-1,0,1\).

    Leading term \(x^3\): positive for \(x\gt 1\); the sign changes at each root:

    \(x\gt 1\): +; \(0\lt x\lt 1\): −; \(-1\lt x\lt 0\): +; \(x\lt -1\): −.

    Answer: \(-1\lt x\lt 0\) or \(x\gt 1\).

    Check: \(x=-\tfrac12\): \(-\tfrac18+\tfrac12\gt 0\) ✓

  10. Decide whether each function is even, odd or neither.

    1. \(f(x)=x^4-3x^2\)
    2. \(g(x)=x^3+2x\)
    3. \(h(x)=x^3+1\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Replace \(x\) by \(-x\) and simplify. \(f(-x)=f(x)\): even. \(f(-x)=-f(x)\): odd.

    (a) \(f(-x)=(-x)^4-3(-x)^2=x^4-3x^2=f(x)\): even.

    (b) \(g(-x)=(-x)^3+2(-x)=-x^3-2x=-(x^3+2x)=-g(x)\): odd.

    (c) \(h(-x)=-x^3+1\). It is not \(h(x)=x^3+1\), and not \(-h(x)=-x^3-1\): neither.

    Answer: (a) even; (b) odd; (c) neither.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Let \(f(x)=x^3-4x^2+4x\).

    1. Factor \(f\).
    2. Find the roots and describe the graph at each.
    3. Make a sign table.
    4. Solve \(f(x)\le 0\).
    Show solution

    Idea. Factor to see the roots and their multiplicities; the sign table follows; the inequality is read from the table.

    (a) Common factor \(x\): \(x(x^2-4x+4)=x(x-2)^2\).

    (b) Root 0 (multiplicity 1, crosses); root 2 (multiplicity 2, touches).

    (c) \((x-2)^2\ge0\), so the sign is the sign of \(x\): negative for \(x\lt 0\); positive for \(0\lt x\lt 2\) and \(x\gt 2\); zero at 0 and 2.

    (d) \(f(x)\le0\) where \(f\) is negative or zero: \(x\le0\), and also \(x=2\).

    Answer: (a) \(x(x-2)^2\); (d) \(x\le0\) or \(x=2\).

    Watch out: do not forget the single point \(x=2\), where \(f=0\).

  2. A polynomial of degree 4 has a double root at \(-2\), simple roots at \(1\) and \(3\), and \(f(0)=-24\).

    1. Find \(f(x)\).
    2. Describe the end behaviour.
    3. Where is \(f\) positive?
    Show solution

    Idea. Write the factored form from the roots, find \(a\) from \(f(0)\), then use the signs of the factors.

    (a) \(f(x)=a(x+2)^2(x-1)(x-3)\). \(f(0)=a\cdot4\cdot(-1)\cdot(-3)=12a=-24\Rightarrow a=-2\).

    (b) Degree 4 (even) and \(a=-2\lt 0\): both ends go down.

    (c) Far right: negative. Crossing at 3: positive on \((1,3)\). Crossing at 1: negative on \((-2,1)\). Touching at \(-2\): stays negative for \(x\lt -2\).

    Check: \(f(2)=-2\cdot16\cdot1\cdot(-1)=32\gt 0\) ✓

    Answer: (a) \(f(x)=-2(x+2)^2(x-1)(x-3)\); (b) both ends down; (c) positive only for \(1\lt x\lt 3\).

  3. A ball is thrown upward. Its height after \(t\) seconds is \(h(t)=20t-5t^2\) metres.

    1. Find the average speed between \(t=0\) and \(t=2\).
    2. Find the average rate of change between \(t=2\) and \(t=4\). What does its sign mean?
    3. When does the ball hit the ground?
    4. What is its greatest height?
    Show solution

    Idea. Average speed is the average rate of change of the height. The ball is on the ground when \(h=0\); the greatest height is at the vertex of the parabola.

    (a) \(h(0)=0\), \(h(2)=40-20=20\). \(\dfrac{20-0}{2-0}=10\) m/s.

    (b) \(h(4)=80-80=0\). \(\dfrac{0-20}{4-2}=-10\) m/s. Negative: the height decreases, the ball is falling.

    (c) \(20t-5t^2=5t(4-t)=0\Rightarrow t=0\) (the throw) or \(t=4\). It lands after 4 s.

    (d) The vertex is halfway between the roots 0 and 4: \(t=2\), \(h(2)=20\).

    Answer: (a) 10 m/s; (b) \(-10\) m/s (falling); (c) 4 s; (d) 20 m.

  4. From a square sheet of side 12 cm, squares of side \(x\) are cut from the corners and the sides are folded up to make an open box.

    1. Express the volume \(V(x)\).
    2. For which values of \(x\) does the box exist?
    3. Make a table of \(V\) for \(x=1,2,3,4,5\).
    4. Estimate the value of \(x\) that gives the largest volume.
    Show solution

    Idea. Volume = base area × height. The base is a square whose side is 12 minus two cut squares.

    (a) Each side of the base is \(12-2x\); the height is \(x\). \(V(x)=x(12-2x)^2\).

    (b) Lengths must be positive: \(x\gt 0\) and \(12-2x\gt 0\Rightarrow x\lt 6\). So \(0\lt x\lt 6\).

    (c) \(V(1)=1\cdot10^2=100\); \(V(2)=2\cdot8^2=128\); \(V(3)=3\cdot6^2=108\); \(V(4)=4\cdot4^2=64\); \(V(5)=5\cdot2^2=20\).

    (d) The table rises to 128 at \(x=2\) and then falls, so the maximum is near \(x=2\).

    Answer: \(V(x)=x(12-2x)^2\), \(0\lt x\lt 6\); largest volume near \(x=2\) (exactly \(V(2)=128\) cm³, proved in Unit 13).

  5. Let \(p(x)=x^3-3x+1\). Show that \(p\) has three roots, one in each of the intervals \((-2,-1)\), \((0,1)\) and \((1,2)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. If an unbroken graph is negative at one end of an interval and positive at the other, it must cross zero inside the interval.

    \(p(-2)=-8+6+1=-1\); \(p(-1)=-1+3+1=3\): the sign changes on \((-2,-1)\).

    \(p(0)=1\); \(p(1)=1-3+1=-1\): the sign changes on \((0,1)\).

    \(p(2)=8-6+1=3\): the sign changes on \((1,2)\).

    So there is a root in each interval: three roots. A polynomial of degree 3 has at most 3 roots, so there are no others.

    Answer: one root in each of the three intervals, and exactly three roots in all.

  6. Find \(a\) and \(b\) so that \(f(x)=x^3+ax^2+bx\) has roots at \(2\) and \(-3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. There is no constant term, so \(x=0\) is also a root. Three roots of a cubic with leading coefficient 1 give its factored form.

    \(f(x)=x(x-2)(x+3)\).

    Multiply: \((x-2)(x+3)=x^2+x-6\), so \(f(x)=x^3+x^2-6x\).

    Compare with \(x^3+ax^2+bx\): \(a=1\), \(b=-6\).

    Answer: \(a=1\), \(b=-6\).

    Check: \(f(2)=8+4-12=0\) ✓, \(f(-3)=-27+9+18=0\) ✓

  7. For \(y=-(x+1)^4+16\), find the roots, the extreme point and the y-intercept.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(-(x+1)^4+16\) is \(-x^4\) (an upside-down "U") moved 1 left and 16 up, so its highest point is \((-1,16)\).

    Roots: \((x+1)^4=16\Rightarrow x+1=2\) or \(x+1=-2\) (both have fourth power 16) \(\Rightarrow x=1\) or \(x=-3\).

    Maximum point: \((-1,16)\), because \(-(x+1)^4\le0\) with equality only at \(x=-1\).

    y-intercept: \(-(1)^4+16=15\).

    Answer: roots 1 and \(-3\); maximum \((-1,16)\); y-intercept 15.

  8. The graph of a polynomial of degree 3 crosses the x-axis at \(-2\), touches it at \(1\), and passes through \((0,4)\). Find the polynomial.

    Show solution

    Idea. "Crosses at \(-2\)" means a root of odd multiplicity (here 1); "touches at 1" means an even multiplicity (here 2). Degree 3 = 1 + 2.

    \(f(x)=a(x+2)(x-1)^2\).

    \(f(0)=a\cdot2\cdot1=2a=4\Rightarrow a=2\).

    Answer: \(f(x)=2(x+2)(x-1)^2\).

  9. For which values of \(x\) is it true that:

    1. \(x^3\gt x^2\)
    2. \(x^4\gt x^2\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Move everything to one side and factor; then use signs (do not divide by \(x\)).

    (a) \(x^3-x^2\gt 0\Rightarrow x^2(x-1)\gt 0\). \(x^2\gt 0\) for \(x\ne0\), so we need \(x-1\gt 0\), i.e. \(x\gt 1\). (At \(x=0\) the expression is 0, not positive.)

    (b) \(x^4-x^2\gt 0\Rightarrow x^2(x^2-1)\gt 0\). For \(x\ne0\) we need \(x^2\gt 1\), i.e. \(x\lt -1\) or \(x\gt 1\).

    Answer: (a) \(x\gt 1\); (b) \(x\lt -1\) or \(x\gt 1\).

  10. Let \(f(x)=x^2\). Show that the average rate of change of \(f\) on \([a,a+h]\) equals \(2a+h\). What happens when \(h\) becomes very small?

    Show solution

    Idea. Write the average rate of change, expand \((a+h)^2\) and simplify before thinking about small \(h\).

    \(\dfrac{(a+h)^2-a^2}{h}=\dfrac{a^2+2ah+h^2-a^2}{h}=\dfrac{2ah+h^2}h=\dfrac{h(2a+h)}h=2a+h\).

    As \(h\) becomes very small, \(2a+h\) gets as close as we like to \(2a\).

    Answer: \(2a+h\); it approaches \(2a\), the rate of change of \(x^2\) exactly at \(x=a\). This is the derivative \((x^2)'=2x\) (Unit 07).
