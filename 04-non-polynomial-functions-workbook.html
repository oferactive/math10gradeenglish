Before you start: For absolute value questions, think "distance". For square root questions, write down the domain first and check every answer at the end.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Compute.

    1. \(|-7|\)
    2. \(|3-8|\)
    3. \(|2|-|-5|\)
    Show solution

    Idea. The absolute value of a number is its distance from 0. Work inside the bars first.

    (a) \(|-7|=7\).

    (b) \(|3-8|=|-5|=5\).

    (c) \(|2|-|-5|=2-5=-3\). Here the bars are on each number separately, so the result can be negative.

    Answer: (a) 7; (b) 5; (c) \(-3\).

  2. Find the domain of each function.

    1. \(\sqrt{x-3}\)
    2. \(\dfrac1{x+2}\)
    3. \(\dfrac1{x^2}\)
    4. \(\sqrt{5-x}\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Two rules: no negative number under a square root, and no zero in a denominator.

    (a) \(x-3\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge3\).

    (b) \(x+2\ne0\Rightarrow x\ne-2\).

    (c) \(x^2\ne0\Rightarrow x\ne0\).

    (d) \(5-x\ge0\Rightarrow x\le5\).

    Answer: (a) \(x\ge3\); (b) \(x\ne-2\); (c) \(x\ne0\); (d) \(x\le5\).

  3. Solve.

    1. \(|x|=4\)
    2. \(|x-2|=5\)
    3. \(|x+1|=0\)
    4. \(|x|=-3\)
    Show solution

    Idea. \(|x-a|=d\) means "the distance from \(a\) is \(d\)": two answers if \(d\gt 0\), one if \(d=0\), none if \(d\lt 0\).

    (a) Distance 4 from 0: \(x=4\) or \(x=-4\).

    (b) Distance 5 from 2: \(x=2+5=7\) or \(x=2-5=-3\).

    (c) Distance 0 from \(-1\): only \(x=-1\).

    (d) A distance cannot be negative: no solution.

    Answer: (a) \(\pm4\); (b) 7 or \(-3\); (c) \(-1\); (d) none.

  4. For \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\), compute \(f(-2)\), \(f(0.5)\) and \(f(100)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute each value into \(\tfrac1x\).

    \(f(-2)=\tfrac1{-2}=-0.5\); \(f(0.5)=\tfrac1{0.5}=2\); \(f(100)=\tfrac1{100}=0.01\).

    Answer: \(-0.5\), 2, 0.01. Small inputs give large outputs, and large inputs give outputs close to 0.

  5. Compute \(\sqrt{49}\), \(\sqrt{0.25}\), and \(\sqrt2\) to three decimal places.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\sqrt a\) is the non-negative number whose square is \(a\).

    \(\sqrt{49}=7\) because \(7^2=49\).

    \(\sqrt{0.25}=0.5\) because \(0.5^2=0.25\).

    \(\sqrt2\approx1.414\) because \(1.414^2\approx1.999\).

    Answer: 7, 0.5, 1.414.

  6. Decide whether each function is even, odd, or neither: \(|x|\), \(\tfrac1x\), \(\tfrac1{x^2}\), \(\sqrt x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Even: \(f(-x)=f(x)\). Odd: \(f(-x)=-f(x)\). The domain must be symmetric for either to be possible.

    \(|-x|=|x|\): even.

    \(\dfrac1{-x}=-\dfrac1x\): odd.

    \(\dfrac1{(-x)^2}=\dfrac1{x^2}\): even.

    \(\sqrt x\) is defined only for \(x\ge0\); \(\sqrt{-x}\) is undefined for \(x\gt 0\), so it cannot be compared: neither.

    Answer: \(|x|\) even; \(\tfrac1x\) odd; \(\tfrac1{x^2}\) even; \(\sqrt x\) neither.

  7. Write down the asymptotes of \(y=\tfrac1x\) and of \(y=\tfrac1{x^2}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Both functions are undefined at \(x=0\) and grow without limit near it; both approach 0 for large \(|x|\).

    Vertical asymptote: \(x=0\) for both.

    Horizontal asymptote: \(y=0\) for both.

    Answer: both have \(x=0\) and \(y=0\).

  8. Where is \(\tfrac1x\) positive? Where is \(\tfrac1{x^2}\) positive?

    Show solution

    Idea. A fraction with numerator 1 has the sign of its denominator.

    \(\tfrac1x\gt 0\) exactly when \(x\gt 0\).

    \(x^2\gt 0\) for every \(x\ne0\), so \(\tfrac1{x^2}\gt 0\) for every \(x\ne0\).

    Answer: \(\tfrac1x\) is positive for \(x\gt 0\); \(\tfrac1{x^2}\) is positive for all \(x\ne0\).

  9. Solve the inequalities.

    1. \(|x|\lt 3\)
    2. \(|x|\ge2\)
    Show solution

    Idea. \(|x|\) is the distance from 0.

    (a) Distance less than 3: all numbers between \(-3\) and 3, \(-3\lt x\lt 3\).

    (b) Distance at least 2: \(x\le-2\) or \(x\ge2\).

    Answer: (a) \(-3\lt x\lt 3\); (b) \(x\le-2\) or \(x\ge2\).

  10. Write \(|x-3|\) without the absolute value sign.

    Show solution

    Idea. Split by the sign of what is inside the bars: \(|a|=a\) when \(a\ge0\) and \(-a\) when \(a\lt 0\).

    When \(x\ge3\), \(x-3\ge0\): \(|x-3|=x-3\).

    When \(x\lt 3\), \(x-3\lt 0\): \(|x-3|=-(x-3)=3-x\).

    Answer: \(x-3\) for \(x\ge3\); \(3-x\) for \(x\lt 3\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Solve.

    1. \(\sqrt x=5\)
    2. \(\sqrt{x+1}=3\)
    3. \(\sqrt x=-2\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Square both sides; both sides must be non-negative, which also tells us when there is no solution.

    (a) \(x=5^2=25\).

    (b) \(x+1=9\Rightarrow x=8\). Check: \(\sqrt9=3\) ✓

    (c) A square root is never negative, so \(\sqrt x=-2\) is impossible.

    Answer: (a) 25; (b) 8; (c) no solution.

  2. Solve \(|2x-1|=7\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The expression inside the bars is either 7 or \(-7\).

    \(2x-1=7\Rightarrow 2x=8\Rightarrow x=4\).

    \(2x-1=-7\Rightarrow 2x=-6\Rightarrow x=-3\).

    Answer: \(x=4\) or \(x=-3\).

    Check: \(|7|=7\) ✓, \(|-7|=7\) ✓

  3. Solve \(|x-1|\lt 4\) and explain the answer in terms of distance.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(|x-1|\lt 4\) means "the distance from 1 is less than 4", which is the same as \(-4\lt x-1\lt 4\).

    Add 1 to all three parts: \(-3\lt x\lt 5\).

    Answer: \(-3\lt x\lt 5\): every number less than 4 units away from 1, on either side.

  4. Find the average rate of change of \(\sqrt x\) on \([0,1]\), \([1,4]\) and \([4,9]\). What do you notice?

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute \(\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\) on each interval; perfect squares make the roots easy.

    \([0,1]\): \(\dfrac{1-0}{1}=1\). \([1,4]\): \(\dfrac{2-1}{3}=\tfrac13\). \([4,9]\): \(\dfrac{3-2}{5}=\tfrac15\).

    Each time the function rises by only 1, but over longer and longer intervals.

    Answer: \(1,\ \tfrac13,\ \tfrac15\). \(\sqrt x\) always increases, but more and more slowly: its graph flattens.

  5. Find the average rate of change of \(\tfrac1x\) on \([1,2]\) and on \([2,4]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute \(\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\) and compare.

    \([1,2]\): \(\dfrac{0.5-1}{1}=-0.5\). \([2,4]\): \(\dfrac{0.25-0.5}{2}=-0.125\).

    Both are negative: the function decreases on both intervals. The second is closer to 0: the graph becomes flatter.

    Answer: \(-0.5\) and \(-0.125\).

  6. Solve.

    1. \(\tfrac1x=4\)
    2. \(\tfrac1{x^2}=9\)
    3. \(\tfrac1x=x\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Multiply both sides by the denominator (it is not zero), then solve.

    (a) \(1=4x\Rightarrow x=\tfrac14\).

    (b) \(1=9x^2\Rightarrow x^2=\tfrac19\Rightarrow x=\tfrac13\) or \(x=-\tfrac13\).

    (c) \(1=x^2\Rightarrow x=1\) or \(x=-1\).

    Answer: (a) \(\tfrac14\); (b) \(\pm\tfrac13\); (c) \(\pm1\).

  7. Find where \(y=|x|\) meets \(y=\tfrac12x+3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(|x|\) is \(x\) for \(x\ge0\) and \(-x\) for \(x\lt 0\). Solve each case and keep only answers that fit the case.

    Case \(x\ge0\): \(x=\tfrac12x+3\Rightarrow\tfrac12x=3\Rightarrow x=6\) (fits). \(y=6\).

    Case \(x\lt 0\): \(-x=\tfrac12x+3\Rightarrow-\tfrac32x=3\Rightarrow x=-2\) (fits). \(y=2\).

    Answer: \((6,6)\) and \((-2,2)\).

  8. Find where \(y=\sqrt x\) meets \(y=x\). For which \(x\) is \(\sqrt x\gt x\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Square both sides of \(\sqrt x=x\) (with \(x\ge0\)); then a test value shows which graph is above between the meeting points.

    \(x=x^2\Rightarrow x^2-x=0\Rightarrow x(x-1)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=1\). Points \((0,0)\), \((1,1)\).

    Test \(x=0.25\): \(\sqrt{0.25}=0.5\gt 0.25\). Test \(x=4\): \(2\lt 4\).

    Answer: they meet at \((0,0)\) and \((1,1)\); \(\sqrt x\gt x\) for \(0\lt x\lt 1\).

  9. For which \(x\) is \(\tfrac1{x^2}\gt \tfrac1x\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Multiplying an inequality by a positive number keeps its direction. \(x^2\) is positive for \(x\ne0\), so multiply by \(x^2\).

    \(\dfrac1{x^2}\gt \dfrac1x\Rightarrow 1\gt x\).

    Remove \(x=0\), where neither side is defined.

    Answer: \(x\lt 0\) or \(0\lt x\lt 1\).

    Check: \(x=-1\): \(1\gt -1\) ✓; \(x=2\): \(\tfrac14\gt \tfrac12\) ✗ ✓

    Watch out: multiplying by \(x\) would be wrong, because \(x\) can be negative.

  10. Is \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\) a decreasing function? Explain.

    Show solution

    Idea. "Decreasing" means: whenever \(a\lt b\) in the domain, \(f(a)\gt f(b)\). One counter-example is enough to show it fails.

    On \(x\lt 0\) and on \(x\gt 0\) separately, \(\tfrac1x\) decreases.

    But \(-1\lt 1\) and \(f(-1)=-1\lt 1=f(1)\): a larger input gives a larger output.

    Answer: no. It decreases on each branch, but not on its whole domain; the break at \(x=0\) spoils it.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Solve \(|x-3|=2x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The right side \(2x\) must be \(\ge0\) (it equals an absolute value), and we split into the two cases of \(|x-3|\).

    Case \(x\ge3\): \(x-3=2x\Rightarrow x=-3\). But \(-3\) is not \(\ge3\): reject.

    Case \(x\lt 3\): \(3-x=2x\Rightarrow 3=3x\Rightarrow x=1\) (fits).

    Check: \(|1-3|=2\) and \(2\cdot1=2\) ✓

    Answer: \(x=1\).

  2. Solve \(|x+2|+|x-4|=10\), and explain the result in terms of distances.

    Show solution

    Idea. The bars change form at \(-2\) and at 4, so split the number line into three cases.

    \(x\ge4\): both inside are \(\ge0\): \((x+2)+(x-4)=2x-2=10\Rightarrow x=6\) (fits).

    \(-2\le x\lt 4\): \((x+2)+(4-x)=6\). \(6\ne10\): no solution here.

    \(x\lt -2\): both negative: \(-(x+2)-(x-4)=-2x+2=10\Rightarrow x=-4\) (fits).

    In words: \(|x+2|+|x-4|\) is the sum of the distances from \(-2\) and from 4. Between them the sum is always 6 (the gap); outside it grows.

    Answer: \(x=-4\) or \(x=6\).

  3. Solve \(\sqrt{x+6}=x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Square both sides, solve, and check every candidate, because squaring can create false solutions.

    \(x+6=x^2\Rightarrow x^2-x-6=0\Rightarrow(x-3)(x+2)=0\Rightarrow x=3\) or \(x=-2\).

    Check \(x=3\): \(\sqrt9=3\) ✓. Check \(x=-2\): \(\sqrt4=2\ne-2\) ✗

    Answer: \(x=3\).

    Watch out: the right-hand side \(x\) must be \(\ge0\), which rules out \(-2\) at once.

  4. Consider \(f(x)=\sqrt x\) and \(g(x)=\tfrac1x\) for \(x\gt 0\).

    1. Find where the graphs meet.
    2. For which \(x\) is \(f(x)\gt g(x)\)?
    Show solution

    Idea. Solve \(\sqrt x=\tfrac1x\) by multiplying by \(x\); compare the two functions with the number 1.

    (a) \(x\sqrt x=1\Rightarrow x^{3/2}=1\Rightarrow x=1\). The point \((1,1)\).

    (b) For \(x\gt 1\): \(\sqrt x\gt 1\) and \(\tfrac1x\lt 1\), so \(\sqrt x\gt \tfrac1x\).

    For \(0\lt x\lt 1\): \(\sqrt x\lt 1\) and \(\tfrac1x\gt 1\), so \(\sqrt x\lt \tfrac1x\).

    Answer: (a) \((1,1)\); (b) \(x\gt 1\).

  5. Find the area of the region between \(y=|x|\) and \(y=4\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find where the line meets the "V"; the region is a triangle with its apex at the origin.

    \(|x|=4\Rightarrow x=\pm4\): the points \((-4,4)\) and \((4,4)\).

    The triangle has vertices \((0,0)\), \((-4,4)\), \((4,4)\): base 8 (along \(y=4\)) and height 4 (from \(y=0\) to \(y=4\)).

    Answer: area \(=\dfrac{8\cdot4}2=16\).

  6. \(P\) is a point on the graph of \(y=\tfrac1x\) with \(x\gt 0\). A rectangle has corners \(O(0,0)\), \((x,0)\), \(P\) and \((0,\tfrac1x)\). Show that its area does not depend on where \(P\) is.

    Show solution

    Idea. The rectangle's sides are the coordinates of \(P\): width \(x\) and height \(y=\tfrac1x\).

    Area \(=x\cdot\tfrac1x=1\).

    The \(x\) cancels, so the result is the same for every \(x\gt 0\).

    Answer: the area is always 1. (This is a special property of the hyperbola \(y=\tfrac1x\).)

  7. For which \(x\) is \(\tfrac1{x^2}\gt 100\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Both sides are positive for \(x\ne0\), so flip both: a larger reciprocal means a smaller number.

    \(\dfrac1{x^2}\gt 100\Rightarrow x^2\lt \dfrac1{100}\) (multiply by \(\tfrac{x^2}{100}\gt 0\)).

    \(x^2\lt 0.01\Rightarrow -0.1\lt x\lt 0.1\). Remove \(x=0\).

    Answer: \(-0.1\lt x\lt 0.1\), \(x\ne0\).

  8. Solve the inequalities.

    1. \(\sqrt x\le3\)
    2. \(\sqrt{x-2}\gt 1\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Start with the domain, then square (both sides are non-negative, so the direction is kept).

    (a) Domain \(x\ge0\). \(\sqrt x\le3\Rightarrow x\le9\). Together: \(0\le x\le9\).

    (b) Domain \(x\ge2\). \(\sqrt{x-2}\gt 1\Rightarrow x-2\gt 1\Rightarrow x\gt 3\) (this is inside the domain).

    Answer: (a) \(0\le x\le9\); (b) \(x\gt 3\).

  9. Solve \(|x^2-4|=3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(|A|=3\) means \(A=3\) or \(A=-3\). Here \(A=x^2-4\).

    \(x^2-4=3\Rightarrow x^2=7\Rightarrow x=\pm\sqrt7\).

    \(x^2-4=-3\Rightarrow x^2=1\Rightarrow x=\pm1\).

    Answer: four solutions: \(x=\pm1\) and \(x=\pm\sqrt7\).

  10. Find every number whose distance from 5 is twice its distance from 2.

    Show solution

    Idea. "Distance from 5" is \(|x-5|\) and "distance from 2" is \(|x-2|\). Two absolute values are equal (up to a factor) when the insides are equal or opposite.

    \(|x-5|=2|x-2|\Rightarrow x-5=2(x-2)\) or \(x-5=-2(x-2)\).

    First: \(x-5=2x-4\Rightarrow x=-1\).

    Second: \(x-5=-2x+4\Rightarrow 3x=9\Rightarrow x=3\).

    Check: \(x=-1\): distances 6 and 3 ✓. \(x=3\): distances 2 and 1 ✓

    Answer: \(x=-1\) or \(x=3\).
