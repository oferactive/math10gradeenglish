Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Describe how each graph is obtained from \(y=x^2\).

    1. \(y=(x-3)^2\)
    2. \(y=x^2-5\)
    3. \(y=(x+1)^2+2\)
    4. \(y=-x^2\)
    Show solution

    Idea. \(f(x-p)\) moves the graph right by \(p\); \(f(x)+k\) moves it up by \(k\); \(-f(x)\) reflects it in the x-axis.

    (a) \((x-3)^2\): \(p=3\), 3 units right. Vertex \((3,0)\).

    (b) \(x^2-5\): \(k=-5\), 5 units down. Vertex \((0,-5)\).

    (c) \((x+1)^2+2\): 1 unit left and 2 units up. Vertex \((-1,2)\).

    (d) \(-x^2\): every value changes sign: reflection in the x-axis (the parabola opens downward).

    Answer: (a) 3 right; (b) 5 down; (c) 1 left, 2 up; (d) reflection in the x-axis.

  2. Write the equation of the new graph.

    1. \(y=\sqrt x\) moved 2 right and 1 down
    2. \(y=\tfrac1x\) moved 3 left and 2 up
    3. \(y=x^3\) reflected in the x-axis and then moved 4 up
    Show solution

    Idea. To move right by \(p\), replace \(x\) by \(x-p\); to move up by \(k\), add \(k\); to reflect in the x-axis, put a minus in front of the whole function.

    (a) \(\sqrt x\to\sqrt{x-2}\) (2 right) \(\to\sqrt{x-2}-1\) (1 down).

    (b) \(\tfrac1x\to\tfrac1{x+3}\) (3 left) \(\to\tfrac1{x+3}+2\) (2 up).

    (c) \(x^3\to-x^3\) (reflection) \(\to-x^3+4\) (4 up).

    Answer: (a) \(y=\sqrt{x-2}-1\); (b) \(y=\dfrac1{x+3}+2\); (c) \(y=-x^3+4\).

    Watch out: in (c) the order matters: reflecting after the shift would give \(-(x^3+4)=-x^3-4\).

  3. Find the asymptotes.

    1. \(y=\dfrac1{x-2}+5\)
    2. \(y=\dfrac3{x+1}\)
    3. \(y=-\dfrac1x-4\)
    Show solution

    Idea. For \(\dfrac a{x-p}+k\), the asymptotes are \(x=p\) (where the denominator is 0) and \(y=k\) (the value approached far away).

    (a) \(p=2\), \(k=5\): \(x=2\), \(y=5\).

    (b) \(x+1=x-(-1)\): \(p=-1\); there is no added constant: \(k=0\). \(x=-1\), \(y=0\).

    (c) \(p=0\), \(k=-4\): \(x=0\), \(y=-4\).

    Answer: (a) \(x=2,\ y=5\); (b) \(x=-1,\ y=0\); (c) \(x=0,\ y=-4\).

  4. Find the domain and the starting point of \(y=\sqrt{x+3}-2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\sqrt{x-p}+k\) starts where the root is 0, at \(x=p\), with the value \(k\).

    Domain: \(x+3\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge-3\).

    At \(x=-3\): \(y=\sqrt0-2=-2\).

    Answer: domain \(x\ge-3\); starting point \((-3,-2)\).

  5. \((2,5)\) is on the graph of \(f\). Find the matching point on each graph.

    1. \(f(x)+3\)
    2. \(f(x-1)\)
    3. \(-f(x)\)
    4. \(f(-x)\)
    5. \(2f(x)\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Ask where each point goes: \(f(x)+3\) up 3; \(f(x-1)\) right 1; \(-f(x)\) flips the y-value; \(f(-x)\) flips the x-value; \(2f(x)\) doubles the y-value.

    (a) \((2,5+3)=(2,8)\).

    (b) \((2+1,5)=(3,5)\): the new function reaches the old value 5 when \(x-1=2\), i.e. at \(x=3\).

    (c) \((2,-5)\).

    (d) \((-2,5)\): the new function reaches \(f(2)\) when \(-x=2\), i.e. at \(x=-2\).

    (e) \((2,2\cdot5)=(2,10)\).

    Answer: (a) \((2,8)\); (b) \((3,5)\); (c) \((2,-5)\); (d) \((-2,5)\); (e) \((2,10)\).

  6. \(f(x)=x^2\), \(g(x)=x+1\). Compute:

    1. \((f+g)(2)\)
    2. \((f-g)(-1)\)
    3. \((f\cdot g)(3)\)
    4. \(\left(\tfrac fg\right)(1)\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Operations on functions act on their values: compute \(f\) and \(g\) at the point, then add, subtract, multiply or divide.

    (a) \(f(2)=4\), \(g(2)=3\): \(4+3=7\).

    (b) \(f(-1)=1\), \(g(-1)=0\): \(1-0=1\).

    (c) \(f(3)=9\), \(g(3)=4\): \(9\cdot4=36\).

    (d) \(f(1)=1\), \(g(1)=2\): \(\tfrac12\).

    Answer: (a) 7; (b) 1; (c) 36; (d) \(\tfrac12\).

  7. \(f(x)=\sqrt x\), \(g(x)=x-4\). Find the domain of \(\dfrac fg\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The quotient needs both functions defined (\(\sqrt x\) needs \(x\ge0\)) and a non-zero denominator (\(x-4\ne0\)).

    \(x\ge0\) and \(x\ne4\).

    Answer: \(x\ge0,\ x\ne4\).

  8. Find the vertex and the roots of \(y=-2(x-1)^2+8\).

    Show solution

    Idea. In vertex form \(a(x-p)^2+k\) the vertex is \((p,k)\). Roots: set \(y=0\).

    Vertex: \((1,8)\). Since \(a=-2\lt 0\), it is a maximum.

    \(-2(x-1)^2+8=0\Rightarrow(x-1)^2=4\Rightarrow x-1=\pm2\Rightarrow x=3\) or \(x=-1\).

    Answer: vertex \((1,8)\); roots 3 and \(-1\).

    Check: the roots are symmetric about \(x=1\) ✓

  9. How is \(y=|x-2|\) obtained from \(y=|x|\)? Where is its corner?

    Show solution

    Idea. \(|x-2|=f(x-2)\) with \(f(x)=|x|\): a shift of 2 to the right. The corner moves with it.

    The corner of \(|x|\) is at \((0,0)\); it moves to \((2,0)\).

    Answer: shift 2 units right; corner \((2,0)\).

  10. The graph of \(y=ax^2\) passes through \((2,12)\). Find \(a\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute the point to find \(a\).

    \(12=a\cdot2^2=4a\Rightarrow a=3\).

    Answer: \(a=3\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. For \(y=\dfrac2{x-1}+3\), find the asymptotes and the points where the graph meets the axes.

    Show solution

    Idea. Read the asymptotes from the form \(\tfrac a{x-p}+k\); the y-intercept is the value at \(x=0\); the root is where \(y=0\).

    Asymptotes: \(x=1\) and \(y=3\).

    y-axis: \(y=\dfrac2{0-1}+3=-2+3=1\): \((0,1)\).

    x-axis: \(\dfrac2{x-1}+3=0\Rightarrow\dfrac2{x-1}=-3\Rightarrow 2=-3(x-1)\Rightarrow x-1=-\tfrac23\Rightarrow x=\tfrac13\).

    Answer: asymptotes \(x=1\), \(y=3\); meets the axes at \((0,1)\) and \(\left(\tfrac13,0\right)\).

  2. For \(y=-\sqrt{x+4}+2\), find the domain, the starting point, the root and whether the function increases or decreases.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(a\sqrt{x-p}+k\) starts at \((p,k)\); with \(a\lt 0\) it goes down from there.

    Domain: \(x+4\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge-4\). Start: \((-4,2)\).

    Root: \(-\sqrt{x+4}+2=0\Rightarrow\sqrt{x+4}=2\Rightarrow x+4=4\Rightarrow x=0\).

    \(a=-1\lt 0\): the graph of \(\sqrt x\) is flipped, so the function decreases.

    Answer: domain \(x\ge-4\); start \((-4,2)\); root \(x=0\); decreasing.

  3. The parabola \(y=a(x-2)^2+k\) has its vertex at \((2,-3)\) and passes through \((4,5)\). Find \(a\) and \(k\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The vertex gives \(p\) and \(k\) directly; the extra point gives \(a\).

    Vertex \((2,-3)\): \(p=2\), \(k=-3\).

    Through \((4,5)\): \(5=a(4-2)^2-3=4a-3\Rightarrow 4a=8\Rightarrow a=2\).

    Answer: \(a=2\), \(k=-3\).

  4. The graph of \(y=\dfrac{a}{x-p}+k\) has asymptotes \(x=3\) and \(y=-1\) and passes through \((4,1)\). Find \(a\), \(p\) and \(k\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The asymptotes give \(p\) and \(k\); the point gives \(a\).

    \(x=3\Rightarrow p=3\); \(y=-1\Rightarrow k=-1\).

    Through \((4,1)\): \(1=\dfrac a{4-3}-1\Rightarrow a=2\).

    Answer: \(a=2\), \(p=3\), \(k=-1\).

  5. \(f(x)=x-3\). Describe the graph of \(g(x)=\dfrac1{f(x)}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\dfrac1f\) has a vertical asymptote where \(f=0\), and approaches 0 where \(f\) grows.

    \(g(x)=\dfrac1{x-3}\): the graph of \(\tfrac1x\) moved 3 units right.

    Vertical asymptote: \(x=3\) (the root of \(f\)). Horizontal asymptote: \(y=0\).

    \(g\) has the same sign as \(f\): negative for \(x\lt 3\), positive for \(x\gt 3\).

    Answer: a hyperbola with asymptotes \(x=3\) and \(y=0\).

  6. \(f(x)=x^2+1\) and \(g(x)=\dfrac1{f(x)}\).

    1. Find the domain of \(g\).
    2. Find the extreme point of \(g\).
    3. Find the asymptotes of \(g\) and describe its symmetry.
    Show solution

    Idea. Use the properties of \(\tfrac1f\): defined where \(f\ne0\); a minimum of \(f\) (with \(f\gt 0\)) becomes a maximum of \(g\); \(f\to\infty\) gives \(g\to0\).

    (a) \(x^2+1\ge1\gt 0\), so it is never 0: \(g\) is defined for all \(x\).

    (b) \(f\) has its minimum 1 at \(x=0\). Then \(g=\tfrac1f\) has its largest value \(\tfrac11=1\) there: maximum \((0,1)\).

    (c) As \(|x|\) grows, \(f\to\infty\) and \(g\to0\): horizontal asymptote \(y=0\). There is no vertical asymptote. \(g(-x)=g(x)\): even. \(g\gt 0\) everywhere.

    Answer: (a) all \(x\); (b) maximum \((0,1)\); (c) \(y=0\); even and positive.

  7. The graph of \(y=(x-1)^2\) is reflected in the y-axis. Find the equation of the new graph and its vertex.

    Show solution

    Idea. A reflection in the y-axis replaces \(x\) by \(-x\).

    \(y=(-x-1)^2=(-(x+1))^2=(x+1)^2\).

    The old vertex \((1,0)\) moves to \((-1,0)\).

    Answer: \(y=(x+1)^2\), vertex \((-1,0)\).

  8. \(f(x)=x\), \(g(x)=x-2\). Find where \(f\cdot g\) and \(\dfrac fg\) are negative. What is the difference between the two functions?

    Show solution

    Idea. A product or a quotient is negative where the two functions have opposite signs.

    \(f=x\) is negative for \(x\lt 0\); \(g=x-2\) is negative for \(x\lt 2\). They have opposite signs for \(0\lt x\lt 2\).

    So both \(f\cdot g=x(x-2)\) and \(\dfrac fg=\dfrac x{x-2}\) are negative on \(0\lt x\lt 2\).

    The difference: at \(x=2\), \(f\cdot g=0\), but \(\dfrac fg\) is undefined and has a vertical asymptote.

    Answer: both are negative for \(0\lt x\lt 2\); only \(\dfrac fg\) breaks at \(x=2\).

  9. \(h(x)=x+\dfrac1x\). Find the domain, check whether \(h\) is odd, and compute \(h(1)\), \(h(2)\), \(h(-1)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Domain: the fraction needs \(x\ne0\). Odd: check \(h(-x)=-h(x)\).

    Domain: \(x\ne0\).

    \(h(-x)=-x+\dfrac1{-x}=-x-\dfrac1x=-\left(x+\dfrac1x\right)=-h(x)\): odd.

    \(h(1)=1+1=2\); \(h(2)=2+0.5=2.5\); \(h(-1)=-1-1=-2\) (\(=-h(1)\), as an odd function must).

    Answer: domain \(x\ne0\); odd; \(2,\ 2.5,\ -2\).

  10. For \(y=3|x+2|-6\), find the corner point and the roots.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(3|x+2|-6\) is \(|x|\) moved 2 left, stretched by 3, and moved 6 down; the corner moves to \((-2,-6)\).

    Corner: where \(x+2=0\): \(x=-2\), \(y=-6\).

    Roots: \(3|x+2|=6\Rightarrow|x+2|=2\Rightarrow x+2=\pm2\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=-4\).

    Answer: corner \((-2,-6)\); roots 0 and \(-4\).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Let \(f(x)=x^2-4\) and \(g(x)=\dfrac1{f(x)}\).

    1. Find the domain of \(g\).
    2. Find the asymptotes of \(g\).
    3. Find \(g(0)\) and explain why it is an extreme value.
    4. Where is \(g\) positive and where negative?
    5. Is \(g\) even, odd, or neither?
    Show solution

    Idea. Study \(g=\tfrac1f\) through \(f=x^2-4\): roots of \(f\) become vertical asymptotes, the sign stays, a minimum of \(f\) (where \(f\lt 0\)) becomes a maximum of \(g\).

    (a) \(x^2-4\ne0\Rightarrow x\ne\pm2\).

    (b) Vertical asymptotes \(x=2\), \(x=-2\). Since \(f\to\infty\) as \(|x|\to\infty\), \(g\to0\): horizontal asymptote \(y=0\).

    (c) \(g(0)=\dfrac1{-4}=-\tfrac14\). On \(-2\lt x\lt 2\), \(f\) is negative and takes its lowest value \(-4\) at 0. Its reciprocal is closest to 0 there, which makes \(-\tfrac14\) the largest value of \(g\) on that interval: a maximum.

    (d) \(g\) has the sign of \(f\): positive for \(|x|\gt 2\), negative for \(|x|\lt 2\).

    (e) \(f\) is even, so \(g(-x)=\tfrac1{f(-x)}=\tfrac1{f(x)}=g(x)\): even.

    Answer: (a) \(x\ne\pm2\); (b) \(x=\pm2\), \(y=0\); (c) \(-\tfrac14\), a maximum; (d) as above; (e) even.

  2. Let \(y=\dfrac{2x+1}{x-1}\).

    1. Show that \(y=2+\dfrac3{x-1}\).
    2. Find the asymptotes.
    3. Find the points where the graph meets the axes.
    Show solution

    Idea. Write the numerator as a multiple of the denominator plus a remainder; then the function is a shifted hyperbola.

    (a) \(2x+1=2(x-1)+3\), so \(\dfrac{2x+1}{x-1}=\dfrac{2(x-1)}{x-1}+\dfrac3{x-1}=2+\dfrac3{x-1}\).

    (b) This is \(\tfrac3x\) moved 1 right and 2 up: asymptotes \(x=1\) and \(y=2\).

    (c) x-axis: the fraction is 0 when the numerator is 0: \(2x+1=0\Rightarrow x=-\tfrac12\). y-axis: \(y(0)=\dfrac1{-1}=-1\).

    Answer: (b) \(x=1\), \(y=2\); (c) \(\left(-\tfrac12,0\right)\) and \((0,-1)\).

  3. The graph of \(y=a\sqrt{x-p}+k\) starts at \((1,-2)\) and passes through \((5,2)\).

    1. Find \(a\), \(p\) and \(k\).
    2. Find the root.
    3. Does the graph meet the y-axis?
    Show solution

    Idea. The starting point gives \(p\) and \(k\); the second point gives \(a\).

    (a) Start \((1,-2)\): \(p=1\), \(k=-2\). Through \((5,2)\): \(2=a\sqrt{5-1}-2=2a-2\Rightarrow a=2\).

    (b) \(2\sqrt{x-1}-2=0\Rightarrow\sqrt{x-1}=1\Rightarrow x=2\).

    (c) The domain is \(x\ge1\); the y-axis is \(x=0\), outside the domain.

    Answer: (a) \(a=2,\ p=1,\ k=-2\); (b) \(x=2\); (c) no.

  4. The graph of \(f\) passes through \((-2,0)\), \((0,4)\), \((3,0)\) and has a maximum at \((1,5)\).

    1. Find the matching points on the graph of \(g(x)=f(x-2)-4\).
    2. Find the matching points on the graph of \(h(x)=-f(x)+1\). What happens to the maximum?
    Show solution

    Idea. \(f(x-2)-4\) moves every point 2 right and 4 down. \(-f(x)+1\) first flips every y-value, then adds 1.

    (a) \((x,y)\to(x+2,y-4)\): \((-2,0)\to(0,-4)\); \((0,4)\to(2,0)\); \((3,0)\to(5,-4)\); maximum \((1,5)\to(3,1)\), still a maximum.

    (b) \((x,y)\to(x,1-y)\): \((-2,0)\to(-2,1)\); \((0,4)\to(0,-3)\); \((3,0)\to(3,1)\); \((1,5)\to(1,-4)\).

    The flip turns the highest point into the lowest: the maximum becomes a minimum.

    Answer: (a) \((0,-4),(2,0),(5,-4)\), max \((3,1)\); (b) \((-2,1),(0,-3),(3,1)\), min \((1,-4)\).

  5. \(f(x)=\sqrt{x+2}\) and \(g(x)=\sqrt{3-x}\). Find the domain of \(f+g\) and of \(\dfrac fg\).

    Show solution

    Idea. A sum needs both functions defined; a quotient also needs the denominator to be non-zero.

    \(f\): \(x+2\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge-2\). \(g\): \(3-x\ge0\Rightarrow x\le3\).

    \(f+g\): both: \(-2\le x\le3\).

    \(\dfrac fg\): also \(g\ne0\), i.e. \(x\ne3\): \(-2\le x\lt 3\).

    Answer: \(f+g\): \(-2\le x\le3\); \(\dfrac fg\): \(-2\le x\lt 3\).

  6. Solve \(\dfrac1{x+1}=x-1\), and interpret the answer as the meeting points of two graphs.

    Show solution

    Idea. Multiply by the denominator (allowed for \(x\ne-1\)) to get a polynomial equation. Each solution is the x-coordinate of a meeting point of the two graphs.

    \(1=(x-1)(x+1)=x^2-1\Rightarrow x^2=2\Rightarrow x=\sqrt2\) or \(x=-\sqrt2\). Neither is \(-1\), so both are valid.

    Answer: \(x=\pm\sqrt2\): the hyperbola \(y=\tfrac1{x+1}\) meets the line \(y=x-1\) at two points, \(\left(\sqrt2,\sqrt2-1\right)\) and \(\left(-\sqrt2,-\sqrt2-1\right)\).

  7. The graph of \(y=|x^2-4|\) is obtained from \(y=x^2-4\) by reflecting the part below the x-axis. For which values of \(k\) does \(|x^2-4|=k\) have exactly four solutions? Three? Two?

    Show solution

    Idea. Count meeting points of the horizontal line \(y=k\) with the graph of \(|x^2-4|\). The part of \(x^2-4\) between \(-2\) and 2 (below the axis, lowest point \((0,-4)\)) is flipped up, making a hump with top \((0,4)\).

    For \(0\lt k\lt 4\): the line cuts the two outer branches and the hump twice: 4 points.

    For \(k=4\): it touches the top of the hump once and cuts the outer branches: 3 points.

    For \(k\gt 4\): it is above the hump and cuts only the outer branches: 2 points.

    For \(k=0\): only the roots \(\pm2\): 2 points. For \(k\lt 0\): none.

    Answer: four for \(0\lt k\lt 4\); three for \(k=4\); two for \(k\gt 4\) or \(k=0\).

  8. \(f(x)=x^2\) and \(g(x)=(x-a)^2\). For which value of \(a\ne0\) do the graphs meet at \(x=3\)? Are there other meeting points?

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute \(x=3\) into both and set them equal; then solve the general equation to find all meeting points.

    \(f(3)=9\), \(g(3)=(3-a)^2\). \((3-a)^2=9\Rightarrow3-a=\pm3\Rightarrow a=0\) or \(a=6\). We need \(a\ne0\): \(a=6\).

    All meeting points: \(x^2=(x-6)^2=x^2-12x+36\Rightarrow 12x=36\Rightarrow x=3\).

    Answer: \(a=6\); \(x=3\) is the only meeting point. The two parabolas are mirror images in the line \(x=3\), so they meet only on it.

  9. \(h(x)=(x-1)\sqrt{x+2}\). Find the domain, the roots, and where \(h\) is positive.

    Show solution

    Idea. Domain from the root; roots from each factor; the sign from the factors (the root factor is never negative).

    Domain: \(x+2\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge-2\).

    Roots: \(x-1=0\Rightarrow x=1\), or \(\sqrt{x+2}=0\Rightarrow x=-2\).

    For \(x\gt -2\), \(\sqrt{x+2}\gt 0\), so \(h\) has the sign of \(x-1\).

    Answer: domain \(x\ge-2\); roots \(-2\) and 1; positive for \(x\gt 1\), negative for \(-2\lt x\lt 1\).

  10. \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\) and \(g(x)=f(x-2)+1\).

    1. Write \(g(x)\) and find its asymptotes.
    2. Find the points where the graph of \(g\) meets the axes.
    3. Find where the graph of \(g\) meets the line \(y=x\).
    Show solution

    Idea. \(f(x-2)+1\) is \(\tfrac1x\) moved 2 right and 1 up. Then find the intercepts and solve with the line.

    (a) \(g(x)=\dfrac1{x-2}+1\); asymptotes \(x=2\), \(y=1\).

    (b) x-axis: \(\dfrac1{x-2}=-1\Rightarrow x-2=-1\Rightarrow x=1\): \((1,0)\). y-axis: \(g(0)=-\tfrac12+1=\tfrac12\): \(\left(0,\tfrac12\right)\).

    (c) \(\dfrac1{x-2}+1=x\Rightarrow\dfrac1{x-2}=x-1\Rightarrow 1=(x-1)(x-2)=x^2-3x+2\Rightarrow x^2-3x+1=0\).

    \(x=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{9-4}}2=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt5}2\) (about 2.62 and 0.38).

    Answer: (a) asymptotes \(x=2\), \(y=1\); (b) \((1,0)\) and \(\left(0,\tfrac12\right)\); (c) \(x=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt5}2\).
