Before you start: For every inverse you find, check it: substitute it into the original function and make sure you get \(x\) back.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. \(f(x)=2x+1\), \(g(x)=x^2\). Compute:

    1. \(f(g(2))\)
    2. \(g(f(2))\)
    3. \(f(f(1))\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Work from the inside out: compute the inner value, then apply the outer function.

    (a) \(g(2)=4\), then \(f(4)=2\cdot4+1=9\).

    (b) \(f(2)=5\), then \(g(5)=25\).

    (c) \(f(1)=3\), then \(f(3)=7\).

    Answer: (a) 9; (b) 25; (c) 7.

  2. Name the inner and the outer function.

    1. \((3x-1)^5\)
    2. \(\sqrt{x^2+1}\)
    3. \(|x-4|\)
    4. \(\dfrac1{x+2}\)
    Show solution

    Idea. The outer function is the last operation done; the inner function is everything inside it.

    (a) First \(3x-1\), last the fifth power: inner \(3x-1\), outer \(u^5\).

    (b) First \(x^2+1\), last the root: inner \(x^2+1\), outer \(\sqrt u\).

    (c) First \(x-4\), last the absolute value: inner \(x-4\), outer \(|u|\).

    (d) First \(x+2\), last the reciprocal: inner \(x+2\), outer \(\tfrac1u\).

    Answer: as listed.

  3. \(f(x)=x+5\), \(g(x)=3x\). Find \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\). Are they equal?

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute the whole inner function in place of \(x\) in the outer function.

    \(f(g(x))=f(3x)=3x+5\).

    \(g(f(x))=g(x+5)=3(x+5)=3x+15\).

    They differ (for example at \(x=0\): 5 and 15).

    Answer: \(3x+5\) and \(3x+15\); not equal. Composition is not commutative.

  4. Find the inverse.

    1. \(f(x)=x+4\)
    2. \(f(x)=5x\)
    3. \(f(x)=2x-3\)
    Show solution

    Idea. An inverse undoes the function: reverse each operation in reverse order. (Or swap \(x,y\) and solve.)

    (a) \(f\) adds 4, so \(f^{-1}\) subtracts 4: \(x-4\).

    (b) \(f\) multiplies by 5, so \(f^{-1}\) divides by 5: \(\tfrac x5\).

    (c) \(f\) multiplies by 2, then subtracts 3. Undo: add 3, then divide by 2: \(\dfrac{x+3}2\).

    Answer: (a) \(x-4\); (b) \(\tfrac x5\); (c) \(\dfrac{x+3}2\).

    Check (c): \(2\cdot\tfrac{x+3}2-3=x\) ✓

  5. Compute.

    1. \(\sqrt{(-5)^2}\)
    2. \((\sqrt9)^2\)
    3. \(\sqrt[3]{-8}\)
    4. \(16^{1/2}\)
    5. \(27^{1/3}\)
    Show solution

    Idea. \(\sqrt{a^2}=|a|\); \((\sqrt a)^2=a\) for \(a\ge0\); \(a^{1/2}=\sqrt a\); \(a^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]a\).

    (a) \(\sqrt{25}=5\).

    (b) \((\sqrt9)^2=3^2=9\).

    (c) \((-2)^3=-8\), so \(\sqrt[3]{-8}=-2\).

    (d) \(16^{1/2}=\sqrt{16}=4\).

    (e) \(27^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]{27}=3\).

    Answer: (a) 5; (b) 9; (c) \(-2\); (d) 4; (e) 3.

  6. Which functions are one-to-one on all real numbers (or on their whole domain)?

    1. \(y=x^3\)
    2. \(y=x^2\)
    3. \(y=\tfrac1x\)
    4. \(y=|x|\)
    Show solution

    Idea. A function is one-to-one if every horizontal line meets its graph at most once. Always increasing or always decreasing functions pass this test.

    (a) \(x^3\) always increases: yes.

    (b) \(x^2\): \(2^2=(-2)^2=4\): no.

    (c) \(\tfrac1x\): negative inputs give negative outputs and positive give positive, and each branch decreases, so no value repeats: yes.

    (d) \(|3|=|-3|=3\): no.

    Answer: (a) yes; (b) no; (c) yes; (d) no.

  7. \(f\) has an inverse and \(f(3)=7\). Find \(f^{-1}(7)\). Which point is on the graph of \(f^{-1}\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. The inverse sends each output back to its input: \(f(3)=7\) means \(f^{-1}(7)=3\).

    Answer: \(f^{-1}(7)=3\); the point \((7,3)\) is on the graph of \(f^{-1}\).

  8. Find the domain of \(y=\sqrt{2x+8}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The expression under the root must be \(\ge0\).

    \(2x+8\ge0\Rightarrow 2x\ge-8\Rightarrow x\ge-4\).

    Answer: \(x\ge-4\).

  9. \(f(x)=x^2\), \(g(x)=x-1\). Solve \(f(g(x))=9\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Write the composition, then solve: a square equals 9 when the base is 3 or \(-3\).

    \(f(g(x))=(x-1)^2\).

    \((x-1)^2=9\Rightarrow x-1=3\) or \(x-1=-3\Rightarrow x=4\) or \(x=-2\).

    Answer: \(x=4\) or \(x=-2\).

  10. Write \(h(x)=\sqrt{5-x}\) as a composition \(f(g(x))\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The last operation is the outer function, the first is the inner one.

    For a given \(x\) we compute \(5-x\) first, and then take its square root.

    Answer: \(g(x)=5-x\) (inner), \(f(u)=\sqrt u\) (outer).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. \(f(x)=x^2-4\). Where are the graphs of \(f\) and \(|f|\) different? Compute \(|f(0)|\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(|f|=f\) where \(f\ge0\), and \(|f|=-f\) where \(f\lt 0\). So the graphs differ only where \(f\) is negative.

    \(x^2-4\lt 0\Rightarrow x^2\lt 4\Rightarrow -2\lt x\lt 2\).

    There the graph of \(|f|\) is the graph of \(f\) reflected up above the x-axis.

    \(|f(0)|=|0-4|=4\).

    Answer: they differ for \(-2\lt x\lt 2\); \(|f(0)|=4\).

  2. Find the domain of each function. For (b), show that the graph is half of a circle.

    1. \(y=\sqrt{x^2-9}\)
    2. \(y=\sqrt{4-x^2}\)
    Show solution

    Idea. The expression under the root must be \(\ge0\). For (b), square \(y=\sqrt{4-x^2}\) and recognise the circle equation.

    (a) \(x^2-9\ge0\Rightarrow x^2\ge9\Rightarrow x\le-3\) or \(x\ge3\).

    (b) \(4-x^2\ge0\Rightarrow -2\le x\le2\). If \(y=\sqrt{4-x^2}\), then \(y\ge0\) and \(y^2=4-x^2\), i.e. \(x^2+y^2=4\).

    This is the circle with centre \(O\) and radius 2; the condition \(y\ge0\) keeps the upper half.

    Answer: (a) \(x\le-3\) or \(x\ge3\); (b) \(-2\le x\le2\), the upper half of the circle of radius 2.

  3. Find the inverse of \(f(x)=\dfrac1{x-2}\). Compare the asymptotes of \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Swap \(x\) and \(y\) and solve. The graph of the inverse is the reflection in \(y=x\), which swaps vertical and horizontal lines.

    \(y=\dfrac1{x-2}\to x=\dfrac1{y-2}\Rightarrow y-2=\dfrac1x\Rightarrow y=\dfrac1x+2\).

    \(f\): asymptotes \(x=2\) (vertical) and \(y=0\) (horizontal).

    \(f^{-1}\): asymptotes \(x=0\) and \(y=2\).

    Answer: \(f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac1x+2\); the asymptotes swap: \(x=2\leftrightarrow y=2\) and \(y=0\leftrightarrow x=0\).

  4. Find the inverse of \(f(x)=x^2+1\), \(x\ge0\), and its domain and range.

    Show solution

    Idea. Swap and solve; because \(x\ge0\) in the original, the inverse takes the positive root. The domain and range swap.

    \(x=y^2+1\Rightarrow y^2=x-1\Rightarrow y=\sqrt{x-1}\) (positive root, since \(y\ge0\)).

    The range of \(f\) is \(y\ge1\) (the smallest value is \(f(0)=1\)); this becomes the domain of \(f^{-1}\).

    Answer: \(f^{-1}(x)=\sqrt{x-1}\); domain \(x\ge1\); range \(y\ge0\).

  5. Find the inverse of \(f(x)=x^3-2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(x^3\) is one-to-one on all numbers, so the inverse exists everywhere and uses the cube root.

    \(x=y^3-2\Rightarrow y^3=x+2\Rightarrow y=\sqrt[3]{x+2}\).

    Answer: \(f^{-1}(x)=\sqrt[3]{x+2}\), for every \(x\).

    Check: \(\left(\sqrt[3]{x+2}\right)^3-2=x\) ✓

  6. Show that \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\) and \(g(x)=-x+3\) are each their own inverse. What do their graphs have in common?

    Show solution

    Idea. A function is its own inverse when swapping \(x\) and \(y\) gives back the same equation.

    \(y=\tfrac1x\to x=\tfrac1y\Rightarrow y=\tfrac1x\): the same.

    \(y=-x+3\to x=-y+3\Rightarrow y=-x+3\): the same.

    Since each graph equals its own reflection in \(y=x\), each graph is symmetric about the line \(y=x\).

    Answer: both are self-inverse; both graphs are symmetric about \(y=x\).

  7. \(f(x)=2x+1\), \(g(x)=\dfrac{x-1}2\). Show that \(g\) is the inverse of \(f\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(g\) is the inverse of \(f\) if each undoes the other: \(f(g(x))=x\) and \(g(f(x))=x\).

    \(f(g(x))=2\cdot\dfrac{x-1}2+1=(x-1)+1=x\).

    \(g(f(x))=\dfrac{(2x+1)-1}2=\dfrac{2x}2=x\).

    Answer: both compositions give \(x\), so \(g=f^{-1}\).

  8. \(f(x)=\sqrt x\), \(g(x)=x-3\). Find \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\) with their domains.

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute, then find where each composition is defined: the root needs a non-negative input.

    \(f(g(x))=\sqrt{x-3}\): needs \(x-3\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge3\).

    \(g(f(x))=\sqrt x-3\): needs \(x\ge0\).

    Answer: \(\sqrt{x-3}\) with \(x\ge3\); \(\sqrt x-3\) with \(x\ge0\).

  9. Solve each equation.

    1. \(\sqrt{x^2}=4\)
    2. \((\sqrt x)^2=4\)
    Show solution

    Idea. \(\sqrt{x^2}=|x|\) is defined for every \(x\); \((\sqrt x)^2=x\) is defined only for \(x\ge0\).

    (a) \(|x|=4\Rightarrow x=4\) or \(x=-4\).

    (b) The domain is \(x\ge0\); there \((\sqrt x)^2=x\), so \(x=4\).

    Answer: (a) \(\pm4\); (b) 4 only.

  10. Find the point where the graph of \(f(x)=2x-3\) meets the graph of its inverse.

    Show solution

    Idea. A graph and its inverse are mirror images in \(y=x\). For an increasing function they meet on that line, so solve \(f(x)=x\).

    \(2x-3=x\Rightarrow x=3\); the point \((3,3)\).

    Check: \(f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{x+3}2\) and \(f^{-1}(3)=3\) ✓

    Answer: \((3,3)\).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. \(f(x)=x^2-2x\), \(x\ge1\).

    1. Show that \(f\) is one-to-one.
    2. Find \(f^{-1}\) and its domain.
    3. Find where the graph of \(f\) meets the line \(y=x\).
    Show solution

    Idea. Complete the square to see the vertex; a parabola is one-to-one on one side of its vertex. Then swap and solve, choosing the root that matches.

    (a) \(x^2-2x=(x-1)^2-1\). The vertex is at \(x=1\); for \(x\ge1\) the parabola only rises, so each value appears once.

    (b) \(x=(y-1)^2-1\Rightarrow(y-1)^2=x+1\Rightarrow y-1=\sqrt{x+1}\) (positive, because \(y\ge1\)) \(\Rightarrow y=1+\sqrt{x+1}\).

    The range of \(f\) is \(y\ge-1\) (its lowest value), so the domain of \(f^{-1}\) is \(x\ge-1\).

    (c) \(x^2-2x=x\Rightarrow x^2-3x=0\Rightarrow x(x-3)=0\). \(x=0\) is not in the domain \(x\ge1\); \(x=3\) is. Point \((3,3)\).

    Answer: (b) \(f^{-1}(x)=1+\sqrt{x+1}\), \(x\ge-1\); (c) \((3,3)\).

  2. \(h(x)=\sqrt{9-x^2}\).

    1. Find the domain and the range.
    2. Describe the graph and its symmetry.
    3. Solve \(h(x)=\sqrt5\).
    Show solution

    Idea. As in question 12, \(y=\sqrt{9-x^2}\) is the upper half of a circle; its domain, range and symmetry follow.

    (a) \(9-x^2\ge0\Rightarrow -3\le x\le3\). The values go from 0 (at \(x=\pm3\)) to 3 (at \(x=0\)): \(0\le y\le3\).

    (b) \(y^2=9-x^2\Rightarrow x^2+y^2=9\) with \(y\ge0\): the upper half of the circle of radius 3. \(h(-x)=h(x)\): even.

    (c) \(\sqrt{9-x^2}=\sqrt5\Rightarrow 9-x^2=5\Rightarrow x^2=4\Rightarrow x=\pm2\).

    Answer: (a) \([-3,3]\) and \([0,3]\); (b) upper half circle, even; (c) \(x=\pm2\).

  3. \(f(x)=|x-2|-3\) and \(g(x)=|f(x)|\).

    1. Find the roots of \(f\).
    2. Find \(g(2)\) and describe the graph of \(g\) between the roots.
    3. Solve \(g(x)=1\).
    Show solution

    Idea. \(|x-2|-3\) is a "V" with its corner at \((2,-3)\). Taking the absolute value flips the part below the axis up.

    (a) \(|x-2|=3\Rightarrow x-2=\pm3\Rightarrow x=5\) or \(x=-1\).

    (b) \(f(2)=-3\), so \(g(2)=3\). Between the roots \(f\) is negative; \(g\) flips this part up into an upside-down V with peak \((2,3)\).

    (c) \(|f(x)|=1\Rightarrow f(x)=1\) or \(f(x)=-1\).

    \(|x-2|=4\Rightarrow x=6\) or \(x=-2\). \(|x-2|=2\Rightarrow x=4\) or \(x=0\).

    Answer: (a) 5 and \(-1\); (b) \(g(2)=3\), a peak; (c) \(x=-2,0,4,6\).

  4. \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\), \(g(x)=x+1\).

    1. Find \(f(g(x))\), \(g(f(x))\) and \(f(f(x))\).
    2. Solve \(f(g(x))=g(f(x))\).
    Show solution

    Idea. Compose by substituting; then solve the equation by cross-multiplying.

    (a) \(f(g(x))=f(x+1)=\dfrac1{x+1}\). \(g(f(x))=g\left(\tfrac1x\right)=\dfrac1x+1\). \(f(f(x))=\dfrac1{1/x}=x\) (for \(x\ne0\)).

    (b) \(\dfrac1{x+1}=\dfrac{1+x}x\). Cross-multiply: \(x=(x+1)^2=x^2+2x+1\Rightarrow x^2+x+1=0\).

    \(\Delta=1-4=-3\lt 0\): no real solution.

    Answer: (a) as above; (b) no solution.

  5. Find all linear functions \(f(x)=ax+b\) with \(f(f(x))=4x+9\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute \(f(f(x))\) in terms of \(a,b\) and compare coefficients with \(4x+9\).

    \(f(f(x))=a(ax+b)+b=a^2x+ab+b\).

    Comparing: \(a^2=4\) and \(ab+b=9\), i.e. \(b(a+1)=9\).

    \(a=2\): \(3b=9\Rightarrow b=3\). \(a=-2\): \(-b=9\Rightarrow b=-9\).

    Answer: \(f(x)=2x+3\) or \(f(x)=-2x-9\).

    Check: \(2(2x+3)+3=4x+9\) ✓; \(-2(-2x-9)-9=4x+9\) ✓

  6. Find the inverse of \(f(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\). What do you notice?

    Show solution

    Idea. Swap \(x\) and \(y\), multiply out, and collect the \(y\) terms on one side.

    \(x=\dfrac{y+1}{y-1}\Rightarrow x(y-1)=y+1\Rightarrow xy-x=y+1\Rightarrow xy-y=x+1\Rightarrow y(x-1)=x+1\Rightarrow y=\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\).

    This is the same formula as \(f\).

    Answer: \(f^{-1}=f\). The function is its own inverse, so its graph is symmetric about \(y=x\).

  7. \(f(x)=\sqrt{x+4}\).

    1. Find \(f^{-1}\) and explain why its domain is \(x\ge0\).
    2. Find where the graph of \(f\) meets the line \(y=x\).
    Show solution

    Idea. Swap and solve. The values of \(f\) are never negative, so the inverse is defined only for \(x\ge0\). For (b), square and check.

    (a) \(x=\sqrt{y+4}\) (so \(x\ge0\)) \(\Rightarrow x^2=y+4\Rightarrow y=x^2-4\). \(f^{-1}(x)=x^2-4\), \(x\ge0\).

    Without the restriction, \(x^2-4\) would include the left half of the parabola, which is not the reflection of \(f\).

    (b) \(\sqrt{x+4}=x\) needs \(x\ge0\). Square: \(x+4=x^2\Rightarrow x^2-x-4=0\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{1\pm\sqrt{17}}2\).

    \(\dfrac{1-\sqrt{17}}2\lt 0\): rejected. \(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{17}}2\approx2.56\): kept.

    Answer: (a) \(f^{-1}(x)=x^2-4\), \(x\ge0\); (b) \(x=\dfrac{1+\sqrt{17}}2\).

  8. Cube roots.

    1. Solve \(\sqrt[3]{x-1}=2\).
    2. Solve \(x^3=-27\).
    3. Explain why \(\sqrt[3]{x^3}=x\) for every \(x\), while \(\sqrt{x^2}=x\) is not always true.
    Show solution

    Idea. Cubing undoes a cube root, for every number, positive or negative.

    (a) \(\sqrt[3]{x-1}=2\Rightarrow x-1=2^3=8\Rightarrow x=9\).

    (b) \(x^3=-27\Rightarrow x=\sqrt[3]{-27}=-3\).

    (c) \(x^3\) is one-to-one on all real numbers (always increasing), so its inverse \(\sqrt[3]{\ }\) undoes it everywhere: \(\sqrt[3]{x^3}=x\). \(x^2\) is not one-to-one (\(x\) and \(-x\) give the same square), so \(\sqrt{x^2}\) cannot recover the sign: it gives \(|x|\).

    Answer: (a) 9; (b) \(-3\); (c) as explained.

  9. Simplify \(\sqrt{(x-3)^2}\) for \(x\lt 3\), and solve \(\sqrt{(x-3)^2}=x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\sqrt{a^2}=|a|\). For \(x\lt 3\) the expression \(x-3\) is negative, so \(|x-3|=3-x\).

    \(\sqrt{(x-3)^2}=|x-3|=3-x\) for \(x\lt 3\).

    Solve \(|x-3|=x\) (needs \(x\ge0\)):

    If \(x\ge3\): \(x-3=x\Rightarrow-3=0\), impossible.

    If \(x\lt 3\): \(3-x=x\Rightarrow x=1.5\).

    Answer: \(3-x\); the solution is \(x=1.5\).

    Check: \(\sqrt{(1.5-3)^2}=1.5\) ✓

  10. The temperature in Fahrenheit is \(F=1.8C+32\), where \(C\) is the temperature in Celsius.

    1. Find the inverse function, giving \(C\) in terms of \(F\).
    2. Convert 212 °F to Celsius.
    3. At which temperature do both scales show the same number?
    Show solution

    Idea. The inverse function reverses the conversion: solve the formula for \(C\). Equal readings mean \(F=C\).

    (a) \(F=1.8C+32\Rightarrow F-32=1.8C\Rightarrow C=\dfrac{F-32}{1.8}\).

    (b) \(C=\dfrac{212-32}{1.8}=\dfrac{180}{1.8}=100\). (Water boils at 212 °F = 100 °C.)

    (c) \(C=1.8C+32\Rightarrow-0.8C=32\Rightarrow C=-40\).

    Answer: (a) \(C=\dfrac{F-32}{1.8}\); (b) 100 °C; (c) \(-40\) (where \(-40\) °C \(=-40\) °F).
