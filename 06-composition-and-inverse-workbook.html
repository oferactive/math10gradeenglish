Before you start: For every inverse you find, check it: substitute it into the original function and make sure you get \(x\) back.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. \(f(x)=2x+1\), \(g(x)=x^2\). Compute:

    1. \(f(g(2))\)
    2. \(g(f(2))\)
    3. \(f(f(1))\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(f(4)=9\)

    (b) \(g(5)=25\)

    (c) \(f(3)=7\)

  2. Name the inner and the outer function.

    1. \((3x-1)^5\)
    2. \(\sqrt{x^2+1}\)
    3. \(|x-4|\)
    4. \(\dfrac1{x+2}\)
    Show solution

    (a) inner \(3x-1\), outer \(u^5\)

    (b) inner \(x^2+1\), outer \(\sqrt u\)

    (c) inner \(x-4\), outer \(|u|\)

    (d) inner \(x+2\), outer \(\tfrac1u\)

  3. \(f(x)=x+5\), \(g(x)=3x\). Find \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\). Are they equal?

    Show solution

    \(f(g(x))=3x+5\), \(g(f(x))=3(x+5)=3x+15\). Not equal.

  4. Find the inverse.

    1. \(f(x)=x+4\)
    2. \(f(x)=5x\)
    3. \(f(x)=2x-3\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(x-4\)

    (b) \(\tfrac x5\)

    (c) \(\dfrac{x+3}2\)

  5. Compute.

    1. \(\sqrt{(-5)^2}\)
    2. \((\sqrt9)^2\)
    3. \(\sqrt[3]{-8}\)
    4. \(16^{1/2}\)
    5. \(27^{1/3}\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(5\)

    (b) \(9\)

    (c) \(-2\)

    (d) \(4\)

    (e) \(3\)

  6. Which functions are one-to-one on all real numbers (or on their whole domain)?

    1. \(y=x^3\)
    2. \(y=x^2\)
    3. \(y=\tfrac1x\)
    4. \(y=|x|\)
    Show solution

    (a) yes, always increasing

    (b) no: \(f(2)=f(-2)\)

    (c) yes: each branch is decreasing, and the two branches have values of different sign

    (d) no: \(|3|=|-3|\)

  7. \(f\) has an inverse and \(f(3)=7\). Find \(f^{-1}(7)\). Which point is on the graph of \(f^{-1}\)?

    Show solution

    \(f^{-1}(7)=3\). The point \((7,3)\).

  8. Find the domain of \(y=\sqrt{2x+8}\).

    Show solution

    \(2x+8\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge-4\)

  9. \(f(x)=x^2\), \(g(x)=x-1\). Solve \(f(g(x))=9\).

    Show solution

    \((x-1)^2=9\Rightarrow x-1=\pm3\Rightarrow x=4\) or \(x=-2\).

  10. Write \(h(x)=\sqrt{5-x}\) as a composition \(f(g(x))\).

    Show solution

    \(g(x)=5-x\), \(f(u)=\sqrt u\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. \(f(x)=x^2-4\). Where are the graphs of \(f\) and \(|f|\) different? Compute \(|f(0)|\).

    Show solution

    Where \(f\lt 0\): \(-2\lt x\lt 2\). There the graph of \(|f|\) is the reflection of \(f\) in the x-axis.

    \(|f(0)|=|-4|=4\).

  2. Find the domain of each function. For (b), show that the graph is half of a circle.

    1. \(y=\sqrt{x^2-9}\)
    2. \(y=\sqrt{4-x^2}\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(x^2-9\ge0\Rightarrow x\le-3\) or \(x\ge3\).

    (b) \(-2\le x\le2\). With \(y\ge0\) and \(y^2=4-x^2\): \(x^2+y^2=4\), the upper half of the circle of radius 2.

  3. Find the inverse of \(f(x)=\dfrac1{x-2}\). Compare the asymptotes of \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\).

    Show solution

    \(x=\dfrac1{y-2}\Rightarrow y-2=\dfrac1x\Rightarrow f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac1x+2\).

    \(f\): asymptotes \(x=2\), \(y=0\). \(f^{-1}\): asymptotes \(x=0\), \(y=2\). They are swapped, as expected from the reflection in \(y=x\).

  4. Find the inverse of \(f(x)=x^2+1\), \(x\ge0\), and its domain and range.

    Show solution

    \(f^{-1}(x)=\sqrt{x-1}\). Domain \(x\ge1\) (the range of \(f\)); range \(y\ge0\) (the domain of \(f\)).

  5. Find the inverse of \(f(x)=x^3-2\).

    Show solution

    \(x=y^3-2\Rightarrow y=\sqrt[3]{x+2}\). \(f^{-1}(x)=\sqrt[3]{x+2}\), for all \(x\).

  6. Show that \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\) and \(g(x)=-x+3\) are each their own inverse. What do their graphs have in common?

    Show solution

    \(x=\tfrac1y\Rightarrow y=\tfrac1x\). \(x=-y+3\Rightarrow y=-x+3\).

    Both graphs are symmetric about the line \(y=x\).

  7. \(f(x)=2x+1\), \(g(x)=\dfrac{x-1}2\). Show that \(g\) is the inverse of \(f\).

    Show solution

    \(f(g(x))=2\cdot\dfrac{x-1}2+1=x\) and \(g(f(x))=\dfrac{2x+1-1}2=x\).

  8. \(f(x)=\sqrt x\), \(g(x)=x-3\). Find \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\) with their domains.

    Show solution

    \(f(g(x))=\sqrt{x-3}\), \(x\ge3\).

    \(g(f(x))=\sqrt x-3\), \(x\ge0\).

  9. Solve each equation.

    1. \(\sqrt{x^2}=4\)
    2. \((\sqrt x)^2=4\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(|x|=4\Rightarrow x=\pm4\).

    (b) Domain \(x\ge0\): \(x=4\) only.

  10. Find the point where the graph of \(f(x)=2x-3\) meets the graph of its inverse.

    Show solution

    The graphs are mirror images in \(y=x\), and here they meet on that line: \(2x-3=x\Rightarrow x=3\). The point \((3,3)\).

    (Check: \(f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{x+3}2\) and \(f^{-1}(3)=3\) ✓.)

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. \(f(x)=x^2-2x\), \(x\ge1\).

    1. Show that \(f\) is one-to-one.
    2. Find \(f^{-1}\) and its domain.
    3. Find where the graph of \(f\) meets the line \(y=x\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(f(x)=(x-1)^2-1\): vertex at \(x=1\), so for \(x\ge1\) the function increases and is one-to-one.

    (b) \(x=(y-1)^2-1\Rightarrow (y-1)^2=x+1\Rightarrow y=1+\sqrt{x+1}\) (because \(y\ge1\)). Domain \(x\ge-1\).

    (c) \((x-1)^2-1=x\Rightarrow x^2-3x=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=3\). Only \(x=3\) is in the domain: \((3,3)\).

  2. \(h(x)=\sqrt{9-x^2}\).

    1. Find the domain and the range.
    2. Describe the graph and its symmetry.
    3. Solve \(h(x)=\sqrt5\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(-3\le x\le3\), \(0\le y\le3\).

    (b) the upper half of the circle \(x^2+y^2=9\); even.

    (c) \(9-x^2=5\Rightarrow x=\pm2\).

  3. \(f(x)=|x-2|-3\) and \(g(x)=|f(x)|\).

    1. Find the roots of \(f\).
    2. Find \(g(2)\) and describe the graph of \(g\) between the roots.
    3. Solve \(g(x)=1\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(|x-2|=3\Rightarrow x=5\) or \(x=-1\).

    (b) \(f(2)=-3\Rightarrow g(2)=3\). Between the roots the V of \(f\) is reflected up, giving a peak at \((2,3)\).

    (c) \(|x-2|-3=1\Rightarrow x=6,-2\); \(|x-2|-3=-1\Rightarrow x=4,0\). Four solutions: \(-2,0,4,6\).

  4. \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\), \(g(x)=x+1\).

    1. Find \(f(g(x))\), \(g(f(x))\) and \(f(f(x))\).
    2. Solve \(f(g(x))=g(f(x))\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(f(g(x))=\dfrac1{x+1}\), \(g(f(x))=\dfrac1x+1\), \(f(f(x))=x\) (for \(x\ne0\)).

    (b) \(\dfrac1{x+1}=\dfrac{1+x}x\Rightarrow x=(x+1)^2\Rightarrow x^2+x+1=0\). \(\Delta=-3\lt 0\): no solution.

  5. Find all linear functions \(f(x)=ax+b\) with \(f(f(x))=4x+9\).

    Show solution

    \(f(f(x))=a(ax+b)+b=a^2x+ab+b\).

    \(a^2=4\) and \(ab+b=9\).

    \(a=2\Rightarrow 3b=9\Rightarrow b=3\): \(f(x)=2x+3\).

    \(a=-2\Rightarrow -b=9\Rightarrow b=-9\): \(f(x)=-2x-9\).

  6. Find the inverse of \(f(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\). What do you notice?

    Show solution

    \(x=\dfrac{y+1}{y-1}\Rightarrow xy-x=y+1\Rightarrow y(x-1)=x+1\Rightarrow y=\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\).

    \(f^{-1}=f\): the function is its own inverse, and its graph is symmetric about \(y=x\).

  7. \(f(x)=\sqrt{x+4}\).

    1. Find \(f^{-1}\) and explain why its domain is \(x\ge0\).
    2. Find where the graph of \(f\) meets the line \(y=x\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(x=\sqrt{y+4}\) with \(x\ge0\Rightarrow f^{-1}(x)=x^2-4\), \(x\ge0\). The restriction is needed because the values of \(f\) are never negative.

    (b) \(\sqrt{x+4}=x\Rightarrow x^2-x-4=0\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{1\pm\sqrt{17}}2\). Only the positive root works: \(x=\dfrac{1+\sqrt{17}}2\approx2.56\).

  8. Cube roots.

    1. Solve \(\sqrt[3]{x-1}=2\).
    2. Solve \(x^3=-27\).
    3. Explain why \(\sqrt[3]{x^3}=x\) for every \(x\), while \(\sqrt{x^2}=x\) is not always true.
    Show solution

    (a) \(x-1=8\Rightarrow x=9\).

    (b) \(x=\sqrt[3]{-27}=-3\).

    (c) \(x^3\) is one-to-one on all numbers, so \(\sqrt[3]{x^3}=x\) and \((\sqrt[3]x)^3=x\) for every \(x\). For square roots this works only for \(x\ge0\).

  9. Simplify \(\sqrt{(x-3)^2}\) for \(x\lt 3\), and solve \(\sqrt{(x-3)^2}=x\).

    Show solution

    \(\sqrt{(x-3)^2}=|x-3|=3-x\) for \(x\lt 3\).

    \(|x-3|=x\): \(x-3=x\) is impossible; \(3-x=x\Rightarrow x=1.5\).

  10. The temperature in Fahrenheit is \(F=1.8C+32\), where \(C\) is the temperature in Celsius.

    1. Find the inverse function, giving \(C\) in terms of \(F\).
    2. Convert 212 °F to Celsius.
    3. At which temperature do both scales show the same number?
    Show solution

    (a) \(C=\dfrac{F-32}{1.8}\).

    (b) \(C=\dfrac{212-32}{1.8}=100\).

    (c) \(C=1.8C+32\Rightarrow -0.8C=32\Rightarrow C=-40\). At \(-40\) degrees both scales agree.
