1. Rate of change in real life

Many questions in science and daily life are about how fast something changes: how fast a car moves, how fast the fuel runs out, how fast a cup of tea cools down.

If the change is uniform (always the same speed), the graph is a straight line, and its slope is the rate of change. Usually the rate is not uniform: a car speeds up and slows down, tea cools fast at first and slowly later.

Example 1

A car drives 150 km in the first 2 hours and 90 km in the next 1.5 hours. Find its average speed in each part and on the whole trip.

Solution.

First part: \(\dfrac{150}{2}=75\) km/h. Second part: \(\dfrac{90}{1.5}=60\) km/h.

Whole trip: \(\dfrac{240}{3.5}\approx68.6\) km/h. (Not the average of 75 and 60!)

2. Average rate of change: the slope of a secant

For a function \(f\), the average rate of change between \(x_0\) and \(x_0+\Delta x\) is the "step" \(\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\). On the graph, it is the slope of the secant: the line through the two points of the graph.

\(\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=\dfrac{f(x_0+\Delta x)-f(x_0)}{\Delta x}\)
average rate of change = slope of the secant

3. From the secant to the tangent

Now make the step narrower: keep \(x_0\) fixed and let \(\Delta x\) become smaller and smaller. The second point slides towards the first one, and the secant turns towards a limiting line: the tangent at \(x_0\).

Another way to see it: zoom in on the graph near the point. A smooth curve looks more and more like a straight line. That line is the tangent, the best straight-line approximation of the curve near the point.

Example 2

For \(f(x)=x^2\) at \(x_0=1\), compute the slope of the secant for smaller and smaller \(\Delta x\).

Solution.

\(\Delta x\)10.10.010.001
\(\dfrac{f(1+\Delta x)-f(1)}{\Delta x}\)32.12.012.001

The slopes approach 2. The slope of the tangent at \((1,1)\) is 2.

4. The definition of the derivative

The derivative of \(f\) at \(x_0\) is the limit of the average rate of change as \(\Delta x\) goes to 0. It is the instantaneous rate of change of \(f\) at \(x_0\), and the slope of the tangent there.

\(f'(x_0)=\lim\limits_{\Delta x\to0}\dfrac{f(x_0+\Delta x)-f(x_0)}{\Delta x}\)
the derivative at \(x_0\)

We often write \(h\) instead of \(\Delta x\). To compute the limit: simplify the fraction until \(h\) cancels from the denominator, then put \(h=0\).

Example 3

Use the definition to find \(f'(3)\) for \(f(x)=x^2\).

Solution.

\(\dfrac{f(3+h)-f(3)}h=\dfrac{9+6h+h^2-9}h=\dfrac{6h+h^2}h=6+h\)

As \(h\to0\): \(f'(3)=6\).

The derivative function. Doing the same at a general point \(x\) gives a new function \(f'(x)\), which tells the slope at every point:

Example 4

Find \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x)=x^2\).

Solution.

\(\dfrac{(x+h)^2-x^2}h=\dfrac{2xh+h^2}h=2x+h\ \longrightarrow\ 2x\)

So \(f'(x)=2x\). Check: \(f'(1)=2\) and \(f'(3)=6\), as before.

Reading the sign. \(f'(x_0)\gt 0\): the function increases there. \(f'(x_0)\lt 0\): it decreases. \(f'(x_0)=0\): the tangent is horizontal.

5. When there is no derivative

The limit does not always exist. Two typical cases:

At a corner there is no single tangent. For \(\sqrt x\) at 0, the ratio \(\dfrac{\sqrt h-0}{h}=\dfrac1{\sqrt h}\) grows without limit: there is a tangent, but it is vertical, and a vertical line has no slope. So a tangent can exist even when the derivative does not.

6. Common mistakes

  • Computing the average speed of a trip as the average of the speeds of its parts. Divide total distance by total time.
  • Putting \(h=0\) before simplifying. You get \(\tfrac00\), which means nothing. Cancel \(h\) first.
  • Expanding \((x+h)^2\) as \(x^2+h^2\). The correct expansion is \(x^2+2xh+h^2\).
  • Mixing up \(f(x_0)\) (the height of the point) with \(f'(x_0)\) (the slope at the point).
  • Thinking that "no derivative" means "no tangent". \(\sqrt x\) has a vertical tangent at 0.

7. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=x^2\) on \([2,4]\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{16-4}{2}=6\)

  2. Find the average rate of change of \(y=-3x+1\) on any interval.

    Show solution

    \(-3\), the slope of the line.

  3. Given \(f'(x)=2x\) for \(f(x)=x^2\), find the slope of the tangent at \(x=5\).

    Show solution

    \(f'(5)=10\)

  4. For \(f(x)=x^2\), simplify \(\dfrac{f(1+h)-f(1)}h\) and find \(f'(1)\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{1+2h+h^2-1}h=2+h\to2\). \(f'(1)=2\).

  5. What does \(f'(2)=0\) tell you about the graph of \(f\)?

    Show solution

    At \(x=2\) the tangent is horizontal.

  6. Find the tangent to \(y=x^2\) at \(x=-1\).

    Show solution

    Point \((-1,1)\), slope \(2\cdot(-1)=-2\): \(y-1=-2(x+1)\Rightarrow y=-2x-1\).

  7. What is the derivative of a constant function?

    Show solution

    \(0\): its graph is horizontal everywhere.

  8. Does \(|x|\) have a derivative at \(x=0\)?

    Show solution

    No. The graph has a corner: the slope is 1 on the right and \(-1\) on the left.
