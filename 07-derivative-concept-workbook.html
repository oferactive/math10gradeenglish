Before you start: In questions that use the definition, write the difference quotient, expand, cancel \(h\), and only then let \(h\to0\). Keep this order every time.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. A car drives 150 km in 2 hours, then 90 km in 1.5 hours. Find its average speed on each part.

    Show solution

    Idea. Average speed is distance divided by time.

    Part 1: \(\dfrac{150}{2}=75\) km/h.

    Part 2: \(\dfrac{90}{1.5}=60\) km/h.

    Answer: 75 km/h and 60 km/h.

  2. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=3x-2\) on \([0,5]\) and on \([-4,1]\). Explain the result.

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute \(\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\) on each interval.

    \([0,5]\): \(f(5)=13\), \(f(0)=-2\): \(\dfrac{13-(-2)}{5}=\dfrac{15}5=3\).

    \([-4,1]\): \(f(1)=1\), \(f(-4)=-14\): \(\dfrac{1-(-14)}{5}=\dfrac{15}5=3\).

    Answer: both are 3. The graph is a line with slope 3, and a line has the same rate of change on every interval.

  3. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=x^2+1\) on \([1,3]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Average rate of change \(=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\).

    \(f(3)=10\), \(f(1)=2\).

    \(\dfrac{10-2}{3-1}=\dfrac82=4\).

    Answer: 4.

  4. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=x^3\) on \([-1,2]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Average rate of change \(=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\). Be careful with the negative values.

    \(f(2)=8\), \(f(-1)=(-1)^3=-1\).

    \(\dfrac{8-(-1)}{2-(-1)}=\dfrac93=3\).

    Answer: 3.

  5. Find the average rate of change of \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\) on \([1,2]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Average rate of change \(=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\).

    \(f(2)=\tfrac12\), \(f(1)=1\).

    \(\dfrac{\tfrac12-1}{2-1}=-\tfrac12\).

    Answer: \(-\tfrac12\). The function decreases on this interval.

  6. For \(f(x)=x^2\), compute \(\dfrac{f(2+h)-f(2)}h\) for \(h=1\), \(0.1\) and \(0.01\). Guess \(f'(2)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute the difference quotient for smaller and smaller \(h\) and see which number it approaches.

    \(f(2)=4\).

    \(h=1\): \(\dfrac{9-4}1=5\).

    \(h=0.1\): \(\dfrac{4.41-4}{0.1}=4.1\).

    \(h=0.01\): \(\dfrac{4.0401-4}{0.01}=4.01\).

    The values approach 4.

    Answer: \(5,\ 4.1,\ 4.01\); guess \(f'(2)=4\).

    Check: algebraically the quotient is \(4+h\), which tends to 4 ✓

  7. Use the definition to find the derivative.

    1. \(f(x)=5x\)
    2. \(f(x)=7\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute into \(\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h\), simplify, and let \(h\to0\).

    (a) \(\dfrac{5(x+h)-5x}h=\dfrac{5h}h=5\). This does not depend on \(h\), so the limit is 5.

    (b) \(f(x+h)=7\) as well, so \(\dfrac{7-7}h=0\).

    Answer: (a) \(f'(x)=5\); (b) \(f'(x)=0\).

  8. It is known that \(f'(3)=-2\). What does this tell you about the graph of \(f\) near \(x=3\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. \(f'(3)\) is the slope of the tangent at \(x=3\), and it describes how \(f\) behaves very close to that point.

    The slope is negative, so the graph goes down there.

    Slope \(-2\) means: a small step \(\Delta x\) to the right changes \(y\) by about \(-2\Delta x\).

    Answer: near \(x=3\) the function decreases, about 2 units down for each unit to the right.

  9. The tangent to \(y=x^2\) at \(x=1\) has slope 2. Find its equation.

    Show solution

    Idea. Find the point on the curve, then use \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\).

    Point: \(y(1)=1\), so \((1,1)\). Slope \(m=2\).

    \(y-1=2(x-1)\Rightarrow y=2x-1\).

    Answer: \(y=2x-1\).

  10. For \(f(x)=x^2\), what is the sign of \(f'(-2)\), \(f'(0)\) and \(f'(3)\)? Explain with the graph.

    Show solution

    Idea. Use \(f'(x)=2x\) and look at the sign; connect it with where the parabola goes up or down.

    \(f'(-2)=-4\lt 0\), \(f'(0)=0\), \(f'(3)=6\gt 0\).

    On the left of the vertex the parabola goes down (negative slope); at the vertex the tangent is horizontal; on the right it goes up (positive slope).

    Answer: negative, zero, positive.

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Use the definition to find \(f'(-1)\) for \(f(x)=x^2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Use \(\dfrac{f(-1+h)-f(-1)}h\) and let \(h\to0\).

    \(f(-1+h)=(-1+h)^2=1-2h+h^2\), \(f(-1)=1\).

    \(\dfrac{1-2h+h^2-1}h=\dfrac{-2h+h^2}h=-2+h\).

    As \(h\to0\): \(-2\).

    Answer: \(f'(-1)=-2\).

    Check: \(2x\) at \(x=-1\) gives \(-2\) ✓

  2. Use the definition to find \(f'(2)\) for \(f(x)=x^2+3x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute \(f(2+h)\) and \(f(2)\), subtract, divide by \(h\), and let \(h\to0\).

    \(f(2+h)=(2+h)^2+3(2+h)=4+4h+h^2+6+3h=10+7h+h^2\).

    \(f(2)=4+6=10\).

    \(\dfrac{10+7h+h^2-10}h=\dfrac{7h+h^2}h=7+h\to7\).

    Answer: \(f'(2)=7\).

  3. Use the definition to show that the derivative of \(f(x)=x^2\) is \(f'(x)=2x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Use the general point \(x\) in the definition.

    \((x+h)^2=x^2+2xh+h^2\).

    \(\dfrac{x^2+2xh+h^2-x^2}h=\dfrac{2xh+h^2}h=2x+h\).

    As \(h\to0\): \(2x+h\to2x\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=2x\).

  4. Use the definition to show that the derivative of \(f(x)=x^3\) is \(f'(x)=3x^2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Expand \((x+h)^3\); after subtracting \(x^3\) every term has a factor \(h\).

    \((x+h)^3=x^3+3x^2h+3xh^2+h^3\).

    \(\dfrac{(x+h)^3-x^3}h=\dfrac{3x^2h+3xh^2+h^3}h=3x^2+3xh+h^2\).

    As \(h\to0\) the terms with \(h\) vanish.

    Answer: \(f'(x)=3x^2\).

  5. Use the definition to find \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x)=2x^2-x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Write \(f(x+h)-f(x)\), simplify, divide by \(h\), let \(h\to0\).

    \(f(x+h)=2(x+h)^2-(x+h)=2x^2+4xh+2h^2-x-h\).

    \(f(x+h)-f(x)=4xh+2h^2-h\).

    \(\dfrac{4xh+2h^2-h}h=4x+2h-1\to4x-1\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=4x-1\).

  6. Use the definition to find \(f'(2)\) for \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Put the two fractions over a common denominator, then cancel \(h\).

    \(\dfrac1{2+h}-\dfrac12=\dfrac{2-(2+h)}{2(2+h)}=\dfrac{-h}{2(2+h)}\).

    Divide by \(h\): \(\dfrac{-1}{2(2+h)}\).

    As \(h\to0\): \(\dfrac{-1}{2\cdot2}=-\dfrac14\).

    Answer: \(f'(2)=-\tfrac14\).

  7. Find the tangent to \(y=x^3\) at \(x=1\) (use \(f'(x)=3x^2\)).

    Show solution

    Idea. Point from the function, slope from the derivative, then \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\).

    Point: \((1,1)\). Slope: \(3\cdot1^2=3\).

    \(y-1=3(x-1)\Rightarrow y=3x-2\).

    Answer: \(y=3x-2\).

  8. A stone falls \(s(t)=5t^2\) metres in \(t\) seconds.

    1. Find the average speed between \(t=1\) and \(t=3\).
    2. Use the definition to find the speed at \(t=2\).
    Show solution

    Idea. Average speed is \(\dfrac{s(b)-s(a)}{b-a}\); the speed at an instant is the derivative \(s'(t)\).

    (a) \(s(3)=45\), \(s(1)=5\): \(\dfrac{45-5}{3-1}=20\) m/s.

    (b) \(s(2+h)=5(4+4h+h^2)=20+20h+5h^2\), \(s(2)=20\).

    \(\dfrac{20h+5h^2}h=20+5h\to20\).

    Answer: (a) 20 m/s; (b) 20 m/s.

  9. The temperature of water being heated was measured: at 0, 2, 4 and 6 minutes it was 20°, 32°, 40° and 44°. Find the average rate of heating in each 2-minute interval. What happens to the rate?

    Show solution

    Idea. In each interval divide the temperature change by the time (2 minutes).

    0–2: \(\dfrac{32-20}2=6\) degrees per minute.

    2–4: \(\dfrac{40-32}2=4\) degrees per minute.

    4–6: \(\dfrac{44-40}2=2\) degrees per minute.

    Answer: 6, 4 and 2 degrees per minute. The rate gets smaller: the water heats more and more slowly.

  10. At which point of the parabola \(y=x^2\) is the slope of the tangent equal to 6?

    Show solution

    Idea. The slope of the tangent at \(x\) is \(f'(x)=2x\). Set it equal to 6.

    \(2x=6\Rightarrow x=3\), and \(y=3^2=9\).

    Answer: the point \((3,9)\).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Use the definition to find \(f'(4)\) for \(f(x)=\sqrt x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Roots do not cancel directly; multiply by the conjugate \(\sqrt{4+h}+2\) to remove the root from the numerator.

    \(\dfrac{\sqrt{4+h}-2}h\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{4+h}+2}{\sqrt{4+h}+2}=\dfrac{(4+h)-4}{h(\sqrt{4+h}+2)}\).

    \(=\dfrac{h}{h(\sqrt{4+h}+2)}=\dfrac1{\sqrt{4+h}+2}\).

    As \(h\to0\): \(\dfrac1{2+2}=\dfrac14\).

    Answer: \(f'(4)=\tfrac14\).

  2. The tangent to \(y=x^2\) at the point \((a,a^2)\) has slope \(2a\).

    1. Show that the tangent is \(y=2ax-a^2\).
    2. Find the tangents to the parabola that pass through \((0,-4)\).
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Use point and slope. (b) Demand that the tangent pass through \((0,-4)\) and solve for \(a\).

    (a) \(y-a^2=2a(x-a)\Rightarrow y=2ax-2a^2+a^2=2ax-a^2\).

    (b) Substitute \(x=0\), \(y=-4\): \(-4=0-a^2\Rightarrow a^2=4\Rightarrow a=\pm2\).

    \(a=2\): \(y=4x-4\). \(a=-2\): \(y=-4x-4\).

    Answer: (a) shown; (b) \(y=4x-4\) (touching at \((2,4)\)) and \(y=-4x-4\) (touching at \((-2,4)\)).

    Check: \(y=4x-4\) at \(x=0\) gives \(-4\) ✓

  3. Water drains from a tank. After \(t\) minutes the tank holds \(V(t)=200-20t+0.5t^2\) litres (\(0\le t\le20\)).

    1. How much water is there at the start and at the end?
    2. Find the average rate of change on \([0,20]\).
    3. Use the definition to find \(V'(t)\), and compute \(V'(0)\) and \(V'(10)\). Explain the sign.
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Substitute \(t=0\) and \(t=20\). (b) Average rate \(=\dfrac{V(20)-V(0)}{20}\). (c) Definition of the derivative.

    (a) \(V(0)=200\) litres. \(V(20)=200-400+200=0\): the tank is empty.

    (b) \(\dfrac{0-200}{20-0}=-10\) litres per minute.

    (c) \(V(t+h)-V(t)=-20h+0.5(2th+h^2)=-20h+th+0.5h^2\).

    Divide by \(h\): \(-20+t+0.5h\to t-20\). So \(V'(t)=t-20\).

    \(V'(0)=-20\), \(V'(10)=-10\).

    Answer: (a) 200 and 0 litres; (b) \(-10\) L/min; (c) \(V'(t)=t-20\), \(V'(0)=-20\), \(V'(10)=-10\). The negative sign means the volume is decreasing; the water drains fast at first and slower later.

  4. Show that \(f(x)=|x|\) has no derivative at \(x=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Look at the difference quotient for \(h\gt 0\) and \(h\lt 0\) separately.

    \(\dfrac{f(0+h)-f(0)}h=\dfrac{|h|}h\).

    For \(h\gt 0\): \(|h|=h\), so the quotient is 1.

    For \(h\lt 0\): \(|h|=-h\), so the quotient is \(-1\).

    The quotient does not approach one number, so the limit does not exist.

    Answer: \(f'(0)\) does not exist; the graph has a corner at the origin.

  5. Show that \(f(x)=\sqrt x\) has no derivative at \(x=0\), and describe the tangent there.

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute the difference quotient at 0 and see what happens as \(h\to0^+\) (only \(h\gt 0\) is allowed in \(\sqrt h\)).

    \(\dfrac{\sqrt h-0}h=\dfrac{\sqrt h}{h}=\dfrac1{\sqrt h}\).

    As \(h\to0^+\), \(\sqrt h\to0\), so \(\dfrac1{\sqrt h}\) grows without limit.

    Answer: no derivative at 0. The secants become steeper and steeper; the tangent is the vertical line \(x=0\), which has no slope.

  6. Find the point on \(y=x^2\) where the tangent is parallel to \(y=4x+1\), and find that tangent.

    Show solution

    Idea. Parallel lines have equal slopes. The given line has slope 4, so solve \(f'(x)=4\).

    \(2x=4\Rightarrow x=2\), point \((2,4)\).

    \(y-4=4(x-2)\Rightarrow y=4x-4\).

    Answer: the point \((2,4)\) and the tangent \(y=4x-4\).

  7. \(f(x)=x^2-4x\).

    1. Use the definition to find \(f'(x)\).
    2. Where is \(f'(x)=0\)? What is special about that point?
    3. Where is \(f\) increasing?
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Definition. (b) \(f'=0\) gives a horizontal tangent. (c) \(f\) increases where \(f'\gt 0\).

    (a) \(f(x+h)-f(x)=(x+h)^2-4(x+h)-x^2+4x=2xh+h^2-4h\).

    Divide by \(h\): \(2x+h-4\to2x-4\).

    (b) \(2x-4=0\Rightarrow x=2\); \(f(2)=4-8=-4\). This is the vertex \((2,-4)\), where the tangent is horizontal.

    (c) \(2x-4\gt 0\Rightarrow x\gt 2\).

    Answer: (a) \(f'(x)=2x-4\); (b) at \(x=2\), the vertex (minimum) \((2,-4)\); (c) increasing for \(x\gt 2\), decreasing for \(x\lt 2\).

  8. The tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(x=4\) has slope \(\tfrac14\) (question 21). Use the tangent to estimate \(\sqrt{4.2}\), and compare with a calculator.

    Show solution

    Idea. Near the point of tangency the tangent line is very close to the curve, so its \(y\)-value is a good estimate.

    Point: \((4,2)\), slope \(\tfrac14\): \(y=2+\tfrac14(x-4)\).

    At \(x=4.2\): \(y=2+\tfrac14\cdot0.2=2+0.05=2.05\).

    A calculator gives \(\sqrt{4.2}\approx2.0494\).

    Answer: \(\sqrt{4.2}\approx2.05\); the error is only about 0.0006.

  9. Show that the slope of the secant of \(y=x^2\) between \(x=a\) and \(x=b\) is \(a+b\). Then show that it is parallel to the tangent at the midpoint \(x=\tfrac{a+b}2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Factor \(b^2-a^2\) as a difference of squares, then compare with the derivative \(2x\) at the midpoint.

    Secant slope: \(\dfrac{b^2-a^2}{b-a}=\dfrac{(b-a)(b+a)}{b-a}=a+b\).

    Tangent at \(x=\tfrac{a+b}2\): \(2\cdot\tfrac{a+b}2=a+b\).

    Equal slopes, so the lines are parallel.

    Answer: shown. For example, \(a=1\), \(b=3\): secant slope 4, tangent at \(x=2\) has slope 4.

  10. \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\).

    1. Use the definition to find \(f'(1)\).
    2. Find the tangent at \(x=1\).
    3. Find the area of the triangle formed by the tangent and the axes.
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Definition with a common denominator. (b) Point and slope. (c) Find where the tangent cuts the axes; the triangle is right-angled at the origin.

    (a) \(\dfrac1{1+h}-1=\dfrac{1-(1+h)}{1+h}=\dfrac{-h}{1+h}\). Divide by \(h\): \(\dfrac{-1}{1+h}\to-1\).

    (b) Point \((1,1)\), slope \(-1\): \(y-1=-(x-1)\Rightarrow y=-x+2\).

    (c) \(y=0\Rightarrow x=2\); \(x=0\Rightarrow y=2\). Legs 2 and 2.

    Area \(=\dfrac{2\cdot2}2=2\).

    Answer: (a) \(f'(1)=-1\); (b) \(y=-x+2\); (c) area 2.
