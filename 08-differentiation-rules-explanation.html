1. The power rule

In Unit 07 we found from the definition that \((x^2)'=2x\) and \((x^3)'=3x^2\). The pattern continues for every whole power:

\((x^n)'=n\,x^{n-1}\)
bring the power down and lower it by one
Constant\((c)'=0\)
Identity\((x)'=1\)
Examples\((x^5)'=5x^4,\quad(x^{10})'=10x^9\)

Why does it work? In \((x+h)^n=x^n+n x^{n-1}h+(\text{terms with }h^2,h^3,\dots)\), only the term \(nx^{n-1}h\) survives after dividing by \(h\) and letting \(h\to0\).

2. Constant multiples, sums and differences

Constant multiple\((a\cdot f)'=a\cdot f'\)
Sum and difference\((f\pm g)'=f'\pm g'\)

Proof of the sum rule: \(\dfrac{(f+g)(x+h)-(f+g)(x)}h=\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h+\dfrac{g(x+h)-g(x)}h\to f'(x)+g'(x)\). The constant multiple rule is proved the same way.

Together with the power rule, these rules let us differentiate every polynomial term by term.

Example 1

Differentiate \(f(x)=4x^3-5x^2+7x-2\).

Solution.

\(f'(x)=12x^2-10x+7\)

Example 2

Differentiate \(f(x)=(x+1)(x-2)\).

Solution.

Expand first: \(f(x)=x^2-x-2\), so \(f'(x)=2x-1\).

Note: \(f'\) is not the product of the derivatives \(1\cdot1=1\).

3. The product rule

\((f\cdot g)'=f'\cdot g+f\cdot g'\)
derivative of the first times the second, plus the first times the derivative of the second

Proof. Add and subtract \(f(x)g(x+h)\):

\(\dfrac{f(x+h)g(x+h)-f(x)g(x)}h=\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h\cdot g(x+h)+f(x)\cdot\dfrac{g(x+h)-g(x)}h\)

As \(h\to0\) the first fraction tends to \(f'(x)\), \(g(x+h)\) tends to \(g(x)\), and the last fraction tends to \(g'(x)\).

Example 3

Differentiate \(f(x)=(x^2+1)(x-3)\) with the product rule, and check by expanding.

Solution.

\(f'(x)=2x(x-3)+(x^2+1)\cdot1=3x^2-6x+1\)

Check: \(f(x)=x^3-3x^2+x-3\Rightarrow f'(x)=3x^2-6x+1\) ✓

Note: the quotient rule, for \(\left(\tfrac fg\right)'\), is taught in Unit 11 together with rational functions.

4. The chain rule

For a composition \(f(g(x))\) (Unit 06), the rates of change multiply:

\(\big(f(g(x))\big)'=f'(g(x))\cdot g'(x)\)
derivative of the outer function (with the inner one left inside) times the derivative of the inner function

Intuition. If \(u=g(x)\) changes 3 times as fast as \(x\), and \(y=f(u)\) changes 2 times as fast as \(u\), then \(y\) changes \(2\cdot3=6\) times as fast as \(x\).

FunctionInnerDerivative
\((3x-1)^5\)\(3x-1\)\(5(3x-1)^4\cdot3=15(3x-1)^4\)
\((x^2+1)^3\)\(x^2+1\)\(3(x^2+1)^2\cdot2x=6x(x^2+1)^2\)
\((1-x)^6\)\(1-x\)\(6(1-x)^5\cdot(-1)=-6(1-x)^5\)
Example 4

Differentiate \(f(x)=x(x-4)^2\).

Solution.

Product rule, with the chain rule for \((x-4)^2\):

\(f'(x)=1\cdot(x-4)^2+x\cdot2(x-4)=(x-4)(x-4+2x)=(x-4)(3x-4)\)

5. The graph of \(f\) and the graph of \(f'\)

For a smooth function, the graph of \(f'\) records the slope of \(f\) at every point:

On the graph of \(f\)On the graph of \(f'\)
\(f\) increases\(f'\) is positive (above the x-axis)
\(f\) decreases\(f'\) is negative (below the x-axis)
horizontal tangent (maximum, minimum, or flat point)\(f'\) meets the x-axis
\(f\) is steepest\(|f'|\) is largest

6. Common mistakes

  • Differentiating a product as the product of the derivatives. \((fg)'\ne f'g'\).
  • Forgetting the inner derivative in the chain rule: \(\big((3x-1)^5\big)'=15(3x-1)^4\), not \(5(3x-1)^4\).
  • Lowering the power in the chain rule inside the bracket: in \(\big((x^2+1)^3\big)'\) the bracket stays \(x^2+1\).
  • Writing \((7)'=7\) or \((5x)'=5x\). A constant has derivative 0, and \((5x)'=5\).
  • Expanding a large power such as \((2x-1)^8\) instead of using the chain rule.

7. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Differentiate \(x^7\).

    Show solution

    \(7x^6\)

  2. Differentiate \(5x^3-2x+9\).

    Show solution

    \(15x^2-2\)

  3. Differentiate \(x(x+1)\).

    Show solution

    \(x^2+x\Rightarrow 2x+1\)

  4. Differentiate \((x-3)^4\).

    Show solution

    \(4(x-3)^3\cdot1=4(x-3)^3\)

  5. Differentiate \((5x+2)^2\).

    Show solution

    \(2(5x+2)\cdot5=10(5x+2)\)

  6. Differentiate \(x(x^2+1)\) with the product rule.

    Show solution

    \(1\cdot(x^2+1)+x\cdot2x=3x^2+1\)

  7. \(f(x)=2x^3\). Find \(f'(1)\).

    Show solution

    \(f'(x)=6x^2\Rightarrow f'(1)=6\)

  8. \(f(x)=x^2-6x\). Where is \(f'(x)=0\)?

    Show solution

    \(2x-6=0\Rightarrow x=3\)
