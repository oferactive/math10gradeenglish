Before you start: Before differentiating, decide the structure: a sum? a product? a composition? Then choose the rule. Simplify the answer at the end by taking out common factors.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Differentiate.

    1. \(x^5\)
    2. \(4x^3\)
    3. \(-2x\)
    4. \(7\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Power rule \((x^n)'=nx^{n-1}\); a constant factor stays; the derivative of a constant is 0.

    (a) \(5x^4\).

    (b) \(4\cdot3x^2=12x^2\).

    (c) \(-2x=-2x^1\), derivative \(-2\).

    (d) \(7\) is constant, derivative \(0\).

    Answer: (a) \(5x^4\); (b) \(12x^2\); (c) \(-2\); (d) \(0\).

  2. Differentiate \(f(x)=3x^2-5x+1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate each term separately.

    \((3x^2)'=6x\), \((-5x)'=-5\), \((1)'=0\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=6x-5\).

  3. Differentiate \(f(x)=x^4-2x^3+x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate term by term with the power rule.

    \((x^4)'=4x^3\), \((-2x^3)'=-6x^2\), \((x)'=1\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=4x^3-6x^2+1\).

  4. Differentiate \(f(x)=\tfrac12x^2+\tfrac x3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Keep the fractions as coefficients: \(\tfrac x3=\tfrac13x\).

    \(\left(\tfrac12x^2\right)'=\tfrac12\cdot2x=x\); \(\left(\tfrac13x\right)'=\tfrac13\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=x+\tfrac13\).

  5. Differentiate \(f(x)=(x+1)(x-2)\) by expanding first.

    Show solution

    Idea. Expand into a polynomial, then differentiate term by term.

    \((x+1)(x-2)=x^2-2x+x-2=x^2-x-2\).

    Derivative: \(2x-1\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=2x-1\).

  6. \(f(x)=x^3-3x\). Find \(f'(2)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate, then substitute.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-3\).

    \(f'(2)=3\cdot4-3=9\).

    Answer: 9.

  7. \(f(x)=2x^2-8x\). Solve \(f'(x)=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find \(f'\), set it equal to 0.

    \(f'(x)=4x-8\).

    \(4x-8=0\Rightarrow x=2\).

    Answer: \(x=2\).

  8. Differentiate \((2x+1)^3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule with inner \(u=2x+1\), \(u'=2\).

    \(3(2x+1)^2\cdot2\).

    Answer: \(6(2x+1)^2\).

  9. Differentiate \((x^2-3)^4\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule with inner \(u=x^2-3\), \(u'=2x\).

    \(4(x^2-3)^3\cdot2x\).

    Answer: \(8x(x^2-3)^3\).

  10. \(f(x)=x^5-x^3\). Find \(f'(-1)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate, then substitute \(x=-1\). Even powers of \(-1\) give \(+1\).

    \(f'(x)=5x^4-3x^2\).

    \(f'(-1)=5\cdot1-3\cdot1=2\).

    Answer: 2.

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Differentiate \(f(x)=(x^2+1)(x-3)\) with the product rule, and check by expanding.

    Show solution

    Idea. Product rule with \(u=x^2+1\), \(v=x-3\).

    \(u'=2x\), \(v'=1\).

    \(f'(x)=2x(x-3)+(x^2+1)\cdot1=2x^2-6x+x^2+1=3x^2-6x+1\).

    Check by expanding: \(f(x)=x^3-3x^2+x-3\), so \(f'(x)=3x^2-6x+1\) ✓

    Answer: \(f'(x)=3x^2-6x+1\).

  2. Differentiate \(f(x)=x^3(2x-1)^4\) and factor the answer.

    Show solution

    Idea. Product rule; the second factor needs the chain rule. Then take out the common factor \(x^2(2x-1)^3\).

    \(u=x^3\Rightarrow u'=3x^2\). \(v=(2x-1)^4\Rightarrow v'=4(2x-1)^3\cdot2=8(2x-1)^3\).

    \(f'(x)=3x^2(2x-1)^4+8x^3(2x-1)^3\).

    Factor: \(x^2(2x-1)^3\big[3(2x-1)+8x\big]=x^2(2x-1)^3(6x-3+8x)\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=x^2(2x-1)^3(14x-3)\).

  3. Differentiate \((1-x)^6\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule with inner \(u=1-x\), whose derivative is \(-1\). This minus sign is easy to forget.

    \(6(1-x)^5\cdot(-1)\).

    Answer: \(-6(1-x)^5\).

  4. Differentiate \(f(x)=x(x-4)^2\) and factor.

    Show solution

    Idea. Product rule with \(u=x\), \(v=(x-4)^2\), then take out the common factor \((x-4)\).

    \(u'=1\), \(v'=2(x-4)\).

    \(f'(x)=(x-4)^2+x\cdot2(x-4)=(x-4)\big[(x-4)+2x\big]\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=(x-4)(3x-4)\).

  5. \(f(x)=ax^3-x\) and \(f'(1)=8\). Find \(a\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate with \(a\) as a number, substitute \(x=1\), and solve the equation for \(a\).

    \(f'(x)=3ax^2-1\).

    \(f'(1)=3a-1=8\Rightarrow3a=9\Rightarrow a=3\).

    Answer: \(a=3\).

  6. Find the points on the graph of \(f(x)=x^3-12x\) where the tangent is horizontal.

    Show solution

    Idea. A horizontal tangent means slope 0: solve \(f'(x)=0\), then find the \(y\)-values.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-12=0\Rightarrow x^2=4\Rightarrow x=\pm2\).

    \(f(2)=8-24=-16\); \(f(-2)=-8+24=16\).

    Answer: \((2,-16)\) and \((-2,16)\).

  7. \(f(x)=(x^2-1)^3\). Find \(f'(x)\) and solve \(f'(x)=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule, then a product equals zero when one of its factors is zero.

    \(f'(x)=3(x^2-1)^2\cdot2x=6x(x^2-1)^2\).

    \(6x=0\Rightarrow x=0\); \((x^2-1)^2=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=6x(x^2-1)^2\); \(x=0\), \(x=1\), \(x=-1\).

  8. Find the tangent to \(y=(2x-3)^4\) at \(x=1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Point from \(y(1)\), slope from \(y'(1)\) with the chain rule, then \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\).

    \(y(1)=(2-3)^4=1\): point \((1,1)\).

    \(y'=4(2x-3)^3\cdot2=8(2x-3)^3\), so \(y'(1)=8\cdot(-1)^3=-8\).

    \(y-1=-8(x-1)\Rightarrow y=-8x+9\).

    Answer: \(y=-8x+9\).

  9. Use the definition of the derivative to prove that \((f-g)'=f'-g'\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Write the difference quotient of \(f-g\), split it into two quotients, and use that the limit of a difference is the difference of the limits.

    \(\dfrac{(f-g)(x+h)-(f-g)(x)}h=\dfrac{f(x+h)-g(x+h)-f(x)+g(x)}h\).

    \(=\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h-\dfrac{g(x+h)-g(x)}h\).

    As \(h\to0\) the first quotient tends to \(f'(x)\) and the second to \(g'(x)\).

    Answer: \((f-g)'=f'-g'\).

  10. It is known that \(f(2)=3\), \(f'(2)=-1\), \(g(2)=4\), \(g'(2)=5\). Compute at \(x=2\):

    1. \((f+g)'\)
    2. \((f\cdot g)'\)
    3. \((3f)'\)
    4. \((f^2)'\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Use the rules: sum, product \((fg)'=f'g+fg'\), constant multiple, and the chain rule for \(f^2\).

    (a) \(f'(2)+g'(2)=-1+5=4\).

    (b) \(f'(2)g(2)+f(2)g'(2)=(-1)\cdot4+3\cdot5=-4+15=11\).

    (c) \(3f'(2)=-3\).

    (d) \((f^2)'=2f\cdot f'=2\cdot3\cdot(-1)=-6\).

    Answer: (a) 4; (b) 11; (c) \(-3\); (d) \(-6\).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. \(f(x)=x^3-3x^2\).

    1. Find \(f'(x)\).
    2. Where is \(f'\) positive and where negative?
    3. Describe how \(f\) behaves, and find its extreme points.
    4. Describe the graph of \(f'\). What happens on the graph of \(f\) where \(f'\) is smallest?
    Show solution

    Idea. The sign of \(f'\) tells where \(f\) increases or decreases; where \(f'\) changes sign, \(f\) has an extreme point.

    (a) \(f'(x)=3x^2-6x=3x(x-2)\).

    (b) \(f'\) is an upward parabola with roots 0 and 2: positive outside the roots, negative between them.

    (c) \(f\) increases for \(x\lt 0\), decreases on \(0\lt x\lt 2\), increases for \(x\gt 2\).

    Maximum at \(x=0\): \(f(0)=0\). Minimum at \(x=2\): \(f(2)=8-12=-4\).

    (d) The vertex of \(f'\) is at \(x=1\): \(f'(1)=3-6=-3\). There \(f\) has its steepest descent.

    Answer: (a) \(3x(x-2)\); (b) positive for \(x\lt 0\) or \(x\gt 2\), negative for \(0\lt x\lt 2\); (c) max \((0,0)\), min \((2,-4)\); (d) a parabola with lowest point \((1,-3)\); at \(x=1\), \(f\) falls most steeply.

  2. \(f(x)=(x-1)^2(x+2)\). Find \(f'(x)\) in factored form, and the points with a horizontal tangent.

    Show solution

    Idea. Product rule, then factor out \((x-1)\); solve \(f'=0\) and find the \(y\)-values.

    \(f'(x)=2(x-1)(x+2)+(x-1)^2\cdot1\).

    \(=(x-1)\big[2(x+2)+(x-1)\big]=(x-1)(3x+3)=3(x-1)(x+1)\).

    \(f'=0\Rightarrow x=1\) or \(x=-1\).

    \(f(1)=0\); \(f(-1)=(-2)^2\cdot1=4\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=3(x-1)(x+1)\); points \((1,0)\) and \((-1,4)\).

  3. The graph of \(f(x)=x^3+ax^2+b\) has a horizontal tangent at \((1,3)\). Find \(a\) and \(b\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Two conditions give two equations: the point is on the graph (\(f(1)=3\)) and the tangent is horizontal there (\(f'(1)=0\)).

    \(f'(x)=3x^2+2ax\Rightarrow f'(1)=3+2a=0\Rightarrow a=-1.5\).

    \(f(1)=1+a+b=3\Rightarrow1-1.5+b=3\Rightarrow b=3.5\).

    Answer: \(a=-1.5\), \(b=3.5\).

  4. \(h(x)=f(g(x))\), where \(g(1)=2\), \(g'(1)=3\) and \(f'(2)=-4\). Find \(h'(1)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule: \(h'(x)=f'(g(x))\cdot g'(x)\). Evaluate \(f'\) at \(g(1)\), not at 1.

    \(h'(1)=f'(g(1))\cdot g'(1)=f'(2)\cdot3\).

    \(=-4\cdot3=-12\).

    Answer: \(h'(1)=-12\).

  5. \(f(x)=(x^2+x)^3\). Find \(f'(x)\) and all \(x\) where \(f'(x)=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule, then factor \(x^2+x=x(x+1)\) and set each factor to zero.

    \(f'(x)=3(x^2+x)^2\cdot(2x+1)\).

    \((x^2+x)^2=x^2(x+1)^2\), so \(f'(x)=3x^2(x+1)^2(2x+1)\).

    Zeros: \(x=0\), \(x=-1\), \(x=-\tfrac12\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=3x^2(x+1)^2(2x+1)\); \(x=0,\ -1,\ -\tfrac12\).

  6. Complete the proof of the product rule: show that \(f(x+h)g(x+h)-f(x)g(x)=[f(x+h)-f(x)]\,g(x+h)+f(x)\,[g(x+h)-g(x)]\), and use it to find \((fg)'\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Adding and subtracting the same term \(f(x)g(x+h)\) changes nothing, but lets us split into two familiar difference quotients.

    Right side: \(f(x+h)g(x+h)-f(x)g(x+h)+f(x)g(x+h)-f(x)g(x)\).

    The two middle terms cancel, leaving the left side ✓

    Divide by \(h\): \(\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h\cdot g(x+h)+f(x)\cdot\dfrac{g(x+h)-g(x)}h\).

    As \(h\to0\): \(f'(x)\,g(x)+f(x)\,g'(x)\) (since \(g(x+h)\to g(x)\)).

    Answer: \((fg)'=f'g+fg'\).

  7. \(f(x)=x^3\). Find the tangent at \(x=0\). Is \((0,0)\) a maximum or a minimum?

    Show solution

    Idea. \(f'(0)=0\) gives a horizontal tangent, but to decide max or min we must look at the sign of \(f'\) or at the values around 0.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2\Rightarrow f'(0)=0\). Tangent: \(y=0\).

    \(f'(x)=3x^2\ge0\) on both sides, so \(f\) increases before and after 0.

    \(f(-1)=-1\lt 0\lt 1=f(1)\).

    Answer: tangent \(y=0\); neither a maximum nor a minimum. The tangent crosses the graph at the origin.

    Watch out: \(f'(a)=0\) does not always mean an extreme point; the derivative must also change sign.

  8. The derivative of a function is \(f'(x)=x^2-4\). Where does \(f\) increase? Where does it have a maximum and a minimum?

    Show solution

    Idea. \(f\) increases where \(f'\gt 0\). An extreme point is where \(f'\) changes sign.

    \(x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2)\): positive for \(x\lt -2\) or \(x\gt 2\), negative for \(-2\lt x\lt 2\).

    At \(x=-2\): from \(+\) to \(-\), so increasing then decreasing: a maximum.

    At \(x=2\): from \(-\) to \(+\): a minimum.

    Answer: increasing for \(x\lt -2\) and \(x\gt 2\); maximum at \(x=-2\), minimum at \(x=2\).

  9. Find the points on \(y=x^3-3x+1\) where the tangent is parallel to \(y=9x\), and write the tangents.

    Show solution

    Idea. Parallel to \(y=9x\) means slope 9. Solve \(y'=9\), then write each tangent.

    \(y'=3x^2-3=9\Rightarrow x^2=4\Rightarrow x=\pm2\).

    \(x=2\): \(y=8-6+1=3\). \(y-3=9(x-2)\Rightarrow y=9x-15\).

    \(x=-2\): \(y=-8+6+1=-1\). \(y+1=9(x+2)\Rightarrow y=9x+17\).

    Answer: at \((2,3)\): \(y=9x-15\); at \((-2,-1)\): \(y=9x+17\).

  10. The side of a square grows with time: \(s(t)=2t+1\) cm. Its area is \(A=s^2\). Use the chain rule to find how fast the area grows at \(t=1\), and check by expanding.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(A\) depends on \(s\), and \(s\) depends on \(t\): chain rule \(A'(t)=\dfrac{dA}{ds}\cdot s'(t)=2s\cdot s'\).

    \(s'(t)=2\).

    \(A'(t)=2(2t+1)\cdot2=4(2t+1)\).

    \(A'(1)=4\cdot3=12\).

    Check: \(A=(2t+1)^2=4t^2+4t+1\Rightarrow A'=8t+4\Rightarrow A'(1)=12\) ✓

    Answer: 12 cm² per second.
