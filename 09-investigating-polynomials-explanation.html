Investigating Polynomial Functions

1. The steps of an investigation

To "investigate" a function means to find all its main features and then sketch it. For a polynomial, follow these steps:

  1. Domain: all real numbers.
  2. Intercepts: y-axis at \(f(0)\); x-axis where \(f(x)=0\) (when it can be solved).
  3. Even or odd? Check \(f(-x)\).
  4. Derivative: find \(f'(x)\) and factor it.
  5. Critical points: solve \(f'(x)=0\).
  6. Sign table of \(f'\): where \(f'\gt 0\) the function increases (↗), where \(f'\lt 0\) it decreases (↘).
  7. Extreme points: ↗ then ↘ gives a maximum; ↘ then ↗ gives a minimum; no change of sign gives no extreme point.
  8. End behaviour: from the leading term.
  9. Sketch through all the points found.

2. A full example

Example 1

Investigate \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+9x\) and sketch its graph.

Solution.

Intercepts: \(f(0)=0\). \(f(x)=x(x^2-6x+9)=x(x-3)^2\): roots \(0\) and \(3\) (double, the graph touches).

\(f'(x)=3x^2-12x+9=3(x-1)(x-3)\). Critical points \(x=1\), \(x=3\).

\(x\)\(x\lt 1\)\(1\)\(1\lt x\lt 3\)\(3\)\(x\gt 3\)
\(f'\)+0−0+
\(f\)↗max↘min↗

Maximum \((1,f(1))=(1,4)\). Minimum \((3,0)\).

End behaviour: degree 3, leading coefficient positive: down on the left, up on the right.

Neither even nor odd (\(f(-1)=-16\), \(f(1)=4\)).

Example 2

Investigate \(f(x)=x^4-2x^2\).

Solution.

\(f(-x)=f(x)\): even, symmetric about the y-axis.

Roots: \(x^2(x^2-2)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\ \pm\sqrt2\).

\(f'(x)=4x^3-4x=4x(x-1)(x+1)\)

\(x\)\(x\lt -1\)\(-1\)\(-1\lt x\lt 0\)\(0\)\(0\lt x\lt 1\)\(1\)\(x\gt 1\)
\(f'\)−0+0−0+
\(f\)↘min↗max↘min↗

Minimum points \((-1,-1)\) and \((1,-1)\); maximum \((0,0)\). Both ends go up.

3. Largest and smallest value on a closed interval

On a closed interval \([a,b]\), the largest and smallest values of \(f\) are reached either at a critical point inside the interval or at one of the ends. So:

  1. Find the critical points inside \([a,b]\).
  2. Compute \(f\) at those points and at \(a\) and \(b\).
  3. The largest of these numbers is the maximum value, the smallest is the minimum value.
Example 3

Find the largest and smallest values of \(f(x)=x^3-3x\) on \([-2,3]\).

Solution.

\(f'(x)=3x^2-3=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1\), both inside the interval.

\(f(-2)=-2\), \(f(-1)=2\), \(f(1)=-2\), \(f(3)=18\).

Largest value \(18\) (at \(x=3\)); smallest value \(-2\) (at \(x=-2\) and at \(x=1\)).

Note: a local maximum is not always the largest value on the interval. Here the maximum point \((-1,2)\) is lower than the end point \((3,18)\).

4. Counting solutions of an equation

How many solutions does \(f(x)=k\) have? Draw the horizontal line \(y=k\) across the graph of \(f\) and count the meeting points. The extreme values are the boundaries where the number changes.

Example 4

For \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+9x\) (Example 1), find how many solutions \(f(x)=k\) has for each \(k\).

Solution.

Maximum value 4, minimum value 0. End behaviour: from \(-\infty\) to \(\infty\).

\(0\lt k\lt 4\): three solutions. \(k=0\) or \(k=4\): two solutions. \(k\lt 0\) or \(k\gt 4\): one solution.

Exactly one root. If \(f'(x)\gt 0\) everywhere (except perhaps single points), \(f\) is always increasing, so it meets every horizontal line at most once. For example \(x^3+x-5\) has \(f'=3x^2+1\gt 0\), and it has exactly one root (between 1 and 2, since \(f(1)=-3\lt 0\lt f(2)=5\)).

5. Common mistakes

  • Deciding that every point with \(f'=0\) is an extreme point. Check the sign of \(f'\) on both sides: \(x^3\) at 0 has \(f'=0\) but no extreme point.
  • Giving an extreme point as only an x-value. A point needs both coordinates: compute \(f\) there.
  • Making the sign table of \(f\) instead of \(f'\) when looking for increase and decrease.
  • On a closed interval, forgetting to check the ends of the interval.
  • Saying "increasing for \(x\lt 1\) and \(x\gt 3\)" as if it were one interval. Write the two intervals separately.

6. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Find the extreme point of \(f(x)=x^2-2x\) and its type.

    Show solution

    \(f'(x)=2x-2=0\Rightarrow x=1\). Minimum \((1,-1)\).

  2. Find the critical points of \(f(x)=x^3-3x^2\).

    Show solution

    \(3x^2-6x=3x(x-2)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\ x=2\).

  3. Where is \(f(x)=x^3-12x\) increasing?

    Show solution

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-12\gt 0\Rightarrow x\lt -2\) or \(x\gt 2\).

  4. Find the largest value of \(f(x)=x^3-3x\) on \([0,2]\).

    Show solution

    \(f(0)=0\), \(f(1)=-2\), \(f(2)=2\). Largest value 2.

  5. Is \(f(x)=x^3+x\) increasing everywhere?

    Show solution

    Yes: \(f'(x)=3x^2+1\gt 0\) for every \(x\).

  6. The sign of \(f'\) changes from + to − at \(x=1\). What is at \(x=1\)?

    Show solution

    A maximum point.

  7. How many solutions does \(x^3-3x=0\) have?

    Show solution

    \(x(x^2-3)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\ \pm\sqrt3\): three.

  8. Does \(f(x)=x^4\) have an extreme point at \(x=0\)?

    Show solution

    \(f'(x)=4x^3\): negative for \(x\lt 0\), positive for \(x\gt 0\). Yes, a minimum \((0,0)\).
