Before you start: Always factor \(f'(x)\) before making the sign table. One test value in each interval is enough.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the extreme point of \(f(x)=x^2-4x+3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find \(f'\), its zero, and its sign on each side.

    \(f'(x)=2x-4=0\Rightarrow x=2\).

    \(x\lt 2\): \(f'\lt 0\), decreasing. \(x\gt 2\): \(f'\gt 0\), increasing.

    \(f(2)=4-8+3=-1\).

    Answer: decreasing for \(x\lt 2\), increasing for \(x\gt 2\); minimum \((2,-1)\).

  2. Find the extreme points of \(f(x)=x^3-3x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Solve \(f'=0\) and use the sign table of \(f'\).

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-3=3(x-1)(x+1)\); zeros \(x=\pm1\).

    Signs: \(+\) for \(x\lt -1\), \(-\) on \((-1,1)\), \(+\) for \(x\gt 1\).

    \(f(-1)=-1+3=2\), \(f(1)=1-3=-2\).

    Answer: maximum \((-1,2)\), minimum \((1,-2)\).

  3. Find the extreme point of \(f(x)=-x^2+6x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Solve \(f'=0\). For a downward parabola the vertex is a maximum.

    \(f'(x)=-2x+6=0\Rightarrow x=3\).

    \(f(3)=-9+18=9\).

    Answer: maximum \((3,9)\).

  4. Find the extreme points of \(f(x)=x^3-12x+1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Solve \(f'=0\), check the signs, then compute the \(y\)-values.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-12=0\Rightarrow x=\pm2\).

    Signs: \(+,\ -,\ +\).

    \(f(-2)=-8+24+1=17\); \(f(2)=8-24+1=-15\).

    Answer: maximum \((-2,17)\), minimum \((2,-15)\).

  5. Find the extreme points of \(f(x)=2x^3-3x^2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Factor \(f'\), find the zeros, check the signs.

    \(f'(x)=6x^2-6x=6x(x-1)\); zeros 0 and 1.

    Signs: \(+\) for \(x\lt 0\), \(-\) on \((0,1)\), \(+\) for \(x\gt 1\).

    \(f(0)=0\); \(f(1)=2-3=-1\).

    Answer: maximum \((0,0)\), minimum \((1,-1)\).

  6. Find the intervals of increase and decrease of \(f(x)=x^3+3x^2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The sign of \(f'\) gives the intervals.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2+6x=3x(x+2)\); zeros \(-2\) and 0.

    Positive outside the roots, negative between them.

    Answer: increasing for \(x\lt -2\) and for \(x\gt 0\); decreasing for \(-2\lt x\lt 0\).

  7. Show that \(f(x)=x^3\) has no extreme points.

    Show solution

    Idea. An extreme point needs \(f'\) to change sign.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2\ge0\); it is 0 only at \(x=0\).

    On both sides of 0 the derivative is positive, so \(f\) keeps increasing.

    Answer: no change of sign, so no extreme points; \(f\) is increasing everywhere.

  8. Find the intercepts of \(f(x)=x^3-4x\) and check whether \(f\) is even or odd.

    Show solution

    Idea. Roots: set \(f(x)=0\) and factor. Even/odd: compare \(f(-x)\) with \(f(x)\).

    \(x^3-4x=x(x^2-4)=x(x-2)(x+2)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\ \pm2\).

    \(f(0)=0\): the \(y\)-intercept is 0.

    \(f(-x)=(-x)^3-4(-x)=-x^3+4x=-f(x)\).

    Answer: roots \(0,\ \pm2\); \(y\)-intercept 0; \(f\) is odd (symmetric about the origin).

  9. Is \(f(x)=x^4-x^2\) even, odd, or neither?

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute \(f(-x)\). Even powers of \(-x\) give the same value as for \(x\).

    \(f(-x)=(-x)^4-(-x)^2=x^4-x^2=f(x)\).

    Answer: even (symmetric about the \(y\)-axis).

  10. Find the extreme point of \(f(x)=x^4\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Check the sign of \(f'\) on both sides of its zero.

    \(f'(x)=4x^3=0\Rightarrow x=0\).

    \(x\lt 0\): \(f'\lt 0\). \(x\gt 0\): \(f'\gt 0\).

    Answer: minimum \((0,0)\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Investigate \(f(x)=-x^3+3x^2\): intercepts, intervals of increase and decrease, extreme points and end behaviour.

    Show solution

    Idea. Full investigation: roots, \(f'\) and its signs, extreme points, and the end behaviour from the leading term \(-x^3\).

    Roots: \(-x^3+3x^2=x^2(3-x)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) (double root, the graph touches) and \(x=3\).

    \(f'(x)=-3x^2+6x=-3x(x-2)\): zeros 0 and 2.

    Signs: \(-\) for \(x\lt 0\), \(+\) on \((0,2)\), \(-\) for \(x\gt 2\).

    \(f(0)=0\) (minimum); \(f(2)=-8+12=4\) (maximum).

    Leading term \(-x^3\): as \(x\to-\infty\), \(f\to+\infty\); as \(x\to\infty\), \(f\to-\infty\).

    Answer: roots 0, 3; decreasing for \(x\lt 0\) and \(x\gt 2\), increasing on \((0,2)\); minimum \((0,0)\), maximum \((2,4)\); up on the left, down on the right.

  2. Investigate \(f(x)=x^4-8x^2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Use symmetry (even function), then roots, derivative and signs.

    \(f(-x)=f(x)\): even, symmetric about the \(y\)-axis.

    Roots: \(x^2(x^2-8)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\ \pm\sqrt8=\pm2\sqrt2\).

    \(f'(x)=4x^3-16x=4x(x-2)(x+2)\): zeros \(0,\ \pm2\).

    Signs: \(-\) for \(x\lt -2\), \(+\) on \((-2,0)\), \(-\) on \((0,2)\), \(+\) for \(x\gt 2\).

    \(f(\pm2)=16-32=-16\) (minimum points), \(f(0)=0\) (maximum).

    Answer: minima \((\pm2,-16)\), maximum \((0,0)\), roots \(0,\ \pm2\sqrt2\); both ends go up.

  3. Find the largest and smallest values of \(f(x)=x^3-3x\) on \([0,2]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Closed interval: compare the critical points inside the interval with the end points.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-3=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1\); only \(x=1\) is in \([0,2]\).

    \(f(0)=0\), \(f(1)=-2\), \(f(2)=2\).

    Answer: largest value 2, smallest value \(-2\).

  4. Find the largest and smallest values of \(f(x)=x^2-6x\) on \([-1,4]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find the critical point, check it is in the interval, and compare with the ends.

    \(f'(x)=2x-6=0\Rightarrow x=3\), inside \([-1,4]\).

    \(f(-1)=1+6=7\), \(f(3)=9-18=-9\), \(f(4)=16-24=-8\).

    Answer: largest value 7 (at \(x=-1\)), smallest \(-9\) (at \(x=3\)).

  5. Find the extreme points of \(f(x)=2x^3-9x^2+12x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Take out the common factor 6 from \(f'\), factor, check signs.

    \(f'(x)=6x^2-18x+12=6(x^2-3x+2)=6(x-1)(x-2)\).

    Signs: \(+,\ -,\ +\).

    \(f(1)=2-9+12=5\); \(f(2)=16-36+24=4\).

    Answer: maximum \((1,5)\), minimum \((2,4)\).

  6. How many solutions does \(x^3-3x=k\) have, depending on \(k\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. The solutions of \(f(x)=k\) are where the horizontal line \(y=k\) meets the graph of \(f(x)=x^3-3x\). The extreme values decide the count.

    From question 2: maximum value 2 at \(x=-1\), minimum value \(-2\) at \(x=1\).

    A line between them crosses all three "arms" of the graph.

    A line at height \(\pm2\) touches an extreme point and crosses once more.

    A line above 2 or below \(-2\) crosses only once.

    Answer: three solutions for \(-2\lt k\lt 2\); two for \(k=\pm2\); one for \(k\gt 2\) or \(k\lt -2\).

  7. For which \(a\) does \(f(x)=x^3-ax\) have an extreme point at \(x=2\)? What type is it?

    Show solution

    Idea. At an extreme point \(f'=0\). Find \(a\), then check the sign change.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-a\). \(f'(2)=12-a=0\Rightarrow a=12\).

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-12=3(x-2)(x+2)\): around \(x=2\) it goes from \(-\) to \(+\).

    \(f(2)=8-24=-16\).

    Answer: \(a=12\); a minimum at \((2,-16)\).

  8. \(f(x)=x^3+ax^2+bx\) has extreme points at \(x=1\) and \(x=3\). Find \(a\) and \(b\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The extreme points are the roots of \(f'\). A quadratic with leading coefficient 3 and roots 1, 3 is \(3(x-1)(x-3)\).

    \(f'(x)=3x^2+2ax+b\).

    \(3(x-1)(x-3)=3x^2-12x+9\).

    Compare: \(2a=-12\Rightarrow a=-6\); \(b=9\).

    Answer: \(a=-6\), \(b=9\).

  9. Show that \(f(x)=x^3+x-5\) has exactly one root, and find an interval of length 1 that contains it.

    Show solution

    Idea. An increasing function crosses the \(x\)-axis at most once. A sign change between two values shows that a root exists.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2+1\gt 0\): \(f\) always increases, so at most one root.

    \(f(1)=1+1-5=-3\lt 0\); \(f(2)=8+2-5=5\gt 0\).

    The graph is continuous and goes from negative to positive, so it crosses 0 between 1 and 2.

    Answer: exactly one root, in \((1,2)\).

  10. \(f(x)=x^4-4x^3\). Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the extreme points. What happens at \(x=0\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Factor \(f'\). A squared factor \(x^2\) does not change sign, so it does not create an extreme point.

    \(f'(x)=4x^3-12x^2=4x^2(x-3)\).

    \(4x^2\ge0\), so the sign of \(f'\) is the sign of \(x-3\).

    Decreasing for \(x\lt 3\), increasing for \(x\gt 3\).

    \(f(3)=81-108=-27\).

    Answer: minimum \((3,-27)\). At \(x=0\), \(f'=0\) but the sign does not change: a horizontal tangent, not an extreme point.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. \(f(x)=x^3-3x^2-9x+11\).

    1. Find the extreme points.
    2. Show that \(x=1\) is a root and find the other roots.
    3. Describe the graph.
    4. How many solutions does \(f(x)=k\) have?
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Derivative and signs. (b) Substitute, then divide by \((x-1)\). (c) Combine. (d) Use the extreme values as in question 16.

    (a) \(f'(x)=3x^2-6x-9=3(x^2-2x-3)=3(x-3)(x+1)\). Signs \(+,\ -,\ +\).

    \(f(-1)=-1-3+9+11=16\) (maximum); \(f(3)=27-27-27+11=-16\) (minimum).

    (b) \(f(1)=1-3-9+11=0\). Division: \(f(x)=(x-1)(x^2-2x-11)\).

    \(x^2-2x-11=0\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{2\pm\sqrt{4+44}}2=1\pm2\sqrt3\).

    (c) Increasing for \(x\lt -1\), decreasing on \((-1,3)\), increasing for \(x\gt 3\); \(y\)-intercept 11; three roots.

    (d) The line \(y=k\) crosses three times between the extreme values.

    Answer: (a) max \((-1,16)\), min \((3,-16)\); (b) roots \(1,\ 1\pm2\sqrt3\); (c) as described; (d) three for \(-16\lt k\lt 16\), two for \(k=\pm16\), one otherwise.

  2. \(f(x)=x^3-3x^2+k\) on the interval \([-1,3]\).

    1. Find the largest and smallest values in terms of \(k\).
    2. Find \(k\) if the largest value is 10.
    Show solution

    Idea. The constant \(k\) only shifts the graph up or down; it does not change \(f'\). Compare the values at the critical points and the ends.

    (a) \(f'(x)=3x^2-6x=3x(x-2)\): critical points 0 and 2, both in \([-1,3]\).

    \(f(-1)=-1-3+k=k-4\); \(f(0)=k\); \(f(2)=8-12+k=k-4\); \(f(3)=27-27+k=k\).

    Largest \(k\), smallest \(k-4\).

    (b) \(k=10\).

    Answer: (a) largest \(k\), smallest \(k-4\); (b) \(k=10\) (then the smallest value is 6).

  3. The derivative of a function is \(f'(x)=(x-1)^2(x+2)\). Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the extreme points (x-values).

    Show solution

    Idea. The sign of a product is decided by the factors that can change sign. \((x-1)^2\) is never negative.

    Sign of \(f'\) = sign of \(x+2\) (except at \(x=1\), where \(f'=0\)).

    \(x\lt -2\): \(f'\lt 0\), decreasing. \(x\gt -2\): \(f'\gt 0\), increasing.

    Answer: decreasing for \(x\lt -2\), increasing for \(x\gt -2\); minimum at \(x=-2\). At \(x=1\) the tangent is horizontal but there is no extreme point.

  4. \(f(x)=x^4-4x^3+4x^2\).

    1. Factor \(f\) and find its roots.
    2. Find the extreme points.
    3. How many solutions does \(f(x)=k\) have?
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Take out \(x^2\) and recognise a perfect square. (b) Derivative and signs. (c) Horizontal lines \(y=k\) against the graph.

    (a) \(x^4-4x^3+4x^2=x^2(x^2-4x+4)=x^2(x-2)^2\). Roots 0 and 2, both double (the graph touches the axis).

    (b) \(f'(x)=4x^3-12x^2+8x=4x(x^2-3x+2)=4x(x-1)(x-2)\).

    Signs: \(-,\ +,\ -,\ +\). Minimum \((0,0)\), maximum \(f(1)=1\cdot1=1\), minimum \((2,0)\).

    (c) The graph is a "W" with bottoms at height 0 and a top at height 1.

    Answer: (a) \(x^2(x-2)^2\), roots 0, 2; (b) min \((0,0)\), max \((1,1)\), min \((2,0)\); (c) four solutions for \(0\lt k\lt 1\), three for \(k=1\), two for \(k\gt 1\) or \(k=0\), none for \(k\lt 0\).

  5. Prove that \(x^3\ge3x-2\) for every \(x\ge0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. To prove \(A\ge B\), study \(g=A-B\) and show its smallest value is \(\ge0\).

    \(g(x)=x^3-3x+2\). \(g'(x)=3x^2-3=0\Rightarrow x=1\) (the only one with \(x\ge0\)).

    On \([0,1]\), \(g'\lt 0\); for \(x\gt 1\), \(g'\gt 0\). So the smallest value on \(x\ge0\) is \(g(1)=1-3+2=0\).

    Hence \(g(x)\ge0\), i.e. \(x^3\ge3x-2\).

    Answer: proved. Another way: \(g(x)=(x-1)^2(x+2)\ge0\) for \(x\ge0\).

  6. Find a polynomial of degree 3 with a maximum at \((0,4)\) and a minimum at \((2,0)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Write the general cubic and translate each piece of information into an equation: two points and two horizontal tangents.

    \(f(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\), \(f'(x)=3ax^2+2bx+c\).

    \(f(0)=4\Rightarrow d=4\). \(f'(0)=0\Rightarrow c=0\).

    \(f'(2)=12a+4b=0\Rightarrow b=-3a\).

    \(f(2)=8a+4b+4=0\Rightarrow8a-12a+4=0\Rightarrow a=1\), so \(b=-3\).

    Answer: \(f(x)=x^3-3x^2+4\).

    Check: \(f'(x)=3x(x-2)\), signs \(+,\ -,\ +\): maximum at 0, minimum at 2; \(f(2)=8-12+4=0\) ✓

  7. \(f(x)=x^3-3x\) on the interval \([-2,a]\) with \(a\gt -1\). For which values of \(a\) is the largest value reached at the right end \(x=a\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. The candidates for the largest value are the inner maximum \((-1,2)\), the left end, and the right end. The right end wins if \(f(a)\ge2\).

    \(f(-2)=-8+6=-2\), which is smaller than 2.

    Need \(a^3-3a\ge2\), i.e. \(a^3-3a-2\ge0\).

    Factor: \(a^3-3a-2=(a-2)(a+1)^2\). Since \((a+1)^2\gt 0\) for \(a\gt -1\), we need \(a-2\ge0\).

    Answer: \(a\ge2\) (at \(a=2\), the value 2 is reached both at \(x=-1\) and at \(x=2\)).

  8. A body moves along a line. Its position after \(t\) seconds is \(s(t)=t^3-9t^2+24t\) metres (\(t\ge0\)).

    1. Find the velocity \(v(t)=s'(t)\).
    2. When does the body move forward and when backward?
    3. Find the total distance travelled in the first 5 seconds.
    Show solution

    Idea. Velocity is the derivative of position; its sign gives the direction. Distance adds the lengths of each stretch, forward and back.

    (a) \(v(t)=3t^2-18t+24=3(t^2-6t+8)=3(t-2)(t-4)\).

    (b) \(v\gt 0\) for \(0\lt t\lt 2\) and \(t\gt 4\): forward. \(v\lt 0\) for \(2\lt t\lt 4\): backward.

    (c) \(s(0)=0\), \(s(2)=8-36+48=20\), \(s(4)=64-144+96=16\), \(s(5)=125-225+120=20\).

    Distances: \(0\to2\): 20 m; \(2\to4\): 4 m back; \(4\to5\): 4 m.

    Answer: (a) \(3(t-2)(t-4)\); (b) forward for \(t\lt 2\) and \(t\gt 4\), backward for \(2\lt t\lt 4\); (c) 28 m.

    Watch out: \(s(5)-s(0)=20\) is the displacement, not the distance.

  9. \(f(x)=x^3+px\). Show that for \(p\gt 0\) the function has no extreme points, and for \(p\lt 0\) it has two.

    Show solution

    Idea. Study the sign of \(f'(x)=3x^2+p\) in each case.

    \(p\gt 0\): \(3x^2+p\gt 0\) for all \(x\), so \(f\) always increases: no extreme points.

    \(p\lt 0\): \(3x^2+p=0\Rightarrow x^2=-\tfrac p3\gt 0\Rightarrow x=\pm\sqrt{-p/3}\).

    \(f'\) is an upward parabola: \(+,\ -,\ +\) around the two roots, so the sign changes at both.

    Answer: for \(p\gt 0\) none; for \(p\lt 0\) a maximum at \(x=-\sqrt{-p/3}\) and a minimum at \(x=\sqrt{-p/3}\).

  10. For which values of \(c\) does \(x^4-4x+c=0\) have two solutions? One? None?

    Show solution

    Idea. Move \(c\) to the other side: \(x^4-4x=-c\). Count the crossings of the horizontal line \(y=-c\) with the graph of \(f(x)=x^4-4x\).

    \(f'(x)=4x^3-4=4(x-1)(x^2+x+1)\); \(x^2+x+1\gt 0\), so the only zero is \(x=1\).

    Signs: \(-\) then \(+\): a minimum \(f(1)=1-4=-3\). Both ends go up.

    The graph is a "U" with lowest value \(-3\).

    Two solutions when \(-c\gt -3\), i.e. \(c\lt 3\). One when \(-c=-3\), i.e. \(c=3\). None when \(c\gt 3\).

    Answer: two for \(c\lt 3\), one for \(c=3\), none for \(c\gt 3\).
