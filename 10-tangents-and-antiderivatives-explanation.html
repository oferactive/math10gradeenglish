Tangent Lines and Antiderivatives of Polynomials

The equation of a tangent, tangents with a given slope or through a given point, and going backwards: finding a function from its derivative. Practice for this unit is in the workbook.

1. The equation of the tangent at a point

The tangent to the graph of \(f\) at \(x=a\) passes through the point \((a,f(a))\) and has slope \(f'(a)\). With the point-slope form of a line (Unit 01):

\(y-f(a)=f'(a)\,(x-a)\)
the tangent at \(x=a\)

Three numbers are needed: \(a\), \(f(a)\) and \(f'(a)\).

Example 1

Find the tangent to \(f(x)=x^3-2x\) at \(x=1\).

Solution.

\(f(1)=-1\). \(f'(x)=3x^2-2\Rightarrow f'(1)=1\).

\(y+1=1\cdot(x-1)\Rightarrow y=x-2\).

2. A tangent with a given slope

If the slope \(m\) of the tangent is known (or the tangent must be parallel or perpendicular to a given line), solve \(f'(x)=m\) to find the point of tangency first.

Example 2

Find the tangent to \(y=x^2-4x\) that is parallel to \(y=2x+5\).

Solution.

\(2x-4=2\Rightarrow x=3\), point \((3,-3)\).

\(y+3=2(x-3)\Rightarrow y=2x-9\).

Horizontal tangents have slope 0: solve \(f'(x)=0\).

The normal at a point is the line perpendicular to the tangent there. Its slope is \(-\dfrac1{f'(a)}\) (when \(f'(a)\ne0\)).

3. A tangent through a point outside the graph

If the tangent must pass through a point \(P\) that is not on the graph, we do not know the point of tangency. Call it \((a,f(a))\), write the tangent in terms of \(a\), and require it to pass through \(P\).

Example 3

Find the tangents to \(y=x^2\) that pass through \(P(1,-3)\).

Solution.

At \((a,a^2)\) the slope is \(2a\): \(y-a^2=2a(x-a)\Rightarrow y=2ax-a^2\).

Through \(P\): \(-3=2a-a^2\Rightarrow a^2-2a-3=0\Rightarrow a=3\) or \(a=-1\).

Tangents: \(y=6x-9\) and \(y=-2x-1\).

4. Antiderivatives: going backwards

An antiderivative of \(f\) is a function \(F\) with \(F'=f\). We "undo" differentiation.

Since \((x^{n+1})'=(n+1)x^n\), reversing the power rule gives:

\(f(x)=x^n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(x)=\dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C\)
raise the power by one and divide by the new power
\(f(x)\)Antiderivative \(F(x)\)
\(k\) (constant)\(kx+C\)
\(x\)\(\tfrac{x^2}2+C\)
\(x^2\)\(\tfrac{x^3}3+C\)
\(3x^2-4x+1\)\(x^3-2x^2+x+C\)

Is the antiderivative unique? No. \(x^2\), \(x^2+5\) and \(x^2-3\) all have derivative \(2x\), because the derivative of a constant is 0. All antiderivatives of a function differ by a constant \(C\); their graphs are vertical shifts of each other.

To pick one, we need one more fact, for example a point on its graph.

Example 4

Find \(F\) with \(F'(x)=6x^2-4x\) whose graph passes through \((1,5)\).

Solution.

\(F(x)=2x^3-2x^2+C\). \(F(1)=2-2+C=5\Rightarrow C=5\).

\(F(x)=2x^3-2x^2+5\).

Example 5

A body moves with velocity \(v(t)=10-2t\) m/s and starts at position \(s(0)=0\). Find its position \(s(t)\). When does it stop, and where?

Solution.

\(s'(t)=v(t)\Rightarrow s(t)=10t-t^2+C\), and \(s(0)=0\Rightarrow C=0\).

It stops when \(v=0\): \(t=5\) s, at \(s(5)=25\) m.

Note: in Grade 11 the antiderivative becomes the integral and is used to compute areas.

5. Common mistakes

  • Using \(f(a)\) as the slope, or \(f'(a)\) as the y-coordinate. The point is \((a,f(a))\); the slope is \(f'(a)\).
  • Writing the tangent as \(y=f'(x)\): the tangent is a straight line, so its slope is the number \(f'(a)\), not the function \(f'(x)\).
  • For a tangent through an outside point, using that point as the point of tangency.
  • Forgetting \(+C\) in an antiderivative, or forgetting to find \(C\) when a point is given.
  • Antidifferentiating \(x^2\) as \(2x\) (that is differentiating) or as \(x^3\) (forgetting to divide by 3).

6. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Find the tangent to \(y=x^2\) at \(x=3\).

    Show solution

    Point \((3,9)\), slope 6: \(y=6x-9\).

  2. Find the slope of the tangent to \(y=x^3-x\) at \(x=0\).

    Show solution

    \(y'=3x^2-1\Rightarrow -1\).

  3. Find all antiderivatives of \(6x^2\).

    Show solution

    \(2x^3+C\)

  4. Find \(F\) with \(F'(x)=2x+1\) and \(F(0)=2\).

    Show solution

    \(F(x)=x^2+x+2\)

  5. At which point does \(y=x^2+2x\) have a horizontal tangent?

    Show solution

    \(2x+2=0\Rightarrow x=-1\). Point \((-1,-1)\).

  6. Is \(x^2+7\) an antiderivative of \(2x\)?

    Show solution

    Yes: \((x^2+7)'=2x\).

  7. Find all antiderivatives of \(x^3\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{x^4}4+C\)

  8. Find the tangent to \(y=x^2\) parallel to \(y=-2x\).

    Show solution

    \(2x=-2\Rightarrow x=-1\), point \((-1,1)\): \(y=-2x-1\).
