Before you start: For every tangent question, write the three numbers first: the x-coordinate \(a\), the y-coordinate \(f(a)\) and the slope \(f'(a)\). After finding an antiderivative, differentiate it to check.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the tangent to \(y=x^2+1\) at \(x=2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Point from \(y(2)\), slope from \(y'(2)\), then \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\).

    \(y(2)=5\); \(y'=2x\Rightarrow y'(2)=4\).

    \(y-5=4(x-2)\Rightarrow y=4x-3\).

    Answer: \(y=4x-3\).

  2. Find the tangent to \(y=x^3\) at \(x=-1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Point and slope; take care with signs.

    \(y(-1)=-1\); \(y'=3x^2\Rightarrow y'(-1)=3\).

    \(y+1=3(x+1)\Rightarrow y=3x+2\).

    Answer: \(y=3x+2\).

  3. Find the tangent to \(y=2x^2-3x\) at \(x=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Point and slope at \(x=0\).

    \(y(0)=0\); \(y'=4x-3\Rightarrow y'(0)=-3\).

    \(y-0=-3(x-0)\).

    Answer: \(y=-3x\).

  4. Find the slope of the tangent to \(y=x^4\) at \(x=1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The slope is \(y'(1)\).

    \(y'=4x^3\Rightarrow y'(1)=4\).

    Answer: 4.

  5. Find all antiderivatives.

    1. \(2x\)
    2. \(3x^2\)
    3. \(5\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Ask: which function has this derivative? Then add \(C\).

    (a) \((x^2)'=2x\).

    (b) \((x^3)'=3x^2\).

    (c) \((5x)'=5\).

    Answer: (a) \(x^2+C\); (b) \(x^3+C\); (c) \(5x+C\).

  6. Find all antiderivatives of \(4x^3-6x+1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find the antiderivative of each term separately: \(x^n\to\dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}\).

    \(4x^3\to x^4\); \(-6x\to-3x^2\); \(1\to x\).

    Answer: \(x^4-3x^2+x+C\).

    Check: \((x^4-3x^2+x)'=4x^3-6x+1\) ✓

  7. Find all antiderivatives.

    1. \(x^2\)
    2. \(x\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Raise the power by 1, divide by the new power.

    (a) \(x^2\to\dfrac{x^3}3\).

    (b) \(x=x^1\to\dfrac{x^2}2\).

    Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{x^3}3+C\); (b) \(\dfrac{x^2}2+C\).

  8. Check that \(F(x)=x^3-x\) is an antiderivative of \(f(x)=3x^2-1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate \(F\) and compare it with \(f\).

    \(F'(x)=3x^2-1\), which is exactly \(f(x)\).

    Answer: yes, \(F\) is an antiderivative of \(f\).

  9. Find \(F\) with \(F'(x)=2x\) and \(F(0)=3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. General antiderivative, then find \(C\) from \(F(0)=3\).

    \(F(x)=x^2+C\).

    \(F(0)=C=3\).

    Answer: \(F(x)=x^2+3\).

  10. At which point of \(y=x^2\) is the slope of the tangent \(-4\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. The slope of the tangent at \(x\) is \(y'=2x\). Set it equal to \(-4\).

    \(2x=-4\Rightarrow x=-2\); \(y=4\).

    Answer: \((-2,4)\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Find the tangent to \(y=x^2-4x\) that is parallel to \(y=2x+5\).

    Show solution

    Idea. A parallel tangent has the same slope as the line, 2.

    \(y'=2x-4=2\Rightarrow x=3\); \(y(3)=9-12=-3\).

    \(y+3=2(x-3)\Rightarrow y=2x-9\).

    Answer: \(y=2x-9\).

  2. Find the horizontal tangents of \(y=x^3-3x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. A horizontal tangent has slope 0, so its equation is \(y=\) the \(y\)-value of the point.

    \(y'=3x^2-3=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1\).

    \(y(1)=1-3=-2\); \(y(-1)=-1+3=2\).

    Answer: \(y=-2\) and \(y=2\).

  3. Find the tangent to \(y=x^2\) that is perpendicular to the line \(y=-\tfrac14x+1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is \(-1\). The slope \(-\tfrac14\) gives a perpendicular slope 4.

    \(m=-1\div\left(-\tfrac14\right)=4\).

    \(2x=4\Rightarrow x=2\), point \((2,4)\).

    \(y-4=4(x-2)\Rightarrow y=4x-4\).

    Answer: \(y=4x-4\).

  4. Find the tangents to \(y=x^2\) that pass through \((1,-3)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The point \((1,-3)\) is not on the parabola. Write the tangent at an unknown point \((a,a^2)\), then demand that it pass through \((1,-3)\).

    Tangent at \((a,a^2)\): \(y-a^2=2a(x-a)\Rightarrow y=2ax-a^2\).

    Through \((1,-3)\): \(-3=2a-a^2\Rightarrow a^2-2a-3=0\Rightarrow(a-3)(a+1)=0\).

    \(a=3\): \(y=6x-9\). \(a=-1\): \(y=-2x-1\).

    Answer: \(y=6x-9\) and \(y=-2x-1\).

    Check: \(6-9=-3\) ✓ and \(-2-1=-3\) ✓

  5. Find the normal to \(y=x^2\) at \((1,1)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The normal is perpendicular to the tangent at the same point, so its slope is \(-\dfrac1{m}\).

    Tangent slope: \(y'(1)=2\). Normal slope: \(-\tfrac12\).

    \(y-1=-\tfrac12(x-1)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac12x+\tfrac12+1\).

    Answer: \(y=-\tfrac12x+\tfrac32\).

  6. Find \(F\) with \(F'(x)=6x^2-4x\) whose graph passes through \((1,5)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. General antiderivative, then substitute the point \((1,5)\) to find \(C\).

    \(F(x)=6\cdot\dfrac{x^3}3-4\cdot\dfrac{x^2}2+C=2x^3-2x^2+C\).

    \(F(1)=2-2+C=5\Rightarrow C=5\).

    Answer: \(F(x)=2x^3-2x^2+5\).

  7. A body moves with velocity \(v(t)=10-2t\) m/s and starts at \(s(0)=0\). Find \(s(t)\). When does the body stop, and where is it then?

    Show solution

    Idea. Position is an antiderivative of velocity; the starting position fixes \(C\). The body stops when \(v=0\).

    \(s(t)=10t-t^2+C\), and \(s(0)=0\Rightarrow C=0\).

    \(v=10-2t=0\Rightarrow t=5\).

    \(s(5)=50-25=25\).

    Answer: \(s(t)=10t-t^2\); it stops at \(t=5\) s, 25 m from the start.

  8. Find all antiderivatives of \((x+1)^2\) by expanding. Show that \(\dfrac{(x+1)^3}3\) is also an antiderivative, and explain why there is no contradiction.

    Show solution

    Idea. Two antiderivatives of the same function differ only by a constant. Expand to compare.

    \((x+1)^2=x^2+2x+1\Rightarrow F(x)=\dfrac{x^3}3+x^2+x+C\).

    Chain rule: \(\left(\dfrac{(x+1)^3}3\right)'=\dfrac{3(x+1)^2\cdot1}3=(x+1)^2\) ✓

    Expand: \(\dfrac{(x+1)^3}3=\dfrac{x^3+3x^2+3x+1}3=\dfrac{x^3}3+x^2+x+\dfrac13\).

    Answer: \(\dfrac{x^3}3+x^2+x+C\). There is no contradiction: \(\dfrac{(x+1)^3}3\) is the member of the family with \(C=\tfrac13\).

  9. The tangent to \(y=x^3-2x\) at \(x=1\) is \(y=x-2\). Find the other point where it meets the graph.

    Show solution

    Idea. Intersections of a line and a curve: set them equal. \(x=1\) is a double root (the tangency point), so \((x-1)^2\) is a factor.

    \(x^3-2x=x-2\Rightarrow x^3-3x+2=0\).

    Divide by \((x-1)^2=x^2-2x+1\): \(x^3-3x+2=(x-1)^2(x+2)\).

    \(x=-2\): \(y=-2-2=-4\).

    Answer: \((-2,-4)\).

  10. For which \(a\) is the line \(y=4x+a\) tangent to \(y=x^2\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Tangency needs two things: the same slope and a common point.

    Slope 4: \(2x=4\Rightarrow x=2\), point \((2,4)\) on the parabola.

    The line must pass through it: \(4=4\cdot2+a\Rightarrow a=-4\).

    Answer: \(a=-4\).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. \(f(x)=x^3-3x^2+2\).

    1. Find the tangent at \(x=1\).
    2. Find the area of the triangle between the tangent and the axes.
    3. Does the tangent meet the graph at another point?
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Point and slope. (b) Intercepts of the tangent give a right triangle. (c) Solve curve = tangent.

    (a) \(f(1)=1-3+2=0\); \(f'(x)=3x^2-6x\Rightarrow f'(1)=-3\). \(y=-3(x-1)=-3x+3\).

    (b) \(y=0\Rightarrow x=1\); \(x=0\Rightarrow y=3\). Area \(=\dfrac{1\cdot3}2=1.5\).

    (c) \(x^3-3x^2+2=-3x+3\Rightarrow x^3-3x^2+3x-1=0\Rightarrow(x-1)^3=0\).

    Answer: (a) \(y=-3x+3\); (b) 1.5; (c) no, the only common point is \(x=1\) (a triple root: the tangent crosses the curve there).

  2. Two tangents to \(y=x^2\) are drawn from the point \(P(2,3)\).

    1. Find the points of tangency and the tangents.
    2. Find the area of the triangle formed by the two tangents and the x-axis.
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Tangent at \((a,a^2)\) is \(y=2ax-a^2\); make it pass through \(P\). (b) Find the \(x\)-intercepts; the height of the triangle is the \(y\)-value of \(P\).

    (a) \(3=2a\cdot2-a^2\Rightarrow a^2-4a+3=0\Rightarrow a=1\) or \(a=3\).

    \(a=1\): point \((1,1)\), \(y=2x-1\). \(a=3\): point \((3,9)\), \(y=6x-9\).

    (b) \(2x-1=0\Rightarrow x=\tfrac12\); \(6x-9=0\Rightarrow x=\tfrac32\). Base \(=1\), height \(=3\).

    Area \(=\dfrac{1\cdot3}2=1.5\).

    Answer: (a) \((1,1)\): \(y=2x-1\); \((3,9)\): \(y=6x-9\); (b) 1.5.

  3. Find \(b\) and \(c\) so that the parabola \(y=x^2+bx+c\) is tangent to the line \(y=2x\) at the point \((1,2)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Tangent at \((1,2)\) means: the parabola passes through the point, and its slope there equals the line's slope 2.

    \(y(1)=1+b+c=2\).

    \(y'=2x+b\Rightarrow y'(1)=2+b=2\Rightarrow b=0\).

    Then \(c=1\).

    Answer: \(b=0\), \(c=1\): \(y=x^2+1\).

  4. \(F'(x)=3x^2-6x\), and the minimum value of \(F\) at its minimum point is 0. Find \(F\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find the family \(F+C\), locate the minimum from \(F'\), and use the given minimum value to find \(C\).

    \(F(x)=x^3-3x^2+C\).

    \(F'(x)=3x(x-2)\): signs \(+,\ -,\ +\), so the minimum is at \(x=2\).

    \(F(2)=8-12+C=0\Rightarrow C=4\).

    Answer: \(F(x)=x^3-3x^2+4\).

  5. Find the antiderivative \(F\) of \(f(x)=3x^2+2\) whose tangent at \(x=0\) is the line \(y=2x+1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The tangent at \(x=0\) passes through \((0,F(0))\), so \(F(0)\) is its \(y\)-intercept 1. Its slope must be \(F'(0)\).

    \(F(x)=x^3+2x+C\).

    \(F(0)=C=1\).

    Check the slope: \(F'(0)=f(0)=2\), the slope of \(y=2x+1\) ✓

    Answer: \(F(x)=x^3+2x+1\).

  6. A body moves with velocity \(v(t)=3t^2-12t+9\) m/s and starts at \(s(0)=0\).

    1. Find \(s(t)\).
    2. When does it stop?
    3. Where is it at those moments? Describe the motion.
    Show solution

    Idea. Position is the antiderivative of velocity; it stops when \(v=0\); compare positions at those times.

    (a) \(s(t)=t^3-6t^2+9t+C\), \(s(0)=0\Rightarrow C=0\).

    (b) \(v=3(t^2-4t+3)=3(t-1)(t-3)=0\Rightarrow t=1,\ t=3\).

    (c) \(s(1)=1-6+9=4\); \(s(3)=27-54+27=0\).

    \(v\gt 0\) for \(t\lt 1\), \(v\lt 0\) for \(1\lt t\lt 3\), \(v\gt 0\) for \(t\gt 3\).

    Answer: (a) \(s(t)=t^3-6t^2+9t\); (b) \(t=1\) and \(t=3\); (c) at 4 m and at 0 m: forward to 4 m, back to the start, then forward again.

  7. Two functions \(F\) and \(G\) are antiderivatives of the same function \(f\). Explain why \(F-G\) is constant, and what this means for their graphs.

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate \(F-G\). A function with derivative 0 everywhere is constant.

    \((F-G)'=F'-G'=f-f=0\).

    Its tangent is horizontal at every point, so it never rises or falls: \(F-G=C\).

    Answer: \(F=G+C\). The graph of \(F\) is the graph of \(G\) shifted vertically by \(C\).

  8. The tangent to \(y=x^3\) at \((a,a^3)\) is \(y=3a^2x-2a^3\).

    1. Prove this.
    2. Find the tangent that passes through \((0,-16)\).
    3. Find where this tangent meets the graph again.
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Point-slope form. (b) Substitute the point into the formula and solve for \(a\). (c) Solve curve = tangent; \(x=2\) is a double root.

    (a) Slope \(3a^2\): \(y-a^3=3a^2(x-a)\Rightarrow y=3a^2x-3a^3+a^3=3a^2x-2a^3\).

    (b) Through \((0,-16)\): \(-2a^3=-16\Rightarrow a^3=8\Rightarrow a=2\). Tangent \(y=12x-16\).

    (c) \(x^3-12x+16=0\). Divide by \((x-2)^2\): \((x-2)^2(x+4)=0\). \(x=-4\Rightarrow y=-64\).

    Answer: (a) shown; (b) \(y=12x-16\); (c) \((-4,-64)\).

  9. For which values of \(c\) is the x-axis tangent to the graph of \(y=x^3-3x+c\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. The \(x\)-axis is the line \(y=0\) with slope 0. It is a tangent at a point where \(y'=0\) and also \(y=0\).

    \(y'=3x^2-3=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1\).

    \(x=1\): \(y=1-3+c=0\Rightarrow c=2\).

    \(x=-1\): \(y=-1+3+c=0\Rightarrow c=-2\).

    Answer: \(c=2\) or \(c=-2\).

  10. Water flows into a tank at the rate \(r(t)=20-2t\) litres per minute (\(0\le t\le10\)). At the start the tank holds 50 litres.

    1. Find the volume \(V(t)\).
    2. How much water is in the tank after 10 minutes?
    3. When is the flow fastest?
    Show solution

    Idea. The rate of flow is \(V'(t)\), so \(V\) is an antiderivative of \(r\); \(V(0)=50\) fixes \(C\).

    (a) \(V(t)=20t-t^2+C\); \(V(0)=C=50\). So \(V(t)=50+20t-t^2\).

    (b) \(V(10)=50+200-100=150\) litres.

    (c) \(r(t)=20-2t\) decreases with \(t\), so it is largest at \(t=0\): \(r(0)=20\).

    Answer: (a) \(V(t)=50+20t-t^2\); (b) 150 litres (100 litres added); (c) at the start, 20 L/min.
