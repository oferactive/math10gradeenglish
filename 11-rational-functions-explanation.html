1. The derivative of \(\tfrac1x\) and of \(\tfrac1f\)

A rational function is a quotient of two polynomials, such as \(\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\) or \(\dfrac{x}{x^2+1}\). We start with the simplest one.

Derivative of \(\tfrac1x\) from the definition

Solution.

\(\dfrac{\frac1{x+h}-\frac1x}h=\dfrac{x-(x+h)}{x(x+h)h}=\dfrac{-1}{x(x+h)}\ \longrightarrow\ -\dfrac1{x^2}\)

Reciprocal of x\(\left(\dfrac1x\right)'=-\dfrac1{x^2}\)
Reciprocal of a function (chain rule)\(\left(\dfrac1f\right)'=-\dfrac{f'}{f^2}\)

The derivative of \(\tfrac1x\) is negative everywhere: the function decreases on each of its two branches (Unit 04).

Example 1

Differentiate \(\dfrac1{x^2+1}\).

Solution.

\(-\dfrac{2x}{(x^2+1)^2}\)

2. The quotient rule

\(\left(\dfrac fg\right)'=\dfrac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}\)
derivative of the top times the bottom, minus the top times the derivative of the bottom, all over the bottom squared

Why? Write \(\dfrac fg=f\cdot\dfrac1g\) and use the product rule: \(f'\cdot\dfrac1g+f\cdot\left(-\dfrac{g'}{g^2}\right)=\dfrac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}\).

Example 2

Differentiate \(f(x)=\dfrac{2x-1}{x+3}\).

Solution.

\(f'(x)=\dfrac{2(x+3)-(2x-1)\cdot1}{(x+3)^2}=\dfrac{7}{(x+3)^2}\)

Note: the order in the numerator matters, because of the minus sign: \(f'g-fg'\), starting with the derivative of the numerator.

3. Where the function is not defined: asymptote or hole?

A rational function is not defined where its denominator is 0. What happens there depends on the numerator:

At \(x=a\) the denominator is 0, and…Then the graph hasExample
the numerator is not 0a vertical asymptote \(x=a\)\(\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\) at \(x=1\)
the factor \((x-a)\) cancels from top and bottoma hole (a removable gap) at \(x=a\)\(\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}=x+2\), \(x\ne2\)
Example 3

Find the asymptotes and holes of \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-4}{x^2-3x+2}\).

Solution.

\(f(x)=\dfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{(x-1)(x-2)}=\dfrac{x+2}{x-1}\), for \(x\ne1,2\).

At \(x=2\) the factor cancels: a hole at \(\left(2,\tfrac{2+2}{2-1}\right)=(2,4)\).

At \(x=1\): vertical asymptote \(x=1\).

4. Horizontal asymptotes

For large \(|x|\), only the highest powers of the numerator and the denominator matter. Compare the degrees:

DegreesHorizontal asymptoteExample
numerator lower than denominator\(y=0\)\(\dfrac{5}{x^2+1}\to0\)
equal degrees\(y=\) ratio of leading coefficients\(\dfrac{3x+1}{x-2}\to3\), so \(y=3\)
numerator highernone (the graph goes to \(\pm\infty\))\(\dfrac{x^3}{x+1}\)

We find these limits by thinking about large values of \(x\), without algebraic techniques. For example, for \(x=1000\): \(\dfrac{3001}{998}\approx3.007\).

5. Sketching and full investigation

Sketch from the structure: domain, asymptotes and holes, intercepts, and the sign of the function (from the signs of the numerator and denominator). Then use the derivative for increase, decrease and extreme points.

Example 4

Investigate \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2}{x^2-1}\).

Solution.

Domain \(x\ne\pm1\). Even: \(f(-x)=f(x)\).

Vertical asymptotes \(x=\pm1\); equal degrees, so the horizontal asymptote is \(y=1\).

Intercepts: only \((0,0)\).

\(f'(x)=\dfrac{2x(x^2-1)-x^2\cdot2x}{(x^2-1)^2}=\dfrac{-2x}{(x^2-1)^2}\).

The denominator is positive, so \(f'\) has the sign of \(-2x\): increasing for \(x\lt 0\) (\(x\ne-1\)), decreasing for \(x\gt 0\) (\(x\ne1\)).

Maximum \((0,0)\), between the two vertical asymptotes.

6. Common mistakes

  • Cancelling terms instead of factors: \(\dfrac{x+2}{x+5}\) cannot be "simplified" by cancelling the \(x\).
  • Calling every zero of the denominator an asymptote. If the factor cancels, it is a hole.
  • After cancelling, forgetting that the original function is still undefined at that point.
  • Swapping the order in the quotient rule: \(fg'-f'g\) gives the wrong sign.
  • Expanding \(g^2\) in the denominator of the derivative. Leave it as \((\dots)^2\): it shows at once that it is positive.
  • Reading the horizontal asymptote of \(\dfrac{3x+1}{x-2}\) as \(y=0\) because "there is an \(x\) in the denominator". Compare the degrees.

7. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Find the domain of \(\dfrac1{x+3}\).

    Show solution

    \(x\ne-3\)

  2. Find the asymptotes of \(\dfrac{2x-1}{x+4}\).

    Show solution

    \(x=-4\) and \(y=2\).

  3. Differentiate \(\dfrac1x\).

    Show solution

    \(-\dfrac1{x^2}\)

  4. Differentiate \(\dfrac{x}{x-1}\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{(x-1)-x}{(x-1)^2}=-\dfrac1{(x-1)^2}\)

  5. Where is the hole of \(\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}\)?

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3\), \(x\ne3\): a hole at \((3,6)\).

  6. Find the horizontal asymptote of \(\dfrac3{x^2+1}\).

    Show solution

    \(y=0\)

  7. Differentiate \(\dfrac1{2x+1}\).

    Show solution

    \(-\dfrac{2}{(2x+1)^2}\)

  8. Find the roots of \(\dfrac{x^2-4}{x+1}\).

    Show solution

    \(x=\pm2\) (both are in the domain).
