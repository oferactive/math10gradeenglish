Before you start: Factor numerator and denominator first. It shows the roots, the asymptotes and the holes at once. In the derivative, factor the numerator and keep the squared denominator: its sign is always positive.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the domain.

    1. \(\dfrac3{x-5}\)
    2. \(\dfrac{x+1}{x^2-9}\)
    3. \(\dfrac1{x^2+4}\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Exclude every \(x\) that makes the denominator 0.

    (a) \(x-5=0\Rightarrow x=5\).

    (b) \(x^2-9=0\Rightarrow x=\pm3\).

    (c) \(x^2+4\ge4\gt 0\): never 0.

    Answer: (a) \(x\ne5\); (b) \(x\ne\pm3\); (c) all real \(x\).

  2. Find the vertical asymptotes.

    1. \(\dfrac{2x}{x+4}\)
    2. \(\dfrac1{x^2-x}\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Vertical asymptotes: zeros of the denominator where the numerator is not zero.

    (a) \(x+4=0\Rightarrow x=-4\) (numerator \(-8\ne0\)).

    (b) \(x^2-x=x(x-1)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\ 1\) (numerator 1).

    Answer: (a) \(x=-4\); (b) \(x=0\) and \(x=1\).

  3. Find the horizontal asymptote.

    1. \(\dfrac{3x+1}{x-2}\)
    2. \(\dfrac5{x^2+1}\)
    3. \(\dfrac{2x^2}{x^2+3}\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Compare degrees. Equal degrees: ratio of leading coefficients. Smaller numerator degree: \(y=0\).

    (a) Equal degrees: \(\dfrac31=3\).

    (b) Degree 0 over degree 2: \(y=0\).

    (c) Equal degrees: \(\dfrac21=2\).

    Answer: (a) \(y=3\); (b) \(y=0\); (c) \(y=2\).

  4. \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\) and \(g(x)=\tfrac3x\). Find \(f'(2)\) and \(g'(x)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\left(\tfrac1x\right)'=-\tfrac1{x^2}\); a constant factor stays.

    \(f'(x)=-\dfrac1{x^2}\Rightarrow f'(2)=-\dfrac14\).

    \(g'(x)=3\cdot\left(-\dfrac1{x^2}\right)\).

    Answer: \(f'(2)=-\tfrac14\); \(g'(x)=-\dfrac3{x^2}\).

  5. Differentiate \(\dfrac{x}{x+1}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Quotient rule with \(u=x\), \(v=x+1\).

    \(\dfrac{1\cdot(x+1)-x\cdot1}{(x+1)^2}=\dfrac{1}{(x+1)^2}\).

    Answer: \(\dfrac1{(x+1)^2}\).

  6. Differentiate \(\dfrac1{x-3}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\dfrac1{x-3}=(x-3)^{-1}\); chain rule with inner derivative 1.

    \(-1\cdot(x-3)^{-2}\cdot1\).

    Answer: \(-\dfrac1{(x-3)^2}\).

  7. Find where \(\dfrac{x-2}{x+1}\) meets the axes.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(x\)-axis: the numerator is 0. \(y\)-axis: substitute \(x=0\).

    \(x-2=0\Rightarrow x=2\): the point \((2,0)\).

    \(f(0)=\dfrac{-2}{1}=-2\): the point \((0,-2)\).

    Answer: \((2,0)\) and \((0,-2)\).

  8. Simplify \(\dfrac{x^2-1}{x-1}\) and find the hole.

    Show solution

    Idea. Factor the numerator and cancel; the cancelled factor leaves a hole.

    \(\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1}=x+1\), for \(x\ne1\).

    \(y\)-value of the hole: \(1+1=2\).

    Answer: \(x+1\) (\(x\ne1\)); hole at \((1,2)\).

  9. Where is \(\dfrac{x-2}{x+1}\) positive?

    Show solution

    Idea. A fraction is positive when the numerator and denominator have the same sign. Use a sign table with the critical points \(-1\) and 2.

    \(x\lt -1\): top \(-\), bottom \(-\): positive.

    \(-1\lt x\lt 2\): top \(-\), bottom \(+\): negative.

    \(x\gt 2\): both \(+\): positive.

    Answer: \(x\lt -1\) or \(x\gt 2\).

  10. Differentiate \(\dfrac{2x-1}{x+3}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Quotient rule with \(u=2x-1\), \(v=x+3\).

    \(u'=2\), \(v'=1\).

    \(\dfrac{2(x+3)-(2x-1)\cdot1}{(x+3)^2}=\dfrac{2x+6-2x+1}{(x+3)^2}\).

    Answer: \(\dfrac7{(x+3)^2}\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Investigate \(f(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\): domain, asymptotes, intercepts, increase and decrease.

    Show solution

    Idea. Go through the checklist: domain, asymptotes, intercepts, then \(f'\) and its sign.

    Domain: \(x\ne1\).

    Asymptotes: \(x=1\) (vertical); \(y=\tfrac11=1\) (horizontal).

    \(x\)-intercept: \(x+1=0\Rightarrow(-1,0)\). \(y\)-intercept: \(f(0)=-1\Rightarrow(0,-1)\).

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{1\cdot(x-1)-(x+1)\cdot1}{(x-1)^2}=\dfrac{-2}{(x-1)^2}\lt 0\).

    Answer: decreasing on \(x\lt 1\) and on \(x\gt 1\) (separately); no extreme points.

    Watch out: \(f\) is not decreasing on its whole domain: \(f(0)=-1\lt f(2)=3\). The asymptote separates the two branches.

  2. Investigate \(f(x)=\dfrac{x}{x^2+1}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Checklist: domain, symmetry, asymptote, root, \(f'\) and its sign.

    \(x^2+1\gt 0\): domain is all \(x\). \(f(-x)=-f(x)\): odd.

    Degree 1 over degree 2: \(y=0\). Root: \(x=0\).

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{1\cdot(x^2+1)-x\cdot2x}{(x^2+1)^2}=\dfrac{1-x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}\).

    The sign is the sign of \(1-x^2\): \(-\) for \(x\lt -1\), \(+\) on \((-1,1)\), \(-\) for \(x\gt 1\).

    \(f(-1)=-\tfrac12\), \(f(1)=\tfrac12\).

    Answer: minimum \(\left(-1,-\tfrac12\right)\), maximum \(\left(1,\tfrac12\right)\); decreasing for \(|x|\gt 1\), increasing for \(|x|\lt 1\).

  3. Investigate \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2}{x^2-1}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Asymptotes from the denominator and the degrees; then \(f'\) and its sign.

    \(x^2-1=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1\): vertical asymptotes. Equal degrees: \(y=1\).

    \(f(-x)=f(x)\): even.

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{2x(x^2-1)-x^2\cdot2x}{(x^2-1)^2}=\dfrac{-2x}{(x^2-1)^2}\).

    Sign of \(f'\) is the sign of \(-2x\): \(+\) for \(x\lt 0\), \(-\) for \(x\gt 0\).

    \(f(0)=0\).

    Answer: asymptotes \(x=\pm1\), \(y=1\); increasing for \(x\lt 0\) (\(x\ne-1\)), decreasing for \(x\gt 0\) (\(x\ne1\)); maximum \((0,0)\).

  4. Find the tangent to \(y=\tfrac1x\) at \(x=2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Point from \(y(2)\), slope from \(y'=-\tfrac1{x^2}\).

    \(y(2)=\tfrac12\); \(y'(2)=-\tfrac14\).

    \(y-\tfrac12=-\tfrac14(x-2)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac14x+\tfrac12+\tfrac12\).

    Answer: \(y=-\tfrac14x+1\).

  5. Find the asymptotes and the holes of \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-4}{x^2-3x+2}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Factor top and bottom. A common factor gives a hole; a remaining zero of the denominator gives an asymptote.

    \(x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2)\); \(x^2-3x+2=(x-1)(x-2)\).

    \(f(x)=\dfrac{x+2}{x-1}\) for \(x\ne1,\ 2\).

    Hole at \(x=2\): \(y=\dfrac{4}{1}=4\).

    Answer: hole \((2,4)\); asymptotes \(x=1\) and \(y=1\).

  6. Differentiate \(f(x)=\dfrac1{x^2+1}\) and find its extreme point.

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule on \((x^2+1)^{-1}\), then the sign of \(f'\).

    \(f'(x)=-1\cdot(x^2+1)^{-2}\cdot2x=-\dfrac{2x}{(x^2+1)^2}\).

    The denominator is positive, so the sign is the sign of \(-2x\): \(+\) for \(x\lt 0\), \(-\) for \(x\gt 0\).

    \(f(0)=1\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=-\dfrac{2x}{(x^2+1)^2}\); maximum \((0,1)\).

  7. \(f(x)=x+\dfrac4x\). Find the extreme points.

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate term by term, solve \(f'=0\), and remember that \(x=0\) is outside the domain.

    \(f'(x)=1-\dfrac4{x^2}=\dfrac{x^2-4}{x^2}\).

    \(f'=0\Rightarrow x=\pm2\).

    Sign of \(x^2-4\): \(+\) for \(|x|\gt 2\), \(-\) for \(|x|\lt 2\) (\(x\ne0\)).

    \(f(-2)=-2-2=-4\); \(f(2)=2+2=4\).

    Answer: maximum \((-2,-4)\), minimum \((2,4)\).

    Watch out: the maximum value is smaller than the minimum value; they are on different branches.

  8. \(f(x)=\dfrac{ax+1}{x-2}\) has the horizontal asymptote \(y=3\). Find \(a\) and \(f'(x)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Equal degrees: the horizontal asymptote is \(y=\tfrac a1\). Then use the quotient rule.

    \(\tfrac a1=3\Rightarrow a=3\).

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{3(x-2)-(3x+1)\cdot1}{(x-2)^2}=\dfrac{-6-1}{(x-2)^2}\).

    Answer: \(a=3\); \(f'(x)=-\dfrac7{(x-2)^2}\).

  9. Solve \(\dfrac{x-3}{x+2}\ge0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Sign table with the zero of the numerator (3) and of the denominator (\(-2\)). The denominator can never be 0.

    \(x\lt -2\): \(\dfrac{-}{-}\gt 0\). \(-2\lt x\lt 3\): \(\dfrac{-}{+}\lt 0\). \(x\gt 3\): \(\dfrac{+}{+}\gt 0\).

    \(x=3\) gives 0, which satisfies \(\ge0\); \(x=-2\) is excluded.

    Answer: \(x\lt -2\) or \(x\ge3\).

  10. Find the tangents to \(y=\dfrac1{x-1}\) with slope \(-1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Set \(y'=-1\) and solve; then write each tangent.

    \(y'=-\dfrac1{(x-1)^2}=-1\Rightarrow(x-1)^2=1\Rightarrow x-1=\pm1\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=2\).

    \(x=0\): \(y=-1\). \(y+1=-x\Rightarrow y=-x-1\).

    \(x=2\): \(y=1\). \(y-1=-(x-2)\Rightarrow y=-x+3\).

    Answer: \(y=-x-1\) and \(y=-x+3\).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. \(f(x)=\dfrac{4x}{x^2+4}\).

    1. Find the domain, symmetry, asymptotes and root.
    2. Find the extreme points.
    3. Which values does \(f\) take?
    4. How many solutions does \(f(x)=k\) have?
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Checklist. (b) Quotient rule and signs. (c) The range lies between the minimum and maximum, since \(f\to0\) at both ends. (d) Horizontal lines \(y=k\).

    (a) \(x^2+4\gt 0\): all \(x\). \(f(-x)=-f(x)\): odd. Degree 1 over 2: \(y=0\). Root 0.

    (b) \(f'(x)=\dfrac{4(x^2+4)-4x\cdot2x}{(x^2+4)^2}=\dfrac{16-4x^2}{(x^2+4)^2}\). Zeros \(x=\pm2\); signs \(-,\ +,\ -\).

    \(f(-2)=\dfrac{-8}8=-1\) (minimum), \(f(2)=1\) (maximum).

    (c) The values go from \(-1\) to 1.

    (d) For \(0\lt k\lt 1\), the line meets the right half twice (up to the maximum and back down towards 0); the same for \(-1\lt k\lt 0\) on the left.

    Answer: (a) all \(x\), odd, \(y=0\), root 0; (b) min \((-2,-1)\), max \((2,1)\); (c) \(-1\le f(x)\le1\); (d) two for \(0\lt |k|\lt 1\), one for \(k=0\) or \(k=\pm1\), none for \(|k|\gt 1\).

  2. \(f(x)=\dfrac1{x^2-4x+3}\).

    1. Find the asymptotes.
    2. Find where \(f\) is positive.
    3. Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the extreme point.
    Show solution

    Idea. Factor the denominator; the sign of \(f\) is the sign of the denominator; \(f'\) by the chain rule.

    (a) \((x-1)(x-3)=0\Rightarrow x=1,\ 3\). Degree 0 over 2: \(y=0\).

    (b) The denominator is an upward parabola: positive outside \([1,3]\), negative inside.

    (c) \(f'(x)=-\dfrac{2x-4}{(x^2-4x+3)^2}\). The sign is the sign of \(-(2x-4)\): \(+\) for \(x\lt 2\), \(-\) for \(x\gt 2\).

    \(f(2)=\dfrac1{4-8+3}=-1\).

    Answer: (a) \(x=1\), \(x=3\), \(y=0\); (b) positive for \(x\lt 1\) or \(x\gt 3\), negative for \(1\lt x\lt 3\); (c) increasing for \(x\lt 2\), decreasing for \(x\gt 2\) (away from the asymptotes); maximum \((2,-1)\).

  3. \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2+a}{x}\) has an extreme point at \(x=3\). Find \(a\) and both extreme points.

    Show solution

    Idea. Split the fraction into \(x+\tfrac ax\), set \(f'(3)=0\), then find both critical points.

    \(f(x)=x+\dfrac ax\), \(f'(x)=1-\dfrac a{x^2}\).

    \(f'(3)=1-\dfrac a9=0\Rightarrow a=9\).

    \(f'(x)=1-\dfrac9{x^2}=0\Rightarrow x=\pm3\); signs \(+,\ -,\ -,\ +\) (around \(-3\), 0, 3).

    \(f(3)=3+3=6\); \(f(-3)=-6\).

    Answer: \(a=9\); minimum \((3,6)\), maximum \((-3,-6)\).

  4. For which \(b\) does \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2+bx+6}{x-2}\) have a hole instead of an asymptote at \(x=2\)? Find the hole.

    Show solution

    Idea. A hole appears when the factor \((x-2)\) cancels, so the numerator must also be 0 at \(x=2\).

    \(2^2+2b+6=0\Rightarrow2b=-10\Rightarrow b=-5\).

    \(x^2-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)\), so \(f(x)=x-3\) for \(x\ne2\).

    \(y\)-value: \(2-3=-1\).

    Answer: \(b=-5\); the hole is at \((2,-1)\).

  5. \(f(x)=\dfrac{2x+3}{x+1}\).

    1. Show that \(f(x)=2+\dfrac1{x+1}\) and find the asymptotes.
    2. Show that \(f\) decreases on each branch.
    3. Find the tangents with slope \(-1\).
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Write the numerator as a multiple of the denominator plus a remainder. (b) Differentiate the simpler form. (c) Solve \(f'=-1\).

    (a) \(2x+3=2(x+1)+1\), so \(f(x)=2+\dfrac1{x+1}\). Asymptotes \(x=-1\) and \(y=2\).

    (b) \(f'(x)=-\dfrac1{(x+1)^2}\lt 0\) wherever \(f\) is defined.

    (c) \((x+1)^2=1\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=-2\).

    \(f(0)=3\): \(y=-x+3\). \(f(-2)=2-1=1\): \(y-1=-(x+2)\Rightarrow y=-x-1\).

    Answer: (a) shown, \(x=-1\), \(y=2\); (b) shown; (c) \(y=-x+3\) and \(y=-x-1\).

  6. Producing \(x\) items costs \(C(x)=100+2x\) shekels. The average cost per item is \(A(x)=\dfrac{C(x)}x\).

    1. Write \(A(x)\).
    2. Show that \(A\) is decreasing.
    3. Find the horizontal asymptote and explain its meaning.
    Show solution

    Idea. Average cost = total cost ÷ number of items. Split the fraction to see the meaning of each part.

    (a) \(A(x)=\dfrac{100+2x}x=\dfrac{100}x+2\).

    (b) \(A'(x)=-\dfrac{100}{x^2}\lt 0\) for every \(x\gt 0\).

    (c) As \(x\) grows, \(\tfrac{100}x\to0\), so \(A\to2\).

    Answer: (a) \(A(x)=2+\dfrac{100}x\); (b) decreasing: each extra item makes the average cheaper; (c) \(y=2\): the fixed cost 100 is spread over many items, and the average approaches the 2 shekels per item.

  7. Use the definition of the derivative to prove that \(\left(\tfrac1x\right)'=-\tfrac1{x^2}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Combine the two fractions over a common denominator, cancel \(h\), then let \(h\to0\).

    \(\dfrac1{x+h}-\dfrac1x=\dfrac{x-(x+h)}{x(x+h)}=\dfrac{-h}{x(x+h)}\).

    Divide by \(h\): \(\dfrac{-1}{x(x+h)}\).

    As \(h\to0\): \(\dfrac{-1}{x\cdot x}\).

    Answer: \(\left(\tfrac1x\right)'=-\dfrac1{x^2}\).

  8. \(f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{x^2}\).

    1. Find the domain, root and asymptotes, and the behaviour near \(x=0\).
    2. Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the extreme point.
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Checklist; near the asymptote look at the sign of top and bottom. (b) Quotient rule, simplify by \(x\).

    (a) Domain \(x\ne0\). Root: \(x=1\). Asymptotes: \(x=0\); degree 1 over 2 gives \(y=0\).

    Near 0: numerator \(\approx-1\), denominator \(x^2\gt 0\) and tiny, so \(f\to-\infty\) from both sides.

    (b) \(f'(x)=\dfrac{1\cdot x^2-(x-1)\cdot2x}{x^4}=\dfrac{x(x-2x+2)}{x^4}=\dfrac{2-x}{x^3}\).

    Sign: \(x\lt 0\): \(\dfrac{+}{-}\lt 0\). \(0\lt x\lt 2\): \(\dfrac{+}{+}\gt 0\). \(x\gt 2\): \(\dfrac{-}{+}\lt 0\).

    \(f(2)=\dfrac1{4}\).

    Answer: (a) \(x\ne0\), root 1, asymptotes \(x=0\), \(y=0\), \(f\to-\infty\) near 0; (b) decreasing for \(x\lt 0\) and \(x\gt 2\), increasing on \((0,2)\); maximum \(\left(2,\tfrac14\right)\).

  9. Prove that every tangent to \(y=\tfrac1x\) (\(x\gt 0\)) forms with the axes a triangle of area 2.

    Show solution

    Idea. Write the tangent at a general point \(\left(a,\tfrac1a\right)\), find its intercepts, and compute the area. If \(a\) cancels, the area is the same for every tangent.

    Slope: \(-\dfrac1{a^2}\). \(y-\dfrac1a=-\dfrac1{a^2}(x-a)\Rightarrow y=-\dfrac x{a^2}+\dfrac2a\).

    \(x=0\Rightarrow y=\dfrac2a\). \(y=0\Rightarrow x=2a\).

    Area \(=\dfrac12\cdot2a\cdot\dfrac2a=2\).

    Answer: the area is always 2, whatever \(a\) is.

  10. Solve \(\dfrac1{x-1}\lt x+1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Do not multiply by \(x-1\) (its sign is unknown). Move everything to one side, combine into one fraction, and use a sign table.

    \(\dfrac1{x-1}-(x+1)=\dfrac{1-(x+1)(x-1)}{x-1}=\dfrac{2-x^2}{x-1}\lt 0\).

    Critical points: \(2-x^2=0\Rightarrow x=\pm\sqrt2\); \(x-1=0\Rightarrow x=1\).

    Signs: \(x\lt -\sqrt2\): \(\dfrac{-}{-}\gt 0\); \(-\sqrt2\lt x\lt 1\): \(\dfrac{+}{-}\lt 0\); \(1\lt x\lt \sqrt2\): \(\dfrac{+}{+}\gt 0\); \(x\gt \sqrt2\): \(\dfrac{-}{+}\lt 0\).

    Answer: \(-\sqrt2\lt x\lt 1\) or \(x\gt \sqrt2\).

    Check: \(x=0\): \(-1\lt 1\) ✓; \(x=2\): \(1\lt 3\) ✓
