Before you start: Factor numerator and denominator first. It shows the roots, the asymptotes and the holes at once. In the derivative, factor the numerator and keep the squared denominator: its sign is always positive.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the domain.

    1. \(\dfrac3{x-5}\)
    2. \(\dfrac{x+1}{x^2-9}\)
    3. \(\dfrac1{x^2+4}\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(x\ne5\)

    (b) \(x\ne\pm3\)

    (c) all \(x\), since \(x^2+4\gt 0\)

  2. Find the vertical asymptotes.

    1. \(\dfrac{2x}{x+4}\)
    2. \(\dfrac1{x^2-x}\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(x=-4\)

    (b) \(x(x-1)=0\): \(x=0\) and \(x=1\)

  3. Find the horizontal asymptote.

    1. \(\dfrac{3x+1}{x-2}\)
    2. \(\dfrac5{x^2+1}\)
    3. \(\dfrac{2x^2}{x^2+3}\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(y=3\)

    (b) \(y=0\)

    (c) \(y=2\)

  4. \(f(x)=\tfrac1x\) and \(g(x)=\tfrac3x\). Find \(f'(2)\) and \(g'(x)\).

    Show solution

    \(f'(2)=-\tfrac14\). \(g'(x)=-\dfrac3{x^2}\).

  5. Differentiate \(\dfrac{x}{x+1}\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{(x+1)-x}{(x+1)^2}=\dfrac1{(x+1)^2}\)

  6. Differentiate \(\dfrac1{x-3}\).

    Show solution

    \(-\dfrac1{(x-3)^2}\)

  7. Find where \(\dfrac{x-2}{x+1}\) meets the axes.

    Show solution

    x-axis: \(x=2\). y-axis: \(\dfrac{-2}{1}=-2\).

  8. Simplify \(\dfrac{x^2-1}{x-1}\) and find the hole.

    Show solution

    \(x+1\), for \(x\ne1\). Hole at \((1,2)\).

  9. Where is \(\dfrac{x-2}{x+1}\) positive?

    Show solution

    Same signs of top and bottom: \(x\lt -1\) or \(x\gt 2\).

  10. Differentiate \(\dfrac{2x-1}{x+3}\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{2(x+3)-(2x-1)}{(x+3)^2}=\dfrac7{(x+3)^2}\)

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Investigate \(f(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\): domain, asymptotes, intercepts, increase and decrease.

    Show solution

    Domain \(x\ne1\). Asymptotes \(x=1\), \(y=1\).

    Intercepts \((-1,0)\) and \((0,-1)\).

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{(x-1)-(x+1)}{(x-1)^2}=\dfrac{-2}{(x-1)^2}\lt 0\): decreasing on \(x\lt 1\) and on \(x\gt 1\); no extreme points.

  2. Investigate \(f(x)=\dfrac{x}{x^2+1}\).

    Show solution

    Domain: all \(x\). Odd. Asymptote \(y=0\). Root \(0\).

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{(x^2+1)-2x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}=\dfrac{1-x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}\).

    Decreasing for \(x\lt -1\), increasing for \(-1\lt x\lt 1\), decreasing for \(x\gt 1\).

    Minimum \(\left(-1,-\tfrac12\right)\), maximum \(\left(1,\tfrac12\right)\).

  3. Investigate \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2}{x^2-1}\).

    Show solution

    Asymptotes \(x=\pm1\), \(y=1\). Even. \(f'(x)=\dfrac{-2x}{(x^2-1)^2}\).

    Increasing for \(x\lt 0\) (\(x\ne-1\)), decreasing for \(x\gt 0\) (\(x\ne1\)). Maximum \((0,0)\).

  4. Find the tangent to \(y=\tfrac1x\) at \(x=2\).

    Show solution

    Point \(\left(2,\tfrac12\right)\), slope \(-\tfrac14\): \(y=-\tfrac14x+1\).

  5. Find the asymptotes and the holes of \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-4}{x^2-3x+2}\).

    Show solution

    \(f(x)=\dfrac{x+2}{x-1}\), \(x\ne1,2\).

    Hole \((2,4)\). Asymptotes \(x=1\) and \(y=1\).

  6. Differentiate \(f(x)=\dfrac1{x^2+1}\) and find its extreme point.

    Show solution

    \(f'(x)=-\dfrac{2x}{(x^2+1)^2}\): positive for \(x\lt 0\), negative for \(x\gt 0\). Maximum \((0,1)\).

  7. \(f(x)=x+\dfrac4x\). Find the extreme points.

    Show solution

    \(f'(x)=1-\dfrac4{x^2}=0\Rightarrow x=\pm2\).

    Sign of \(f'\): + for \(x\lt -2\), − for \(-2\lt x\lt 0\) and \(0\lt x\lt 2\), + for \(x\gt 2\).

    Maximum \((-2,-4)\), minimum \((2,4)\).

  8. \(f(x)=\dfrac{ax+1}{x-2}\) has the horizontal asymptote \(y=3\). Find \(a\) and \(f'(x)\).

    Show solution

    \(a=3\). \(f'(x)=\dfrac{3(x-2)-(3x+1)}{(x-2)^2}=\dfrac{-7}{(x-2)^2}\).

  9. Solve \(\dfrac{x-3}{x+2}\ge0\).

    Show solution

    Roots of top and bottom: \(3\) and \(-2\).

    \(x\lt -2\) or \(x\ge3\) (\(x=-2\) is excluded; \(x=3\) gives 0).

  10. Find the tangents to \(y=\dfrac1{x-1}\) with slope \(-1\).

    Show solution

    \(-\dfrac1{(x-1)^2}=-1\Rightarrow (x-1)^2=1\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=2\).

    \((0,-1)\): \(y=-x-1\). \((2,1)\): \(y=-x+3\).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. \(f(x)=\dfrac{4x}{x^2+4}\).

    1. Find the domain, symmetry, asymptotes and root.
    2. Find the extreme points.
    3. Which values does \(f\) take?
    4. How many solutions does \(f(x)=k\) have?
    Show solution

    (a) All \(x\); odd; asymptote \(y=0\); root \(0\).

    (b) \(f'(x)=\dfrac{4(x^2+4)-8x^2}{(x^2+4)^2}=\dfrac{16-4x^2}{(x^2+4)^2}\). Minimum \((-2,-1)\), maximum \((2,1)\).

    (c) All values from \(-1\) to \(1\).

    (d) Two solutions for \(0\lt |k|\lt 1\); one for \(k=0\) or \(k=\pm1\); none for \(|k|\gt 1\).

  2. \(f(x)=\dfrac1{x^2-4x+3}\).

    1. Find the asymptotes.
    2. Find where \(f\) is positive.
    3. Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the extreme point.
    Show solution

    (a) \(x^2-4x+3=(x-1)(x-3)\): asymptotes \(x=1\), \(x=3\), and \(y=0\).

    (b) Positive for \(x\lt 1\) or \(x\gt 3\); negative for \(1\lt x\lt 3\).

    (c) \(f'(x)=-\dfrac{2x-4}{(x^2-4x+3)^2}\): increasing for \(x\lt 2\), decreasing for \(x\gt 2\) (except at the asymptotes). Maximum \((2,-1)\), between the asymptotes.

  3. \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2+a}{x}\) has an extreme point at \(x=3\). Find \(a\) and both extreme points.

    Show solution

    \(f(x)=x+\dfrac ax\), \(f'(x)=1-\dfrac a{x^2}\). \(f'(3)=0\Rightarrow a=9\).

    Minimum \((3,6)\), maximum \((-3,-6)\).

  4. For which \(b\) does \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2+bx+6}{x-2}\) have a hole instead of an asymptote at \(x=2\)? Find the hole.

    Show solution

    The numerator must be 0 at \(x=2\): \(4+2b+6=0\Rightarrow b=-5\).

    \(\dfrac{(x-2)(x-3)}{x-2}=x-3\): a hole at \((2,-1)\).

  5. \(f(x)=\dfrac{2x+3}{x+1}\).

    1. Show that \(f(x)=2+\dfrac1{x+1}\) and find the asymptotes.
    2. Show that \(f\) decreases on each branch.
    3. Find the tangents with slope \(-1\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(2x+3=2(x+1)+1\Rightarrow f(x)=2+\dfrac1{x+1}\). Asymptotes \(x=-1\), \(y=2\).

    (b) \(f'(x)=-\dfrac1{(x+1)^2}\lt 0\): decreasing on each branch.

    (c) \((x+1)^2=1\Rightarrow x=0,\ -2\). At \((0,3)\): \(y=-x+3\); at \((-2,1)\): \(y=-x-1\).

  6. Producing \(x\) items costs \(C(x)=100+2x\) shekels. The average cost per item is \(A(x)=\dfrac{C(x)}x\).

    1. Write \(A(x)\).
    2. Show that \(A\) is decreasing.
    3. Find the horizontal asymptote and explain its meaning.
    Show solution

    (a) \(A(x)=2+\dfrac{100}x\).

    (b) \(A'(x)=-\dfrac{100}{x^2}\lt 0\): the more items, the cheaper each one.

    (c) \(y=2\): the average cost approaches the cost of materials per item, 2 shekels, as the fixed cost of 100 is shared by more and more items.

  7. Use the definition of the derivative to prove that \(\left(\tfrac1x\right)'=-\tfrac1{x^2}\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{\frac1{x+h}-\frac1x}h=\dfrac{x-x-h}{hx(x+h)}=\dfrac{-1}{x(x+h)}\to-\dfrac1{x^2}\).

  8. \(f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{x^2}\).

    1. Find the domain, root and asymptotes, and the behaviour near \(x=0\).
    2. Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the extreme point.
    Show solution

    (a) Domain \(x\ne0\); root \(1\); asymptotes \(x=0\) and \(y=0\). Near \(0\) the numerator is close to \(-1\) and \(x^2\gt 0\), so \(f\to-\infty\) on both sides.

    (b) \(f'(x)=\dfrac{x^2-(x-1)\cdot2x}{x^4}=\dfrac{2-x}{x^3}\).

    Decreasing for \(x\lt 0\), increasing for \(0\lt x\lt 2\), decreasing for \(x\gt 2\). Maximum \(\left(2,\tfrac14\right)\).

  9. Prove that every tangent to \(y=\tfrac1x\) (\(x\gt 0\)) forms with the axes a triangle of area 2.

    Show solution

    At \(\left(a,\tfrac1a\right)\) the slope is \(-\tfrac1{a^2}\): \(y=-\dfrac{x}{a^2}+\dfrac2a\).

    Intercepts: \(\left(0,\tfrac2a\right)\) and \((2a,0)\).

    Area \(=\dfrac12\cdot2a\cdot\dfrac2a=2\).

  10. Solve \(\dfrac1{x-1}\lt x+1\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac1{x-1}-(x+1)\lt 0\Rightarrow\dfrac{1-(x^2-1)}{x-1}\lt 0\Rightarrow\dfrac{2-x^2}{x-1}\lt 0\).

    Sign table with \(-\sqrt2,\ 1,\ \sqrt2\): negative for \(-\sqrt2\lt x\lt 1\) and for \(x\gt \sqrt2\).
