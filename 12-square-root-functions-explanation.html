Square Root Functions

1. The derivative of \(\sqrt x\)

Using the definition and multiplying by the "conjugate" \(\sqrt{x+h}+\sqrt x\):

\(\dfrac{\sqrt{x+h}-\sqrt x}h=\dfrac{(x+h)-x}{h(\sqrt{x+h}+\sqrt x)}=\dfrac1{\sqrt{x+h}+\sqrt x}\ \longrightarrow\ \dfrac1{2\sqrt x}\)

\(\left(\sqrt x\right)'=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}\qquad(x\gt 0)\)

The derivative is positive, so \(\sqrt x\) always increases. As \(x\) grows, the derivative gets smaller: the graph flattens out. As \(x\to0\), the derivative grows without limit: at the origin the tangent is vertical, and the derivative does not exist there (Unit 07).

2. Derivatives of inverse functions

\(\sqrt x\) is the inverse of \(x^2\) for \(x\ge0\), and their graphs are mirror images in \(y=x\) (Unit 06). Reflection swaps \(\Delta x\) and \(\Delta y\), so it turns a slope \(m\) into \(\tfrac1m\):

\(\text{slope of } f^{-1} \text{ at } (b,a)=\dfrac1{f'(a)}\qquad\text{when } (a,b) \text{ is on the graph of } f\)
Example 1

Check the rule with \(f(x)=x^2\) at \((3,9)\).

Solution.

\(f'(3)=6\). On \(\sqrt x\) at \((9,3)\): \(\dfrac1{2\sqrt9}=\dfrac16\) ✓

3. Roots of a linear expression

With the chain rule:

Root of a linear expression\(\left(\sqrt{ax+b}\right)'=\dfrac{a}{2\sqrt{ax+b}}\)
Root of any function\(\left(\sqrt{g(x)}\right)'=\dfrac{g'(x)}{2\sqrt{g(x)}}\)

The domain is where \(ax+b\ge0\). At the end of the domain, where \(ax+b=0\), the function equals 0, the derivative is not defined, and the tangent is vertical.

Example 2

Differentiate \(f(x)=\sqrt{6-2x}\), and find its domain.

Solution.

\(f'(x)=\dfrac{-2}{2\sqrt{6-2x}}=-\dfrac1{\sqrt{6-2x}}\).

Domain: \(6-2x\ge0\Rightarrow x\le3\). The function decreases and ends at \((3,0)\) with a vertical tangent.

4. Investigating functions with roots

The steps are the same as for polynomials, with two extra points: the domain is limited, and an extreme value can be at the end of the domain.

Example 3

Investigate \(f(x)=x-2\sqrt x\).

Solution.

Domain \(x\ge0\). Roots: \(x=2\sqrt x\Rightarrow x^2=4x\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=4\) (both check).

\(f'(x)=1-\dfrac1{\sqrt x}\). \(f'=0\Rightarrow\sqrt x=1\Rightarrow x=1\).

For \(0\lt x\lt 1\): \(f'\lt 0\), decreasing. For \(x\gt 1\): \(f'\gt 0\), increasing. Minimum \((1,-1)\).

At the end of the domain, \(f(0)=0\): a maximum at the end point (locally, the largest value nearby). As \(x\to0\), \(f'\to-\infty\): the tangent there is vertical.

Example 4

Find the tangent to \(y=\sqrt{x+5}\) at \(x=-1\).

Solution.

\(y(-1)=2\), \(y'=\dfrac1{2\sqrt{x+5}}\Rightarrow y'(-1)=\dfrac14\).

\(y-2=\tfrac14(x+1)\Rightarrow y=\tfrac14x+\tfrac94\).

5. Equations with roots

To solve an equation with a square root: isolate the root, square both sides, solve, and check every answer in the original equation. Squaring can add false solutions.

Example 5

Solve \(\sqrt{2x+1}=x-1\).

Solution.

\(2x+1=x^2-2x+1\Rightarrow x^2-4x=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=4\).

\(x=0\): \(\sqrt1=1\ne-1\) ✗. \(x=4\): \(\sqrt9=3=3\) ✓.

Answer: \(x=4\).

Note: a quick test: the right-hand side must be \(\ge0\), because a square root is never negative. Here \(x-1\ge0\) rules out \(x=0\) at once.

6. Common mistakes

  • Writing \((\sqrt x)'=\dfrac1{\sqrt x}\) or \(\dfrac12\sqrt x\). The derivative is \(\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}\).
  • Forgetting the inner derivative: \((\sqrt{3x+1})'=\dfrac{3}{2\sqrt{3x+1}}\), not \(\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3x+1}}\).
  • Not stating the domain before investigating. Points outside the domain are not solutions, extremes or roots.
  • Ignoring the end of the domain: extreme values can be there even though \(f'\) is not 0.
  • Squaring both sides and not checking the answers.

7. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Differentiate \(\sqrt x\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}\)

  2. Find the slope of the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(x=16\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac1{2\cdot4}=\dfrac18\)

  3. Find the domain of \(\sqrt{x-7}\).

    Show solution

    \(x\ge7\)

  4. Differentiate \(\sqrt{5x}\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{5}{2\sqrt{5x}}\)

  5. Differentiate \(\sqrt{1-x}\).

    Show solution

    \(-\dfrac1{2\sqrt{1-x}}\)

  6. Find the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(x=1\).

    Show solution

    Point \((1,1)\), slope \(\tfrac12\): \(y=\tfrac12x+\tfrac12\).

  7. Solve \(\sqrt x=x-2\).

    Show solution

    \(x=x^2-4x+4\Rightarrow x^2-5x+4=0\Rightarrow x=1\) or \(x=4\).

    \(x=1\): \(1\ne-1\) ✗. \(x=4\): \(2=2\) ✓. Answer \(x=4\).

  8. Does \(\sqrt x\) have a derivative at \(x=0\)?

    Show solution

    No. The tangent there is vertical, and a vertical line has no slope.
