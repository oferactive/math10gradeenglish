Before you start: Write the domain at the top of every question. At the end, check that each answer lies in the domain, and check solutions of equations by substituting them.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the slope of the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(x=4\) and at \(x=9\).

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac14\) and \(\tfrac16\).

  2. Differentiate.

    1. \(3\sqrt x\)
    2. \(\sqrt x+x^2\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(\dfrac3{2\sqrt x}\)

    (b) \(\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}+2x\)

  3. Find the domain.

    1. \(\sqrt{2x-6}\)
    2. \(\sqrt{5-x}\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(x\ge3\)

    (b) \(x\le5\)

  4. Differentiate \(\sqrt{3x+1}\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{3}{2\sqrt{3x+1}}\)

  5. Differentiate \(\sqrt{4-x}\).

    Show solution

    \(-\dfrac1{2\sqrt{4-x}}\)

  6. Find the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(x=4\).

    Show solution

    Point \((4,2)\), slope \(\tfrac14\): \(y=\tfrac14x+1\).

  7. At which point is the slope of the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) equal to \(\tfrac12\)?

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}=\dfrac12\Rightarrow x=1\). Point \((1,1)\).

  8. Solve \(\sqrt{x+3}=5\).

    Show solution

    \(x+3=25\Rightarrow x=22\).

  9. \(f(x)=2\sqrt x\). Find \(f(9)\) and \(f'(9)\).

    Show solution

    \(f(9)=6\). \(f'(x)=\dfrac1{\sqrt x}\Rightarrow f'(9)=\tfrac13\).

  10. Explain why \(\sqrt x\) is increasing on its whole domain.

    Show solution

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}\gt 0\) for every \(x\gt 0\), and \(f(0)=0\) is its smallest value.

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Investigate \(f(x)=x-4\sqrt x\): domain, roots, intervals of increase and decrease, extreme points.

    Show solution

    Domain \(x\ge0\). Roots: \(x=4\sqrt x\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=16\).

    \(f'(x)=1-\dfrac2{\sqrt x}=0\Rightarrow x=4\).

    Decreasing on \(0\lt x\lt 4\), increasing for \(x\gt 4\). Minimum \((4,-4)\); end point \((0,0)\).

  2. Find the tangent to \(y=\sqrt{x+5}\) at \(x=-1\).

    Show solution

    \(y=\tfrac14x+\tfrac94\)

  3. Check the rule for inverse functions: find the slope of \(x^2\) at \((3,9)\) and the slope of \(\sqrt x\) at \((9,3)\).

    Show solution

    \(6\) and \(\dfrac1{2\cdot3}=\dfrac16\): reciprocals ✓

  4. Differentiate \(f(x)=x\sqrt x\) with the product rule.

    Show solution

    \(f'(x)=\sqrt x+x\cdot\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}=\sqrt x+\dfrac{\sqrt x}2=\dfrac32\sqrt x\).

  5. \(f(x)=\sqrt{x^2+9}\). Find \(f'(x)\) and the extreme point.

    Show solution

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2+9}}=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\). Negative for \(x\lt 0\), positive for \(x\gt 0\): minimum \((0,3)\).

  6. Solve \(\sqrt{2x+1}=x-1\).

    Show solution

    \(x=0\) or \(x=4\) after squaring; only \(x=4\) checks.

  7. \(f(x)=\sqrt{6-2x}\). Find the domain and show that \(f\) is decreasing. What happens at the end of the domain?

    Show solution

    Domain \(x\le3\). \(f'(x)=-\dfrac1{\sqrt{6-2x}}\lt 0\).

    At \((3,0)\) the derivative is not defined and the tangent is vertical.

  8. At which point of \(y=\sqrt{2x}\) is the slope of the tangent \(\tfrac14\)?

    Show solution

    \(y'=\dfrac{2}{2\sqrt{2x}}=\dfrac1{\sqrt{2x}}=\dfrac14\Rightarrow\sqrt{2x}=4\Rightarrow x=8\). Point \((8,4)\).

  9. Find where the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(x=1\) meets the axes.

    Show solution

    \(y=\tfrac12x+\tfrac12\): x-axis at \((-1,0)\), y-axis at \(\left(0,\tfrac12\right)\).

  10. \(f(x)=\sqrt x+\sqrt{4-x}\). Find the domain and the largest and smallest values.

    Show solution

    Domain \(0\le x\le4\).

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}-\dfrac1{2\sqrt{4-x}}=0\Rightarrow x=4-x\Rightarrow x=2\).

    \(f(2)=2\sqrt2\approx2.83\), \(f(0)=f(4)=2\). Largest \(2\sqrt2\), smallest \(2\).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. \(f(x)=\sqrt x\,(x-3)\).

    1. Find the domain and the roots.
    2. Show that \(f'(x)=\dfrac{3(x-1)}{2\sqrt x}\).
    3. Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the extreme point.
    4. Describe the tangent at the origin.
    Show solution

    (a) Domain \(x\ge0\); roots \(0\) and \(3\).

    (b) \(f(x)=x\sqrt x-3\sqrt x\), \(f'(x)=\dfrac32\sqrt x-\dfrac3{2\sqrt x}=\dfrac{3(x-1)}{2\sqrt x}\).

    (c) Decreasing for \(0\lt x\lt 1\), increasing for \(x\gt 1\). Minimum \((1,-2)\).

    (d) As \(x\to0\), \(f'\to-\infty\): a vertical tangent at the origin.

  2. \(f(x)=\sqrt{x^2-4}\).

    1. Find the domain and the symmetry.
    2. Find \(f'(x)\).
    3. Describe how the function behaves and find its smallest value.
    Show solution

    (a) \(x\le-2\) or \(x\ge2\). Even.

    (b) \(f'(x)=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2-4}}\): negative for \(x\lt -2\), positive for \(x\gt 2\).

    (c) Decreasing on \(x\le-2\), increasing on \(x\ge2\). The smallest value 0 is at the end points \(x=\pm2\), where the tangents are vertical.

  3. Find the point on the graph of \(y=\sqrt x\) that is closest to \(A(2,0)\), and the shortest distance.

    Show solution

    For the point \((x,\sqrt x)\): \(d^2=(x-2)^2+x=x^2-3x+4\).

    \(d^2\) is smallest where \(d\) is smallest: at the vertex \(x=1.5\).

    Point \(\left(1.5,\sqrt{1.5}\right)\); \(d=\sqrt{2.25-4.5+4}=\sqrt{1.75}=\dfrac{\sqrt7}2\).

  4. \(f(x)=x^2+2\) for \(x\ge0\), and \(g=f^{-1}\).

    1. Find \(g(x)\).
    2. Use the rule for inverse functions to find the slope of \(g\) at \(x=11\).
    3. Check by differentiating \(g\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(g(x)=\sqrt{x-2}\), \(x\ge2\).

    (b) \(g(11)=3\) and \(f'(3)=6\), so the slope is \(\tfrac16\).

    (c) \(g'(x)=\dfrac1{2\sqrt{x-2}}\Rightarrow g'(11)=\dfrac1{2\cdot3}=\dfrac16\) ✓

  5. Find the tangent to \(y=\sqrt{x-1}\) that passes through the origin.

    Show solution

    At \(x=a\): \(y=\sqrt{a-1}+\dfrac{x-a}{2\sqrt{a-1}}\).

    Through \((0,0)\): \(\sqrt{a-1}=\dfrac a{2\sqrt{a-1}}\Rightarrow 2(a-1)=a\Rightarrow a=2\).

    Point \((2,1)\), slope \(\tfrac12\): \(y=\tfrac12x\).

  6. For which values of \(k\) does \(\sqrt x=x+k\) have two solutions?

    Show solution

    Let \(t=\sqrt x\ge0\): \(t=t^2+k\Rightarrow t^2-t+k=0\).

    We need two different roots \(t\ge0\): \(\Delta=1-4k\gt 0\Rightarrow k\lt \tfrac14\), and product \(k\ge0\).

    Answer: \(0\le k\lt \tfrac14\). (At \(k=\tfrac14\) the line \(y=x+\tfrac14\) is tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12\right)\).)

  7. The upper half of the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\) is the graph of \(f(x)=\sqrt{25-x^2}\).

    1. Find \(f'(x)\).
    2. Find the tangent at \(x=3\).
    3. Show that this tangent is perpendicular to the radius to the point of tangency.
    Show solution

    (a) \(f'(x)=\dfrac{-2x}{2\sqrt{25-x^2}}=-\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{25-x^2}}\).

    (b) \(f(3)=4\), \(f'(3)=-\tfrac34\): \(y=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac{25}4\).

    (c) The radius to \((3,4)\) has slope \(\tfrac43\), and \(\tfrac43\cdot\left(-\tfrac34\right)=-1\): the tangent is perpendicular to the radius, as in Unit 02.

  8. \(g(x)=2\sqrt x-x\), \(x\ge0\).

    1. Find the extreme point of \(g\).
    2. Use (a) to prove that \(2\sqrt x\le x+1\) for every \(x\ge0\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(g'(x)=\dfrac1{\sqrt x}-1=0\Rightarrow x=1\). Increasing for \(0\lt x\lt 1\), decreasing for \(x\gt 1\): maximum \((1,1)\).

    (b) So \(g(x)\le1\), that is \(2\sqrt x-x\le1\Rightarrow 2\sqrt x\le x+1\), for every \(x\ge0\). Equality only at \(x=1\).

  9. A body moves so that its position after \(t\) seconds is \(s(t)=4\sqrt t\) metres.

    1. Find the velocity at \(t=4\).
    2. Find the average velocity between \(t=0\) and \(t=4\).
    3. Describe how the velocity changes.
    Show solution

    (a) \(v(t)=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt t}\); \(v(4)=1\) m/s.

    (b) \(\dfrac{8-0}{4}=2\) m/s.

    (c) \(v\) decreases as \(t\) grows: the body keeps moving forward but slows down.

  10. \(f(x)=\sqrt x-ax\), where \(a\gt 0\).

    1. Find the extreme point in terms of \(a\).
    2. Find \(a\) if the largest value of \(f\) is 1.
    Show solution

    (a) \(f'(x)=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}-a=0\Rightarrow\sqrt x=\dfrac1{2a}\Rightarrow x=\dfrac1{4a^2}\).

    The value there: \(\dfrac1{2a}-a\cdot\dfrac1{4a^2}=\dfrac1{4a}\). It is a maximum (\(f\) increases before and decreases after).

    (b) \(\dfrac1{4a}=1\Rightarrow a=\tfrac14\).
