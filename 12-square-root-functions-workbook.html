Before you start: Write the domain at the top of every question. At the end, check that each answer lies in the domain, and check solutions of equations by substituting them.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the slope of the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(x=4\) and at \(x=9\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute into \(y'=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}\).

    \(x=4\): \(\dfrac1{2\cdot2}=\tfrac14\).

    \(x=9\): \(\dfrac1{2\cdot3}=\tfrac16\).

    Answer: \(\tfrac14\) and \(\tfrac16\). The graph becomes flatter as \(x\) grows.

  2. Differentiate.

    1. \(3\sqrt x\)
    2. \(\sqrt x+x^2\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Constant factor stays; sum rule.

    (a) \(3\cdot\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}=\dfrac3{2\sqrt x}\).

    (b) \(\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}+2x\).

    Answer: (a) \(\dfrac3{2\sqrt x}\); (b) \(\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}+2x\).

  3. Find the domain.

    1. \(\sqrt{2x-6}\)
    2. \(\sqrt{5-x}\)
    Show solution

    Idea. The expression under the root must be \(\ge0\).

    (a) \(2x-6\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge3\).

    (b) \(5-x\ge0\Rightarrow x\le5\).

    Answer: (a) \(x\ge3\); (b) \(x\le5\).

  4. Differentiate \(\sqrt{3x+1}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule: \(\dfrac{u'}{2\sqrt u}\) with \(u=3x+1\), \(u'=3\).

    Answer: \(\dfrac3{2\sqrt{3x+1}}\).

  5. Differentiate \(\sqrt{4-x}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule with \(u=4-x\), \(u'=-1\).

    Answer: \(-\dfrac1{2\sqrt{4-x}}\).

  6. Find the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(x=4\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Point and slope.

    Point: \(\sqrt4=2\), so \((4,2)\). Slope: \(\tfrac14\).

    \(y-2=\tfrac14(x-4)\Rightarrow y=\tfrac14x-1+2\).

    Answer: \(y=\tfrac14x+1\).

  7. At which point is the slope of the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) equal to \(\tfrac12\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Set the derivative equal to \(\tfrac12\) and solve for \(x\).

    \(\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}=\dfrac12\Rightarrow2\sqrt x=2\Rightarrow\sqrt x=1\Rightarrow x=1\).

    Answer: \((1,1)\).

  8. Solve \(\sqrt{x+3}=5\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Square both sides; check the answer.

    \(x+3=25\Rightarrow x=22\).

    Check: \(\sqrt{25}=5\) ✓

    Answer: \(x=22\).

  9. \(f(x)=2\sqrt x\). Find \(f(9)\) and \(f'(9)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute for \(f(9)\); for \(f'\) the constant 2 cancels the 2 in the denominator.

    \(f(9)=2\cdot3=6\).

    \(f'(x)=2\cdot\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}=\dfrac1{\sqrt x}\Rightarrow f'(9)=\tfrac13\).

    Answer: \(f(9)=6\), \(f'(9)=\tfrac13\).

  10. Explain why \(\sqrt x\) is increasing on its whole domain.

    Show solution

    Idea. A function increases where its derivative is positive.

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}\): numerator 1 and denominator positive for \(x\gt 0\), so \(f'\gt 0\).

    At the end point \(x=0\), \(f(0)=0\) and every other value is positive.

    Answer: \(f'\gt 0\) on \(x\gt 0\), so \(\sqrt x\) is increasing on its whole domain \(x\ge0\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Investigate \(f(x)=x-4\sqrt x\): domain, roots, intervals of increase and decrease, extreme points.

    Show solution

    Idea. Domain from the root; roots by taking out \(\sqrt x\); \(f'\) and its sign.

    Domain: \(x\ge0\).

    Roots: \(x-4\sqrt x=\sqrt x(\sqrt x-4)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(\sqrt x=4\Rightarrow x=16\).

    \(f'(x)=1-4\cdot\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}=1-\dfrac2{\sqrt x}=0\Rightarrow\sqrt x=2\Rightarrow x=4\).

    \(0\lt x\lt 4\): \(\tfrac2{\sqrt x}\gt 1\), so \(f'\lt 0\). \(x\gt 4\): \(f'\gt 0\).

    \(f(4)=4-8=-4\).

    Answer: domain \(x\ge0\); roots 0 and 16; decreasing on \((0,4)\), increasing for \(x\gt 4\); minimum \((4,-4)\); the graph starts at \((0,0)\).

  2. Find the tangent to \(y=\sqrt{x+5}\) at \(x=-1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Point and slope, using the chain rule (inner derivative 1).

    Point: \(y(-1)=\sqrt4=2\), so \((-1,2)\).

    Slope: \(y'=\dfrac1{2\sqrt{x+5}}\Rightarrow y'(-1)=\tfrac14\).

    \(y-2=\tfrac14(x+1)\Rightarrow y=\tfrac14x+\tfrac14+2\).

    Answer: \(y=\tfrac14x+\tfrac94\).

  3. Check the rule for inverse functions: find the slope of \(x^2\) at \((3,9)\) and the slope of \(\sqrt x\) at \((9,3)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The graphs of a function and its inverse are mirror images in \(y=x\); at matching points their slopes are reciprocals.

    \((x^2)'=2x\Rightarrow\) slope at \(x=3\) is 6.

    \((\sqrt x)'=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}\Rightarrow\) slope at \(x=9\) is \(\dfrac1{2\cdot3}=\tfrac16\).

    Answer: 6 and \(\tfrac16\); \(6\cdot\tfrac16=1\), they are reciprocals ✓

  4. Differentiate \(f(x)=x\sqrt x\) with the product rule.

    Show solution

    Idea. Product rule with \(u=x\), \(v=\sqrt x\); then use \(\dfrac x{\sqrt x}=\sqrt x\).

    \(f'(x)=1\cdot\sqrt x+x\cdot\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}=\sqrt x+\dfrac{\sqrt x}2\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=\tfrac32\sqrt x\).

    Check: \(x\sqrt x=x^{3/2}\), and \(\left(x^{3/2}\right)'=\tfrac32x^{1/2}\) ✓

  5. \(f(x)=\sqrt{x^2+9}\). Find \(f'(x)\) and the extreme point.

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule with \(u=x^2+9\), \(u'=2x\); then the sign of \(f'\). The denominator is always positive.

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2+9}}=\dfrac x{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\).

    The sign is the sign of \(x\): \(-\) for \(x\lt 0\), \(+\) for \(x\gt 0\).

    \(f(0)=\sqrt9=3\).

    Answer: \(f'(x)=\dfrac x{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\); minimum \((0,3)\).

  6. Solve \(\sqrt{2x+1}=x-1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The right side must be \(\ge0\) (a root is never negative). Square, solve, and check.

    \(2x+1=(x-1)^2=x^2-2x+1\Rightarrow x^2-4x=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=4\).

    \(x=0\): \(\sqrt1=1\) but \(0-1=-1\) ✗.

    \(x=4\): \(\sqrt9=3\) and \(4-1=3\) ✓.

    Answer: \(x=4\).

  7. \(f(x)=\sqrt{6-2x}\). Find the domain and show that \(f\) is decreasing. What happens at the end of the domain?

    Show solution

    Idea. Domain: \(6-2x\ge0\). Derivative by the chain rule; its sign shows the direction.

    \(6-2x\ge0\Rightarrow x\le3\).

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{-2}{2\sqrt{6-2x}}=-\dfrac1{\sqrt{6-2x}}\lt 0\) for \(x\lt 3\).

    At \(x=3\): \(f(3)=0\) and the denominator of \(f'\) is 0.

    Answer: domain \(x\le3\); \(f\) is decreasing; at the end point \((3,0)\) there is no derivative, and the tangent is vertical.

  8. At which point of \(y=\sqrt{2x}\) is the slope of the tangent \(\tfrac14\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Chain rule, then solve \(y'=\tfrac14\).

    \(y'=\dfrac2{2\sqrt{2x}}=\dfrac1{\sqrt{2x}}\).

    \(\dfrac1{\sqrt{2x}}=\dfrac14\Rightarrow\sqrt{2x}=4\Rightarrow2x=16\Rightarrow x=8\).

    \(y(8)=\sqrt{16}=4\).

    Answer: \((8,4)\).

  9. Find where the tangent to \(y=\sqrt x\) at \(x=1\) meets the axes.

    Show solution

    Idea. Find the tangent (point and slope), then set \(y=0\) and \(x=0\).

    Tangent at \((1,1)\) with slope \(\tfrac12\): \(y=\tfrac12x+\tfrac12\).

    \(y=0\Rightarrow x=-1\). \(x=0\Rightarrow y=\tfrac12\).

    Answer: \((-1,0)\) and \(\left(0,\tfrac12\right)\).

  10. \(f(x)=\sqrt x+\sqrt{4-x}\). Find the domain and the largest and smallest values.

    Show solution

    Idea. Domain: both roots defined. On a closed interval compare the critical points and the end points.

    \(x\ge0\) and \(4-x\ge0\Rightarrow0\le x\le4\).

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}-\dfrac1{2\sqrt{4-x}}=0\Rightarrow\sqrt x=\sqrt{4-x}\Rightarrow x=4-x\Rightarrow x=2\).

    \(f(2)=\sqrt2+\sqrt2=2\sqrt2\approx2.83\). \(f(0)=0+2=2\); \(f(4)=2+0=2\).

    Answer: domain \([0,4]\); largest value \(2\sqrt2\) (at \(x=2\)), smallest value 2 (at \(x=0\) and \(x=4\)).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. \(f(x)=\sqrt x\,(x-3)\).

    1. Find the domain and the roots.
    2. Show that \(f'(x)=\dfrac{3(x-1)}{2\sqrt x}\).
    3. Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the extreme point.
    4. Describe the tangent at the origin.
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Domain from the root; roots from each factor. (b) Expand and differentiate, then use a common denominator. (c) Sign of \(f'\). (d) Behaviour of \(f'\) near 0.

    (a) Domain \(x\ge0\). \(\sqrt x=0\Rightarrow x=0\); \(x-3=0\Rightarrow x=3\).

    (b) \(f(x)=x\sqrt x-3\sqrt x\). \(f'(x)=\tfrac32\sqrt x-\dfrac3{2\sqrt x}=\dfrac{3x-3}{2\sqrt x}=\dfrac{3(x-1)}{2\sqrt x}\).

    (c) The denominator is positive, so the sign is the sign of \(x-1\). \(f(1)=1\cdot(-2)=-2\).

    (d) As \(x\to0^+\), the numerator \(\to-3\) and the denominator \(\to0^+\): \(f'\to-\infty\).

    Answer: (a) \(x\ge0\); roots 0, 3; (b) shown; (c) decreasing on \((0,1)\), increasing for \(x\gt 1\); minimum \((1,-2)\); (d) a vertical tangent at the origin.

  2. \(f(x)=\sqrt{x^2-4}\).

    1. Find the domain and the symmetry.
    2. Find \(f'(x)\).
    3. Describe how the function behaves and find its smallest value.
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) \(x^2-4\ge0\); compare \(f(-x)\) with \(f(x)\). (b) Chain rule. (c) Signs of \(f'\) on each part of the domain.

    (a) \(x^2\ge4\Rightarrow x\le-2\) or \(x\ge2\). \(f(-x)=\sqrt{x^2-4}=f(x)\): even.

    (b) \(f'(x)=\dfrac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2-4}}=\dfrac x{\sqrt{x^2-4}}\) (for \(|x|\gt 2\)).

    (c) For \(x\lt -2\): \(f'\lt 0\), decreasing. For \(x\gt 2\): \(f'\gt 0\), increasing.

    \(f(\pm2)=0\), and \(f'\) is undefined there.

    Answer: (a) \(|x|\ge2\), even; (b) \(\dfrac x{\sqrt{x^2-4}}\); (c) the smallest value is 0, at \(x=\pm2\), where the tangents are vertical.

  3. Find the point on the graph of \(y=\sqrt x\) that is closest to \(A(2,0)\), and the shortest distance.

    Show solution

    Idea. Write the distance from a general point \((x,\sqrt x)\) to \(A\). Minimising \(d^2\) is easier (no root) and gives the same point.

    \(d^2=(x-2)^2+(\sqrt x-0)^2=x^2-4x+4+x=x^2-3x+4\).

    This parabola has its vertex at \(x=\dfrac32\) (or: \((d^2)'=2x-3=0\)).

    \(d^2=2.25-4.5+4=1.75=\dfrac74\Rightarrow d=\dfrac{\sqrt7}2\approx1.32\).

    Answer: the point \(\left(1.5,\sqrt{1.5}\right)\approx(1.5,\ 1.22)\); the shortest distance is \(\dfrac{\sqrt7}2\).

  4. \(f(x)=x^2+2\) for \(x\ge0\), and \(g=f^{-1}\).

    1. Find \(g(x)\).
    2. Use the rule for inverse functions to find the slope of \(g\) at \(x=11\).
    3. Check by differentiating \(g\).
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Swap \(x\) and \(y\) and solve. (b) Slope of the inverse \(=\dfrac1{f'(\text{matching }x)}\). (c) Differentiate directly.

    (a) \(x=y^2+2\Rightarrow y^2=x-2\Rightarrow y=\sqrt{x-2}\) (positive, since \(y\ge0\)). Domain \(x\ge2\).

    (b) \(g(11)=\sqrt9=3\), so the matching point of \(f\) is \(x=3\). \(f'(x)=2x\Rightarrow f'(3)=6\). Slope of \(g\): \(\tfrac16\).

    (c) \(g'(x)=\dfrac1{2\sqrt{x-2}}\Rightarrow g'(11)=\dfrac1{2\cdot3}=\tfrac16\) ✓

    Answer: (a) \(g(x)=\sqrt{x-2}\); (b) \(\tfrac16\); (c) same result.

  5. Find the tangent to \(y=\sqrt{x-1}\) that passes through the origin.

    Show solution

    Idea. Write the tangent at an unknown point \(x=a\), then demand that it pass through \((0,0)\).

    Slope at \(a\): \(\dfrac1{2\sqrt{a-1}}\). Tangent: \(y=\sqrt{a-1}+\dfrac{x-a}{2\sqrt{a-1}}\).

    \(x=0\), \(y=0\): \(\sqrt{a-1}=\dfrac a{2\sqrt{a-1}}\).

    Multiply by \(2\sqrt{a-1}\): \(2(a-1)=a\Rightarrow a=2\).

    Point \((2,1)\), slope \(\tfrac12\).

    Answer: \(y=\tfrac12x\).

  6. For which values of \(k\) does \(\sqrt x=x+k\) have two solutions?

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute \(t=\sqrt x\). Each solution \(t\ge0\) gives exactly one \(x=t^2\), so we need two different non-negative roots \(t\).

    \(t=t^2+k\Rightarrow t^2-t+k=0\).

    Two different roots: \(\Delta=1-4k\gt 0\Rightarrow k\lt \tfrac14\).

    Both roots \(\ge0\): their sum is \(1\gt 0\), and their product \(k\ge0\).

    Answer: \(0\le k\lt \tfrac14\). At \(k=\tfrac14\) the line is tangent to the graph at \(\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12\right)\); for \(k\lt 0\) one root \(t\) is negative and only one solution remains.

  7. The upper half of the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\) is the graph of \(f(x)=\sqrt{25-x^2}\).

    1. Find \(f'(x)\).
    2. Find the tangent at \(x=3\).
    3. Show that this tangent is perpendicular to the radius to the point of tangency.
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Chain rule. (b) Point and slope. (c) Perpendicular lines: the product of the slopes is \(-1\).

    (a) \(f'(x)=\dfrac{-2x}{2\sqrt{25-x^2}}=-\dfrac x{\sqrt{25-x^2}}\).

    (b) \(f(3)=\sqrt{16}=4\). \(f'(3)=-\tfrac34\). \(y-4=-\tfrac34(x-3)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac94+4=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac{25}4\).

    (c) The radius from \((0,0)\) to \((3,4)\) has slope \(\tfrac43\). \(\tfrac43\cdot\left(-\tfrac34\right)=-1\).

    Answer: (a) \(-\dfrac x{\sqrt{25-x^2}}\); (b) \(y=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac{25}4\); (c) perpendicular, as the circle theorem says.

  8. \(g(x)=2\sqrt x-x\), \(x\ge0\).

    1. Find the extreme point of \(g\).
    2. Use (a) to prove that \(2\sqrt x\le x+1\) for every \(x\ge0\).
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Derivative and its sign. (b) A maximum value bounds every value of the function.

    (a) \(g'(x)=\dfrac{2}{2\sqrt x}-1=\dfrac1{\sqrt x}-1=0\Rightarrow\sqrt x=1\Rightarrow x=1\).

    \(0\lt x\lt 1\): \(\tfrac1{\sqrt x}\gt 1\), \(g'\gt 0\). \(x\gt 1\): \(g'\lt 0\). \(g(1)=2-1=1\).

    (b) The maximum value is 1, so \(g(x)\le1\) for all \(x\ge0\).

    \(2\sqrt x-x\le1\Rightarrow2\sqrt x\le x+1\).

    Answer: (a) maximum \((1,1)\); (b) proved, with equality only at \(x=1\).

    Check: \(x+1-2\sqrt x=(\sqrt x-1)^2\ge0\) ✓

  9. A body moves so that its position after \(t\) seconds is \(s(t)=4\sqrt t\) metres.

    1. Find the velocity at \(t=4\).
    2. Find the average velocity between \(t=0\) and \(t=4\).
    3. Describe how the velocity changes.
    Show solution

    Idea. Velocity is the derivative of position. Average velocity is the change in position over the time.

    (a) \(v(t)=4\cdot\dfrac1{2\sqrt t}=\dfrac2{\sqrt t}\). \(v(4)=\dfrac22=1\) m/s.

    (b) \(s(4)=8\), \(s(0)=0\): \(\dfrac{8-0}4=2\) m/s.

    (c) \(\dfrac2{\sqrt t}\) is positive and gets smaller as \(t\) grows.

    Answer: (a) 1 m/s; (b) 2 m/s; (c) the body always moves forward but keeps slowing down.

  10. \(f(x)=\sqrt x-ax\), where \(a\gt 0\).

    1. Find the extreme point in terms of \(a\).
    2. Find \(a\) if the largest value of \(f\) is 1.
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Solve \(f'=0\) in terms of \(a\) and compute the value. (b) Set that value equal to 1.

    (a) \(f'(x)=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}-a=0\Rightarrow\sqrt x=\dfrac1{2a}\Rightarrow x=\dfrac1{4a^2}\).

    \(f\left(\tfrac1{4a^2}\right)=\dfrac1{2a}-a\cdot\dfrac1{4a^2}=\dfrac1{2a}-\dfrac1{4a}=\dfrac1{4a}\).

    For smaller \(x\), \(\tfrac1{2\sqrt x}\gt a\), so \(f'\gt 0\); for larger \(x\), \(f'\lt 0\): a maximum.

    (b) \(\dfrac1{4a}=1\Rightarrow a=\tfrac14\).

    Answer: (a) maximum \(\left(\dfrac1{4a^2},\ \dfrac1{4a}\right)\); (b) \(a=\tfrac14\).
