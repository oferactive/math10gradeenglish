Optimization: Maximum and Minimum Problems

Word problems where something must be as large or as small as possible: a method in six steps, problems from geometry, analytic geometry, motion and economics, and when the answer is at the end of the interval. Practice for this unit is in the workbook.

1. The method

In an optimization problem we want the largest or smallest possible value of some quantity (an area, a cost, a distance). The method is always the same:

  1. Draw a sketch and name the quantities.
  2. Choose one variable \(x\) and write the quantity to optimise as a function of \(x\) only. Use the conditions of the problem to remove other variables.
  3. State the domain: which values of \(x\) make sense? (lengths are positive, and so on).
  4. Differentiate and solve \(f'(x)=0\).
  5. Check that it is really a maximum or a minimum (sign table of \(f'\)), and compare with the ends of the domain if they are included.
  6. Answer the question that was asked: the dimensions, the value, or both, with units.

2. Geometry problems

Example 1

A farmer has 60 m of fence to enclose a rectangle along a wall (the wall is one side and needs no fence). Find the largest possible area.

Solution.

Let \(y\) be the side parallel to the wall and \(x\) each of the other two sides. Then \(2x+y=60\), so \(y=60-2x\).

Area: \(A(x)=x(60-2x)=60x-2x^2\), with \(0\lt x\lt 30\).

\(A'(x)=60-4x=0\Rightarrow x=15\). \(A'\) changes from + to −: a maximum.

Answer: sides 15 m and 30 m, largest area \(450\) m².

Example 2

An open box is made from a square sheet of side 12 cm by cutting squares of side \(x\) from the corners (Unit 03). Find the largest volume.

Solution.

\(V(x)=x(12-2x)^2\), domain \(0\lt x\lt 6\).

\(V'(x)=(12-2x)^2+x\cdot2(12-2x)(-2)=(12-2x)(12-2x-4x)=(12-2x)(12-6x)\).

In the domain, \(V'=0\) only at \(x=2\); \(V'\) changes from + to −.

Answer: \(x=2\) cm, \(V=2\cdot8^2=128\) cm³.

Example 3

An open box has a square base and a volume of 32 cm³. Find the dimensions that use the least material.

Solution.

Base side \(a\), height \(h\): \(a^2h=32\Rightarrow h=\dfrac{32}{a^2}\).

Surface (base + 4 sides): \(S(a)=a^2+4ah=a^2+\dfrac{128}a\), \(a\gt 0\).

\(S'(a)=2a-\dfrac{128}{a^2}=0\Rightarrow a^3=64\Rightarrow a=4\); \(S'\) changes from − to +: a minimum.

Answer: base \(4\times4\) cm, height \(2\) cm, area \(48\) cm².

3. Distance problems

To find the point of a graph closest to a given point, minimise the square of the distance, \(d^2\). It is smallest exactly where \(d\) is smallest, and it has no square root, so it is easier to differentiate.

Example 4

Find the points of the parabola \(y=x^2\) closest to \(A(0,3)\).

Solution.

For \((x,x^2)\): \(D(x)=d^2=x^2+(x^2-3)^2=x^4-5x^2+9\).

\(D'(x)=4x^3-10x=2x(2x^2-5)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=\pm\sqrt{2.5}\).

Sign table: minimum at \(x=\pm\sqrt{2.5}\), maximum at \(x=0\).

\(D=6.25-12.5+9=2.75\Rightarrow d=\sqrt{2.75}=\dfrac{\sqrt{11}}2\approx1.66\). The points are \(\left(\pm\sqrt{2.5},\ 2.5\right)\).

4. Motion and economics

Example 5

A shop sells \(100-2p\) items per day when the price is \(p\) shekels. Which price gives the largest income?

Solution.

Income \(R(p)=p(100-2p)=100p-2p^2\), \(0\lt p\lt 50\).

\(R'(p)=100-4p=0\Rightarrow p=25\). Income \(R=25\cdot50=1250\) shekels.

Example 6

A car drives 100 km. The cost per hour at speed \(v\) km/h is \(20+\dfrac{v^2}{50}\) shekels. Which speed makes the trip cheapest?

Solution.

Time \(\dfrac{100}v\) hours. Cost \(C(v)=\dfrac{100}v\left(20+\dfrac{v^2}{50}\right)=\dfrac{2000}v+2v\).

\(C'(v)=-\dfrac{2000}{v^2}+2=0\Rightarrow v^2=1000\Rightarrow v\approx31.6\) km/h.

Cost \(\approx63.2+63.2=126.5\) shekels.

5. When the answer is at the end of the domain

Sometimes \(f'(x)=0\) gives the opposite of what we want, and the answer is at an end of the domain.

Example 7

A wire of 20 cm is cut into two pieces, and each piece is bent into a square. How should it be cut to make the total area smallest? Largest?

Solution.

Pieces \(x\) and \(20-x\) (\(0\le x\le20\)). Area \(A(x)=\left(\dfrac x4\right)^2+\left(\dfrac{20-x}4\right)^2\).

\(A'(x)=\dfrac x8-\dfrac{20-x}8=0\Rightarrow x=10\): a minimum, \(A=12.5\) cm².

The largest value is at the ends: \(A(0)=A(20)=25\) cm². So the largest area is when the wire is not cut at all (one square).

Note: some problems can be solved without derivatives. A quadratic has its extreme value at its vertex \(x=-\tfrac{b}{2a}\), and symmetry often points to the answer (as in Example 7).

6. Common mistakes

  • Writing the quantity as a function of two variables and differentiating it as if one of them were a constant. First use the condition to remove one variable.
  • Forgetting the domain, and giving an answer such as a negative length.
  • Stopping after solving \(f'(x)=0\) without checking whether it is a maximum or a minimum.
  • Answering with \(x\) only, when the question asked for the dimensions or for the largest value.
  • In distance problems, differentiating \(d\) with its square root instead of the simpler \(d^2\).

7. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Two numbers have sum 16. What is their largest possible product?

    Show solution

    \(x(16-x)\), maximum at \(x=8\): \(64\).

  2. A rectangle has perimeter 24. What is its largest possible area?

    Show solution

    \(x(12-x)\), maximum at \(x=6\): a square, area \(36\).

  3. Find the smallest value of \(x+\dfrac9x\) for \(x\gt 0\).

    Show solution

    \(1-\dfrac9{x^2}=0\Rightarrow x=3\). Value \(6\).

  4. Find the largest value of \(6x-x^2\).

    Show solution

    At \(x=3\): \(9\).

  5. Find the largest value of \(x^3-3x\) on \([0,3]\).

    Show solution

    \(f(0)=0\), \(f(1)=-2\), \(f(3)=18\). Largest \(18\), at the end \(x=3\).

  6. Why must we check the ends of the domain?

    Show solution

    The largest or smallest value may be at an end, where the derivative need not be 0.

  7. Find the point of the line \(y=x\) closest to \((4,0)\).

    Show solution

    \(d^2=(x-4)^2+x^2=2x^2-8x+16\), smallest at \(x=2\). Point \((2,2)\).

  8. A rectangle has area 16. What is its smallest possible perimeter?

    Show solution

    \(P=2\left(x+\dfrac{16}x\right)\), \(P'=0\Rightarrow x=4\). A \(4\times4\) square, \(P=16\).
