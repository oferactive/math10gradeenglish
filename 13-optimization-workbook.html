Before you start: Follow the six steps every time, even when the problem looks easy. Most mistakes come from skipping the domain or not answering the actual question.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Two numbers have sum 20. Find them if their product is as large as possible.

    Show solution

    Idea. Call the numbers \(x\) and \(20-x\), and maximise their product.

    \(f(x)=x(20-x)=20x-x^2\).

    \(f'(x)=20-2x=0\Rightarrow x=10\); \(+\) then \(-\): a maximum.

    \(f(10)=100\).

    Answer: both numbers are 10; the product is 100.

  2. A rectangle has perimeter 40 cm. Find its largest possible area.

    Show solution

    Idea. Perimeter 40 means the two sides add up to 20.

    Sides \(x\) and \(20-x\): \(A=x(20-x)=20x-x^2\), \(0\lt x\lt 20\).

    \(A'=20-2x=0\Rightarrow x=10\).

    \(A=10\cdot10=100\).

    Answer: 100 cm² (a square of side 10 cm).

  3. A rectangle has area 36 cm². Find the smallest possible perimeter.

    Show solution

    Idea. From the area, the other side is \(\tfrac{36}x\). Minimise the perimeter.

    \(P(x)=2\left(x+\dfrac{36}x\right)\), \(x\gt 0\).

    \(P'(x)=2\left(1-\dfrac{36}{x^2}\right)=0\Rightarrow x^2=36\Rightarrow x=6\).

    \(-\) then \(+\): a minimum. Other side \(=6\).

    \(P=2(6+6)=24\).

    Answer: 24 cm (a \(6\times6\) square).

  4. For which \(x\gt 0\) is \(x+\dfrac1x\) smallest? What is the smallest value?

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate, solve on \(x\gt 0\), and check the signs.

    \(f'(x)=1-\dfrac1{x^2}=0\Rightarrow x=1\).

    \(-\) for \(0\lt x\lt 1\), \(+\) for \(x\gt 1\): a minimum.

    \(f(1)=1+1=2\).

    Answer: at \(x=1\); the smallest value is 2.

  5. Find the largest and smallest values of \(f(x)=-x^2+8x\) on \([0,10]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Closed interval: check the vertex and both ends.

    \(f'(x)=-2x+8=0\Rightarrow x=4\).

    \(f(0)=0\), \(f(4)=-16+32=16\), \(f(10)=-100+80=-20\).

    Answer: largest 16 (at \(x=4\)), smallest \(-20\) (at \(x=10\)).

  6. \(x\) and \(y\) are non-negative and \(x+y=12\). Find the largest value of \(x^2y\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute \(y=12-x\) to get one variable on the closed interval \([0,12]\).

    \(f(x)=x^2(12-x)=12x^2-x^3\).

    \(f'(x)=24x-3x^2=3x(8-x)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=8\).

    \(f(8)=64\cdot4=256\); \(f(0)=f(12)=0\).

    Answer: 256 (at \(x=8\), \(y=4\)).

  7. 60 m of fence encloses a rectangle along a wall (three sides fenced). Find the largest area.

    Show solution

    Idea. Let \(x\) be each of the two sides perpendicular to the wall; the side parallel to the wall is \(60-2x\).

    \(A(x)=x(60-2x)=60x-2x^2\), \(0\lt x\lt 30\).

    \(A'(x)=60-4x=0\Rightarrow x=15\).

    The other side: \(60-30=30\). \(A=15\cdot30=450\).

    Answer: 450 m² (15 m by 30 m, with the 30 m side parallel to the wall).

  8. Find the point of the line \(y=-x+4\) closest to the origin.

    Show solution

    Idea. A point on the line is \((x,\ 4-x)\). Minimise the squared distance to the origin.

    \(d^2=x^2+(4-x)^2=2x^2-8x+16\).

    \((d^2)'=4x-8=0\Rightarrow x=2\), \(y=2\).

    \(d^2=8\Rightarrow d=2\sqrt2\).

    Answer: \((2,2)\), at distance \(2\sqrt2\).

  9. \(x+y=10\). Find the smallest value of \(x^2+y^2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Substitute \(y=10-x\) and minimise a quadratic.

    \(S(x)=x^2+(10-x)^2=2x^2-20x+100\).

    \(S'(x)=4x-20=0\Rightarrow x=5\), \(y=5\).

    \(S=25+25=50\).

    Answer: 50.

  10. Two numbers differ by 8. Find them if their product is as small as possible.

    Show solution

    Idea. Call the numbers \(x\) and \(x+8\). The product can be negative, so its smallest value is below 0.

    \(P(x)=x(x+8)=x^2+8x\).

    \(P'(x)=2x+8=0\Rightarrow x=-4\); the other number is 4.

    \(P=-16\).

    Answer: \(-4\) and 4; product \(-16\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. An open box is made from a square sheet of side 12 cm by cutting squares of side \(x\) from the corners. Find the largest volume.

    Show solution

    Idea. After cutting, the base is \((12-2x)\times(12-2x)\) and the height is \(x\), with \(0\lt x\lt 6\).

    \(V(x)=x(12-2x)^2\).

    Product rule: \(V'(x)=(12-2x)^2+x\cdot2(12-2x)(-2)=(12-2x)(12-2x-4x)=(12-2x)(12-6x)\).

    \(V'=0\Rightarrow x=6\) (no box) or \(x=2\).

    \(+\) before 2, \(-\) after: a maximum. \(V(2)=2\cdot8^2=128\).

    Answer: cut squares of side 2 cm; the largest volume is 128 cm³.

  2. An open box with a square base must hold 32 cm³. Find the dimensions that use the least material.

    Show solution

    Idea. Base side \(a\), height \(h\). The volume condition gives \(h\); the material is the base plus 4 sides (no top).

    \(a^2h=32\Rightarrow h=\dfrac{32}{a^2}\).

    \(S=a^2+4ah=a^2+\dfrac{128}a\).

    \(S'(a)=2a-\dfrac{128}{a^2}=0\Rightarrow a^3=64\Rightarrow a=4\); \(-\) then \(+\): a minimum.

    \(h=\dfrac{32}{16}=2\); \(S=16+32=48\).

    Answer: base \(4\times4\) cm, height 2 cm; 48 cm² of material.

  3. Find the points of the parabola \(y=x^2\) closest to \((0,3)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. A point on the parabola is \((x,x^2)\). Minimise \(D=d^2\).

    \(D=x^2+(x^2-3)^2=x^2+x^4-6x^2+9=x^4-5x^2+9\).

    \(D'=4x^3-10x=2x(2x^2-5)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x^2=2.5\).

    \(D(0)=9\); \(D\) at \(x^2=2.5\): \(6.25-12.5+9=2.75\). The second is smaller (\(x=0\) is a local maximum of \(D\)).

    \(d=\sqrt{2.75}=\dfrac{\sqrt{11}}2\).

    Answer: \(\left(\pm\sqrt{2.5},\ 2.5\right)\), at distance \(\dfrac{\sqrt{11}}2\approx1.66\).

  4. A rectangle has its base on the x-axis and its two upper vertices on the parabola \(y=9-x^2\). Find its largest area.

    Show solution

    Idea. By symmetry the upper vertices are \((\pm x,\ 9-x^2)\): the width is \(2x\), the height is \(9-x^2\).

    \(A(x)=2x(9-x^2)=18x-2x^3\), \(0\lt x\lt 3\).

    \(A'(x)=18-6x^2=0\Rightarrow x^2=3\Rightarrow x=\sqrt3\).

    Height \(9-3=6\). \(A=2\sqrt3\cdot6=12\sqrt3\).

    Answer: \(12\sqrt3\approx20.8\).

  5. A shop sells \(100-2p\) items per day at a price of \(p\) shekels. Find the price that gives the largest income.

    Show solution

    Idea. Income = price × number sold.

    \(R(p)=p(100-2p)=100p-2p^2\).

    \(R'(p)=100-4p=0\Rightarrow p=25\).

    \(R(25)=25\cdot50=1250\).

    Answer: 25 shekels per item, income 1250 shekels per day.

  6. A rectangle has one vertex at the origin, two sides on the axes, and the opposite vertex on the line \(y=6-1.5x\). Find its largest area.

    Show solution

    Idea. The opposite vertex is \((x,\ 6-1.5x)\); the sides are \(x\) and \(6-1.5x\).

    \(A(x)=x(6-1.5x)=6x-1.5x^2\), \(0\lt x\lt 4\).

    \(A'(x)=6-3x=0\Rightarrow x=2\), \(y=6-3=3\).

    \(A=2\cdot3=6\).

    Answer: 6 (sides 2 and 3).

  7. A closed cylindrical can holds \(16\pi\) cm³. Find the radius and height that use the least metal.

    Show solution

    Idea. Use the volume to express \(h\) through \(r\), then minimise the surface area (two discs plus the side).

    \(\pi r^2h=16\pi\Rightarrow h=\dfrac{16}{r^2}\).

    \(S=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh=2\pi r^2+\dfrac{32\pi}r\).

    \(S'=4\pi r-\dfrac{32\pi}{r^2}=0\Rightarrow r^3=8\Rightarrow r=2\); \(-\) then \(+\): a minimum.

    \(h=\dfrac{16}4=4\).

    Answer: \(r=2\) cm, \(h=4\) cm (the height equals the diameter).

  8. The legs of a right triangle add up to 10 cm. Find the largest possible area.

    Show solution

    Idea. Legs \(x\) and \(10-x\). The area of a right triangle is half the product of the legs.

    \(A(x)=\dfrac{x(10-x)}2=5x-\dfrac{x^2}2\).

    \(A'=5-x=0\Rightarrow x=5\).

    \(A=\dfrac{25}2=12.5\).

    Answer: 12.5 cm² (both legs 5 cm).

  9. Find the largest and smallest values of \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+9x\) on \([0,5]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Closed interval: critical points and ends.

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-12x+9=3(x-1)(x-3)\).

    \(f(0)=0\), \(f(1)=1-6+9=4\), \(f(3)=27-54+27=0\), \(f(5)=125-150+45=20\).

    Answer: largest 20 (at the end \(x=5\)), smallest 0 (at \(x=0\) and \(x=3\)).

  10. Ship A starts at the origin and sails north at 3 km/h. Ship B starts at \((10,0)\) and sails west at 4 km/h. When are they closest, and how close?

    Show solution

    Idea. Write both positions in terms of \(t\), then minimise the squared distance between them.

    After \(t\) hours: \(A=(0,3t)\), \(B=(10-4t,\ 0)\).

    \(D=d^2=(10-4t)^2+(3t)^2\).

    \(D'=2(10-4t)(-4)+18t=-80+32t+18t=50t-80=0\Rightarrow t=1.6\).

    At \(t=1.6\): \(10-6.4=3.6\) and \(3\cdot1.6=4.8\). \(D=12.96+23.04=36\).

    Answer: after 1.6 hours (1 h 36 min), at a distance of 6 km.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. A cylinder stands inside a cone of radius 6 and height 12, on the same base. Find the cylinder of largest volume.

    Show solution

    Idea. Look at a vertical cross-section: similar triangles connect the cylinder's radius and height.

    At height \(h\) the cone's radius is \(6\cdot\dfrac{12-h}{12}\). Setting this equal to \(r\): \(h=12-2r\).

    \(V(r)=\pi r^2(12-2r)=\pi(12r^2-2r^3)\), \(0\lt r\lt 6\).

    \(V'=\pi(24r-6r^2)=6\pi r(4-r)=0\Rightarrow r=4\).

    \(h=12-8=4\); \(V=\pi\cdot16\cdot4=64\pi\).

    Answer: \(r=4\), \(h=4\), \(V=64\pi\approx201\).

  2. A rectangle is inscribed in a half-circle of radius 5, with one side on the diameter. Find the largest area.

    Show solution

    Idea. Put the centre at the origin. The upper vertices \((\pm x,\sqrt{25-x^2})\) lie on the circle. Maximise \(A^2\) to avoid the root.

    \(A=2x\sqrt{25-x^2}\), \(0\lt x\lt 5\).

    \(A^2=4x^2(25-x^2)=100x^2-4x^4\).

    \((A^2)'=200x-16x^3=8x(25-2x^2)=0\Rightarrow x^2=12.5\).

    \(A=2\sqrt{12.5}\cdot\sqrt{25-12.5}=2\cdot12.5=25\).

    Answer: 25 (width \(2\sqrt{12.5}=5\sqrt2\), height \(\tfrac{5}{\sqrt2}\)).

  3. Find the point of \(y=\sqrt x\) closest to \((4,0)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. A point on the graph is \((x,\sqrt x)\). Minimise the squared distance.

    \(d^2=(x-4)^2+x=x^2-7x+16\).

    \((d^2)'=2x-7=0\Rightarrow x=3.5\).

    \(d^2=12.25-24.5+16=3.75\Rightarrow d=\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}2\).

    Answer: \(\left(3.5,\sqrt{3.5}\right)\), at distance \(\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}2\approx1.94\).

  4. A factory sells \(x\) thousand items for \(15x\) thousand shekels. The cost is \(C(x)=x^3-6x^2+15x\) thousand shekels. Find the production that gives the largest profit.

    Show solution

    Idea. Profit = income − cost.

    \(P(x)=15x-(x^3-6x^2+15x)=-x^3+6x^2\), \(x\ge0\).

    \(P'(x)=-3x^2+12x=3x(4-x)=0\Rightarrow x=4\); \(+\) then \(-\): a maximum.

    \(P(4)=-64+96=32\).

    Answer: 4 thousand items, profit 32 thousand shekels.

  5. Prove that of all rectangles with perimeter \(P\), the square has the largest area.

    Show solution

    Idea. Use a general perimeter \(P\). Sides \(x\) and \(\tfrac P2-x\).

    \(A(x)=x\left(\tfrac P2-x\right)=\tfrac P2x-x^2\).

    \(A'(x)=\tfrac P2-2x=0\Rightarrow x=\tfrac P4\).

    \(A''\): \(A'\) decreases, so it is a maximum.

    The other side: \(\tfrac P2-\tfrac P4=\tfrac P4\).

    Answer: both sides are \(\tfrac P4\): the rectangle of largest area is the square.

  6. A tangent is drawn to \(y=4-x^2\) at a point \((a,4-a^2)\) with \(0\lt a\lt 2\). Find \(a\) so that the triangle between the tangent and the axes has the smallest area.

    Show solution

    Idea. Write the tangent at \(x=a\), find its intercepts, write the triangle's area as a function of \(a\), and minimise.

    Slope \(y'(a)=-2a\). Tangent: \(y=-2a(x-a)+4-a^2=-2ax+a^2+4\).

    \(y\)-intercept \(a^2+4\); \(x\)-intercept \(\dfrac{a^2+4}{2a}\).

    \(S(a)=\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac{a^2+4}{2a}\cdot(a^2+4)=\dfrac{(a^2+4)^2}{4a}\).

    Quotient rule: \(S'(a)=\dfrac{2(a^2+4)\cdot2a\cdot4a-(a^2+4)^2\cdot4}{16a^2}=\dfrac{(a^2+4)(3a^2-4)}{4a^2}\).

    \(S'=0\Rightarrow a^2=\tfrac43\Rightarrow a=\dfrac2{\sqrt3}\approx1.15\); \(-\) then \(+\): a minimum.

    \(S=\dfrac{(16/3)^2}{8/\sqrt3}=\dfrac{256}9\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}8=\dfrac{32\sqrt3}9\).

    Answer: \(a=\dfrac2{\sqrt3}\); smallest area \(\dfrac{32\sqrt3}9\approx6.16\).

  7. A wire of 20 cm is cut into two pieces and each is bent into a square. How should it be cut to make the total area smallest? Largest?

    Show solution

    Idea. A piece of length \(x\) makes a square of side \(\tfrac x4\) and area \(\tfrac{x^2}{16}\). The domain is closed, \(0\le x\le20\), so check the ends too.

    \(A(x)=\dfrac{x^2+(20-x)^2}{16}\).

    \(A'(x)=\dfrac{2x-2(20-x)}{16}=\dfrac{4x-40}{16}=0\Rightarrow x=10\); \(-\) then \(+\): a minimum.

    \(A(10)=\dfrac{200}{16}=12.5\).

    Ends: \(A(0)=A(20)=\dfrac{400}{16}=25\).

    Answer: smallest total area 12.5 cm² when cut in the middle; largest 25 cm² when not cut at all (one square of side 5).

  8. A rectangle is drawn inside a right triangle with legs 6 and 8, with two sides along the legs. Find the largest area.

    Show solution

    Idea. Put the right angle at the origin and the legs on the axes. The rectangle's far vertex lies on the hypotenuse through \((6,0)\) and \((0,8)\).

    Hypotenuse: \(y=8-\tfrac86x=8-\tfrac43x\).

    \(A(x)=x\left(8-\tfrac43x\right)=8x-\tfrac43x^2\), \(0\lt x\lt 6\).

    \(A'(x)=8-\tfrac83x=0\Rightarrow x=3\), \(y=8-4=4\).

    \(A=12\).

    Answer: 12 (a \(3\times4\) rectangle), half of the triangle's area 24.

  9. A car drives 100 km. The cost per hour at speed \(v\) is \(20+\dfrac{v^2}{50}\) shekels. Find the cheapest speed and the cost.

    Show solution

    Idea. At speed \(v\) the trip takes \(\tfrac{100}v\) hours. Total cost = hours × cost per hour.

    \(C(v)=\dfrac{100}v\left(20+\dfrac{v^2}{50}\right)=\dfrac{2000}v+2v\).

    \(C'(v)=-\dfrac{2000}{v^2}+2=0\Rightarrow v^2=1000\Rightarrow v=\sqrt{1000}\approx31.6\).

    \(-\) then \(+\): a minimum.

    \(C=\dfrac{2000}{31.62}+63.25\approx63.25+63.25=126.5\).

    Answer: about 31.6 km/h; the cost is about 126.5 shekels.

  10. Find the smallest value of \(f(x)=x^2+\dfrac{16}x\) for \(x\gt 0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Differentiate, solve on \(x\gt 0\), and check the signs.

    \(f'(x)=2x-\dfrac{16}{x^2}=0\Rightarrow2x^3=16\Rightarrow x^3=8\Rightarrow x=2\).

    \(-\) for \(x\lt 2\), \(+\) for \(x\gt 2\): a minimum.

    \(f(2)=4+8=12\).

    Answer: 12 (at \(x=2\)).
