1. Measuring angles in radians

The unit circle has centre \(O\) and radius 1. Its circumference is \(2\pi\). Start at \(A(1,0)\) and walk along the circle a distance \(t\) counter-clockwise (clockwise if \(t\lt 0\)). Every real number \(t\) gives a point \(P(t)\) on the circle.

The arc length \(t\) is also the size of the angle \(AOP\) in radians. A full turn is \(2\pi\) radians, so:

\(2\pi\ \text{rad}=360^\circ\qquad 1\ \text{rad}=\dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi}\approx57.3^\circ\)

2. Sine, cosine and tangent on the unit circle

The point \(P(t)\) has coordinates \((\cos t,\ \sin t)\). This is the definition:

  • \(\cos t\) is the x-coordinate of \(P(t)\);
  • \(\sin t\) is the y-coordinate of \(P(t)\);
  • \(\tan t\) is the y-coordinate of the point \(T\) where the line \(OP\) meets the tangent line \(x=1\) to the circle at \(A\).

Triangles \(OQP\) and \(OAT\) are similar (both have a right angle and share the angle at \(O\)), so \(\dfrac{AT}{OA}=\dfrac{QP}{OQ}\), that is:

\(\tan t=\dfrac{\sin t}{\cos t}\qquad(\cos t\ne0)\)

Link to the right triangle. For \(0\lt t\lt \tfrac\pi2\), triangle \(OQP\) is a right triangle with hypotenuse 1. So \(\sin t=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\), \(\cos t=\dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\), \(\tan t=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\), as you learned in earlier grades. The unit circle extends these ratios to every angle.

\(\tan t\)+−+−

Periodicity. Adding \(2\pi\) brings us back to the same point: \(\sin(t+2\pi)=\sin t\), \(\cos(t+2\pi)=\cos t\). Adding \(\pi\) takes us to the opposite point, so \(\tan(t+\pi)=\tan t\): tangent has period \(\pi\).

Symmetry (reflections of the circle):

Reflection in the x-axis\(\sin(-t)=-\sin t,\ \ \cos(-t)=\cos t\)
Reflection in the y-axis\(\sin(\pi-t)=\sin t,\ \ \cos(\pi-t)=-\cos t\)
Opposite point\(\sin(\pi+t)=-\sin t,\ \ \cos(\pi+t)=-\cos t\)
Reflection in \(y=x\)\(\sin\left(\tfrac\pi2-t\right)=\cos t,\ \ \cos\left(\tfrac\pi2-t\right)=\sin t\)

So sine is an odd function and cosine is an even function.

The values come from the square (diagonal at 45°) and the equilateral triangle (cut in half, 30°–60°–90°). With symmetry we get values in every quarter, for example \(\sin\tfrac{5\pi}6=\sin\tfrac\pi6=\tfrac12\) and \(\cos\tfrac{2\pi}3=-\cos\tfrac\pi3=-\tfrac12\).

Example 1

\(\cos t=-\tfrac5{13}\) and \(\tfrac\pi2\lt t\lt \pi\). Find \(\sin t\) and \(\tan t\).

Solution.

\(\sin^2t=1-\tfrac{25}{169}=\tfrac{144}{169}\). In the second quarter \(\sin t\gt 0\): \(\sin t=\tfrac{12}{13}\).

\(\tan t=\dfrac{12/13}{-5/13}=-\tfrac{12}5\).

5. The graphs and their transformations

The transformations of Unit 05 apply. For \(y=a\sin(b(x-c))+d\) with \(a,b\gt 0\):

ParameterEffect
\(a\)amplitude: the graph goes from \(d-a\) to \(d+a\)
\(b\)period \(\dfrac{2\pi}b\)
\(c\)shift right by \(c\)
\(d\)shift up by \(d\) (the middle line \(y=d\))
Example 2

Describe \(y=2\sin(2x)-1\).

Solution.

Amplitude 2, period \(\tfrac{2\pi}2=\pi\), middle line \(y=-1\). Values from \(-3\) to \(1\).

Maximum when \(2x=\tfrac\pi2\), i.e. \(x=\tfrac\pi4\) (and every \(\pi\) after that).

Modelling. Anything that repeats regularly (tides, daylight hours, a turning wheel) can be modelled by such a function.

6. Common mistakes

  • Using degrees where radians are needed. In this unit, \(\sin 2\) means 2 radians (about 115°).
  • Mixing up which coordinate is which: \(\cos t\) is the x-coordinate, \(\sin t\) is the y-coordinate.
  • Forgetting the sign when using \(\sin^2t+\cos^2t=1\): the quarter decides whether the root is positive or negative.
  • Writing \(\sin^2x\) as \(\sin(x^2)\). \(\sin^2x\) means \((\sin x)^2\).
  • Thinking the period of \(\sin 2x\) is \(4\pi\). Multiplying \(x\) by 2 makes the wave twice as fast: the period is \(\pi\).

7. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Convert 120° to radians.

    Show solution

    \(120\cdot\dfrac\pi{180}=\dfrac{2\pi}3\)

  2. Find \(\sin\tfrac\pi2\).

    Show solution

    \(1\): the point \((0,1)\).

  3. Find \(\cos\pi\).

    Show solution

    \(-1\): the point \((-1,0)\).

  4. Find \(\tan\tfrac\pi4\).

    Show solution

    \(1\)

  5. What is the sign of \(\cos t\) for \(\tfrac\pi2\lt t\lt \pi\)?

    Show solution

    Negative: the point is left of the y-axis.

  6. Compute \(\sin^2 0.7+\cos^2 0.7\).

    Show solution

    \(1\), by the Pythagorean identity.

  7. Find the period of \(y=\sin 3x\).

    Show solution

    \(\dfrac{2\pi}3\)

  8. Find \(\sin\tfrac{5\pi}6\).

    Show solution

    \(\sin\left(\pi-\tfrac\pi6\right)=\sin\tfrac\pi6=\tfrac12\)
