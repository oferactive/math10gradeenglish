Before you start: Draw the unit circle for every question about values and signs. Mark the point, and read off the sign of each coordinate before you calculate.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Convert to radians.

    1. 90°
    2. 60°
    3. 270°
    4. 45°
    Show solution

    Idea. Multiply by \(\dfrac\pi{180}\).

    (a) \(90\cdot\tfrac\pi{180}=\tfrac\pi2\).

    (b) \(60\cdot\tfrac\pi{180}=\tfrac\pi3\).

    (c) \(270\cdot\tfrac\pi{180}=\tfrac{3\pi}2\).

    (d) \(45\cdot\tfrac\pi{180}=\tfrac\pi4\).

    Answer: (a) \(\tfrac\pi2\); (b) \(\tfrac\pi3\); (c) \(\tfrac{3\pi}2\); (d) \(\tfrac\pi4\).

  2. Convert to degrees.

    1. \(\tfrac\pi6\)
    2. \(\tfrac{2\pi}3\)
    3. \(\tfrac{5\pi}4\)
    4. \(3\pi\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Replace \(\pi\) by \(180^\circ\).

    (a) \(\tfrac{180}6=30^\circ\).

    (b) \(\tfrac{2\cdot180}3=120^\circ\).

    (c) \(\tfrac{5\cdot180}4=225^\circ\).

    (d) \(3\cdot180=540^\circ\).

    Answer: (a) 30°; (b) 120°; (c) 225°; (d) 540°.

  3. Write the point \(P(t)\) and the values \(\sin t\), \(\cos t\) for \(t=\tfrac\pi2,\ \pi,\ \tfrac{3\pi}2,\ 2\pi\).

    Show solution

    Idea. These are quarter turns. Each lands on an axis; then \(\cos t=x\) and \(\sin t=y\).

    \(\tfrac\pi2\): quarter turn up: \((0,1)\); \(\sin=1\), \(\cos=0\).

    \(\pi\): half turn: \((-1,0)\); \(\sin=0\), \(\cos=-1\).

    \(\tfrac{3\pi}2\): three quarters: \((0,-1)\); \(\sin=-1\), \(\cos=0\).

    \(2\pi\): a full turn, back to the start: \((1,0)\); \(\sin=0\), \(\cos=1\).

    Answer: as listed.

  4. Write the exact value.

    1. \(\sin\tfrac\pi6\)
    2. \(\cos\tfrac\pi3\)
    3. \(\tan\tfrac\pi4\)
    4. \(\sin\tfrac\pi3\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Use the special triangles: 30°-60°-90° with sides \(1,\sqrt3,2\), and 45°-45°-90° with sides \(1,1,\sqrt2\).

    (a) \(\sin30^\circ=\tfrac12\).

    (b) \(\cos60^\circ=\tfrac12\).

    (c) \(\tan45^\circ=1\).

    (d) \(\sin60^\circ=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\).

    Answer: (a) \(\tfrac12\); (b) \(\tfrac12\); (c) 1; (d) \(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\).

  5. Find the signs of \(\sin t\) and \(\cos t\).

    1. \(t=2\)
    2. \(t=4\)
    3. \(t=5\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Compare \(t\) with \(\tfrac\pi2\approx1.57\), \(\pi\approx3.14\), \(\tfrac{3\pi}2\approx4.71\), \(2\pi\approx6.28\) to find the quarter.

    (a) \(1.57\lt 2\lt 3.14\): second quarter (top left): \(\sin\gt 0\), \(\cos\lt 0\).

    (b) \(3.14\lt 4\lt 4.71\): third quarter (bottom left): \(\sin\lt 0\), \(\cos\lt 0\).

    (c) \(4.71\lt 5\lt 6.28\): fourth quarter (bottom right): \(\sin\lt 0\), \(\cos\gt 0\).

    Answer: as above.

  6. Use symmetry to find the exact value.

    1. \(\sin\left(-\tfrac\pi6\right)\)
    2. \(\cos\left(-\tfrac\pi3\right)\)
    3. \(\sin\tfrac{5\pi}6\)
    4. \(\cos\tfrac{2\pi}3\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Symmetries of the circle: \(\sin(-t)=-\sin t\), \(\cos(-t)=\cos t\), \(\sin(\pi-t)=\sin t\), \(\cos(\pi-t)=-\cos t\).

    (a) \(-\sin\tfrac\pi6=-\tfrac12\).

    (b) \(\cos\tfrac\pi3=\tfrac12\).

    (c) \(\sin\left(\pi-\tfrac\pi6\right)=\sin\tfrac\pi6=\tfrac12\).

    (d) \(\cos\left(\pi-\tfrac\pi3\right)=-\cos\tfrac\pi3=-\tfrac12\).

    Answer: (a) \(-\tfrac12\); (b) \(\tfrac12\); (c) \(\tfrac12\); (d) \(-\tfrac12\).

  7. \(\sin t=0.6\) and \(0\lt t\lt \tfrac\pi2\). Find \(\cos t\) and \(\tan t\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\sin^2t+\cos^2t=1\) gives \(\cos t\); the quarter decides its sign. Then \(\tan=\dfrac{\sin}{\cos}\).

    \(\cos^2t=1-0.36=0.64\Rightarrow\cos t=\pm0.8\).

    First quarter: \(\cos t\gt 0\), so \(\cos t=0.8\).

    \(\tan t=\dfrac{0.6}{0.8}=0.75\).

    Answer: \(\cos t=0.8\), \(\tan t=0.75\).

  8. Use periodicity to find the exact value.

    1. \(\sin\tfrac{13\pi}6\)
    2. \(\cos\tfrac{9\pi}4\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Adding \(2\pi\) (a full turn) brings us back to the same point, so the values repeat.

    (a) \(\tfrac{13\pi}6=2\pi+\tfrac\pi6\Rightarrow\sin=\sin\tfrac\pi6=\tfrac12\).

    (b) \(\tfrac{9\pi}4=2\pi+\tfrac\pi4\Rightarrow\cos=\cos\tfrac\pi4=\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\).

    Answer: (a) \(\tfrac12\); (b) \(\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\).

  9. Find the amplitude and the period.

    1. \(y=3\sin x\)
    2. \(y=\sin 2x\)
    3. \(y=\cos\tfrac x2\)
    Show solution

    Idea. In \(y=a\sin(bx)\): amplitude \(|a|\), period \(\dfrac{2\pi}b\).

    (a) \(a=3\), \(b=1\): amplitude 3, period \(2\pi\).

    (b) \(a=1\), \(b=2\): amplitude 1, period \(\tfrac{2\pi}2=\pi\).

    (c) \(a=1\), \(b=\tfrac12\): amplitude 1, period \(\dfrac{2\pi}{1/2}=4\pi\).

    Answer: as listed.

  10. A right triangle has legs 3 and 4 and hypotenuse 5. For the angle \(\alpha\) opposite the side 3, find \(\sin\alpha\), \(\cos\alpha\) and \(\tan\alpha\).

    Show solution

    Idea. In a right triangle: \(\sin=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\), \(\cos=\dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\), \(\tan=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\).

    Opposite \(\alpha\): 3. Adjacent: 4. Hypotenuse: 5.

    Answer: \(\sin\alpha=\tfrac35\), \(\cos\alpha=\tfrac45\), \(\tan\alpha=\tfrac34\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. \(\cos t=-\tfrac5{13}\) and \(\tfrac\pi2\lt t\lt \pi\). Find \(\sin t\) and \(\tan t\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Pythagorean identity, then the sign from the quarter.

    \(\sin^2t=1-\tfrac{25}{169}=\tfrac{144}{169}\Rightarrow\sin t=\pm\tfrac{12}{13}\).

    Second quarter: \(\sin t\gt 0\), so \(\sin t=\tfrac{12}{13}\).

    \(\tan t=\dfrac{12/13}{-5/13}=-\tfrac{12}5\).

    Answer: \(\sin t=\tfrac{12}{13}\), \(\tan t=-\tfrac{12}5\).

  2. \(\tan t=2\) and \(\pi\lt t\lt \tfrac{3\pi}2\). Find \(\sin t\) and \(\cos t\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\tan t=2\) means \(\sin t=2\cos t\). Substitute into \(\sin^2t+\cos^2t=1\); the quarter decides the signs.

    \(4\cos^2t+\cos^2t=1\Rightarrow\cos^2t=\tfrac15\Rightarrow\cos t=\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt5}\).

    Third quarter: both negative. \(\cos t=-\tfrac1{\sqrt5}\), \(\sin t=2\cos t=-\tfrac2{\sqrt5}\).

    Answer: \(\sin t=-\tfrac2{\sqrt5}\), \(\cos t=-\tfrac1{\sqrt5}\).

    Check: \(\tfrac45+\tfrac15=1\) ✓

  3. Simplify \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x+\tan x\cos x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Replace \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x\) by 1 and \(\tan x\) by \(\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\).

    \(1+\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\cdot\cos x=1+\sin x\).

    Answer: \(1+\sin x\) (for \(\cos x\ne0\)).

  4. Prove that \((\sin x+\cos x)^2=1+2\sin x\cos x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Expand the square \((a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2\), then use the identity.

    \((\sin x+\cos x)^2=\sin^2x+2\sin x\cos x+\cos^2x\).

    \(=(\sin^2x+\cos^2x)+2\sin x\cos x=1+2\sin x\cos x\).

    Answer: proved.

  5. Find the exact value.

    1. \(\sin\tfrac{7\pi}6\)
    2. \(\cos\tfrac{5\pi}3\)
    3. \(\tan\tfrac{3\pi}4\)
    4. \(\sin\left(-\tfrac{3\pi}2\right)\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Find the reference angle and the quarter; the quarter gives the sign.

    (a) \(\tfrac{7\pi}6=\pi+\tfrac\pi6\): third quarter, \(\sin\lt 0\): \(-\tfrac12\).

    (b) \(\tfrac{5\pi}3=2\pi-\tfrac\pi3\): fourth quarter, \(\cos\gt 0\): \(\tfrac12\).

    (c) \(\tfrac{3\pi}4=\pi-\tfrac\pi4\): second quarter, \(\tan\lt 0\): \(-1\).

    (d) \(-\tfrac{3\pi}2\) is three quarters clockwise: the point \((0,1)\): \(\sin=1\).

    Answer: (a) \(-\tfrac12\); (b) \(\tfrac12\); (c) \(-1\); (d) 1.

  6. Find the range of \(y=2\sin x+1\) and the values of \(x\) where it is largest.

    Show solution

    Idea. Start from \(-1\le\sin x\le1\) and apply the same operations as in the formula.

    Multiply by 2: \(-2\le2\sin x\le2\). Add 1: \(-1\le y\le3\).

    Largest when \(\sin x=1\): \(x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\).

    Answer: range \([-1,3]\); largest value 3 at \(x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\).

  7. How is \(y=\sin\left(x-\tfrac\pi3\right)\) obtained from \(y=\sin x\)? Find its zeros.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(f(x-c)\) shifts the graph \(c\) units to the right. Zeros of sine are at multiples of \(\pi\).

    Shift \(\tfrac\pi3\) to the right.

    \(\sin\left(x-\tfrac\pi3\right)=0\Rightarrow x-\tfrac\pi3=k\pi\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi3+k\pi\).

    Answer: a shift of \(\tfrac\pi3\) right; zeros \(x=\tfrac\pi3+k\pi\).

  8. Find the range of \(y=1-\cos x\) and where it is smallest.

    Show solution

    Idea. Start from \(-1\le\cos x\le1\). Multiplying by \(-1\) reverses the inequality.

    \(-1\le-\cos x\le1\Rightarrow0\le1-\cos x\le2\).

    Smallest (0) when \(\cos x=1\), i.e. \(x=2\pi k\).

    Answer: range \([0,2]\); smallest at \(x=2\pi k\).

  9. Explain, using the unit circle and similar triangles, why \(\tan t=\dfrac{\sin t}{\cos t}\) for \(0\lt t\lt \tfrac\pi2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Draw the tangent line \(x=1\) to the circle. The ray \(OP\) meets it at \(T(1,\tan t)\). Compare two right triangles.

    Triangle \(OQP\): \(Q=(\cos t,0)\); legs \(OQ=\cos t\), \(QP=\sin t\).

    Triangle \(OAT\): \(A=(1,0)\); legs \(OA=1\), \(AT=\tan t\).

    Both have a right angle and the common angle \(t\) at \(O\), so they are similar.

    Ratios of the legs: \(\dfrac{AT}{OA}=\dfrac{QP}{OQ}\Rightarrow\dfrac{\tan t}1=\dfrac{\sin t}{\cos t}\).

    Answer: proved by similar triangles.

  10. Simplify.

    1. \(\sin(\pi-x)+\cos\left(\tfrac\pi2-x\right)\)
    2. \(\cos(-x)-\cos x\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Use \(\sin(\pi-x)=\sin x\), \(\cos\left(\tfrac\pi2-x\right)=\sin x\), \(\cos(-x)=\cos x\).

    (a) \(\sin x+\sin x=2\sin x\).

    (b) \(\cos x-\cos x=0\).

    Answer: (a) \(2\sin x\); (b) 0.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Prove that \(1+\tan^2x=\dfrac1{\cos^2x}\). Then find \(\tan^2x\) if \(\cos x=\tfrac13\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Write \(\tan^2x=\dfrac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}\), use a common denominator, then the Pythagorean identity.

    \(1+\dfrac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}=\dfrac{\cos^2x+\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}=\dfrac1{\cos^2x}\).

    With \(\cos x=\tfrac13\): \(1+\tan^2x=9\Rightarrow\tan^2x=8\).

    Answer: proved; \(\tan^2x=8\).

  2. \(\sin x+\cos x=1.2\). Find \(\sin x\cos x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Squaring \(\sin x+\cos x\) creates the product \(\sin x\cos x\) (see question 14).

    \((\sin x+\cos x)^2=1.44\).

    \(1+2\sin x\cos x=1.44\Rightarrow2\sin x\cos x=0.44\).

    Answer: \(\sin x\cos x=0.22\).

  3. The height of a seat on a Ferris wheel after \(t\) minutes is \(h(t)=10-8\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{15}\right)\) metres.

    1. Find the lowest and highest heights.
    2. How long does one turn take?
    3. Find the height after 10 minutes.
    Show solution

    Idea. \(\cos\) is between \(-1\) and 1; the period of \(\cos(bt)\) is \(\tfrac{2\pi}b\); then substitute.

    (a) \(\cos=1\): \(h=10-8=2\). \(\cos=-1\): \(h=10+8=18\).

    (b) \(b=\tfrac\pi{15}\): period \(=\dfrac{2\pi}{\pi/15}=30\).

    (c) \(\cos\tfrac{10\pi}{15}=\cos\tfrac{2\pi}3=-\tfrac12\). \(h=10-8\cdot\left(-\tfrac12\right)=14\).

    Answer: (a) 2 m and 18 m; (b) 30 minutes; (c) 14 m.

  4. In a certain city the number of daylight hours on day \(t\) of the year is about \(D(t)=12+2.5\sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi(t-80)}{365}\right)\).

    1. Find the longest and the shortest day.
    2. On which days is \(D=12\)?
    Show solution

    Idea. The sine is between \(-1\) and 1. \(D=12\) when the sine is 0, which happens at the start and in the middle of a period.

    (a) Largest: \(12+2.5=14.5\) h. Smallest: \(12-2.5=9.5\) h.

    (b) Sine is 0 when its argument is \(0\) or \(\pi\): \(\dfrac{2\pi(t-80)}{365}=0\Rightarrow t=80\).

    \(\dfrac{2\pi(t-80)}{365}=\pi\Rightarrow t-80=182.5\Rightarrow t\approx262\).

    Answer: (a) 14.5 and 9.5 hours; (b) \(t=80\) (about 21 March) and \(t\approx262\) (about 19 September), the equinoxes.

  5. Find the largest and smallest values of \(f(x)=3-2\sin^2x\), and write \(f\) using \(\cos x\) only.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(0\le\sin^2x\le1\). For the second part use \(\sin^2x=1-\cos^2x\).

    \(\sin^2x=0\Rightarrow f=3\) (largest). \(\sin^2x=1\Rightarrow f=1\) (smallest).

    \(f(x)=3-2(1-\cos^2x)=3-2+2\cos^2x=1+2\cos^2x\).

    Answer: \(1\le f(x)\le3\); \(f(x)=1+2\cos^2x\).

  6. For which \(t\) in \([0,2\pi]\) does the point \(P(t)\) lie on the line \(y=x\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. On the line \(y=x\) the two coordinates are equal: \(\sin t=\cos t\), i.e. \(\tan t=1\).

    \(\tan t=1\) at \(t=\tfrac\pi4\) (first quarter) and \(t=\pi+\tfrac\pi4=\tfrac{5\pi}4\) (third quarter).

    Answer: \(t=\tfrac\pi4\) and \(t=\tfrac{5\pi}4\).

  7. \(A(1,0)\) and \(P(t)\) is on the unit circle, \(0\lt t\lt \pi\). Find the area of triangle \(OAP\) as a function of \(t\). When is it largest?

    Show solution

    Idea. Take \(OA\) as the base; the height is the \(y\)-coordinate of \(P\), which is \(\sin t\).

    Area \(=\dfrac12\cdot1\cdot\sin t=\dfrac12\sin t\).

    \(\sin t\) is largest (1) at \(t=\tfrac\pi2\).

    Answer: area \(\tfrac12\sin t\); largest, \(\tfrac12\), at \(t=\tfrac\pi2\) (when \(OP\perp OA\)).

  8. Explain with the unit circle why \(\sin\left(\tfrac\pi2-t\right)=\cos t\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Reflection in \(y=x\) swaps the coordinates. A point at angle \(t\) from the \(x\)-axis goes to a point at angle \(t\) from the \(y\)-axis.

    \(P(t)=(\cos t,\sin t)\) goes to \((\sin t,\cos t)\).

    Its angle from the \(x\)-axis is \(\tfrac\pi2-t\), so it is \(P\left(\tfrac\pi2-t\right)=\left(\cos\left(\tfrac\pi2-t\right),\sin\left(\tfrac\pi2-t\right)\right)\).

    Compare the \(y\)-coordinates: \(\sin\left(\tfrac\pi2-t\right)=\cos t\).

    Answer: explained by the reflection.

  9. Which is larger, \(\sin1\) or \(\sin2\)? Answer without a calculator.

    Show solution

    Idea. Move 2 into the first quarter with \(\sin(\pi-t)=\sin t\), then compare where sine increases.

    \(\sin2=\sin(\pi-2)\approx\sin(3.14-2)=\sin1.14\).

    On \(\left[0,\tfrac\pi2\right]\) sine increases, and \(1\lt 1.14\lt 1.57\).

    So \(\sin1\lt \sin1.14=\sin2\).

    Answer: \(\sin2\) is larger.

    Check: \(\sin1\approx0.84\), \(\sin2\approx0.91\) ✓

  10. \(y=2\sin(2x)-1\).

    1. Find the amplitude, period and middle line.
    2. Find the range.
    3. Find \(y(0)\) and the first maximum point for \(x\gt 0\).
    4. Find the zeros.
    Show solution

    Idea. For \(y=a\sin(bx)+d\): amplitude \(|a|\), period \(\tfrac{2\pi}b\), middle line \(y=d\).

    (a) Amplitude 2, period \(\tfrac{2\pi}2=\pi\), middle line \(y=-1\).

    (b) \(-1-2\le y\le-1+2\Rightarrow-3\le y\le1\).

    (c) \(y(0)=2\sin0-1=-1\). The maximum is when \(\sin2x=1\): \(2x=\tfrac\pi2\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi4\), \(y=1\).

    (d) \(2\sin2x-1=0\Rightarrow\sin2x=\tfrac12\Rightarrow2x=\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\) or \(2x=\tfrac{5\pi}6+2\pi k\).

    Divide by 2: \(x=\tfrac\pi{12}+\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac{5\pi}{12}+\pi k\).

    Answer: (a) 2, \(\pi\), \(y=-1\); (b) \([-3,1]\); (c) \(y(0)=-1\), maximum \(\left(\tfrac\pi4,1\right)\); (d) \(x=\tfrac\pi{12}+\pi k\), \(x=\tfrac{5\pi}{12}+\pi k\).
