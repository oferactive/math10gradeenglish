1. The equation \(\sin x=a\)

On the unit circle, \(\sin x=a\) asks: which points have y-coordinate \(a\)? If \(-1\lt a\lt 1\), the horizontal line \(y=a\) meets the circle at two points, symmetric about the y-axis: \(x=\alpha\) and \(x=\pi-\alpha\). Adding full turns gives all solutions:

\(\sin x=a\ \Longrightarrow\ x=\alpha+2\pi k\ \ \text{or}\ \ x=\pi-\alpha+2\pi k\qquad(k \text{ any whole number})\)
\(\alpha\) is one solution, from the table of special values or from \(\sin^{-1}\) on a calculator
Example 1

Solve \(\sin x=\tfrac12\).

Solution.

\(\alpha=\tfrac\pi6\). \(x=\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac{5\pi}6+2\pi k\).

If \(|a|\gt 1\) there is no solution. Special cases: \(\sin x=0\Rightarrow x=\pi k\); \(\sin x=1\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\); \(\sin x=-1\Rightarrow x=-\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\).

2. The equations \(\cos x=a\) and \(\tan x=a\)

\(\cos x=a\): the vertical line \(x=a\) meets the circle at two points, symmetric about the x-axis, at the angles \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\).

\(\tan x=a\): the line through \(O\) with slope \(a\) meets the circle at two opposite points, half a turn apart.

Cosine\(\cos x=a\Rightarrow x=\pm\alpha+2\pi k\)
Tangent\(\tan x=a\Rightarrow x=\alpha+\pi k\)
Example 2

Solve \(\cos x=-\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\) and \(\tan x=\sqrt3\).

Solution.

\(\cos\tfrac{3\pi}4=-\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\Rightarrow x=\pm\tfrac{3\pi}4+2\pi k\).

\(\tan\tfrac\pi3=\sqrt3\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi3+\pi k\).

Example 3 (calculator)

Solve \(\cos x=-0.4\). Give the solutions in \([0,2\pi]\).

Solution.

\(\cos^{-1}(-0.4)\approx1.982\). \(x\approx\pm1.982+2\pi k\).

In \([0,2\pi]\): \(1.982\) and \(2\pi-1.982\approx4.301\).

Note: set the calculator to radians (RAD) for these questions.

3. Equations of the form \(a\sin(bx+c)+d=0\)

Three steps:

  1. Isolate the trigonometric function: \(\sin(bx+c)=-\tfrac da\).
  2. Write the general solution for the whole argument \(bx+c\).
  3. Solve for \(x\): subtract \(c\), then divide by \(b\) (the \(2\pi k\) is divided too).
Example 4

Solve \(2\sin\left(x-\tfrac\pi4\right)+1=0\).

Solution.

\(\sin\left(x-\tfrac\pi4\right)=-\tfrac12\).

\(x-\tfrac\pi4=-\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\) or \(x-\tfrac\pi4=\pi+\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\).

\(x=\tfrac\pi{12}+2\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac{17\pi}{12}+2\pi k\).

Example 5

Solve \(\sin 2x=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\) in \([0,2\pi]\).

Solution.

\(2x=\tfrac\pi3+2\pi k\) or \(2x=\tfrac{2\pi}3+2\pi k\).

\(x=\tfrac\pi6+\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac\pi3+\pi k\).

In \([0,2\pi]\), \(k=0,1\): \(\tfrac\pi6,\ \tfrac\pi3,\ \tfrac{7\pi}6,\ \tfrac{4\pi}3\). Four solutions, because \(\sin2x\) completes two waves in \([0,2\pi]\).

4. Solutions in a given interval

To list the solutions in an interval, write the general solution and substitute \(k=0,\pm1,\pm2,\dots\), keeping the values inside the interval. A sketch of the graph helps you count how many there should be.

Example 6

Solve \(2\sin^2x-\sin x-1=0\).

Solution.

Let \(s=\sin x\): \(2s^2-s-1=0\Rightarrow(2s+1)(s-1)=0\).

\(\sin x=1\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\).

\(\sin x=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow x=-\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac{7\pi}6+2\pi k\).

5. Equations from models

Example 7

The height of a seat on a Ferris wheel is \(h(t)=10-8\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{15}\right)\) metres after \(t\) minutes. During the first turn (\(0\le t\le30\)), when is the seat at 14 m?

Solution.

\(10-8\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{15}\right)=14\Rightarrow\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{15}\right)=-\tfrac12\).

\(\tfrac{\pi t}{15}=\tfrac{2\pi}3\) or \(\tfrac{4\pi}3\) (in the first turn) \(\Rightarrow t=10\) or \(t=20\).

The seat is higher than 14 m from minute 10 to minute 20.

6. Common mistakes

  • Giving only one solution of \(\sin x=a\). In each period there are two: \(\alpha\) and \(\pi-\alpha\).
  • Writing \(\pi-\alpha\) for cosine. For cosine the second solution is \(-\alpha\).
  • Adding \(2\pi k\) for tangent. Tangent has period \(\pi\): add \(\pi k\).
  • In \(\sin2x=a\), dividing \(\alpha\) by 2 but not the \(2\pi k\). The general solution is \(x=\tfrac\alpha2+\pi k\).
  • Using degrees on the calculator when the answer should be in radians.
  • Trying to solve \(\sin x=1.5\). There is no solution, because \(-1\le\sin x\le1\).

7. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Solve \(\sin x=0\).

    Show solution

    \(x=\pi k\)

  2. Solve \(\cos x=1\).

    Show solution

    \(x=2\pi k\)

  3. Solve \(\tan x=1\).

    Show solution

    \(x=\tfrac\pi4+\pi k\)

  4. Solve \(\sin x=\tfrac12\) in \([0,2\pi]\).

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac\pi6\) and \(\tfrac{5\pi}6\).

  5. Solve \(\cos x=2\).

    Show solution

    No solution.

  6. Solve \(2\sin x=-\sqrt3\).

    Show solution

    \(\sin x=-\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\Rightarrow x=-\tfrac\pi3+2\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac{4\pi}3+2\pi k\).

  7. Solve \(\cos x=0\) in \([0,2\pi]\).

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac\pi2\) and \(\tfrac{3\pi}2\).

  8. Solve \(\sin 2x=1\).

    Show solution

    \(2x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi4+\pi k\).
