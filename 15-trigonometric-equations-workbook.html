Before you start: For every equation, first find one angle \(\alpha\), then write the full general solution, and only then pick the values in the interval. Check one answer with the calculator.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Solve.

    1. \(\sin x=0\)
    2. \(\cos x=0\)
    3. \(\tan x=0\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Read the zeros from the unit circle.

    (a) \(\sin x=0\): the points \((\pm1,0)\): \(x=\pi k\).

    (b) \(\cos x=0\): the points \((0,\pm1)\): \(x=\tfrac\pi2+\pi k\).

    (c) \(\tan x=\tfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}=0\) exactly when \(\sin x=0\): \(x=\pi k\).

    Answer: (a) \(\pi k\); (b) \(\tfrac\pi2+\pi k\); (c) \(\pi k\).

  2. Solve.

    1. \(\sin x=1\)
    2. \(\cos x=-1\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Each of these values is reached at only one point of the circle, so there is one family with period \(2\pi\).

    (a) \(\sin x=1\) at the top \((0,1)\): \(x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\).

    (b) \(\cos x=-1\) at \((-1,0)\): \(x=\pi+2\pi k\).

    Answer: (a) \(\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\); (b) \(\pi+2\pi k\).

  3. Solve \(\sin x=\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Basic solution \(x_0\), then \(x_0+2\pi k\) and \(\pi-x_0+2\pi k\).

    \(x_0=\tfrac\pi4\); \(\pi-\tfrac\pi4=\tfrac{3\pi}4\).

    Answer: \(x=\tfrac\pi4+2\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac{3\pi}4+2\pi k\).

  4. Solve \(\cos x=\tfrac12\).

    Show solution

    Idea. For cosine the two solutions are \(\pm x_0\) (mirror images in the \(x\)-axis).

    \(\cos\tfrac\pi3=\tfrac12\), so \(x_0=\tfrac\pi3\).

    Answer: \(x=\pm\tfrac\pi3+2\pi k\).

  5. Solve \(\tan x=1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Tangent has period \(\pi\): one family.

    \(\tan\tfrac\pi4=1\).

    Answer: \(x=\tfrac\pi4+\pi k\).

  6. Solve \(\sin x=-\tfrac12\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Sine is odd: \(\sin\left(-\tfrac\pi6\right)=-\tfrac12\). Then use \(\pi-x_0\).

    \(x_0=-\tfrac\pi6\); \(\pi-\left(-\tfrac\pi6\right)=\tfrac{7\pi}6\).

    Answer: \(x=-\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac{7\pi}6+2\pi k\).

  7. Solve \(\cos x=-\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find \(x_0\) with \(\cos x_0=-\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\) in \([0,\pi]\), then \(\pm x_0\).

    \(\cos\tfrac\pi6=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\), and \(\cos\left(\pi-\tfrac\pi6\right)=-\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\), so \(x_0=\tfrac{5\pi}6\).

    Answer: \(x=\pm\tfrac{5\pi}6+2\pi k\).

  8. Solve \(\tan x=-\sqrt3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Tangent is odd: \(\tan\left(-\tfrac\pi3\right)=-\sqrt3\). Period \(\pi\).

    Answer: \(x=-\tfrac\pi3+\pi k\).

  9. Which of these equations have solutions?

    1. \(\sin x=1.5\)
    2. \(\cos x=-2\)
    3. \(\tan x=100\)
    Show solution

    Idea. Sine and cosine take values only in \([-1,1]\); tangent takes every real value.

    (a) \(1.5\gt 1\): no.

    (b) \(-2\lt -1\): no.

    (c) Tangent goes from \(-\infty\) to \(\infty\) on each period: yes.

    Answer: only (c) has solutions.

  10. Solve \(\sin x=\tfrac12\) in \([0,2\pi]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Same as check question 4: \(x_0\) and \(\pi-x_0\), both inside \([0,2\pi]\).

    \(x_0=\tfrac\pi6\); \(\pi-\tfrac\pi6=\tfrac{5\pi}6\).

    Answer: \(\tfrac\pi6\) and \(\tfrac{5\pi}6\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Solve \(2\cos x-1=0\) in \([0,2\pi]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Isolate \(\cos x\), then take \(\pm x_0\) and move negative values into \([0,2\pi]\) by adding \(2\pi\).

    \(\cos x=\tfrac12\Rightarrow x=\pm\tfrac\pi3+2\pi k\).

    In \([0,2\pi]\): \(\tfrac\pi3\), and \(-\tfrac\pi3+2\pi=\tfrac{5\pi}3\).

    Answer: \(\tfrac\pi3\) and \(\tfrac{5\pi}3\).

  2. Solve \(2\sin\left(x-\tfrac\pi4\right)+1=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Treat \(u=x-\tfrac\pi4\) as one angle: solve \(\sin u=-\tfrac12\), then add \(\tfrac\pi4\).

    \(\sin u=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow u=-\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\) or \(u=\tfrac{7\pi}6+2\pi k\).

    \(x=u+\tfrac\pi4\): \(-\tfrac{2\pi}{12}+\tfrac{3\pi}{12}=\tfrac\pi{12}\); \(\tfrac{14\pi}{12}+\tfrac{3\pi}{12}=\tfrac{17\pi}{12}\).

    Answer: \(x=\tfrac\pi{12}+2\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac{17\pi}{12}+2\pi k\).

  3. Solve \(\sin 2x=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\) in \([0,2\pi]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Solve for \(2x\), divide by 2, then list every solution in \([0,2\pi]\). The period becomes \(\pi\), so there are twice as many solutions.

    \(2x=\tfrac\pi3+2\pi k\) or \(2x=\tfrac{2\pi}3+2\pi k\).

    \(x=\tfrac\pi6+\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac\pi3+\pi k\).

    \(k=0\): \(\tfrac\pi6,\ \tfrac\pi3\). \(k=1\): \(\tfrac{7\pi}6,\ \tfrac{4\pi}3\).

    Answer: \(\tfrac\pi6,\ \tfrac\pi3,\ \tfrac{7\pi}6,\ \tfrac{4\pi}3\).

  4. Solve \(\cos 3x=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Solve \(\cos u=0\) for \(u=3x\), then divide by 3.

    \(3x=\tfrac\pi2+\pi k\).

    \(x=\tfrac\pi6+\tfrac{\pi k}3\).

    Answer: \(x=\tfrac\pi6+\tfrac{\pi k}3\).

  5. Solve \(\tan\left(x+\tfrac\pi6\right)=1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Solve \(\tan u=1\) for \(u=x+\tfrac\pi6\), then subtract \(\tfrac\pi6\).

    \(x+\tfrac\pi6=\tfrac\pi4+\pi k\).

    \(x=\tfrac{3\pi}{12}-\tfrac{2\pi}{12}+\pi k\).

    Answer: \(x=\tfrac\pi{12}+\pi k\).

  6. Solve \(\sin x=0.3\) in \([0,2\pi]\) with a calculator (3 decimal places).

    Show solution

    Idea. The calculator gives one solution \(\sin^{-1}0.3\) (in radian mode). The second is \(\pi\) minus it.

    \(x_0=\sin^{-1}0.3\approx0.305\).

    \(\pi-0.305\approx2.837\).

    Answer: \(x\approx0.305\) and \(x\approx2.837\).

  7. Solve \(\cos x=-0.4\) in \([0,2\pi]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\cos^{-1}\) gives a solution in \([0,\pi]\); the other is \(2\pi\) minus it (that is, \(-x_0\) moved up by \(2\pi\)).

    \(x_0=\cos^{-1}(-0.4)\approx1.982\).

    \(2\pi-1.982\approx4.301\).

    Answer: \(x\approx1.982\) and \(x\approx4.301\).

  8. Solve \(4\sin^2x=3\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Divide by 4 and take square roots; remember both signs.

    \(\sin^2x=\tfrac34\Rightarrow\sin x=\pm\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\).

    \(\sin x=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\): \(\tfrac\pi3,\ \tfrac{2\pi}3\). \(\sin x=-\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\): \(-\tfrac\pi3,\ \tfrac{4\pi}3\).

    These four repeat every \(\pi\): \(\tfrac\pi3+\pi k\) and \(-\tfrac\pi3+\pi k\).

    Answer: \(x=\pm\tfrac\pi3+\pi k\).

  9. Solve \(\sin x=\cos x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Divide by \(\cos x\), but first check that no solution is lost when \(\cos x=0\).

    If \(\cos x=0\), then \(\sin x=\pm1\ne0\): not a solution. So we may divide.

    \(\tan x=1\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi4+\pi k\).

    Answer: \(x=\tfrac\pi4+\pi k\).

  10. How many solutions does \(\sin x=0.4\) have in \([0,4\pi]\)? Explain with a sketch.

    Show solution

    Idea. In one period of length \(2\pi\), the line \(y=0.4\) crosses the sine curve twice (once on the way up, once on the way down). \([0,4\pi]\) has two periods.

    In \([0,2\pi]\): \(x_0\approx0.41\) and \(\pi-x_0\approx2.73\).

    In \([2\pi,4\pi]\): add \(2\pi\) to each.

    Answer: four solutions.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Solve \(2\sin^2x-\sin x-1=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. It is a quadratic in \(s=\sin x\). Factor, then solve each basic equation.

    \(2s^2-s-1=(2s+1)(s-1)=0\Rightarrow s=1\) or \(s=-\tfrac12\).

    \(\sin x=1\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\).

    \(\sin x=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow x=-\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac{7\pi}6+2\pi k\).

    Answer: \(x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\), \(x=-\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\), \(x=\tfrac{7\pi}6+2\pi k\).

  2. Solve \(2\cos^2x+3\sin x-3=0\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Replace \(\cos^2x\) by \(1-\sin^2x\) to get an equation in \(\sin x\) only.

    \(2-2\sin^2x+3\sin x-3=0\Rightarrow-2\sin^2x+3\sin x-1=0\Rightarrow2\sin^2x-3\sin x+1=0\).

    \((2\sin x-1)(\sin x-1)=0\).

    \(\sin x=1\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\). \(\sin x=\tfrac12\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\) or \(\tfrac{5\pi}6+2\pi k\).

    Answer: \(x=\tfrac\pi2+2\pi k\), \(x=\tfrac\pi6+2\pi k\), \(x=\tfrac{5\pi}6+2\pi k\).

  3. Solve \(\tan x=2\sin x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Do not divide by \(\sin x\) (it may be 0). Move everything to one side and take out \(\sin x\).

    \(\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}-2\sin x=0\Rightarrow\sin x\left(\dfrac1{\cos x}-2\right)=0\).

    \(\sin x=0\Rightarrow x=\pi k\) (there \(\cos x=\pm1\), so \(\tan\) is defined).

    \(\dfrac1{\cos x}=2\Rightarrow\cos x=\tfrac12\Rightarrow x=\pm\tfrac\pi3+2\pi k\).

    Answer: \(x=\pi k\) or \(x=\pm\tfrac\pi3+2\pi k\).

    Watch out: dividing by \(\sin x\) would lose the solutions \(x=\pi k\).

  4. A Ferris wheel seat is at height \(h(t)=10-8\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi t}{15}\right)\) metres after \(t\) minutes.

    1. When during the first turn is the seat at 14 m?
    2. For how long in each turn is it higher than 14 m?
    3. When is it at its lowest point?
    Show solution

    Idea. Set \(h=14\), solve for the cosine, then for \(u=\tfrac{\pi t}{15}\) in one turn \(0\le u\le2\pi\).

    (a) \(10-8\cos u=14\Rightarrow\cos u=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow u=\tfrac{2\pi}3\) or \(u=\tfrac{4\pi}3\).

    \(\tfrac{\pi t}{15}=\tfrac{2\pi}3\Rightarrow t=10\); \(\tfrac{\pi t}{15}=\tfrac{4\pi}3\Rightarrow t=20\).

    (b) Between these times \(\cos u\lt -\tfrac12\), so \(h\gt 14\): from \(t=10\) to \(t=20\).

    (c) \(h=2\) when \(\cos u=1\): \(u=2\pi k\Rightarrow t=30k\).

    Answer: (a) at 10 and 20 minutes; (b) 10 minutes out of each 30; (c) at \(t=0,\ 30,\ 60,\dots\)

  5. Find the points where the graphs of \(y=\sin x\) and \(y=\cos x\) meet in \([0,2\pi]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The graphs meet where \(\sin x=\cos x\), which is \(\tan x=1\) (see question 19).

    \(x=\tfrac\pi4\) or \(x=\tfrac{5\pi}4\).

    \(y=\sin\tfrac\pi4=\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\); \(y=\sin\tfrac{5\pi}4=-\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\).

    Answer: \(\left(\tfrac\pi4,\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\right)\) and \(\left(\tfrac{5\pi}4,-\tfrac{\sqrt2}2\right)\).

  6. Use the rule "\(\sin A=\sin B\) when \(A=B+2\pi k\) or \(A=\pi-B+2\pi k\)" to solve \(\sin 2x=\sin x\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Apply the rule with \(A=2x\) and \(B=x\): two cases.

    Case 1: \(2x=x+2\pi k\Rightarrow x=2\pi k\).

    Case 2: \(2x=\pi-x+2\pi k\Rightarrow3x=\pi+2\pi k\Rightarrow x=\tfrac\pi3+\tfrac{2\pi k}3\).

    Answer: \(x=2\pi k\) or \(x=\tfrac\pi3+\tfrac{2\pi k}3\).

    Check: \(x=\tfrac\pi3\): \(\sin\tfrac{2\pi}3=\sin\tfrac\pi3\) ✓

  7. Solve \(\cos\left(x+\tfrac\pi3\right)=\cos x\), using "\(\cos A=\cos B\) when \(A=\pm B+2\pi k\)".

    Show solution

    Idea. Apply the rule with \(A=x+\tfrac\pi3\), \(B=x\): two cases.

    Case \(+\): \(x+\tfrac\pi3=x+2\pi k\Rightarrow\tfrac\pi3=2\pi k\): no integer \(k\) works.

    Case \(-\): \(x+\tfrac\pi3=-x+2\pi k\Rightarrow2x=-\tfrac\pi3+2\pi k\Rightarrow x=-\tfrac\pi6+\pi k\).

    Answer: \(x=-\tfrac\pi6+\pi k\).

  8. Solve \(|\sin x|=\tfrac12\) in \([0,2\pi]\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(|\sin x|=\tfrac12\) means \(\sin x=\tfrac12\) or \(\sin x=-\tfrac12\).

    \(\sin x=\tfrac12\): \(\tfrac\pi6,\ \tfrac{5\pi}6\).

    \(\sin x=-\tfrac12\): \(\tfrac{7\pi}6,\ \tfrac{11\pi}6\).

    Answer: \(\tfrac\pi6,\ \tfrac{5\pi}6,\ \tfrac{7\pi}6,\ \tfrac{11\pi}6\).

  9. For which values of \(a\) does \(\sin x=a\) have exactly three solutions in \([0,2\pi]\)? Exactly one? Exactly two?

    Show solution

    Idea. Count the crossings of the line \(y=a\) with one full wave of \(\sin x\) on \([0,2\pi]\), including both ends.

    \(a=0\): the line meets the wave at \(0,\ \pi,\ 2\pi\): three.

    \(a=1\) or \(a=-1\): it only touches the top \(\left(\tfrac\pi2\right)\) or the bottom \(\left(\tfrac{3\pi}2\right)\): one.

    \(0\lt |a|\lt 1\): it crosses twice (both in the upper half or both in the lower half).

    \(|a|\gt 1\): no crossing.

    Answer: three for \(a=0\); one for \(a=\pm1\); two for \(0\lt |a|\lt 1\); none for \(|a|\gt 1\).

  10. Daylight hours: \(D(t)=12+2.5\sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi(t-80)}{365}\right)\), \(t\) in days. On which days is \(D=13.25\)? For how many days a year is the day longer than 13.25 hours?

    Show solution

    Idea. Set \(D=13.25\), solve for the sine, then for the angle \(u=\dfrac{2\pi(t-80)}{365}\).

    \(2.5\sin u=1.25\Rightarrow\sin u=0.5\Rightarrow u=\tfrac\pi6\) or \(u=\tfrac{5\pi}6\).

    \(t-80=\dfrac{365}{2\pi}\cdot\dfrac\pi6=\dfrac{365}{12}\approx30.4\Rightarrow t\approx110\).

    \(t-80=\dfrac{5\cdot365}{12}\approx152.1\Rightarrow t\approx232\).

    Between these days \(\sin u\gt 0.5\), so \(D\gt 13.25\).

    Answer: around day 110 (20 April) and day 232 (20 August); about 122 days a year are longer than 13.25 hours.
