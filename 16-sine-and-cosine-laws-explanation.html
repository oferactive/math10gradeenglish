Area Formula, Law of Sines, Law of Cosines

Three tools for any triangle, not only right triangles: the area formula \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\), the law of sines with the circumscribed circle, and the law of cosines. How to choose the right tool, and how to use them in proofs. Practice for this unit is in the workbook.

1. Notation

In triangle \(ABC\) the side opposite vertex \(A\) is called \(a\), opposite \(B\) is \(b\), opposite \(C\) is \(c\). The angles are \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) (in degrees in this unit, so set the calculator to DEG). \(R\) is the radius of the circumscribed circle.

2. The area formula

\(S=\tfrac12\,ab\sin C\)
half the product of two sides times the sine of the angle between them

Proof (acute angles). In the figure above, the height \(h\) from \(C\) to side \(c\) is a leg of the right triangle \(AHC\), whose hypotenuse is \(b\). So \(h=b\sin A\) and \(S=\tfrac12c\cdot h=\tfrac12bc\sin A\). The same argument with another height gives \(S=\tfrac12ab\sin C\) and \(S=\tfrac12ac\sin B\).

Obtuse angle. If the angle \(B\) is obtuse, the height from \(C\) falls outside the triangle (figure below). The right triangle \(BHC\) contains the angle \(180^\circ-B\), so \(h=a\sin(180^\circ-B)=a\sin B\), and again \(S=\tfrac12c\cdot h=\tfrac12ac\sin B\).

Example 1

Two sides of a triangle are 8 and 5, and the angle between them is 30°. Find the area.

Solution.

\(S=\tfrac12\cdot8\cdot5\cdot\sin30^\circ=20\cdot\tfrac12=10\)

Note: since \(\sin C=\sin(180^\circ-C)\), the angle \(C\) and the angle \(180^\circ-C\) give the same area. Knowing the area and two sides gives two possible angles.

3. The law of sines

\(\dfrac a{\sin A}=\dfrac b{\sin B}=\dfrac c{\sin C}=2R\)

Proof of the first part. Write the area in two ways: \(\tfrac12bc\sin A=\tfrac12ac\sin B\). Divide by \(\tfrac12c\): \(b\sin A=a\sin B\), so \(\dfrac a{\sin A}=\dfrac b{\sin B}\).

Why \(2R\)? Draw the diameter \(BD\) of the circumscribed circle. The angles \(A\) and \(D\) stand on the same arc \(BC\), so they are equal, and the angle \(BCD\) stands on a diameter, so it is right (Unit 19). In the right triangle \(BCD\): \(a=2R\sin D=2R\sin A\).

When to use it: when you know a side and the angle opposite it, plus one more piece (another angle or another side).

Example 2

In triangle \(ABC\), \(A=40^\circ\), \(B=75^\circ\), \(a=10\). Find the other sides and \(R\).

Solution.

\(C=180^\circ-40^\circ-75^\circ=65^\circ\).

\(b=\dfrac{10\sin75^\circ}{\sin40^\circ}\approx15.03\), \(c=\dfrac{10\sin65^\circ}{\sin40^\circ}\approx14.10\).

\(2R=\dfrac{10}{\sin40^\circ}\Rightarrow R\approx7.78\).

Example 3 (two triangles)

\(a=6\), \(b=8\), \(A=40^\circ\). Find \(B\).

Solution.

\(\sin B=\dfrac{8\sin40^\circ}6\approx0.857\Rightarrow B\approx59.0^\circ\) or \(B\approx121.0^\circ\).

Both are possible, because \(40^\circ+121^\circ\lt 180^\circ\). There are two different triangles with these data.

When we look for an angle with the law of sines, always check whether \(180^\circ\) minus the angle is also possible. If \(\sin B\gt 1\), there is no triangle at all.

4. The law of cosines

\(c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C\)
and the same for the other sides

Proof with coordinates. Put \(C\) at the origin and \(B\) at \((a,0)\). Then \(A=(b\cos C,\ b\sin C)\). By the distance formula:

\(c^2=(a-b\cos C)^2+(b\sin C)^2=a^2-2ab\cos C+b^2(\cos^2C+\sin^2C)=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C\).

For \(C=90^\circ\), \(\cos C=0\) and we get Pythagoras' theorem. If \(C\) is obtuse, \(\cos C\lt 0\) and \(c^2\gt a^2+b^2\).

When to use it: two sides and the angle between them (SAS) to find the third side; three sides (SSS) to find an angle.

Example 4

\(a=7\), \(b=5\), \(C=60^\circ\). Find \(c\).

Solution.

\(c^2=49+25-2\cdot7\cdot5\cdot\tfrac12=39\Rightarrow c=\sqrt{39}\approx6.24\)

Example 5

The sides of a triangle are 3, 5 and 7. Find its largest angle.

Solution.

The largest angle is opposite the longest side: \(\cos C=\dfrac{9+25-49}{2\cdot3\cdot5}=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow C=120^\circ\).

5. Choosing a tool, and checking answers

What is knownUse
two angles and a side (AAS, ASA)law of sines
two sides and the angle opposite one of them (SSA)law of sines (check for two triangles)
two sides and the angle between them (SAS)law of cosines; area formula
three sides (SSS)law of cosines

Dimension control. A length formula must give a length, and an area formula must give a length squared. \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\) is length × length (the sine has no units), so it can be an area. An expression like \(a^2+b\) adds an area to a length, so it cannot be either: such a result signals a mistake.

Identity or equation? \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\) is an identity: true for every \(x\). \(\sin x=\tfrac12\) is an equation: true only for some \(x\).

Approximation or proof? A calculation with a calculator gives an approximate answer for one triangle. A proof uses letters and holds for every triangle.

6. Common mistakes

  • Using the area formula with an angle that is not between the two sides.
  • Finding an angle with the law of sines and forgetting the second possibility \(180^\circ-B\).
  • In the law of cosines, forgetting the factor 2 or the minus sign: \(c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C\).
  • Writing \(c=a+b-2ab\cos C\) (forgetting the squares) or forgetting to take the square root at the end.
  • Using the calculator in radian mode for problems with degrees.
  • Rounding too early. Keep full calculator values until the final answer.

7. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Find the area of a triangle with sides 4 and 5 and an angle of 30° between them.

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot5\cdot\tfrac12=5\)

  2. Write the law of cosines for side \(a\).

    Show solution

    \(a^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos A\)

  3. What does the law of cosines say when \(C=90^\circ\)?

    Show solution

    \(c^2=a^2+b^2\): Pythagoras\' theorem.

  4. \(a=6\) and \(A=30^\circ\). Find \(R\).

    Show solution

    \(2R=\dfrac6{0.5}=12\Rightarrow R=6\)

  5. Two sides are 3 and 4 with an angle of 60° between them. Find the third side.

    Show solution

    \(c^2=9+16-12=13\Rightarrow c=\sqrt{13}\)

  6. \(B=45^\circ\) and \(C=60^\circ\). Find \(A\).

    Show solution

    \(75^\circ\)

  7. \(a=b=4\) and \(C=150^\circ\). Find the area.

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac12\cdot16\cdot\sin150^\circ=8\cdot\tfrac12=4\)

  8. All three sides are known. Which law gives the angles?

    Show solution

    The law of cosines.
