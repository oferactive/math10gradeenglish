Before you start: Sketch the triangle and mark the known data before choosing a law. Keep full calculator values until the end, and give final answers to two decimal places.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the area: \(a=6\), \(b=10\), \(C=30^\circ\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Area \(=\tfrac12ab\sin C\).

    \(\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot10\cdot\sin30^\circ=30\cdot\tfrac12\).

    Answer: 15.

  2. Find the area of a triangle with sides 4 and 9 and an angle of 120° between them.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\), with \(\sin120^\circ=\sin60^\circ=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\).

    \(\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot9\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}2=18\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\).

    Answer: \(9\sqrt3\approx15.59\).

  3. Find the area of an equilateral triangle with side 6.

    Show solution

    Idea. Every angle of an equilateral triangle is \(60^\circ\).

    \(\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot6\cdot\sin60^\circ=18\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\).

    Answer: \(9\sqrt3\approx15.59\).

  4. \(A=30^\circ\), \(a=5\), \(B=45^\circ\). Find \(b\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Law of sines with the pair \(a\), \(A\): \(\dfrac b{\sin B}=\dfrac a{\sin A}\).

    \(b=\dfrac{a\sin B}{\sin A}=\dfrac{5\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt2}2}{\tfrac12}=5\sqrt2\).

    Answer: \(b=5\sqrt2\approx7.07\).

  5. \(a=10\), \(A=50^\circ\), \(b=8\). Find \(B\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Law of sines for \(\sin B\). Sine gives two possible angles, \(B\) and \(180^\circ-B\); check which fits.

    \(\sin B=\dfrac{b\sin A}a=\dfrac{8\sin50^\circ}{10}\approx0.613\).

    \(B\approx37.8^\circ\) or \(B\approx142.2^\circ\).

    \(50^\circ+142.2^\circ\gt 180^\circ\): the second is impossible. (Also \(b\lt a\) forces \(B\lt A\).)

    Answer: \(B\approx37.8^\circ\).

  6. \(b=5\), \(c=8\), \(A=60^\circ\). Find \(a\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Two sides and the angle between them: law of cosines.

    \(a^2=25+64-2\cdot5\cdot8\cdot\cos60^\circ=89-40=49\).

    Answer: \(a=7\).

  7. The sides of a triangle are 3, 5 and 7. Find the largest angle.

    Show solution

    Idea. The largest angle is opposite the largest side (7). Use the law of cosines for its cosine.

    \(\cos C=\dfrac{3^2+5^2-7^2}{2\cdot3\cdot5}=\dfrac{9+25-49}{30}=\dfrac{-15}{30}=-\tfrac12\).

    Answer: \(C=120^\circ\).

  8. \(a=12\) and \(A=30^\circ\). Find the radius \(R\) of the circumscribed circle.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\dfrac a{\sin A}=2R\).

    \(2R=\dfrac{12}{0.5}=24\).

    Answer: \(R=12\).

  9. A triangle has area 20 and two sides 8 and 10. Find the angle between these sides.

    Show solution

    Idea. Use the area formula backwards to find \(\sin C\). A sine value gives two angles between 0° and 180°.

    \(\tfrac12\cdot8\cdot10\cdot\sin C=20\Rightarrow40\sin C=20\Rightarrow\sin C=\tfrac12\).

    Answer: \(C=30^\circ\) or \(C=150^\circ\) (both triangles exist).

  10. Which law would you use first?

    1. three sides known
    2. two angles and one side known
    3. two sides and the angle between them known
    4. two sides and the angle opposite one of them known
    Show solution

    Idea. Law of sines needs an angle and the side opposite it. Otherwise, use the law of cosines.

    (a) No angle known: law of cosines.

    (b) Two angles give the third; every side has its opposite angle: law of sines.

    (c) The angle is between the sides, not opposite a known side: law of cosines.

    (d) A known angle with its opposite side: law of sines.

    Answer: (a) cosines; (b) sines; (c) cosines; (d) sines.

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. \(A=40^\circ\), \(B=75^\circ\), \(a=10\). Find \(C\), \(b\), \(c\) and \(R\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find \(C\) from the angle sum, then the law of sines with the pair \(a\), \(A\).

    \(C=180^\circ-40^\circ-75^\circ=65^\circ\).

    \(\dfrac a{\sin A}=\dfrac{10}{\sin40^\circ}\approx15.557=2R\).

    \(b=15.557\cdot\sin75^\circ\approx15.03\); \(c=15.557\cdot\sin65^\circ\approx14.10\).

    \(R=\tfrac{15.557}2\approx7.78\).

    Answer: \(C=65^\circ\), \(b\approx15.03\), \(c\approx14.10\), \(R\approx7.78\).

  2. \(a=7\), \(b=5\), \(C=60^\circ\). Find \(c\) and the angle \(A\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Law of cosines for \(c\); then the law of cosines again, solved for \(\cos A\).

    \(c^2=49+25-2\cdot7\cdot5\cdot\tfrac12=74-35=39\Rightarrow c=\sqrt{39}\approx6.24\).

    \(\cos A=\dfrac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc}=\dfrac{25+39-49}{2\cdot5\cdot\sqrt{39}}=\dfrac{15}{10\sqrt{39}}\approx0.240\).

    Answer: \(c=\sqrt{39}\approx6.24\), \(A\approx76.1^\circ\).

  3. The sides of a triangle are 5, 7 and 8. Find all its angles and its area.

    Show solution

    Idea. Law of cosines for two angles, the angle sum for the third, then \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\).

    Opposite 7: \(\cos=\dfrac{25+64-49}{2\cdot5\cdot8}=\dfrac{40}{80}=\tfrac12\Rightarrow60^\circ\).

    Opposite 5: \(\cos=\dfrac{49+64-25}{2\cdot7\cdot8}=\dfrac{88}{112}\approx0.786\Rightarrow38.2^\circ\).

    Third: \(180^\circ-60^\circ-38.2^\circ=81.8^\circ\).

    Area with sides 5, 8 and the \(60^\circ\) between them: \(\tfrac12\cdot5\cdot8\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}2=10\sqrt3\).

    Answer: \(60^\circ,\ 38.2^\circ,\ 81.8^\circ\); area \(10\sqrt3\approx17.32\).

  4. A parallelogram has sides 6 and 10 and an angle of 60°. Find its diagonals and its area.

    Show solution

    Idea. Each diagonal cuts the parallelogram into two triangles with sides 6 and 10. The angles of a parallelogram are \(60^\circ\) and \(120^\circ\).

    Short diagonal (opposite \(60^\circ\)): \(d^2=36+100-120\cdot\tfrac12=76\Rightarrow d=\sqrt{76}\approx8.72\).

    Long diagonal (opposite \(120^\circ\)): \(d^2=136-120\cdot\left(-\tfrac12\right)=196\Rightarrow d=14\).

    Area = two triangles: \(2\cdot\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot10\sin60^\circ=30\sqrt3\).

    Answer: diagonals \(\approx8.72\) and 14; area \(30\sqrt3\approx51.96\).

  5. \(a=6\), \(b=8\), \(A=40^\circ\). How many triangles are possible? Find \(B\) in each.

    Show solution

    Idea. Law of sines gives \(\sin B\). Two angles have this sine; keep each one for which the angle sum stays below \(180^\circ\).

    \(\sin B=\dfrac{8\sin40^\circ}6\approx0.857\).

    \(B\approx59.0^\circ\): \(40+59\lt 180\) ✓.

    \(B\approx121.0^\circ\): \(40+121=161\lt 180\) ✓.

    Answer: two triangles, with \(B\approx59.0^\circ\) or \(B\approx121.0^\circ\).

  6. \(a=5\), \(b=8\), \(A=40^\circ\). Show that no such triangle exists.

    Show solution

    Idea. Use the law of sines; a sine can never be larger than 1.

    \(\sin B=\dfrac{8\sin40^\circ}5\approx\dfrac{8\cdot0.643}5\approx1.03\).

    Answer: \(\sin B\gt 1\) is impossible, so no such triangle exists. (Side \(a=5\) is too short to reach the third side.)

  7. Two ships leave a port. One sails 12 km, the other 15 km, and the angle between their directions is 50°. How far apart are they?

    Show solution

    Idea. The port and the two ships form a triangle with sides 12 and 15 and the angle \(50^\circ\) between them.

    \(d^2=12^2+15^2-2\cdot12\cdot15\cos50^\circ=369-360\cdot0.643\approx137.6\).

    Answer: \(d\approx11.73\) km.

  8. Prove that the area of a triangle is \(S=\dfrac{abc}{4R}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Combine \(S=\tfrac12ab\sin C\) with the law of sines \(\dfrac c{\sin C}=2R\).

    From the law of sines: \(\sin C=\dfrac c{2R}\).

    \(S=\tfrac12ab\cdot\dfrac c{2R}=\dfrac{abc}{4R}\).

    Answer: proved.

  9. An isosceles triangle has legs of 10 and an angle of 40° between them. Find the base and the area.

    Show solution

    Idea. The height from the apex is also the angle bisector and the median, so it splits the triangle into two right triangles with angle \(20^\circ\).

    Half the base: \(10\sin20^\circ\approx3.42\). Base \(\approx6.84\).

    Area: \(\tfrac12\cdot10\cdot10\cdot\sin40^\circ=50\cdot0.643\approx32.14\).

    Answer: base \(\approx6.84\), area \(\approx32.14\).

  10. From a point \(A\) the top of a tree is seen at an angle of elevation of 30°. After walking 20 m towards the tree, the angle is 45°. Find the height of the tree.

    Show solution

    Idea. The triangle \(ABT\) (two observation points and the top) has one known side, 20 m. Find its angles, use the law of sines for \(BT\), then the right triangle at the foot of the tree.

    Angle at \(B\) inside \(ABT\): \(180^\circ-45^\circ=135^\circ\). Angle at \(T\): \(180^\circ-30^\circ-135^\circ=15^\circ\).

    \(\dfrac{BT}{\sin30^\circ}=\dfrac{20}{\sin15^\circ}\Rightarrow BT=\dfrac{10}{\sin15^\circ}\approx38.64\).

    Height \(=BT\sin45^\circ\approx38.64\cdot0.7071\approx27.32\).

    Answer: about 27.32 m (exactly \(10(\sqrt3+1)\)).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. In quadrilateral \(ABCD\): \(AB=5\), \(BC=8\), \(\angle B=60^\circ\), \(CD=6\) and \(\angle ACD=90^\circ\).

    1. Find \(AC\).
    2. Find \(AD\).
    3. Find the area of the quadrilateral.
    Show solution

    Idea. The diagonal \(AC\) splits the quadrilateral into two triangles: \(ABC\) (law of cosines) and \(ACD\) (right angle at \(C\)).

    (a) \(AC^2=25+64-2\cdot5\cdot8\cdot\tfrac12=49\Rightarrow AC=7\).

    (b) Pythagoras in \(ACD\): \(AD^2=49+36=85\Rightarrow AD=\sqrt{85}\approx9.22\).

    (c) \(S_{ABC}=\tfrac12\cdot5\cdot8\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}2=10\sqrt3\); \(S_{ACD}=\tfrac12\cdot7\cdot6=21\).

    Answer: (a) 7; (b) \(\sqrt{85}\); (c) \(10\sqrt3+21\approx38.32\).

  2. The sides of a triangle are 7, 8 and 13.

    1. Find the largest angle.
    2. Find the area.
    3. Find \(R\).
    Show solution

    Idea. Largest angle opposite the largest side; then area and the law of sines for \(R\).

    (a) \(\cos C=\dfrac{49+64-169}{2\cdot7\cdot8}=\dfrac{-56}{112}=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow C=120^\circ\).

    (b) \(S=\tfrac12\cdot7\cdot8\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}2=14\sqrt3\).

    (c) \(2R=\dfrac{13}{\sin120^\circ}=\dfrac{13}{\sqrt3/2}\Rightarrow R=\dfrac{13}{\sqrt3}\).

    Answer: (a) \(120^\circ\); (b) \(14\sqrt3\approx24.25\); (c) \(R=\dfrac{13}{\sqrt3}\approx7.51\).

  3. In triangle \(ABC\): \(BC=10\), \(CA=7\), \(AB=9\). \(M\) is the midpoint of \(BC\). Find the length of the median \(AM\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The angle \(B\) belongs to both triangles \(ABC\) and \(ABM\). Find \(\cos B\) in the big triangle, then use it in the small one.

    In \(ABC\): \(\cos B=\dfrac{AB^2+BC^2-AC^2}{2\cdot AB\cdot BC}=\dfrac{81+100-49}{180}=\dfrac{132}{180}\).

    In \(ABM\), \(BM=5\): \(AM^2=81+25-2\cdot9\cdot5\cdot\dfrac{132}{180}=106-66=40\).

    Answer: \(AM=\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}\approx6.32\).

  4. Prove the law of cosines using coordinates: \(C=(0,0)\), \(B=(a,0)\), \(A=(b\cos C,\ b\sin C)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute \(AB^2\) with the distance formula and use \(\sin^2C+\cos^2C=1\).

    \(c^2=(a-b\cos C)^2+(0-b\sin C)^2\).

    \(=a^2-2ab\cos C+b^2\cos^2C+b^2\sin^2C\).

    \(=a^2-2ab\cos C+b^2(\cos^2C+\sin^2C)=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C\).

    Answer: proved.

  5. Prove the area formula \(S=\tfrac12ab\sin C\) when the angle \(C\) is obtuse.

    Show solution

    Idea. Draw the height from \(A\). For an obtuse \(C\) it lands on the extension of \(BC\), beyond \(C\). Use the right triangle formed outside, and \(\sin(180^\circ-C)=\sin C\).

    In that right triangle the hypotenuse is \(AC=b\) and the angle at \(C\) is \(180^\circ-C\).

    \(h=b\sin(180^\circ-C)=b\sin C\).

    \(S=\tfrac12\cdot BC\cdot h=\tfrac12ab\sin C\).

    Answer: the formula holds for an obtuse angle too.

  6. In triangle \(ABC\), \(a=2b\cos C\). Prove that the triangle is isosceles.

    Show solution

    Idea. Replace \(\cos C\) by its expression from the law of cosines, then simplify.

    \(\cos C=\dfrac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}\).

    \(a=2b\cdot\dfrac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}=\dfrac{a^2+b^2-c^2}a\).

    Multiply by \(a\): \(a^2=a^2+b^2-c^2\Rightarrow b^2=c^2\Rightarrow b=c\).

    Answer: \(b=c\), so the triangle is isosceles (\(AB=AC\)).

  7. Prove that the area of a triangle is \(S=2R^2\sin A\sin B\sin C\), and use it for an equilateral triangle in a circle of radius 4.

    Show solution

    Idea. Replace the sides in \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\) using the law of sines: \(a=2R\sin A\), \(b=2R\sin B\).

    \(S=\tfrac12(2R\sin A)(2R\sin B)\sin C=2R^2\sin A\sin B\sin C\).

    Equilateral: all angles \(60^\circ\), \(\sin60^\circ=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\).

    \(S=2\cdot16\cdot\left(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\right)^3=32\cdot\dfrac{3\sqrt3}8=12\sqrt3\).

    Answer: proved; \(S=12\sqrt3\approx20.78\).

  8. Dimension control and identities.

    1. Which of these can be an area: \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\), \(a+b\), \(\dfrac{abc}{4R}\), \(a^2+b\)?
    2. Which are identities and which are equations: \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\), \(\sin x=0.5\), \(\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\)?
    Show solution

    Idea. (a) An area must have units of length². Sines have no units. (b) An identity is true for every allowed \(x\); an equation is true only for some.

    (a) \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\): length·length = area ✓. \(\dfrac{abc}{4R}\): length³/length = area ✓. \(a+b\) is a length. \(a^2+b\) adds an area to a length: meaningless.

    (b) \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\) and \(\tan x=\tfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\) hold for every \(x\) (where defined): identities. \(\sin x=0.5\) holds only for some \(x\): an equation.

    Answer: (a) \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\) and \(\tfrac{abc}{4R}\); (b) two identities and one equation, as above.

  9. A hiker walks 300 m, turns 40° to the right, and walks another 200 m. How far is she from the start?

    Show solution

    Idea. Turning 40° from the direction of travel leaves an angle of \(180^\circ-40^\circ\) inside the triangle at the turning point.

    Angle at the turn: \(140^\circ\), with \(\cos140^\circ\approx-0.766\).

    \(d^2=300^2+200^2-2\cdot300\cdot200\cdot\cos140^\circ\approx130000+91925=221925\).

    Answer: \(d\approx471.1\) m.

    Watch out: using \(40^\circ\) inside the triangle would give about 196 m, much too short.

  10. In triangle \(ABC\), \(AB=AC=10\) and \(BC=12\). Find the angles, the area and \(R\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The height to the base bisects it into two right triangles with legs 6 and 8 and hypotenuse 10.

    \(\cos B=\dfrac{6}{10}=0.6\Rightarrow B=C\approx53.13^\circ\).

    \(A=180^\circ-2\cdot53.13^\circ\approx73.74^\circ\).

    Height \(=\sqrt{100-36}=8\). Area \(=\tfrac12\cdot12\cdot8=48\).

    \(R=\dfrac{abc}{4S}=\dfrac{10\cdot10\cdot12}{4\cdot48}=\dfrac{1200}{192}=6.25\).

    Answer: \(B=C\approx53.13^\circ\), \(A\approx73.74^\circ\); area 48; \(R=6.25\).
