Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Find the area: \(a=6\), \(b=10\), \(C=30^\circ\).

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot10\cdot\tfrac12=15\)

  2. Find the area of a triangle with sides 4 and 9 and an angle of 120° between them.

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot9\cdot\sin120^\circ=18\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}2=9\sqrt3\approx15.59\)

  3. Find the area of an equilateral triangle with side 6.

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac12\cdot36\cdot\sin60^\circ=9\sqrt3\approx15.59\)

  4. \(A=30^\circ\), \(a=5\), \(B=45^\circ\). Find \(b\).

    Show solution

    \(b=\dfrac{5\sin45^\circ}{\sin30^\circ}=5\sqrt2\approx7.07\)

  5. \(a=10\), \(A=50^\circ\), \(b=8\). Find \(B\).

    Show solution

    \(\sin B=\dfrac{8\sin50^\circ}{10}\approx0.613\Rightarrow B\approx37.8^\circ\).

    (\(B\approx142.2^\circ\) is impossible: \(50^\circ+142.2^\circ\gt 180^\circ\). Also \(b\lt a\), so \(B\lt A\).)

  6. \(b=5\), \(c=8\), \(A=60^\circ\). Find \(a\).

    Show solution

    \(a^2=25+64-40=49\Rightarrow a=7\)

  7. The sides of a triangle are 3, 5 and 7. Find the largest angle.

    Show solution

    \(\cos C=\dfrac{9+25-49}{30}=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow C=120^\circ\)

  8. \(a=12\) and \(A=30^\circ\). Find the radius \(R\) of the circumscribed circle.

    Show solution

    \(2R=\dfrac{12}{\sin30^\circ}=24\Rightarrow R=12\)

  9. A triangle has area 20 and two sides 8 and 10. Find the angle between these sides.

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac12\cdot8\cdot10\sin C=20\Rightarrow\sin C=\tfrac12\Rightarrow C=30^\circ\) or \(C=150^\circ\).

  10. Which law would you use first?

    1. three sides known
    2. two angles and one side known
    3. two sides and the angle between them known
    4. two sides and the angle opposite one of them known
    Show solution

    (a) law of cosines

    (b) law of sines

    (c) law of cosines

    (d) law of sines

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. \(A=40^\circ\), \(B=75^\circ\), \(a=10\). Find \(C\), \(b\), \(c\) and \(R\).

    Show solution

    \(C=65^\circ\), \(b\approx15.03\), \(c\approx14.10\), \(R\approx7.78\).

  2. \(a=7\), \(b=5\), \(C=60^\circ\). Find \(c\) and the angle \(A\).

    Show solution

    \(c=\sqrt{39}\approx6.24\).

    \(\cos A=\dfrac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc}=\dfrac{25+39-49}{2\cdot5\sqrt{39}}\approx0.240\Rightarrow A\approx76.1^\circ\).

  3. The sides of a triangle are 5, 7 and 8. Find all its angles and its area.

    Show solution

    Opposite 7: \(\cos=\dfrac{25+64-49}{80}=\tfrac12\Rightarrow60^\circ\).

    Opposite 5: \(\cos=\dfrac{49+64-25}{112}\approx0.786\Rightarrow38.2^\circ\). Third angle \(\approx81.8^\circ\).

    Area \(=\tfrac12\cdot5\cdot8\sin60^\circ=10\sqrt3\approx17.32\).

  4. A parallelogram has sides 6 and 10 and an angle of 60°. Find its diagonals and its area.

    Show solution

    Short diagonal: \(d^2=36+100-120\cos60^\circ=76\Rightarrow d\approx8.72\).

    Long diagonal (angle 120°): \(d^2=136+60=196\Rightarrow d=14\).

    Area \(=6\cdot10\sin60^\circ=30\sqrt3\approx51.96\).

  5. \(a=6\), \(b=8\), \(A=40^\circ\). How many triangles are possible? Find \(B\) in each.

    Show solution

    \(\sin B\approx0.857\): \(B\approx59.0^\circ\) or \(B\approx121.0^\circ\). Both fit, so there are two triangles.

  6. \(a=5\), \(b=8\), \(A=40^\circ\). Show that no such triangle exists.

    Show solution

    \(\sin B=\dfrac{8\sin40^\circ}5\approx1.03\gt 1\): impossible.

  7. Two ships leave a port. One sails 12 km, the other 15 km, and the angle between their directions is 50°. How far apart are they?

    Show solution

    \(d^2=144+225-2\cdot12\cdot15\cos50^\circ\approx137.6\Rightarrow d\approx11.73\) km.

  8. Prove that the area of a triangle is \(S=\dfrac{abc}{4R}\).

    Show solution

    \(S=\tfrac12ab\sin C\) and \(\sin C=\dfrac c{2R}\), so \(S=\tfrac12ab\cdot\dfrac c{2R}=\dfrac{abc}{4R}\).

  9. An isosceles triangle has legs of 10 and an angle of 40° between them. Find the base and the area.

    Show solution

    The height bisects the apex angle: base \(=2\cdot10\sin20^\circ\approx6.84\).

    Area \(=\tfrac12\cdot10\cdot10\sin40^\circ\approx32.14\).

  10. From a point \(A\) the top of a tree is seen at an angle of elevation of 30°. After walking 20 m towards the tree, the angle is 45°. Find the height of the tree.

    Show solution

    Let \(B\) be the second point and \(T\) the top. In triangle \(ABT\): angle \(A=30^\circ\), angle \(ABT=135^\circ\), so angle \(T=15^\circ\).

    \(BT=\dfrac{20\sin30^\circ}{\sin15^\circ}\approx38.64\) m.

    Height \(=BT\sin45^\circ\approx27.32\) m (exactly \(10(\sqrt3+1)\)).

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. In quadrilateral \(ABCD\): \(AB=5\), \(BC=8\), \(\angle B=60^\circ\), \(CD=6\) and \(\angle ACD=90^\circ\).

    1. Find \(AC\).
    2. Find \(AD\).
    3. Find the area of the quadrilateral.
    Show solution

    (a) \(AC^2=25+64-40=49\Rightarrow AC=7\).

    (b) \(AD=\sqrt{49+36}=\sqrt{85}\approx9.22\).

    (c) \(S=\tfrac12\cdot5\cdot8\sin60^\circ+\tfrac12\cdot7\cdot6=10\sqrt3+21\approx38.32\).

  2. The sides of a triangle are 7, 8 and 13.

    1. Find the largest angle.
    2. Find the area.
    3. Find \(R\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(\cos C=\dfrac{49+64-169}{112}=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow C=120^\circ\).

    (b) \(S=\tfrac12\cdot7\cdot8\sin120^\circ=14\sqrt3\approx24.25\).

    (c) \(R=\dfrac{13}{2\sin120^\circ}=\dfrac{13}{\sqrt3}\approx7.51\).

  3. In triangle \(ABC\): \(BC=10\), \(CA=7\), \(AB=9\). \(M\) is the midpoint of \(BC\). Find the length of the median \(AM\).

    Show solution

    In triangle \(ABC\): \(\cos B=\dfrac{100+81-49}{2\cdot10\cdot9}=\dfrac{132}{180}\).

    In triangle \(ABM\) (\(BM=5\)): \(AM^2=81+25-2\cdot9\cdot5\cdot\dfrac{132}{180}=106-66=40\).

    \(AM=\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}\approx6.32\).

  4. Prove the law of cosines using coordinates: \(C=(0,0)\), \(B=(a,0)\), \(A=(b\cos C,\ b\sin C)\).

    Show solution

    \(c^2=AB^2=(a-b\cos C)^2+(b\sin C)^2=a^2-2ab\cos C+b^2\cos^2C+b^2\sin^2C=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C\).

  5. Prove the area formula \(S=\tfrac12ab\sin C\) when the angle \(C\) is obtuse.

    Show solution

    The height from \(A\) to line \(BC\) falls outside the triangle, beyond \(C\). It forms a right triangle with hypotenuse \(b\) and angle \(180^\circ-C\) at \(C\).

    \(h=b\sin(180^\circ-C)=b\sin C\), so \(S=\tfrac12ah=\tfrac12ab\sin C\).

  6. In triangle \(ABC\), \(a=2b\cos C\). Prove that the triangle is isosceles.

    Show solution

    By the law of cosines, \(\cos C=\dfrac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}\).

    \(a=2b\cdot\dfrac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}\Rightarrow a^2=a^2+b^2-c^2\Rightarrow b=c\).

  7. Prove that the area of a triangle is \(S=2R^2\sin A\sin B\sin C\), and use it for an equilateral triangle in a circle of radius 4.

    Show solution

    \(S=\tfrac12ab\sin C\) with \(a=2R\sin A\), \(b=2R\sin B\): \(S=2R^2\sin A\sin B\sin C\).

    Equilateral, \(R=4\): \(S=32\left(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\right)^3=12\sqrt3\approx20.78\).

  8. Dimension control and identities.

    1. Which of these can be an area: \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\), \(a+b\), \(\dfrac{abc}{4R}\), \(a^2+b\)?
    2. Which are identities and which are equations: \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\), \(\sin x=0.5\), \(\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\)?
    Show solution

    (a) \(\tfrac12ab\sin C\) (length × length) and \(\dfrac{abc}{4R}\) (length³ ÷ length) are areas; \(a+b\) is a length; \(a^2+b\) mixes units and cannot be either.

    (b) \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\) and \(\tan x=\tfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\) are identities (true for every allowed \(x\)). \(\sin x=0.5\) is an equation.

  9. A hiker walks 300 m, turns 40° to the right, and walks another 200 m. How far is she from the start?

    Show solution

    The angle between the two legs at the turn is \(180^\circ-40^\circ=140^\circ\).

    \(d^2=300^2+200^2-2\cdot300\cdot200\cos140^\circ\approx221\,925\Rightarrow d\approx471.1\) m.

  10. In triangle \(ABC\), \(AB=AC=10\) and \(BC=12\). Find the angles, the area and \(R\).

    Show solution

    \(\cos B=\dfrac{6}{10}\Rightarrow B=C\approx53.13^\circ\), \(A\approx73.74^\circ\).

    Height \(=8\), area \(=48\).

    \(R=\dfrac{12}{2\sin A}=6.25\) (or \(R=\dfrac{abc}{4S}=\dfrac{1200}{192}=6.25\)).
