Before you start: For every "true or false" question, decide first whether the statement is about "every" object or about "some" object. Then you know whether you need a proof or an example.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Write the hypothesis and the conclusion of: "If a quadrilateral is a rectangle, then its diagonals are equal."

    Show solution

    Hypothesis: the quadrilateral is a rectangle. Conclusion: its diagonals are equal.

  2. Write the converse of "If a triangle is isosceles, then two of its angles are equal." Is it true?

    Show solution

    "If two angles of a triangle are equal, then the triangle is isosceles." True.

  3. Write the converse of "If two angles are vertical angles, they are equal." Is it true?

    Show solution

    "If two angles are equal, they are vertical angles." False: the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal but are not vertical angles.

  4. True or false: "Every quadrilateral with four equal sides is a square."

    Show solution

    False. Counterexample: a rhombus with an angle of 60°.

  5. True or false: "For every number \(x\), \(x^2\gt x\)."

    Show solution

    False: \(x=\tfrac12\) (or \(x=0\), or \(x=1\)).

  6. True or false: "There exists a triangle with angles 100°, 50° and 30°."

    Show solution

    True: \(100+50+30=180\), so such a triangle exists.

  7. Negate: "Every student in the class passed the test."

    Show solution

    There is a student in the class who did not pass the test.

  8. Negate: "There is a rectangle whose diagonals are perpendicular." Which is true, the statement or its negation?

    Show solution

    Negation: "In every rectangle, the diagonals are not perpendicular." The original statement is true (a square), so the negation is false.

  9. Fill in "necessary" or "sufficient": being a square is a ______ condition for being a rectangle; being a rectangle is a ______ condition for being a square.

    Show solution

    Sufficient; necessary.

  10. Which congruence theorem fits?

    1. two sides and the angle between them are equal
    2. three pairs of sides are equal
    3. two angles and the side between them are equal
    Show solution

    (a) SAS

    (b) SSS

    (c) ASA

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Prove that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.

    Show solution

    Triangle \(ABC\) with \(AB=AC\). Draw the bisector \(AD\) of angle \(A\).

    \(AB=AC\), \(\angle BAD=\angle CAD\), \(AD\) is common, so \(\triangle ABD\cong\triangle ACD\) (SAS).

    Hence \(\angle B=\angle C\).

  2. Prove that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

    Show solution

    Alternate angles: \(\angle BAO=\angle DCO\), \(\angle ABO=\angle CDO\); and \(AB=CD\).

    \(\triangle ABO\cong\triangle CDO\) (ASA), so \(AO=CO\) and \(BO=DO\).

  3. Prove the converse: if the diagonals of quadrilateral \(ABCD\) bisect each other at \(O\), then \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram.

    Show solution

    \(AO=CO\), \(BO=DO\), and \(\angle AOB=\angle COD\) (vertical angles), so \(\triangle AOB\cong\triangle COD\) (SAS).

    Then \(\angle OAB=\angle OCD\): these are alternate angles, so \(AB\parallel CD\). In the same way (\(\triangle AOD\cong\triangle COB\)) \(AD\parallel BC\).

  4. Necessary or sufficient?

    1. \(x=2\) and \(x^2=4\)
    2. \(x\gt 3\) and \(x\gt 1\)
    Show solution

    (a) \(x=2\) is sufficient for \(x^2=4\); \(x^2=4\) is necessary for \(x=2\) but not sufficient.

    (b) \(x\gt 3\) is sufficient for \(x\gt 1\), not necessary (\(x=2\)).

  5. Write as one "if and only if" statement: "If a quadrilateral is a parallelogram, its diagonals bisect each other" and its converse.

    Show solution

    A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if and only if its diagonals bisect each other.

  6. Prove by contradiction that a triangle cannot have two obtuse angles.

    Show solution

    Assume two angles are larger than \(90^\circ\). Their sum is larger than \(180^\circ\), so the third angle would be negative: impossible.

  7. Prove by contradiction that from a point \(P\) outside a line \(\ell\), only one perpendicular to \(\ell\) can be drawn.

    Show solution

    Assume there are two, meeting \(\ell\) at \(A\ne B\). Then triangle \(PAB\) has two right angles, at \(A\) and at \(B\): impossible (see Example 5 in the explanation).

  8. Give a counterexample: "If a quadrilateral has a pair of parallel sides and a pair of equal sides, it is a parallelogram."

    Show solution

    An isosceles trapezoid: one pair of parallel sides and equal legs, but it is not a parallelogram.

  9. Is it true that "for every triangle there exists a circle that passes through its three vertices"? What kind of statement is it?

    Show solution

    True: the circumscribed circle (Unit 18). It combines both quantifiers: for every triangle… there exists a circle.

  10. Write the contrapositive of "If \(n\) is odd, then \(n^2\) is odd", and use it to prove "If \(n^2\) is even, then \(n\) is even".

    Show solution

    Contrapositive: "If \(n^2\) is even, then \(n\) is even" — exactly the second statement.

    Proof of the first: \(n=2k+1\Rightarrow n^2=4k^2+4k+1=2(2k^2+2k)+1\), odd. A statement and its contrapositive are equivalent, so the second is proved too.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Prove that \(\sqrt2\) is not a fraction.

    Show solution

    Assume \(\sqrt2=\tfrac pq\) in lowest terms. \(p^2=2q^2\Rightarrow p\) even, \(p=2m\Rightarrow q^2=2m^2\Rightarrow q\) even. Both even contradicts "lowest terms".

  2. In triangle \(ABC\), the median \(AM\) to side \(BC\) equals half of \(BC\). Prove that \(\angle A=90^\circ\).

    Show solution

    \(MA=MB=MC\). Triangles \(MAB\) and \(MAC\) are isosceles: \(\angle B=\angle MAB\), \(\angle C=\angle MAC\).

    Angle sum in \(ABC\): \(\angle B+\angle C+\angle A=180^\circ\), and \(\angle A=\angle MAB+\angle MAC=\angle B+\angle C\).

    So \(2\angle A=180^\circ\Rightarrow\angle A=90^\circ\).

  3. Prove the converse of question 22: in a right triangle, the median to the hypotenuse equals half the hypotenuse. Then state the combined theorem.

    Show solution

    Right angle at \(A\). Extend the median \(AM\) to \(D\) with \(MD=AM\). The diagonals of \(ABDC\) bisect each other, so it is a parallelogram, and with a right angle it is a rectangle.

    The diagonals of a rectangle are equal: \(AD=BC\), so \(AM=\tfrac12AD=\tfrac12BC\).

    Combined: an angle of a triangle is right if and only if the median from it equals half the opposite side.

  4. Prove by contradiction that there is no largest even number.

    Show solution

    Assume \(N\) is the largest even number. Then \(N+2\) is even and larger than \(N\): a contradiction.

  5. A student checked that \(n^2+n+41\) is prime for \(n=1,2,\dots,10\) and concluded that it is prime for every whole number \(n\). What is wrong? Find a counterexample.

    Show solution

    Examples do not prove a "for every" statement.

    Counterexample: \(n=40\): \(1600+40+41=1681=41^2\), not prime.

  6. Decide which statement is true, and explain the difference.

    1. For every number \(x\) there exists a number \(y\) such that \(y\gt x\).
    2. There exists a number \(y\) such that for every number \(x\), \(y\gt x\).
    Show solution

    (a) True: for each \(x\) take \(y=x+1\).

    (b) False: whatever \(y\) is chosen, \(x=y\) breaks it. In (a) \(y\) may depend on \(x\); in (b) one \(y\) must work for all \(x\).

  7. Prove: (a) in a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal; (b) the converse: a quadrilateral whose two pairs of opposite angles are equal is a parallelogram.

    Show solution

    (a) \(\angle A+\angle B=180^\circ\) and \(\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ\) (co-interior angles between parallels), so \(\angle A=\angle C\). Similarly \(\angle B=\angle D\).

    (b) \(\angle A=\angle C=\alpha\), \(\angle B=\angle D=\beta\), and \(2\alpha+2\beta=360^\circ\Rightarrow\alpha+\beta=180^\circ\). Co-interior angles add to \(180^\circ\), so \(AD\parallel BC\) and \(AB\parallel DC\).

  8. Prove that a parallelogram whose diagonals are perpendicular is a rhombus.

    Show solution

    The diagonals bisect each other at \(O\): \(AO=OC\). With \(\angle AOB=\angle COB=90^\circ\) and \(BO\) common, \(\triangle AOB\cong\triangle COB\) (SAS), so \(AB=CB\).

    A parallelogram with two equal adjacent sides has all four sides equal: a rhombus.

  9. Prove that every point on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) is equally far from \(A\) and \(B\). State the converse.

    Show solution

    \(M\) is the midpoint of \(AB\), \(P\) is on the perpendicular bisector. \(AM=BM\), \(\angle PMA=\angle PMB=90^\circ\), \(PM\) common: \(\triangle PMA\cong\triangle PMB\) (SAS), so \(PA=PB\).

    Converse: every point equally far from \(A\) and \(B\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) (proved in Unit 18).

  10. Prove that an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior angles that are not next to it. Deduce that it is larger than each of them.

    Show solution

    At \(C\): exterior angle \(=180^\circ-\angle C=\angle A+\angle B\), since \(\angle A+\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ\).

    Both \(\angle A\) and \(\angle B\) are positive, so the exterior angle is larger than each of them.
