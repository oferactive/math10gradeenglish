Before you start: For every "true or false" question, decide first whether the statement is about "every" object or about "some" object. Then you know whether you need a proof or an example.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Write the hypothesis and the conclusion of: "If a quadrilateral is a rectangle, then its diagonals are equal."

    Show solution

    Idea. The hypothesis follows "if", the conclusion follows "then".

    Answer: hypothesis: the quadrilateral is a rectangle; conclusion: its diagonals are equal.

  2. Write the converse of "If a triangle is isosceles, then two of its angles are equal." Is it true?

    Show solution

    Idea. Swap hypothesis and conclusion, then decide using a known theorem.

    Converse: "If two angles of a triangle are equal, then the triangle is isosceles."

    This is a known theorem: the sides opposite equal angles are equal.

    Answer: the converse is true.

  3. Write the converse of "If two angles are vertical angles, they are equal." Is it true?

    Show solution

    Idea. Swap, then look for a counterexample: two equal angles that are not vertical angles.

    Converse: "If two angles are equal, they are vertical angles."

    The two base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal, but they are not formed by two crossing lines.

    Answer: the converse is false.

  4. True or false: "Every quadrilateral with four equal sides is a square."

    Show solution

    Idea. Look for a quadrilateral with four equal sides but angles that are not \(90^\circ\).

    A rhombus with angles \(60^\circ\) and \(120^\circ\) has four equal sides but is not a square.

    Answer: false.

  5. True or false: "For every number \(x\), \(x^2\gt x\)."

    Show solution

    Idea. A "for every" statement needs only one counterexample. Try numbers between 0 and 1.

    \(x=\tfrac12\): \(x^2=\tfrac14\), which is less than \(\tfrac12\).

    Answer: false (also \(x=0\) or \(x=1\), where \(x^2=x\)).

  6. True or false: "There exists a triangle with angles 100°, 50° and 30°."

    Show solution

    Idea. A triangle with given angles exists exactly when the angles are positive and add up to \(180^\circ\).

    \(100+50+30=180\).

    Answer: true.

  7. Negate: "Every student in the class passed the test."

    Show solution

    Idea. Negation of "every … passed" is "at least one … did not pass".

    Answer: "There is a student in the class who did not pass the test."

    Watch out: "No student passed" is not the negation; it is a much stronger statement.

  8. Negate: "There is a rectangle whose diagonals are perpendicular." Which is true, the statement or its negation?

    Show solution

    Idea. The negation of "there exists" is "for every … not". Then decide which of the two is true.

    Negation: "In every rectangle, the diagonals are not perpendicular."

    A square is a rectangle whose diagonals are perpendicular, so the original statement is true.

    Answer: the statement is true; its negation is false.

  9. Fill in "necessary" or "sufficient": being a square is a ______ condition for being a rectangle; being a rectangle is a ______ condition for being a square.

    Show solution

    Idea. Every square is a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square.

    Square \(\Rightarrow\) rectangle: being a square is enough (sufficient).

    Rectangle is needed for square: you cannot be a square without being a rectangle (necessary).

    Answer: sufficient; necessary.

  10. Which congruence theorem fits?

    1. two sides and the angle between them are equal
    2. three pairs of sides are equal
    3. two angles and the side between them are equal
    Show solution

    Idea. The letters list the equal parts in order; the angle in SAS is between the two sides, the side in ASA is between the two angles.

    (a) Side, angle between, side: SAS.

    (b) Side, side, side: SSS.

    (c) Angle, side between, angle: ASA.

    Answer: (a) SAS; (b) SSS; (c) ASA.

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Prove that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.

    Show solution

    Idea. Build two triangles that are congruent and contain the two base angles. The angle bisector from \(A\) does this.

    Triangle \(ABC\) with \(AB=AC\). Let \(AD\) bisect angle \(A\), with \(D\) on \(BC\).

    In \(\triangle ABD\) and \(\triangle ACD\): \(AB=AC\) (given), \(\angle BAD=\angle CAD\) (bisector), \(AD=AD\) (common).

    So \(\triangle ABD\cong\triangle ACD\) (SAS).

    Corresponding angles are equal: \(\angle B=\angle C\).

    Answer: proved.

  2. Prove that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

    Show solution

    Idea. Let the diagonals meet at \(O\). Show \(\triangle ABO\cong\triangle CDO\); then the matching parts of the diagonals are equal.

    \(AB\parallel CD\), so the alternate angles are equal: \(\angle BAO=\angle DCO\) and \(\angle ABO=\angle CDO\).

    Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal: \(AB=CD\).

    So \(\triangle ABO\cong\triangle CDO\) (ASA).

    Hence \(AO=CO\) and \(BO=DO\).

    Answer: the diagonals bisect each other.

  3. Prove the converse: if the diagonals of quadrilateral \(ABCD\) bisect each other at \(O\), then \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram.

    Show solution

    Idea. Use congruent triangles to get equal alternate angles, which prove the sides are parallel.

    \(AO=CO\), \(BO=DO\) (given), \(\angle AOB=\angle COD\) (vertical angles).

    \(\triangle AOB\cong\triangle COD\) (SAS), so \(\angle OAB=\angle OCD\).

    These are alternate angles on the line \(AC\), so \(AB\parallel CD\).

    In the same way \(\triangle AOD\cong\triangle COB\) gives \(AD\parallel BC\).

    Answer: both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, so \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram.

  4. Necessary or sufficient?

    1. \(x=2\) and \(x^2=4\)
    2. \(x\gt 3\) and \(x\gt 1\)
    Show solution

    Idea. "P is sufficient for Q" means P \(\Rightarrow\) Q. "P is necessary for Q" means Q \(\Rightarrow\) P.

    (a) \(x=2\Rightarrow x^2=4\): \(x=2\) is sufficient. \(x^2=4\) does not give \(x=2\) (also \(-2\)), so \(x^2=4\) is necessary for \(x=2\) but not sufficient.

    (b) \(x\gt 3\Rightarrow x\gt 1\): sufficient. \(x=2\) satisfies \(x\gt 1\) without \(x\gt 3\), so not necessary.

    Answer: as above.

  5. Write as one "if and only if" statement: "If a quadrilateral is a parallelogram, its diagonals bisect each other" and its converse.

    Show solution

    Idea. When a theorem and its converse are both true, write them together with "if and only if".

    Answer: "A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if and only if its diagonals bisect each other."

  6. Prove by contradiction that a triangle cannot have two obtuse angles.

    Show solution

    Idea. Assume the opposite and reach an impossibility with the angle sum.

    Assume angles \(A\gt 90^\circ\) and \(B\gt 90^\circ\).

    Then \(A+B\gt 180^\circ\), so \(C=180^\circ-A-B\lt 0\).

    An angle of a triangle cannot be negative or zero: a contradiction.

    Answer: a triangle has at most one obtuse angle.

  7. Prove by contradiction that from a point \(P\) outside a line \(\ell\), only one perpendicular to \(\ell\) can be drawn.

    Show solution

    Idea. Assume two perpendiculars exist and look at the triangle they form.

    Suppose \(PA\perp\ell\) and \(PB\perp\ell\) with \(A\ne B\) on \(\ell\).

    Triangle \(PAB\) has \(\angle A=90^\circ\) and \(\angle B=90^\circ\).

    Then \(\angle A+\angle B=180^\circ\), leaving \(0^\circ\) for \(\angle P\): impossible.

    Answer: only one perpendicular can be drawn.

  8. Give a counterexample: "If a quadrilateral has a pair of parallel sides and a pair of equal sides, it is a parallelogram."

    Show solution

    Idea. Find a shape with one pair of parallel sides and a pair of equal sides that is not a parallelogram.

    An isosceles trapezoid: its bases are parallel and its legs are equal.

    Its legs are not parallel, so it is not a parallelogram.

    Answer: the isosceles trapezoid is a counterexample.

  9. Is it true that "for every triangle there exists a circle that passes through its three vertices"? What kind of statement is it?

    Show solution

    Idea. The perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet at a point equally far from all three vertices. Look at the order of the quantifiers.

    That point is the centre of the circumscribed circle, so such a circle always exists.

    The form is: "for every triangle, there exists a circle such that…".

    Answer: true; it is a "for every … there exists …" statement.

  10. Write the contrapositive of "If \(n\) is odd, then \(n^2\) is odd", and use it to prove "If \(n^2\) is even, then \(n\) is even".

    Show solution

    Idea. The contrapositive of "If P then Q" is "If not Q then not P"; it is always equivalent to the original.

    Not Q: "\(n^2\) is not odd" = "\(n^2\) is even". Not P: "\(n\) is even".

    Contrapositive: "If \(n^2\) is even, then \(n\) is even."

    Prove the original: \(n=2k+1\Rightarrow n^2=4k^2+4k+1=2(2k^2+2k)+1\): odd.

    Answer: the contrapositive is exactly the second statement, so proving the first proves it.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Prove that \(\sqrt2\) is not a fraction.

    Show solution

    Idea. Proof by contradiction. Assume \(\sqrt2=\tfrac pq\) as a fully reduced fraction, and show that \(p\) and \(q\) are both even.

    \(\sqrt2=\tfrac pq\Rightarrow2=\tfrac{p^2}{q^2}\Rightarrow p^2=2q^2\).

    So \(p^2\) is even, hence \(p\) is even (question 20): \(p=2m\).

    \(4m^2=2q^2\Rightarrow q^2=2m^2\): so \(q\) is even too.

    Both even means the fraction could be reduced by 2: a contradiction.

    Answer: \(\sqrt2\) is not a fraction (it is irrational).

  2. In triangle \(ABC\), the median \(AM\) to side \(BC\) equals half of \(BC\). Prove that \(\angle A=90^\circ\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(AM=BM=CM\) gives two isosceles triangles. Their base angles together make up angle \(A\).

    \(M\) is the midpoint, so \(BM=CM=\tfrac12BC=AM\).

    In \(\triangle MAB\): \(MA=MB\Rightarrow\angle MAB=\angle B\). In \(\triangle MAC\): \(\angle MAC=\angle C\).

    \(\angle A=\angle MAB+\angle MAC=\angle B+\angle C\).

    Angle sum: \(\angle A+(\angle B+\angle C)=180^\circ\Rightarrow2\angle A=180^\circ\).

    Answer: \(\angle A=90^\circ\).

  3. Prove the converse of question 22: in a right triangle, the median to the hypotenuse equals half the hypotenuse. Then state the combined theorem.

    Show solution

    Idea. Double the median to make a quadrilateral whose diagonals bisect each other (a parallelogram), then use the right angle.

    Extend \(AM\) to \(D\) with \(MD=AM\). Now \(BM=MC\) and \(AM=MD\): the diagonals of \(ABDC\) bisect each other, so it is a parallelogram (question 13).

    A parallelogram with a right angle at \(A\) is a rectangle.

    The diagonals of a rectangle are equal: \(AD=BC\).

    \(AM=\tfrac12AD=\tfrac12BC\).

    Answer: proved. Combined: an angle of a triangle is right if and only if the median from its vertex equals half the opposite side.

  4. Prove by contradiction that there is no largest even number.

    Show solution

    Idea. Assume the largest even number exists and build a larger one.

    Assume \(N\) is the largest even number.

    \(N+2\) is also even, and \(N+2\gt N\).

    This contradicts "\(N\) is the largest".

    Answer: there is no largest even number.

  5. A student checked that \(n^2+n+41\) is prime for \(n=1,2,\dots,10\) and concluded that it is prime for every whole number \(n\). What is wrong? Find a counterexample.

    Show solution

    Idea. Checking examples can never prove a "for every" statement; one counterexample disproves it. Look for an \(n\) that makes every term divisible by 41.

    Ten examples say nothing about \(n=11,12,\dots\)

    \(n=40\): \(40^2+40+41=40\cdot41+41=41\cdot41=1681\).

    Answer: the reasoning is wrong; \(n=40\) gives \(1681=41^2\), which is not prime.

  6. Decide which statement is true, and explain the difference.

    1. For every number \(x\) there exists a number \(y\) such that \(y\gt x\).
    2. There exists a number \(y\) such that for every number \(x\), \(y\gt x\).
    Show solution

    Idea. The order of "for every" and "there exists" matters. In (a) \(y\) may be chosen after \(x\); in (b) one \(y\) must work for all \(x\) at once.

    (a) Given any \(x\), choose \(y=x+1\gt x\). True.

    (b) Suppose such a \(y\) exists. Take \(x=y\): then \(y\gt y\) is false. So (b) is false.

    Answer: (a) is true, (b) is false.

  7. Prove: (a) in a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal; (b) the converse: a quadrilateral whose two pairs of opposite angles are equal is a parallelogram.

    Show solution

    Idea. (a) Co-interior angles between parallel lines add to \(180^\circ\). (b) Use the angle sum \(360^\circ\) of a quadrilateral to get co-interior angles adding to \(180^\circ\).

    (a) \(AD\parallel BC\): \(\angle A+\angle B=180^\circ\). \(AB\parallel DC\): \(\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ\).

    Subtract: \(\angle A=\angle C\). Similarly \(\angle B=\angle D\).

    (b) Let \(\angle A=\angle C=\alpha\) and \(\angle B=\angle D=\beta\). The angle sum: \(2\alpha+2\beta=360^\circ\Rightarrow\alpha+\beta=180^\circ\).

    \(\angle A+\angle B=180^\circ\Rightarrow AD\parallel BC\); \(\angle A+\angle D=180^\circ\Rightarrow AB\parallel DC\).

    Answer: both proved.

  8. Prove that a parallelogram whose diagonals are perpendicular is a rhombus.

    Show solution

    Idea. Show two adjacent sides are equal using congruent triangles at the centre; a parallelogram with equal adjacent sides is a rhombus.

    The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other: \(AO=CO\).

    \(\angle AOB=\angle COB=90^\circ\) (given), and \(BO\) is common.

    \(\triangle AOB\cong\triangle COB\) (SAS), so \(AB=CB\).

    In a parallelogram \(AB=CD\) and \(BC=DA\), so all four sides are equal.

    Answer: the parallelogram is a rhombus.

  9. Prove that every point on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) is equally far from \(A\) and \(B\). State the converse.

    Show solution

    Idea. Join \(P\) to \(A\) and \(B\), and compare the two triangles on either side of the bisector.

    \(AM=BM\) (midpoint), \(\angle PMA=\angle PMB=90^\circ\), \(PM\) is common.

    \(\triangle PMA\cong\triangle PMB\) (SAS), so \(PA=PB\).

    Converse: "Every point that is equally far from \(A\) and \(B\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\)."

    Answer: proved; the converse is also true (Unit 18), so the perpendicular bisector is exactly the set of points equally far from \(A\) and \(B\).

  10. Prove that an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior angles that are not next to it. Deduce that it is larger than each of them.

    Show solution

    Idea. The exterior angle and the interior angle at \(C\) together form a straight angle (\(180^\circ\)). Compare with the angle sum of the triangle.

    Exterior angle at \(C\) \(=180^\circ-\angle C\).

    Angle sum: \(\angle A+\angle B=180^\circ-\angle C\).

    So the exterior angle \(=\angle A+\angle B\).

    \(\angle B\gt 0\), so \(\angle A+\angle B\gt \angle A\); likewise it is larger than \(\angle B\).

    Answer: proved.
