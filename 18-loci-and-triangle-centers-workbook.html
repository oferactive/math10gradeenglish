Before you start: Draw an accurate figure with a ruler for every question. Many claims about special points are easy to see in a good drawing before you prove them.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Describe the locus of points at distance 3 cm from a point \(O\).

    Show solution

    Idea. All points at a fixed distance from a point form a circle.

    Answer: the circle with centre \(O\) and radius 3 cm.

  2. Describe the locus of points equally far from two points \(A\) and \(B\).

    Show solution

    Idea. This is the perpendicular bisector theorem and its converse (questions 11 and Unit 17).

    Answer: the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) (the line through the midpoint of \(AB\), perpendicular to it).

  3. Describe the locus of points equally far from two intersecting lines.

    Show solution

    Idea. A point equally far from two sides of an angle lies on its bisector. Two crossing lines form four angles, so there are two bisectors.

    The two bisectors are perpendicular, since adjacent angles add to \(180^\circ\) and their halves add to \(90^\circ\).

    Answer: the two bisectors of the angles between the lines (a pair of perpendicular lines).

  4. Describe the locus of points at distance 2 cm from a line \(\ell\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Points at a fixed distance from a line can be on either side of it.

    Answer: two lines parallel to \(\ell\), one on each side, each at distance 2 cm.

  5. Find the centroid of the triangle \((0,0)\), \((6,0)\), \((3,9)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The centroid is the average of the vertices: \(\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2+x_3}3,\dfrac{y_1+y_2+y_3}3\right)\).

    \(x=\dfrac{0+6+3}3=3\), \(y=\dfrac{0+0+9}3=3\).

    Answer: \(G(3,3)\).

  6. The median \(AM\) of a triangle is 12 cm. Find \(AG\) and \(GM\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(AG:GM=2:1\): split the median into 3 equal parts.

    One part \(=\tfrac{12}3=4\).

    Answer: \(AG=8\) cm, \(GM=4\) cm.

  7. A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. Where is the circumcentre, and what is \(R\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. In a right triangle the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse.

    Hypotenuse \(=\sqrt{36+64}=10\).

    Answer: the midpoint of the hypotenuse; \(R=5\).

  8. Is the triangle with sides 9, 12, 15 right-angled?

    Show solution

    Idea. Converse of Pythagoras with the longest side 15.

    \(9^2+12^2=81+144=225=15^2\).

    Answer: yes (it is the 3-4-5 triangle scaled by 3).

  9. Name the special point.

    1. the centre of the circle through the three vertices
    2. the centre of the circle that touches the three sides
    Show solution

    Idea. A circle through the vertices is circumscribed; a circle touching the sides is inscribed.

    Answer: (a) the circumcentre; (b) the incentre.

  10. Where is the circumcentre of an obtuse triangle?

    Show solution

    Idea. The angle at the obtuse vertex stands on an arc larger than a semicircle, so the centre is on the other side of the longest side.

    Answer: outside the triangle, beyond the longest side (opposite the obtuse angle).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Prove: if \(PA=PB\), then \(P\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Join \(P\) to the midpoint \(M\) of \(AB\). Congruent triangles show that \(PM\) is perpendicular to \(AB\).

    \(PA=PB\) (given), \(AM=BM\) (midpoint), \(PM\) common.

    \(\triangle PAM\cong\triangle PBM\) (SSS), so \(\angle PMA=\angle PMB\).

    They are adjacent angles on a line: their sum is \(180^\circ\), so each is \(90^\circ\).

    Answer: \(PM\) is perpendicular to \(AB\) through its midpoint, so \(P\) is on the perpendicular bisector.

  2. Prove: every point on the bisector of an angle is equally far from its two sides.

    Show solution

    Idea. The distance from a point to a line is measured along a perpendicular. Drop perpendiculars to both sides and compare the two right triangles.

    \(PK\perp\) one side, \(PL\perp\) the other.

    \(\angle PAK=\angle PAL\) (bisector), \(\angle K=\angle L=90^\circ\), so the third angles \(\angle APK=\angle APL\) are equal too.

    \(AP\) is common. \(\triangle APK\cong\triangle APL\) (ASA).

    Answer: \(PK=PL\): \(P\) is equally far from both sides.

  3. Prove that the three perpendicular bisectors of a triangle meet at one point.

    Show solution

    Idea. Take the point where two perpendicular bisectors meet, and show that it is also on the third.

    Let \(O\) be on the bisector of \(AB\): \(OA=OB\).

    \(O\) is on the bisector of \(BC\): \(OB=OC\).

    So \(OA=OC\), and by question 11 \(O\) is on the bisector of \(AC\).

    Answer: all three bisectors pass through \(O\), which is equally far from the three vertices.

  4. Find the circumcentre and \(R\) for \(A(0,0)\), \(B(8,0)\), \(C(0,6)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(AB\) is on the \(x\)-axis and \(AC\) on the \(y\)-axis, so the right angle is at \(A\). The circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse \(BC\).

    Midpoint of \(B(8,0)\) and \(C(0,6)\): \((4,3)\).

    \(BC=\sqrt{64+36}=10\Rightarrow R=5\).

    Answer: \(O(4,3)\), \(R=5\).

    Check: \(OA=\sqrt{16+9}=5\) ✓

  5. Find the circumcentre and \(R\) for \(A(0,0)\), \(B(6,0)\), \(C(2,4)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Intersect two perpendicular bisectors. The one of \(AB\) is easy (\(AB\) is horizontal); for the second use \(OA=OC\).

    Midpoint of \(AB\) is \((3,0)\): the bisector is \(x=3\).

    \(OA^2=OC^2\): \(x^2+y^2=(x-2)^2+(y-4)^2\Rightarrow0=-4x+4-8y+16\Rightarrow x+2y=5\).

    With \(x=3\): \(2y=2\Rightarrow y=1\).

    \(R=OA=\sqrt{9+1}=\sqrt{10}\).

    Answer: \(O(3,1)\), \(R=\sqrt{10}\).

    Check: \(OC=\sqrt{1+9}=\sqrt{10}\) ✓

  6. A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. Find the radius of the inscribed circle.

    Show solution

    Idea. Use \(S=r\cdot s\) (question 24): \(r=\dfrac Ss\).

    Hypotenuse 10. \(S=\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot8=24\). \(s=\tfrac{6+8+10}2=12\).

    \(r=\dfrac{24}{12}=2\).

    Answer: \(r=2\).

  7. Where is the orthocentre of a right triangle? Explain.

    Show solution

    Idea. An altitude from a vertex is perpendicular to the opposite side. In a right triangle the legs are perpendicular to each other.

    The altitude from \(B\) to \(AC\) is the leg \(BC\) itself (if the right angle is at \(C\)); the altitude from \(A\) is the leg \(AC\).

    Two altitudes meet at \(C\), so the third must pass through \(C\) too.

    Answer: the orthocentre is the vertex of the right angle.

  8. Find the centroid of \(A(1,2)\), \(B(7,4)\), \(C(4,9)\), and check the ratio \(AG:GM\), where \(M\) is the midpoint of \(BC\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Average the vertices for \(G\); find \(M\); compare the vectors \(\overrightarrow{AG}\) and \(\overrightarrow{GM}\).

    \(G=\left(\tfrac{1+7+4}3,\tfrac{2+4+9}3\right)=(4,5)\).

    \(M=\left(\tfrac{7+4}2,\tfrac{4+9}2\right)=(5.5,\ 6.5)\).

    \(\overrightarrow{AG}=(3,3)\), \(\overrightarrow{GM}=(1.5,1.5)\).

    Answer: \(G(4,5)\); \(\overrightarrow{AG}=2\overrightarrow{GM}\), so \(AG:GM=2:1\) ✓

  9. In triangles \(ABC\) and \(DEF\): \(AB=DE=5\), \(AC=DF=7\), \(\angle B=\angle E=70^\circ\). Are they congruent? What if instead \(\angle C=\angle F\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Two sides and a non-included angle determine the triangle only when the angle is opposite the larger of the two sides (the fourth congruence theorem).

    With \(\angle B\): it is opposite \(AC=7\), the larger side. So the triangles are congruent.

    With \(\angle C\): it is opposite \(AB=5\), the smaller side. The side \(AB\) can "swing" to two positions, giving two different triangles.

    Answer: with \(\angle B=\angle E\), yes; with \(\angle C=\angle F\), not necessarily.

  10. Show that a triangle with sides \(2n\), \(n^2-1\), \(n^2+1\) (\(n\gt 1\)) is right-angled.

    Show solution

    Idea. Check \(a^2+b^2=c^2\) with \(c=n^2+1\), the longest side, and use the converse of Pythagoras.

    \((2n)^2+(n^2-1)^2=4n^2+n^4-2n^2+1=n^4+2n^2+1\).

    \(=(n^2+1)^2\).

    Answer: the triangle is right-angled, with the right angle opposite \(n^2+1\). (\(n=2\): 4, 3, 5.)

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Find the orthocentre of \(A(0,0)\), \(B(6,0)\), \(C(2,4)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Intersect two altitudes. Each passes through a vertex and is perpendicular to the opposite side.

    \(AB\) is horizontal, so the altitude from \(C\) is \(x=2\).

    \(BC\): slope \(\dfrac{4-0}{2-6}=-1\). The altitude from \(A\) has slope 1: \(y=x\).

    Intersection: \(x=2\), \(y=2\).

    Answer: \(H(2,2)\).

  2. For the triangle of question 21, the circumcentre is \(O(3,1)\) (question 15). Find the centroid \(G\), and show that \(O\), \(G\), \(H\) are on one line with \(HG=2\,GO\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Compute \(G\), then compare the vectors \(\overrightarrow{HG}\) and \(\overrightarrow{GO}\). If one is a multiple of the other, the points are on one line.

    \(G=\left(\tfrac{0+6+2}3,\tfrac{0+0+4}3\right)=\left(\tfrac83,\tfrac43\right)\).

    \(\overrightarrow{HG}=\left(\tfrac83-2,\tfrac43-2\right)=\left(\tfrac23,-\tfrac23\right)\).

    \(\overrightarrow{GO}=\left(3-\tfrac83,1-\tfrac43\right)=\left(\tfrac13,-\tfrac13\right)\).

    \(\overrightarrow{HG}=2\overrightarrow{GO}\).

    Answer: \(G\left(\tfrac83,\tfrac43\right)\); \(H\), \(G\), \(O\) are collinear (Euler's line) and \(HG=2\,GO\).

  3. Prove that the centroid divides each median in the ratio \(2:1\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The segment joining the midpoints of two sides (a midsegment) is parallel to the third side and half as long. It creates two similar triangles with ratio \(\tfrac12\).

    \(D\) is the midpoint of \(BC\), \(E\) of \(AC\). \(DE\parallel AB\), \(DE=\tfrac12AB\).

    Alternate angles: \(\angle GDE=\angle GAB\), \(\angle GED=\angle GBA\).

    \(\triangle GDE\sim\triangle GAB\) (AA), ratio \(\dfrac{DE}{AB}=\tfrac12\).

    So \(GD=\tfrac12GA\), i.e. \(AG:GD=2:1\).

    The third median also cuts \(AD\) at the point dividing it \(2:1\), which is the same point \(G\).

    Answer: proved; the three medians meet at \(G\), and \(G\) divides each in the ratio \(2:1\).

  4. Prove that \(S=r\cdot s\), where \(r\) is the radius of the inscribed circle and \(s\) half the perimeter.

    Show solution

    Idea. Split the triangle into three triangles from the incentre \(I\). The radii to the points of contact are perpendicular to the sides, so each small triangle has height \(r\).

    \(S_{IBC}=\tfrac12ar\), \(S_{ICA}=\tfrac12br\), \(S_{IAB}=\tfrac12cr\).

    \(S=\tfrac12r(a+b+c)=r\cdot\dfrac{a+b+c}2=r\cdot s\).

    Answer: proved.

  5. A triangle has sides 13, 14 and 15. The height to the side 14 is 12. Find the area, \(r\) and \(R\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Area from the base and height; \(r=\dfrac Ss\); \(R=\dfrac{abc}{4S}\).

    \(S=\tfrac12\cdot14\cdot12=84\).

    \(s=\tfrac{13+14+15}2=21\Rightarrow r=\tfrac{84}{21}=4\).

    \(R=\dfrac{13\cdot14\cdot15}{4\cdot84}=\dfrac{2730}{336}=8.125\).

    Answer: \(S=84\), \(r=4\), \(R=8.125\).

  6. \(A\) and \(B\) are fixed, \(AB=6\). Find the locus of points \(P\) for which triangle \(PAB\) has area 12.

    Show solution

    Idea. With base \(AB=6\) fixed, the area depends only on the height \(h\), the distance from \(P\) to the line \(AB\).

    \(\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot h=12\Rightarrow h=4\).

    Points at distance 4 from a line: two parallel lines (question 4).

    Answer: two lines parallel to \(AB\), at distance 4 on each side.

  7. Find the locus of centres of circles that touch both sides of an angle. A circle of radius 2 touches both sides of a 60° angle: how far is its centre from the vertex?

    Show solution

    Idea. A circle touching both sides has its centre equally far from them, so the centre is on the bisector. Then use the right triangle: centre, vertex, point of contact.

    Locus: the bisector of the angle (inside it).

    The bisector makes \(30^\circ\) with each side. The radius 2 is perpendicular to the side, opposite the \(30^\circ\) angle.

    \(\sin30^\circ=\dfrac2d\Rightarrow d=\dfrac2{0.5}=4\).

    Answer: the angle bisector; the centre is 4 from the vertex.

  8. \(A\) and \(B\) are fixed. Find the locus of points \(C\) for which \(\angle ACB=90^\circ\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Use the theorem: the angle at \(C\) is right exactly when the median from \(C\) equals half of \(AB\) (Unit 17).

    \(\angle ACB=90^\circ\iff MC=\tfrac12AB\), where \(M\) is the midpoint of \(AB\).

    The points at distance \(\tfrac12AB\) from \(M\) form the circle with diameter \(AB\).

    \(A\) and \(B\) themselves do not form a triangle.

    Answer: the circle with diameter \(AB\), without \(A\) and \(B\) (Thales' theorem).

  9. Prove that in an isosceles triangle, the median to the base is also the altitude, the angle bisector and the perpendicular bisector of the base. What follows for the four special points?

    Show solution

    Idea. Show the two halves of the triangle are congruent; their equal angles give the bisector and the right angle.

    \(AB=AC\), \(BM=CM\), \(AM\) common: \(\triangle ABM\cong\triangle ACM\) (SSS).

    \(\angle BAM=\angle CAM\): \(AM\) is the angle bisector.

    \(\angle AMB=\angle AMC\), and they add to \(180^\circ\), so both are \(90^\circ\): \(AM\) is the altitude, and since \(M\) is the midpoint, also the perpendicular bisector.

    Answer: one line plays all four roles, so the circumcentre, incentre, centroid and orthocentre all lie on it.

  10. An equilateral triangle has side 6. Show that all four special points coincide, and find \(R\) and \(r\).

    Show solution

    Idea. In an equilateral triangle every side can be taken as the base of an isosceles triangle, so question 29 applies three times. Then the centroid divides the height \(2:1\).

    For each side, the median, altitude, bisector and perpendicular bisector coincide. Three such lines meet at one point, which is all four special points.

    Height \(=\sqrt{36-9}=\sqrt{27}=3\sqrt3\).

    \(R\) = distance from the centre to a vertex \(=\tfrac23\cdot3\sqrt3=2\sqrt3\).

    \(r\) = distance from the centre to a side \(=\tfrac13\cdot3\sqrt3=\sqrt3\).

    Answer: the points coincide; \(R=2\sqrt3\approx3.46\), \(r=\sqrt3\approx1.73\) (and \(R=2r\)).
