Before you start: Draw an accurate figure with a ruler for every question. Many claims about special points are easy to see in a good drawing before you prove them.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Describe the locus of points at distance 3 cm from a point \(O\).

    Show solution

    A circle with centre \(O\) and radius 3 cm.

  2. Describe the locus of points equally far from two points \(A\) and \(B\).

    Show solution

    The perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

  3. Describe the locus of points equally far from two intersecting lines.

    Show solution

    The two bisectors of the angles between the lines (two perpendicular lines).

  4. Describe the locus of points at distance 2 cm from a line \(\ell\).

    Show solution

    Two lines parallel to \(\ell\), at distance 2 cm on each side.

  5. Find the centroid of the triangle \((0,0)\), \((6,0)\), \((3,9)\).

    Show solution

    \(\left(\tfrac{9}3,\tfrac93\right)=(3,3)\)

  6. The median \(AM\) of a triangle is 12 cm. Find \(AG\) and \(GM\).

    Show solution

    \(AG=8\) cm, \(GM=4\) cm.

  7. A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. Where is the circumcentre, and what is \(R\)?

    Show solution

    Hypotenuse \(10\). The circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse; \(R=5\).

  8. Is the triangle with sides 9, 12, 15 right-angled?

    Show solution

    \(81+144=225\). Yes.

  9. Name the special point.

    1. the centre of the circle through the three vertices
    2. the centre of the circle that touches the three sides
    Show solution

    (a) the circumcentre

    (b) the incentre

  10. Where is the circumcentre of an obtuse triangle?

    Show solution

    Outside the triangle, beyond the longest side.

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Prove: if \(PA=PB\), then \(P\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

    Show solution

    \(M\) midpoint of \(AB\). \(PA=PB\), \(AM=BM\), \(PM\) common: \(\triangle PAM\cong\triangle PBM\) (SSS).

    \(\angle PMA=\angle PMB\) and their sum is \(180^\circ\), so both are \(90^\circ\).

  2. Prove: every point on the bisector of an angle is equally far from its two sides.

    Show solution

    \(P\) on the bisector of angle \(A\); \(PK\) and \(PL\) are perpendicular to the sides.

    Triangles \(APK\) and \(APL\): right angles at \(K,L\), equal angles at \(A\), common hypotenuse \(AP\). So they are congruent (the third angles are equal too, ASA), and \(PK=PL\).

  3. Prove that the three perpendicular bisectors of a triangle meet at one point.

    Show solution

    \(O\) on the bisectors of \(AB\) and \(BC\) gives \(OA=OB=OC\). \(OA=OC\) means \(O\) is on the bisector of \(AC\).

  4. Find the circumcentre and \(R\) for \(A(0,0)\), \(B(8,0)\), \(C(0,6)\).

    Show solution

    Right angle at \(A\): the circumcentre is the midpoint of \(BC\), \((4,3)\). \(R=\tfrac12BC=5\).

  5. Find the circumcentre and \(R\) for \(A(0,0)\), \(B(6,0)\), \(C(2,4)\).

    Show solution

    Perpendicular bisector of \(AB\): \(x=3\).

    \(OA=OC\): \(x^2+y^2=(x-2)^2+(y-4)^2\Rightarrow x+2y=5\Rightarrow y=1\).

    \(O(3,1)\), \(R=\sqrt{10}\).

  6. A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. Find the radius of the inscribed circle.

    Show solution

    Area \(24\), half-perimeter \(s=12\): \(r=\dfrac{S}{s}=2\).

  7. Where is the orthocentre of a right triangle? Explain.

    Show solution

    At the vertex of the right angle: each leg is the altitude to the other leg, and the legs meet at that vertex.

  8. Find the centroid of \(A(1,2)\), \(B(7,4)\), \(C(4,9)\), and check the ratio \(AG:GM\), where \(M\) is the midpoint of \(BC\).

    Show solution

    \(G(4,5)\), \(M(5.5,\ 6.5)\).

    \(\overrightarrow{AG}=(3,3)\), \(\overrightarrow{GM}=(1.5,1.5)\): ratio \(2:1\) ✓

  9. In triangles \(ABC\) and \(DEF\): \(AB=DE=5\), \(AC=DF=7\), \(\angle B=\angle E=70^\circ\). Are they congruent? What if instead \(\angle C=\angle F\)?

    Show solution

    \(\angle B\) is opposite \(AC=7\), the larger of the two sides: congruent by the fourth theorem.

    \(\angle C\) is opposite the smaller side \(AB=5\): not enough; there may be two different triangles.

  10. Show that a triangle with sides \(2n\), \(n^2-1\), \(n^2+1\) (\(n\gt 1\)) is right-angled.

    Show solution

    \((2n)^2+(n^2-1)^2=4n^2+n^4-2n^2+1=n^4+2n^2+1=(n^2+1)^2\). By the converse of Pythagoras the angle opposite \(n^2+1\) is right.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Find the orthocentre of \(A(0,0)\), \(B(6,0)\), \(C(2,4)\).

    Show solution

    \(H(2,2)\) (see Example 2).

  2. For the triangle of question 21, the circumcentre is \(O(3,1)\) (question 15). Find the centroid \(G\), and show that \(O\), \(G\), \(H\) are on one line with \(HG=2\,GO\).

    Show solution

    \(G\left(\tfrac83,\tfrac43\right)\).

    \(\overrightarrow{GO}=\left(\tfrac13,-\tfrac13\right)\), \(\overrightarrow{HG}=\left(\tfrac23,-\tfrac23\right)=2\overrightarrow{GO}\).

    The three points lie on one line (Euler's line) and \(HG=2\,GO\).

  3. Prove that the centroid divides each median in the ratio \(2:1\).

    Show solution

    Medians \(AD\) and \(BE\) meet at \(G\). \(DE\) is a midsegment: \(DE\parallel AB\), \(DE=\tfrac12AB\).

    \(\angle GDE=\angle GAB\), \(\angle GED=\angle GBA\) (alternate angles), so \(\triangle GDE\sim\triangle GAB\) with ratio \(\tfrac12\).

    \(GD=\tfrac12GA\), so \(AG:GD=2:1\). The third median also cuts \(AD\) in this ratio, so it passes through the same point.

  4. Prove that \(S=r\cdot s\), where \(r\) is the radius of the inscribed circle and \(s\) half the perimeter.

    Show solution

    Join \(I\) to the vertices. Triangles \(IBC\), \(ICA\), \(IAB\) have bases \(a,b,c\) and height \(r\).

    \(S=\tfrac12r(a+b+c)=r\cdot s\).

  5. A triangle has sides 13, 14 and 15. The height to the side 14 is 12. Find the area, \(r\) and \(R\).

    Show solution

    \(S=\tfrac12\cdot14\cdot12=84\). \(s=21\Rightarrow r=4\).

    \(R=\dfrac{abc}{4S}=\dfrac{13\cdot14\cdot15}{336}=8.125\).

  6. \(A\) and \(B\) are fixed, \(AB=6\). Find the locus of points \(P\) for which triangle \(PAB\) has area 12.

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot h=12\Rightarrow h=4\). The locus: two lines parallel to \(AB\) at distance 4 from it (without the points on line \(AB\), where there is no triangle).

  7. Find the locus of centres of circles that touch both sides of an angle. A circle of radius 2 touches both sides of a 60° angle: how far is its centre from the vertex?

    Show solution

    The centre is equally far from both sides: the locus is the angle bisector (inside the angle).

    The bisector makes \(30^\circ\) with each side: \(2=d\sin30^\circ\Rightarrow d=4\).

  8. \(A\) and \(B\) are fixed. Find the locus of points \(C\) for which \(\angle ACB=90^\circ\).

    Show solution

    In a right triangle the circumcentre is the midpoint \(M\) of the hypotenuse, so \(MC=\tfrac12AB\). Conversely, if \(MC=\tfrac12AB\), the angle at \(C\) is right (Unit 17, question 22).

    The locus is the circle with diameter \(AB\), without the points \(A\) and \(B\).

  9. Prove that in an isosceles triangle, the median to the base is also the altitude, the angle bisector and the perpendicular bisector of the base. What follows for the four special points?

    Show solution

    \(AB=AC\), \(M\) midpoint of \(BC\): \(\triangle ABM\cong\triangle ACM\) (SSS).

    So \(\angle BAM=\angle CAM\) (bisector) and \(\angle AMB=\angle AMC=90^\circ\) (altitude and perpendicular bisector).

    All four special points lie on this line.

  10. An equilateral triangle has side 6. Show that all four special points coincide, and find \(R\) and \(r\).

    Show solution

    By question 29, applied to each side, all the special lines coincide, so the points coincide.

    Height \(=3\sqrt3\). The centroid is \(\tfrac23\) of the way down: \(R=2\sqrt3\), \(r=\sqrt3\).
