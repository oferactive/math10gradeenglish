1. Basic notions and chords

A chord joins two points of the circle; a diameter is a chord through the centre. A chord cuts the circle into two arcs. A central angle has its vertex at the centre; an inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle and its sides are chords.

We measure an arc by its central angle: an arc of 80° is the arc seen from the centre at an angle of 80°.

Theoremand the converse
Equal chords have equal central angles and equal arcs.Equal central angles (or arcs) have equal chords.
The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.The line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it.
Equal chords are equally far from the centre.Chords equally far from the centre are equal.
Example 1

A circle has radius 5 and a chord of length 8. How far is the chord from the centre?

Solution.

The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord: half the chord is 4.

Pythagoras: \(d=\sqrt{5^2-4^2}=3\).

2. The inscribed angle theorem

\(\text{inscribed angle}=\tfrac12\cdot\text{central angle on the same arc}\)

Proof (when the centre lies on a side of the inscribed angle). Let \(BC\) pass through \(O\). Triangle \(OAC\) is isosceles (\(OA=OC=r\)), so \(\angle OAC=\angle OCA=\alpha\). The central angle \(\angle AOB\) is an exterior angle of triangle \(OAC\), so \(\angle AOB=2\alpha\). The other cases follow by adding or subtracting two such angles.

Consequences:

  • Inscribed angles standing on the same arc are equal.
  • An inscribed angle standing on a diameter is a right angle (half of 180°). Conversely, a right inscribed angle stands on a diameter.
Example 2

\(AB\) is a diameter, \(C\) is on the circle and \(\angle CAB=28^\circ\). Find \(\angle CBA\).

Solution.

\(\angle ACB=90^\circ\), so \(\angle CBA=180^\circ-90^\circ-28^\circ=62^\circ\).

3. Cyclic quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on a circle is called cyclic (inscribed in a circle).

\(\angle A+\angle C=180^\circ,\qquad\angle B+\angle D=180^\circ\)
in a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles add up to 180°

Proof. \(\angle A\) and \(\angle C\) stand on the two arcs between \(B\) and \(D\). Their central angles add up to 360°, so the inscribed angles add up to 180°.

Converse. If two opposite angles of a quadrilateral add up to 180°, the quadrilateral is cyclic. This is the usual way to show that four points lie on one circle.

4. Tangents

A tangent is a line that meets the circle at exactly one point.

TheoremComment
The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.and conversely: the line perpendicular to a radius at its end on the circle is a tangent
Two tangents from an outside point are equal.and the line from the point to the centre bisects the angle between them
The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the inscribed angle on the arc between them.both are half of the same central angle
In a quadrilateral with an inscribed circle, \(AB+CD=BC+DA\).from the equal tangents at each vertex

Proof that the tangents are equal. Triangles \(OAP\) and \(OBP\) have right angles at \(A\) and \(B\), equal legs \(OA=OB=r\) and the common hypotenuse \(OP\). By Pythagoras \(PA=\sqrt{OP^2-r^2}=PB\).

Example 3

From a point \(P\) at distance 13 from the centre, a tangent is drawn to a circle of radius 5. Find its length.

Solution.

\(PA=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=12\)

5. Angles between chords and secants

Two chords meeting inside the circle\(\angle=\tfrac12(\text{arc}_1+\text{arc}_2)\)
Two secants (or tangents) meeting outside\(\angle=\tfrac12(\text{far arc}-\text{near arc})\)

Both follow from the exterior angle theorem and the inscribed angle theorem: join two of the points to make a triangle whose angles are inscribed angles.

Example 4

Chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) meet at \(E\) inside the circle. The arcs \(AC\) and \(BD\) (inside the angle and opposite it) are 70° and 50°. Find \(\angle AEC\).

Solution.

\(\tfrac12(70^\circ+50^\circ)=60^\circ\)

6. Constructions with compass and ruler

The centre of a given circle. Draw two chords that are not parallel, and construct their perpendicular bisectors. Each passes through the centre, so they meet at the centre.

The tangent at a point \(A\) on the circle. Draw the radius \(OA\) and construct the perpendicular to it at \(A\).

The tangents from an outside point \(P\). Find the midpoint \(M\) of \(OP\) and draw the circle with centre \(M\) through \(O\) and \(P\). It meets the given circle at \(A\) and \(B\). The angles \(OAP\) and \(OBP\) stand on the diameter \(OP\), so they are right: \(PA\) and \(PB\) are the tangents.

7. Common mistakes

  • Doubling instead of halving: the inscribed angle is half the central angle on the same arc.
  • Comparing an inscribed angle with a central angle on a different arc.
  • Adding the angles of a cyclic quadrilateral that are next to each other. It is the opposite angles that add up to 180°.
  • Forgetting that the tangent is perpendicular to the radius, which is often the key to a right triangle.
  • Using "angle on a diameter is right" when the side is a chord that does not pass through the centre.

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

    Show solution

  2. What is the size of an inscribed angle that stands on a diameter?

    Show solution

    \(90^\circ\)

  3. In a cyclic quadrilateral \(\angle A=75^\circ\). Find \(\angle C\).

    Show solution

    \(105^\circ\)

  4. What is the angle between a tangent and the radius at the point of tangency?

    Show solution

    \(90^\circ\)

  5. Two tangents are drawn from a point \(P\). What can you say about their lengths?

    Show solution

    They are equal.

  6. A circle has radius 13 and a chord of length 24. How far is the chord from the centre?

    Show solution

    \(\sqrt{169-144}=5\)

  7. \(\angle ACB\) and \(\angle ADB\) are inscribed angles on the same arc \(AB\), and \(\angle ACB=40^\circ\). Find \(\angle ADB\).

    Show solution

    \(40^\circ\)

  8. How do you find the centre of a given circle?

    Show solution

    Draw two chords and their perpendicular bisectors; they meet at the centre.
