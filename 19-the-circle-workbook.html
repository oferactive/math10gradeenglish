Before you start: In every circle question, mark the centre and draw the radii to the important points. Many triangles with two radii as sides are isosceles, and that is often the key.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. A central angle is 80°. Find the inscribed angle on the same arc.

    Show solution

    Idea. Inscribed angle \(=\tfrac12\) central angle.

    \(\tfrac12\cdot80^\circ\).

    Answer: \(40^\circ\).

  2. An inscribed angle is 35°. Find the central angle on the same arc.

    Show solution

    Idea. Central angle \(=2\cdot\) inscribed angle.

    \(2\cdot35^\circ\).

    Answer: \(70^\circ\).

  3. \(AB\) is a diameter, \(C\) is on the circle, \(\angle CAB=28^\circ\). Find \(\angle CBA\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(\angle ACB\) stands on the diameter, so it is \(90^\circ\). Then use the angle sum in the triangle.

    \(\angle CBA=180^\circ-90^\circ-28^\circ\).

    Answer: \(62^\circ\).

  4. \(ABCD\) is a cyclic quadrilateral with \(\angle A=110^\circ\) and \(\angle B=85^\circ\). Find \(\angle C\) and \(\angle D\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Opposite angles are supplementary: \(A\) with \(C\), \(B\) with \(D\).

    \(\angle C=180^\circ-110^\circ=70^\circ\).

    \(\angle D=180^\circ-85^\circ=95^\circ\).

    Answer: \(\angle C=70^\circ\), \(\angle D=95^\circ\).

    Check: \(110+85+70+95=360\) ✓

  5. \(PA\) and \(PB\) are tangents from \(P\) to a circle with centre \(O\). \(PA=7\) and \(\angle APB=50^\circ\). Find \(PB\) and \(\angle AOB\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Tangents from one point are equal. The radii to the points of tangency make right angles, and the angles of quadrilateral \(OAPB\) add to \(360^\circ\).

    \(PB=PA=7\).

    \(\angle OAP=\angle OBP=90^\circ\).

    \(\angle AOB=360^\circ-90^\circ-90^\circ-50^\circ=130^\circ\).

    Answer: \(PB=7\), \(\angle AOB=130^\circ\).

  6. A circle has radius 5 and a chord of length 8. How far is the chord from the centre?

    Show solution

    Idea. Right triangle: radius as hypotenuse, half the chord and the distance as legs.

    Half the chord: 4.

    \(d=\sqrt{25-16}=3\).

    Answer: 3.

  7. A point is 13 units from the centre of a circle of radius 5. Find the length of the tangent from it.

    Show solution

    Idea. The tangent is perpendicular to the radius. Radius, tangent and the line to the centre form a right triangle with hypotenuse 13.

    \(t=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{169-25}=\sqrt{144}\).

    Answer: 12.

  8. The arc \(AB\) is 100°. Find the angle between the chord \(AB\) and the tangent at \(A\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The angle between a tangent and a chord equals half the arc between them (like an inscribed angle).

    \(\tfrac12\cdot100^\circ\).

    Answer: \(50^\circ\).

  9. \(C\) and \(D\) are on the same side of chord \(AB\), and \(\angle ACB=40^\circ\). Find \(\angle ADB\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Points on the same side of the chord see it at the same inscribed angle.

    Answer: \(40^\circ\).

  10. \(AB=CD\) are chords of a circle with centre \(O\), and \(\angle AOB=70^\circ\). Find \(\angle COD\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Equal chords cut off equal arcs, so their central angles are equal. (Triangles \(OAB\) and \(OCD\) are congruent by SSS.)

    Answer: \(\angle COD=70^\circ\).

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Prove the inscribed angle theorem when the centre \(O\) lies on the side \(CB\) of the inscribed angle \(ACB\).

    Show solution

    Idea. \(OA=OC\) (radii) makes \(\triangle OAC\) isosceles. \(\angle AOB\) is its exterior angle.

    Let \(\angle ACB=\angle ACO=\alpha\). Then \(\angle OAC=\alpha\) too (base angles).

    Exterior angle theorem: \(\angle AOB=\angle OAC+\angle OCA=2\alpha\).

    Answer: the central angle is twice the inscribed angle.

  2. Prove that an inscribed angle standing on a diameter is a right angle.

    Show solution

    Idea. Use the inscribed angle theorem with the central angle on the diameter.

    A diameter \(AB\) passes through \(O\), so the "central angle" \(AOB\) is a straight angle, \(180^\circ\).

    The inscribed angle \(ACB=\tfrac12\cdot180^\circ\).

    Answer: \(90^\circ\).

  3. Prove that opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.

    Show solution

    Idea. Each of the opposite angles \(A\) and \(C\) stands on one of the two arcs \(BD\). The two arcs together make the full circle.

    \(\angle A=\tfrac12\cdot\) (central angle of arc \(BCD\)).

    \(\angle C=\tfrac12\cdot\) (central angle of arc \(BAD\)).

    The two central angles add to \(360^\circ\), so \(\angle A+\angle C=\tfrac12\cdot360^\circ=180^\circ\).

    Answer: proved; the same holds for \(\angle B+\angle D\).

  4. Prove that the two tangents from an outside point \(P\) are equal.

    Show solution

    Idea. Two right triangles with a common hypotenuse and an equal leg have equal other legs (Pythagoras).

    \(\angle OAP=\angle OBP=90^\circ\) (tangent ⊥ radius), \(OA=OB\) (radii), \(OP\) common.

    \(PA^2=OP^2-OA^2\) and \(PB^2=OP^2-OB^2\).

    These are equal, so \(PA=PB\).

    Answer: proved.

  5. Prove that the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.

    Show solution

    Idea. The perpendicular \(OK\) creates two right triangles with equal hypotenuses \(OA=OB\) and a common leg \(OK\).

    \(AK^2=OA^2-OK^2\) and \(BK^2=OB^2-OK^2\).

    \(OA=OB\), so \(AK=BK\).

    Answer: \(K\) is the midpoint of \(AB\).

  6. Triangle \(ABC\) is inscribed in a circle, \(\angle A=50^\circ\), \(\angle B=60^\circ\). Find the three arcs \(BC\), \(CA\), \(AB\) (each not containing the third vertex).

    Show solution

    Idea. Each inscribed angle is half its arc, so each arc is twice the opposite angle of the triangle.

    \(\angle C=180^\circ-50^\circ-60^\circ=70^\circ\).

    Arc \(BC=2\cdot50^\circ=100^\circ\); arc \(CA=2\cdot60^\circ=120^\circ\); arc \(AB=2\cdot70^\circ=140^\circ\).

    Answer: \(100^\circ\), \(120^\circ\), \(140^\circ\) (sum \(360^\circ\) ✓).

  7. A circle is inscribed in quadrilateral \(ABCD\), with \(AB=8\), \(BC=10\), \(CD=7\). Find \(DA\).

    Show solution

    Idea. In a quadrilateral with an inscribed circle, the sums of opposite sides are equal (question 23).

    \(AB+CD=BC+DA\Rightarrow8+7=10+DA\).

    Answer: \(DA=5\).

  8. Chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) meet at \(E\) inside a circle. Arc \(AC=70^\circ\), arc \(BD=50^\circ\). Find \(\angle AEC\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The angle between two chords inside the circle is half the sum of the arcs it and its vertical angle cut off (question 28).

    \(\angle AEC=\tfrac12(70^\circ+50^\circ)\).

    Answer: \(60^\circ\).

  9. Two secants are drawn from an outside point \(P\). The arcs between them are 110° (far) and 30° (near). Find \(\angle P\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The angle between two secants from an outside point is half the difference of the arcs they cut off.

    \(\angle P=\tfrac12(110^\circ-30^\circ)\).

    Answer: \(40^\circ\).

  10. Describe how to construct (a) the centre of a given circle, (b) the tangents from an outside point \(P\). Explain why each construction works.

    Show solution

    Idea. (a) The centre is on every chord's perpendicular bisector. (b) The point of tangency \(A\) must satisfy \(\angle OAP=90^\circ\): such points lie on the circle with diameter \(OP\).

    (a) Draw two chords that are not parallel; construct their perpendicular bisectors; they meet at the centre, since the centre is equally far from the ends of each chord.

    (b) Find the midpoint of \(OP\) and draw the circle with diameter \(OP\). It meets the given circle at \(A\) and \(B\). \(\angle OAP=\angle OBP=90^\circ\) (angles on a diameter), so \(PA\perp OA\) and \(PB\perp OB\): they are tangents.

    Answer: as described.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Prove that the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the inscribed angle on the arc between them.

    Show solution

    Idea. Express both angles through the central angle \(\angle AOB\), using the isosceles triangle \(OAB\) and the right angle between tangent and radius.

    \(OA=OB\): \(\angle OAB=\angle OBA=\dfrac{180^\circ-\angle AOB}2=90^\circ-\tfrac12\angle AOB\).

    Tangent \(\perp OA\): the angle between the tangent and the chord is \(90^\circ-\angle OAB=\tfrac12\angle AOB\).

    An inscribed angle on arc \(AB\) is also \(\tfrac12\angle AOB\).

    Answer: they are equal.

  2. Prove the converse: if \(\angle A+\angle C=180^\circ\) in quadrilateral \(ABCD\), then it is cyclic.

    Show solution

    Idea. Proof by contradiction. Draw the circle through three of the vertices; if the fourth were not on it, an exterior angle would equal an interior angle not next to it.

    Draw the circle through \(A\), \(B\), \(D\). Suppose \(C\) is not on it; line \(BC\) meets the circle again at \(C'\ne C\).

    \(ABC'D\) is cyclic: \(\angle BC'D=180^\circ-\angle A\). Given: \(\angle BCD=180^\circ-\angle A\). So \(\angle BC'D=\angle BCD\).

    In \(\triangle DCC'\), one of these is an exterior angle and the other an interior angle not next to it; an exterior angle is always larger (Unit 17). Contradiction.

    Answer: \(C\) lies on the circle, so \(ABCD\) is cyclic.

  3. A circle is inscribed in quadrilateral \(ABCD\). Prove that \(AB+CD=BC+DA\).

    Show solution

    Idea. From each vertex the two tangent segments are equal. Name them and add.

    Let the tangents from \(A,B,C,D\) have lengths \(a,b,c,d\).

    \(AB=a+b\), \(BC=b+c\), \(CD=c+d\), \(DA=d+a\).

    \(AB+CD=a+b+c+d=BC+DA\).

    Answer: proved.

  4. Prove that an isosceles trapezoid is cyclic.

    Show solution

    Idea. Show that one pair of opposite angles adds to \(180^\circ\), then use the converse (question 22).

    Isosceles trapezoid \(ABCD\), \(AB\parallel CD\): the base angles are equal, \(\angle A=\angle B\).

    Co-interior angles between the parallels: \(\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ\).

    So \(\angle A+\angle C=180^\circ\).

    Answer: the trapezoid is cyclic.

  5. Triangle \(ABC\) is inscribed in a circle. \(AD\) is the altitude to \(BC\), and \(AE\) is a diameter. Prove that \(\angle BAD=\angle CAE\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Both angles are \(90^\circ\) minus equal angles. \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle AEC\) stand on the same arc \(AC\).

    \(\angle ABC=\angle AEC\) (same arc \(AC\)).

    In \(\triangle ABD\): \(\angle ADB=90^\circ\), so \(\angle BAD=90^\circ-\angle ABC\).

    In \(\triangle ACE\): \(\angle ACE=90^\circ\) (on the diameter), so \(\angle CAE=90^\circ-\angle AEC\).

    Answer: \(\angle BAD=\angle CAE\).

  6. \(P\) is 10 units from the centre \(O\) of a circle of radius 6. Tangents \(PA\), \(PB\) are drawn.

    1. Find \(PA\).
    2. Find \(\angle APB\).
    3. Find the area of quadrilateral \(OAPB\).
    Show solution

    Idea. Right triangle \(OAP\) (right angle at \(A\)); the line \(OP\) bisects \(\angle APB\) by symmetry.

    (a) \(PA=\sqrt{10^2-6^2}=8\).

    (b) \(\sin\angle OPA=\dfrac{OA}{OP}=0.6\Rightarrow\angle OPA\approx36.87^\circ\). \(\angle APB=2\cdot36.87^\circ\approx73.74^\circ\).

    (c) \(OAPB\) is two congruent right triangles: \(2\cdot\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot8=48\).

    Answer: (a) 8; (b) \(\approx73.74^\circ\); (c) 48.

  7. Two parallel chords of lengths 12 and 16 are drawn in a circle of radius 10. Find the distance between them (two cases).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find each chord's distance from the centre. Parallel chords can be on the same side of the centre or on opposite sides.

    Chord 12: half 6, \(d_1=\sqrt{100-36}=8\).

    Chord 16: half 8, \(d_2=\sqrt{100-64}=6\).

    Same side: \(8-6=2\). Opposite sides: \(8+6=14\).

    Answer: 2 or 14.

  8. Chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) meet at \(E\) inside a circle. Prove that \(\angle AEC=\tfrac12(\text{arc }AC+\text{arc }BD)\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Join \(A\) to \(D\). \(\angle AEC\) is an exterior angle of triangle \(AED\); its two remote interior angles are inscribed angles.

    \(\angle AEC=\angle EAD+\angle EDA\) (exterior angle).

    \(\angle EAD=\angle BAD\), an inscribed angle on arc \(BD\): \(\tfrac12\) arc \(BD\).

    \(\angle EDA=\angle CDA\), on arc \(AC\): \(\tfrac12\) arc \(AC\).

    Answer: \(\angle AEC=\tfrac12(\text{arc }AC+\text{arc }BD)\).

  9. A square and a regular hexagon are inscribed in a circle of radius \(r\). Find their sides.

    Show solution

    Idea. Square: the diagonal is a diameter (angle \(90^\circ\) on it). Hexagon: the six central angles are \(60^\circ\).

    Square: diagonal \(2r=s\sqrt2\Rightarrow s=\dfrac{2r}{\sqrt2}=r\sqrt2\).

    Hexagon: triangle \(O\)–vertex–vertex has two sides \(r\) and a \(60^\circ\) angle between them; the other two angles are each \(60^\circ\), so it is equilateral: side \(=r\).

    Answer: square \(r\sqrt2\); hexagon \(r\).

  10. Two circles with centres \(O_1\) and \(O_2\) meet at \(A\) and \(B\). Prove that \(O_1O_2\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Each centre is equally far from \(A\) and \(B\), so each lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

    \(O_1A=O_1B\) (radii of the first circle) \(\Rightarrow O_1\) is on the perpendicular bisector.

    \(O_2A=O_2B\) \(\Rightarrow O_2\) is on it too.

    Exactly one line passes through two points.

    Answer: the line \(O_1O_2\) is the perpendicular bisector of the common chord \(AB\).
