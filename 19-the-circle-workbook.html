Workbook: The Circle

30 questions in three levels, each with a full worked solution. Learn by doing: solve on paper first, then open the solution and compare your method. The explanation is on the page The Circle.

Before you start: In every circle question, mark the centre and draw the radii to the important points. Many triangles with two radii as sides are isosceles, and that is often the key.

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. A central angle is 80°. Find the inscribed angle on the same arc.

    Show solution

    \(40^\circ\)

  2. An inscribed angle is 35°. Find the central angle on the same arc.

    Show solution

    \(70^\circ\)

  3. \(AB\) is a diameter, \(C\) is on the circle, \(\angle CAB=28^\circ\). Find \(\angle CBA\).

    Show solution

    \(\angle ACB=90^\circ\Rightarrow\angle CBA=62^\circ\)

  4. \(ABCD\) is a cyclic quadrilateral with \(\angle A=110^\circ\) and \(\angle B=85^\circ\). Find \(\angle C\) and \(\angle D\).

    Show solution

    \(\angle C=70^\circ\), \(\angle D=95^\circ\).

  5. \(PA\) and \(PB\) are tangents from \(P\) to a circle with centre \(O\). \(PA=7\) and \(\angle APB=50^\circ\). Find \(PB\) and \(\angle AOB\).

    Show solution

    \(PB=7\). In quadrilateral \(OAPB\) the angles at \(A\) and \(B\) are \(90^\circ\): \(\angle AOB=360^\circ-180^\circ-50^\circ=130^\circ\).

  6. A circle has radius 5 and a chord of length 8. How far is the chord from the centre?

    Show solution

    \(\sqrt{25-16}=3\)

  7. A point is 13 units from the centre of a circle of radius 5. Find the length of the tangent from it.

    Show solution

    \(\sqrt{169-25}=12\)

  8. The arc \(AB\) is 100°. Find the angle between the chord \(AB\) and the tangent at \(A\).

    Show solution

    Half the arc: \(50^\circ\).

  9. \(C\) and \(D\) are on the same side of chord \(AB\), and \(\angle ACB=40^\circ\). Find \(\angle ADB\).

    Show solution

    \(40^\circ\): inscribed angles on the same arc.

  10. \(AB=CD\) are chords of a circle with centre \(O\), and \(\angle AOB=70^\circ\). Find \(\angle COD\).

    Show solution

    \(70^\circ\): equal chords have equal central angles.

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. Prove the inscribed angle theorem when the centre \(O\) lies on the side \(CB\) of the inscribed angle \(ACB\).

    Show solution

    \(OA=OC\Rightarrow\angle OAC=\angle OCA=\alpha\).

    \(\angle AOB\) is an exterior angle of \(\triangle OAC\): \(\angle AOB=2\alpha=2\angle ACB\).

  2. Prove that an inscribed angle standing on a diameter is a right angle.

    Show solution

    The central angle on a diameter is a straight angle, \(180^\circ\). The inscribed angle is half: \(90^\circ\).

  3. Prove that opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.

    Show solution

    \(\angle A=\tfrac12\) (central angle of arc \(BCD\)), \(\angle C=\tfrac12\) (central angle of arc \(BAD\)). The two central angles add to \(360^\circ\), so \(\angle A+\angle C=180^\circ\).

  4. Prove that the two tangents from an outside point \(P\) are equal.

    Show solution

    \(\triangle OAP\) and \(\triangle OBP\): right angles at \(A,B\); \(OA=OB\); common hypotenuse \(OP\).

    \(PA^2=OP^2-OA^2=OP^2-OB^2=PB^2\).

  5. Prove that the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.

    Show solution

    \(OK\perp AB\). \(\triangle OKA\) and \(\triangle OKB\): right angles, \(OA=OB\), \(OK\) common. By Pythagoras \(AK=BK\).

  6. Triangle \(ABC\) is inscribed in a circle, \(\angle A=50^\circ\), \(\angle B=60^\circ\). Find the three arcs \(BC\), \(CA\), \(AB\) (each not containing the third vertex).

    Show solution

    \(\angle C=70^\circ\). Each arc is twice the opposite angle: \(BC=100^\circ\), \(CA=120^\circ\), \(AB=140^\circ\) (sum \(360^\circ\) ✓).

  7. A circle is inscribed in quadrilateral \(ABCD\), with \(AB=8\), \(BC=10\), \(CD=7\). Find \(DA\).

    Show solution

    \(AB+CD=BC+DA\Rightarrow15=10+DA\Rightarrow DA=5\).

  8. Chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) meet at \(E\) inside a circle. Arc \(AC=70^\circ\), arc \(BD=50^\circ\). Find \(\angle AEC\).

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac12(70^\circ+50^\circ)=60^\circ\)

  9. Two secants are drawn from an outside point \(P\). The arcs between them are 110° (far) and 30° (near). Find \(\angle P\).

    Show solution

    \(\tfrac12(110^\circ-30^\circ)=40^\circ\)

  10. Describe how to construct (a) the centre of a given circle, (b) the tangents from an outside point \(P\). Explain why each construction works.

    Show solution

    (a) Two chords and their perpendicular bisectors; each bisector passes through the centre (the centre is equally far from the ends of every chord).

    (b) The circle with diameter \(OP\) meets the given circle at \(A\), \(B\); \(\angle OAP=\angle OBP=90^\circ\) (angle on a diameter), so \(PA,PB\) are perpendicular to radii: tangents.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Prove that the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the inscribed angle on the arc between them.

    Show solution

    Tangent at \(A\), chord \(AB\), centre \(O\). \(\triangle OAB\) is isosceles: \(\angle OAB=\angle OBA=\tfrac12(180^\circ-\angle AOB)=90^\circ-\tfrac12\angle AOB\).

    The tangent is perpendicular to \(OA\), so the tangent–chord angle \(=90^\circ-\angle OAB=\tfrac12\angle AOB\), which equals the inscribed angle on arc \(AB\).

  2. Prove the converse: if \(\angle A+\angle C=180^\circ\) in quadrilateral \(ABCD\), then it is cyclic.

    Show solution

    Draw the circle through \(A\), \(B\), \(D\). Assume \(C\) is not on it, and let line \(BC\) meet the circle again at \(C'\).

    \(ABC'D\) is cyclic, so \(\angle BC'D=180^\circ-\angle A=\angle BCD\). But then in triangle \(DCC'\) one of these angles is an exterior angle equal to an interior angle not next to it: impossible. So \(C\) is on the circle.

  3. A circle is inscribed in quadrilateral \(ABCD\). Prove that \(AB+CD=BC+DA\).

    Show solution

    From each vertex the two tangents are equal: call them \(a,b,c,d\) at \(A,B,C,D\).

    \(AB+CD=(a+b)+(c+d)\) and \(BC+DA=(b+c)+(d+a)\): equal.

  4. Prove that an isosceles trapezoid is cyclic.

    Show solution

    In an isosceles trapezoid the base angles are equal: \(\angle A=\angle B\). Since \(AB\parallel CD\), \(\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ\), so \(\angle A+\angle C=180^\circ\). By the converse, it is cyclic.

  5. Triangle \(ABC\) is inscribed in a circle. \(AD\) is the altitude to \(BC\), and \(AE\) is a diameter. Prove that \(\angle BAD=\angle CAE\).

    Show solution

    \(\angle ABC=\angle AEC\) (inscribed angles on arc \(AC\)).

    \(\angle ACE=90^\circ\) (angle on the diameter \(AE\)), and \(\angle ADB=90^\circ\).

    \(\angle BAD=90^\circ-\angle ABC=90^\circ-\angle AEC=\angle CAE\).

  6. \(P\) is 10 units from the centre \(O\) of a circle of radius 6. Tangents \(PA\), \(PB\) are drawn.

    1. Find \(PA\).
    2. Find \(\angle APB\).
    3. Find the area of quadrilateral \(OAPB\).
    Show solution

    (a) \(PA=\sqrt{100-36}=8\).

    (b) In \(\triangle OAP\): \(\sin\angle OPA=\tfrac6{10}\Rightarrow\angle OPA\approx36.87^\circ\), so \(\angle APB\approx73.74^\circ\).

    (c) \(2\cdot\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot8=48\).

  7. Two parallel chords of lengths 12 and 16 are drawn in a circle of radius 10. Find the distance between them (two cases).

    Show solution

    Distances from the centre: \(\sqrt{100-36}=8\) and \(\sqrt{100-64}=6\).

    Same side of the centre: \(8-6=2\). Opposite sides: \(8+6=14\).

  8. Chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) meet at \(E\) inside a circle. Prove that \(\angle AEC=\tfrac12(\text{arc }AC+\text{arc }BD)\).

    Show solution

    Join \(A\) and \(D\). \(\angle AEC\) is an exterior angle of \(\triangle AED\): \(\angle AEC=\angle EAD+\angle EDA=\angle BAD+\angle CDA\).

    These are inscribed angles on arcs \(BD\) and \(AC\): \(\tfrac12\text{arc }BD+\tfrac12\text{arc }AC\).

  9. A square and a regular hexagon are inscribed in a circle of radius \(r\). Find their sides.

    Show solution

    Square: the diagonal is a diameter \(2r\), so the side is \(r\sqrt2\).

    Hexagon: each side has a central angle of \(60^\circ\); the triangle with two radii is isosceles with a \(60^\circ\) angle, so it is equilateral: side \(r\).

  10. Two circles with centres \(O_1\) and \(O_2\) meet at \(A\) and \(B\). Prove that \(O_1O_2\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

    Show solution

    \(O_1A=O_1B\) (radii), so \(O_1\) is on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\). In the same way \(O_2\) is on it. Two points determine a line, so the line \(O_1O_2\) is the perpendicular bisector.
