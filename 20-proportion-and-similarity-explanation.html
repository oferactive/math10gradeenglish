1. Proportions

A proportion is an equality of two ratios, \(\dfrac ab=\dfrac cd\). Useful properties:

Cross products\(\dfrac ab=\dfrac cd\iff ad=bc\)
Adding the denominator\(\dfrac ab=\dfrac cd\Rightarrow\dfrac{a+b}b=\dfrac{c+d}d\)
Swapping the middle\(\dfrac ab=\dfrac cd\Rightarrow\dfrac ac=\dfrac bd\)

"\(D\) divides \(AB\) in the ratio \(2:3\)" means \(AD:DB=2:3\), so \(AD=\tfrac25AB\) and \(DB=\tfrac35AB\).

2. Thales' theorem and the midsegment

Thales' theorem (the intercept theorem). In triangle \(ABC\), if \(DE\parallel BC\) with \(D\) on \(AB\) and \(E\) on \(AC\), then:

\(\dfrac{AD}{DB}=\dfrac{AE}{EC}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\dfrac{AD}{AB}=\dfrac{AE}{AC}=\dfrac{DE}{BC}\)

Converse. If \(\dfrac{AD}{DB}=\dfrac{AE}{EC}\), then \(DE\parallel BC\).

Midsegment of a triangle. The segment joining the midpoints of two sides is parallel to the third side and equal to half of it.

Midsegment of a trapezoid. The segment joining the midpoints of the legs is parallel to the bases and equal to half their sum: \(\dfrac{a+b}2\).

Example 1

\(DE\parallel BC\), \(AD=4\), \(DB=6\), \(AE=5\). Find \(EC\).

Solution.

\(\dfrac46=\dfrac5{EC}\Rightarrow EC=7.5\)

3. Similar triangles

Two triangles are similar if their angles are equal and their sides are proportional with a ratio \(k\), the ratio of similarity. To prove similarity, one of these is enough:

TheoremWhat must be equal or proportional
AAtwo pairs of equal angles
SAStwo pairs of proportional sides, and the angles between them are equal
SSSall three pairs of sides are proportional

What else is in the ratio \(k\)? Perimeters, medians, heights, angle bisectors, and the radii of the circumscribed and inscribed circles. But areas are in the ratio \(k^2\), because an area is a length times a length.

Example 2

Two similar triangles have ratio \(2:3\). The smaller has perimeter 24 and area 20. Find the perimeter and the area of the larger.

Solution.

Perimeter: \(24\cdot\tfrac32=36\). Area: \(20\cdot\left(\tfrac32\right)^2=45\).

Example 3

A person 1.8 m tall casts a shadow of 2.4 m. At the same time a tree casts a shadow of 16 m. How tall is the tree?

Solution.

The sun\'s rays make the same angle, so the triangles are similar (AA): \(\dfrac h{16}=\dfrac{1.8}{2.4}\Rightarrow h=12\) m.

4. The angle bisector theorem

\(\dfrac{BD}{DC}=\dfrac{AB}{AC}\)
the bisector of angle \(A\) divides \(BC\) in the ratio of the two sides next to \(A\)

Proof with areas. \(D\) is on the bisector, so it is equally far (distance \(t\)) from \(AB\) and \(AC\). Triangles \(ABD\) and \(ACD\) have the same height from \(A\) to \(BC\), so \(\dfrac{S_{ABD}}{S_{ACD}}=\dfrac{BD}{DC}\). Also \(\dfrac{S_{ABD}}{S_{ACD}}=\dfrac{\tfrac12AB\cdot t}{\tfrac12AC\cdot t}=\dfrac{AB}{AC}\).

Example 4

\(AB=6\), \(AC=9\), \(BC=10\). Find \(BD\) and \(DC\).

Solution.

\(BD:DC=6:9=2:3\). \(BD=\tfrac25\cdot10=4\), \(DC=6\).

5. Similarity in the right triangle

In a right triangle, the altitude \(h\) to the hypotenuse cuts it into \(p\) and \(q\) and creates two smaller triangles, both similar to the whole triangle (AA: each shares an acute angle with it).

Altitude\(h^2=p\cdot q\)
Legs\(a^2=p\cdot c,\quad b^2=q\cdot c\)

Adding the two leg formulas: \(a^2+b^2=(p+q)c=c^2\). This is a proof of Pythagoras' theorem.

Example 5

The altitude to the hypotenuse cuts it into segments 4 and 9. Find the altitude and the legs.

Solution.

\(h=\sqrt{36}=6\). \(c=13\): legs \(\sqrt{4\cdot13}=2\sqrt{13}\) and \(\sqrt{9\cdot13}=3\sqrt{13}\).

6. Similarity in the circle

Two chords meeting at \(E\)\(AE\cdot EB=CE\cdot ED\)
Tangent \(PA\) and secant \(PBC\)\(PA^2=PB\cdot PC\)
Two secants from \(P\)\(PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD\)

Proof for two chords. \(\angle CAB=\angle CDB\) (inscribed angles on arc \(CB\)) and \(\angle AEC=\angle DEB\) (vertical angles). So \(\triangle AEC\sim\triangle DEB\) (AA), and \(\dfrac{AE}{DE}=\dfrac{CE}{BE}\Rightarrow AE\cdot EB=CE\cdot ED\).

Example 6

From a point \(P\), a secant meets the circle at \(B\) and \(C\) with \(PB=4\), \(PC=9\). Find the tangent \(PA\).

Solution.

\(PA^2=4\cdot9=36\Rightarrow PA=6\)

7. Dividing a segment

Into \(n\) equal parts. From \(A\) draw any ray and mark \(n\) equal steps on it with the compass, ending at \(K\). Join \(K\) to \(B\), and draw lines parallel to \(KB\) through the other marks. By Thales' theorem they cut \(AB\) into \(n\) equal parts.

In a given ratio \(m:n\). The same, with \(m+n\) equal steps; the parallel through the \(m\)-th mark gives the dividing point.

In coordinates. The point dividing \(AB\) in the ratio \(m:n\) is \(A+\dfrac m{m+n}(B-A)\).

8. Common mistakes

  • Writing Thales' ratios with the wrong pieces: \(\dfrac{AD}{DB}=\dfrac{DE}{BC}\) is wrong. The ratio with \(DE\) uses whole sides: \(\dfrac{AD}{AB}=\dfrac{DE}{BC}\).
  • Matching the wrong vertices in similar triangles. Write the similarity with the vertices in matching order, \(\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC\), and read the ratios from it.
  • Using the ratio \(k\) for areas. Areas are in the ratio \(k^2\).
  • In the angle bisector theorem, using the sides opposite instead of the sides next to the angle.
  • In the secant theorems, using the outside part times the inside part. It is always (outside part) × (whole secant).

9. Check yourself

Short exercises to check that you understood the explanation. Try each one before you open the solution. For more practice, go to the workbook.

  1. Solve \(\dfrac x4=\dfrac9{12}\).

    Show solution

    \(x=3\)

  2. \(DE\parallel BC\), \(AD:AB=1:3\) and \(BC=12\). Find \(DE\).

    Show solution

    \(DE=4\)

  3. Two similar triangles have ratio 3. What is the ratio of their areas?

    Show solution

    \(9\)

  4. A side of a triangle is 10. How long is the midsegment parallel to it?

    Show solution

    \(5\)

  5. In triangle \(ABC\), \(AB=4\), \(AC=6\) and \(AD\) bisects angle \(A\). Find \(BD:DC\).

    Show solution

    \(2:3\)

  6. Chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) meet at \(E\). Which products are equal?

    Show solution

    \(AE\cdot EB=CE\cdot ED\)

  7. How many pairs of equal angles are needed for AA similarity?

    Show solution

    Two (then the third pair is equal too).

  8. The altitude to the hypotenuse cuts it into 2 and 8. Find the altitude.

    Show solution

    \(h=\sqrt{16}=4\)
