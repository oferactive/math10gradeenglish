Before you start: Before using any ratio, write the similarity with matching vertices, for example \(\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC\). Then every ratio is read in the same order: \(\tfrac{AD}{AB}=\tfrac{AE}{AC}=\tfrac{DE}{BC}\).

Level A: Basic Questions 1–10

Use the definitions and formulas directly.

  1. Solve \(\dfrac x6=\dfrac{10}{15}\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Cross-multiply.

    \(15x=60\Rightarrow x=4\).

    Answer: \(x=4\).

  2. In triangle \(ABC\), \(DE\parallel BC\), \(AD=4\), \(DB=6\), \(AE=5\). Find \(EC\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Thales' theorem: a line parallel to one side cuts the other two sides in the same ratio: \(\dfrac{AD}{DB}=\dfrac{AE}{EC}\).

    \(\dfrac46=\dfrac5{EC}\Rightarrow4\,EC=30\Rightarrow EC=7.5\).

    Answer: \(EC=7.5\).

  3. \(D\) and \(E\) are the midpoints of \(AB\) and \(AC\), and \(BC=14\). Find \(DE\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Midsegment theorem.

    \(DE=\tfrac12\cdot14\).

    Answer: 7.

  4. Two similar triangles have ratio \(2:3\). The smaller has perimeter 24 and area 20. Find the perimeter and the area of the larger.

    Show solution

    Idea. Perimeters scale by \(k\), areas by \(k^2\). Here \(k=\tfrac32\) from the smaller to the larger.

    Perimeter: \(24\cdot\tfrac32=36\).

    Area: \(20\cdot\left(\tfrac32\right)^2=20\cdot\tfrac94=45\).

    Answer: perimeter 36, area 45.

  5. \(AB=6\), \(AC=9\), \(BC=10\), and \(AD\) bisects angle \(A\). Find \(BD\) and \(DC\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Angle bisector theorem: \(BD:DC=AB:AC\); then split \(BC=10\) in that ratio.

    \(6:9=2:3\). Five parts make 10, so one part is 2.

    \(BD=2\cdot2=4\), \(DC=3\cdot2=6\).

    Answer: \(BD=4\), \(DC=6\).

  6. Are the triangles with sides 3, 4, 5 and 6, 8, 10 similar?

    Show solution

    Idea. SSS similarity: all three ratios of matching sides must be equal.

    \(\tfrac63=\tfrac84=\tfrac{10}5=2\).

    Answer: yes, similar with ratio 2.

  7. The altitude to the hypotenuse of a right triangle cuts it into 4 and 9. Find the altitude.

    Show solution

    Idea. \(h^2=pq\).

    \(h^2=4\cdot9=36\).

    Answer: \(h=6\).

  8. Chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) meet at \(E\): \(AE=4\), \(EB=6\), \(CE=3\). Find \(ED\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Intersecting chords: \(AE\cdot EB=CE\cdot ED\).

    \(4\cdot6=3\cdot ED\Rightarrow ED=\tfrac{24}3\).

    Answer: \(ED=8\).

  9. From \(P\), a secant meets a circle at \(B\) and \(C\) (\(PB=4\), \(PC=9\)). Find the tangent from \(P\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Tangent–secant theorem: \(PT^2=PB\cdot PC\) (near part × whole secant).

    \(PT^2=4\cdot9=36\).

    Answer: the tangent is 6.

  10. The bases of a trapezoid are 8 and 14. Find its midsegment.

    Show solution

    Idea. The midsegment of a trapezoid is the average of the bases.

    \(\dfrac{8+14}2\).

    Answer: 11.

Level B: Practice Questions 11–20

Questions with two or three steps, mixing ideas from the unit.

  1. In triangle \(ABC\), \(DE\parallel BC\), \(AD=3\), \(AB=9\), \(BC=12\), and the area of \(ABC\) is 54.

    1. Find \(DE\).
    2. Find the area of triangle \(ADE\) and of the trapezoid \(DBCE\).
    Show solution

    Idea. \(DE\parallel BC\) gives similar triangles with \(k=\dfrac{AD}{AB}\). Lengths scale by \(k\), areas by \(k^2\).

    (a) \(k=\tfrac39=\tfrac13\). \(DE=\tfrac13\cdot12=4\).

    (b) \(S_{ADE}=54\cdot\left(\tfrac13\right)^2=54\cdot\tfrac19=6\). \(S_{DBCE}=54-6=48\).

    Answer: (a) 4; (b) 6 and 48.

  2. A person 1.8 m tall casts a shadow of 2.4 m. At the same time a tree casts a shadow of 16 m. How tall is the tree?

    Show solution

    Idea. At the same moment the sun's rays make the same angle, so the person with his shadow and the tree with its shadow form similar right triangles.

    \(\dfrac h{16}=\dfrac{1.8}{2.4}=0.75\).

    \(h=0.75\cdot16=12\).

    Answer: 12 m.

  3. A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. Find the altitude to the hypotenuse and the two segments of the hypotenuse.

    Show solution

    Idea. Use \(a^2=pc\) for one segment, then \(q=c-p\), then \(h^2=pq\).

    \(c=\sqrt{36+64}=10\).

    \(6^2=p\cdot10\Rightarrow p=3.6\); \(q=10-3.6=6.4\).

    \(h^2=3.6\cdot6.4=23.04\Rightarrow h=4.8\).

    Check: \(h=\dfrac{ab}c=\dfrac{48}{10}=4.8\) ✓ (two ways of writing the area).

    Answer: \(h=4.8\); segments 3.6 and 6.4.

  4. \(AB=12\), \(AC=8\), \(BC=15\), and \(AD\) bisects angle \(A\). Find \(BD\) and \(DC\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Angle bisector theorem, then divide \(BC=15\) in the ratio.

    \(BD:DC=12:8=3:2\). Five parts make 15: one part is 3.

    \(BD=9\), \(DC=6\).

    Answer: \(BD=9\), \(DC=6\).

  5. Two secants from \(P\) meet a circle at \(A,B\) and \(C,D\): \(PA=4\), \(PB=10\), \(PC=5\). Find \(PD\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Two secants from one point: \(PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD\) (near part × whole, on each secant).

    \(4\cdot10=5\cdot PD\Rightarrow PD=8\).

    Answer: \(PD=8\).

  6. A triangle has sides 5, 6 and 8. A similar triangle has longest side 12. Find its other sides and the ratio of the areas.

    Show solution

    Idea. Match the longest sides to find \(k\); scale the others; the area ratio is \(k^2\).

    \(k=\dfrac{12}8=1.5\).

    \(5\cdot1.5=7.5\); \(6\cdot1.5=9\).

    Area ratio \(1.5^2=2.25\).

    Answer: sides 7.5 and 9; areas in the ratio \(2.25:1\) (that is, \(9:4\)).

  7. Prove the midsegment theorem with similarity.

    Show solution

    Idea. Show \(\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC\) by SAS (two sides in ratio \(\tfrac12\) and the common angle), then read off the length and the equal angles.

    \(\dfrac{AD}{AB}=\dfrac{AE}{AC}=\dfrac12\), and \(\angle A\) is common.

    \(\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC\) (SAS), ratio \(\tfrac12\).

    So \(DE=\tfrac12BC\).

    Also \(\angle ADE=\angle ABC\): corresponding angles are equal, so \(DE\parallel BC\).

    Answer: the midsegment is parallel to the third side and half of it.

  8. \(D\) on \(AB\), \(E\) on \(AC\): \(AD=6\), \(DB=4\), \(AE=9\), \(EC=6\). Is \(DE\parallel BC\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Converse of Thales: if the two sides are cut in the same ratio, the segment is parallel to the third side.

    \(\dfrac{AD}{DB}=\dfrac64=1.5\) and \(\dfrac{AE}{EC}=\dfrac96=1.5\).

    Answer: the ratios are equal, so \(DE\parallel BC\).

  9. In trapezoid \(ABCD\) (\(AB\parallel CD\)), the diagonals meet at \(O\). \(AB=12\), \(CD=4\), \(AC=20\). Find \(AO\).

    Show solution

    Idea. The parallel bases create equal alternate angles, so the two triangles at the diagonals' crossing are similar with ratio \(AB:CD\).

    \(\angle OAB=\angle OCD\), \(\angle OBA=\angle ODC\) (alternate angles).

    \(\triangle AOB\sim\triangle COD\), ratio \(\dfrac{12}4=3\). So \(AO=3\,CO\).

    \(AO+CO=20\Rightarrow4\,CO=20\Rightarrow CO=5\), \(AO=15\).

    Answer: \(AO=15\).

  10. Describe how to divide a segment \(AB\) into 5 equal parts with compass and ruler, and explain why it works.

    Show solution

    Idea. Equal steps on an auxiliary ray are carried onto \(AB\) by parallel lines (Thales' theorem).

    1. From \(A\) draw any ray that is not along \(AB\).

    2. With the compass, mark 5 equal steps along it: \(A_1,\dots,A_5\) (\(A_5=K\)).

    3. Join \(K\) to \(B\).

    4. Through \(A_1,\dots,A_4\) draw lines parallel to \(KB\).

    They cut \(AB\) at four points.

    Why: parallel lines cut two transversals in the same ratio, so the equal steps on the ray become equal steps on \(AB\).

    Answer: as described.

Level C: Exam level Questions 21–30

Longer questions in the style of the matriculation (bagrut) exam.

  1. Prove the angle bisector theorem.

    Show solution

    Idea. Compare the areas of triangles \(ABD\) and \(ACD\) in two ways: with the common height from \(A\), and with the equal distances from \(D\) to the sides.

    Same height from \(A\) to \(BC\): \(\dfrac{S_{ABD}}{S_{ACD}}=\dfrac{BD}{DC}\).

    \(D\) is on the bisector, so it is equally far, \(t\), from \(AB\) and from \(AC\): \(\dfrac{S_{ABD}}{S_{ACD}}=\dfrac{\tfrac12AB\cdot t}{\tfrac12AC\cdot t}=\dfrac{AB}{AC}\).

    Answer: \(\dfrac{BD}{DC}=\dfrac{AB}{AC}\).

  2. Prove that if chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) meet at \(E\), then \(AE\cdot EB=CE\cdot ED\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Join \(A\) to \(C\) and \(D\) to \(B\). The two triangles are similar (AA), and cross-multiplying the ratio gives the products.

    \(\angle CAB=\angle CDB\) (inscribed angles on arc \(CB\)).

    \(\angle AEC=\angle DEB\) (vertical angles).

    \(\triangle AEC\sim\triangle DEB\) (AA).

    \(\dfrac{AE}{DE}=\dfrac{CE}{BE}\Rightarrow AE\cdot BE=CE\cdot DE\).

    Answer: proved.

  3. \(PA\) is a tangent and \(PBC\) a secant. Prove that \(PA^2=PB\cdot PC\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Find two similar triangles with \(PA\) in both. The tangent–chord angle equals the inscribed angle on the same arc.

    \(\angle PAB\) (between tangent \(PA\) and chord \(AB\)) \(=\angle ACB=\angle PCA\) (inscribed on arc \(AB\)).

    \(\angle P\) is common.

    \(\triangle PAB\sim\triangle PCA\) (AA).

    \(\dfrac{PA}{PC}=\dfrac{PB}{PA}\Rightarrow PA^2=PB\cdot PC\).

    Answer: proved.

  4. In a right triangle, the altitude \(h\) to the hypotenuse \(c\) cuts it into \(p\) (next to leg \(a\)) and \(q\). Prove \(a^2=pc\) and \(h^2=pq\), and deduce Pythagoras' theorem.

    Show solution

    Idea. The altitude splits the right triangle into two smaller right triangles; each shares an acute angle with the whole one, so all three are similar.

    Small triangle at leg \(a\) (sides \(p\), \(h\), \(a\)) and the whole triangle (sides \(a\), \(b\), \(c\)): common acute angle and a right angle.

    Ratio "short side next to the angle : hypotenuse": \(\dfrac pa=\dfrac ac\Rightarrow a^2=pc\).

    The two small triangles are similar to each other: \(\dfrac hp=\dfrac qh\Rightarrow h^2=pq\).

    In the same way \(b^2=qc\). Add: \(a^2+b^2=pc+qc=(p+q)c=c\cdot c\).

    Answer: \(a^2=pc\), \(h^2=pq\), and \(a^2+b^2=c^2\).

  5. A square stands on the base of a triangle with base 12 and height 8, with its two upper corners on the other sides. Find the side of the square.

    Show solution

    Idea. The top side of the square is parallel to the base, so the small triangle above it is similar to the whole triangle. Compare base to height.

    Small triangle: base \(s\), height \(8-s\). Whole: base 12, height 8.

    \(\dfrac s{12}=\dfrac{8-s}8\Rightarrow8s=12(8-s)=96-12s\).

    \(20s=96\Rightarrow s=4.8\).

    Answer: the side of the square is 4.8.

  6. In triangle \(ABC\), \(D\) is on \(AB\) with \(AD:DB=1:2\), and \(E\) is on \(AC\) with \(AE:EC=1:2\). What fraction of the area of \(ABC\) is the quadrilateral \(DBCE\)?

    Show solution

    Idea. Equal ratios on both sides give \(DE\parallel BC\) and a similar triangle with \(k=\tfrac{AD}{AB}\).

    \(\dfrac{AD}{DB}=\dfrac{AE}{EC}=\tfrac12\Rightarrow DE\parallel BC\).

    \(\dfrac{AD}{AB}=\dfrac13\), so \(k=\tfrac13\) and \(S_{ADE}=\tfrac19S_{ABC}\).

    \(S_{DBCE}=S_{ABC}-\tfrac19S_{ABC}\).

    Answer: \(\tfrac89\) of the area.

  7. Two similar triangles have areas 25 and 49. A median of the smaller one is 10. Find the matching median of the larger.

    Show solution

    Idea. The area ratio is \(k^2\); every length (including medians) scales by \(k\).

    \(k^2=\tfrac{49}{25}\Rightarrow k=\tfrac75\).

    \(10\cdot\tfrac75=14\).

    Answer: 14.

  8. Chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) meet at \(E\): \(AE=x\), \(EB=x+5\), \(CE=4\), \(ED=6\). Find \(x\) and \(AB\).

    Show solution

    Idea. Intersecting chords give a quadratic for \(x\); a length must be positive.

    \(x(x+5)=4\cdot6=24\Rightarrow x^2+5x-24=0\Rightarrow(x+8)(x-3)=0\).

    \(x=3\) (\(x=-8\) is not a length).

    \(AB=x+(x+5)=3+8=11\).

    Answer: \(x=3\), \(AB=11\).

  9. The tangent from \(P\) to a circle is 12. The line from \(P\) through the centre meets the circle first at a distance 8 from \(P\). Find the radius.

    Show solution

    Idea. The line through the centre is a secant. Its near part is 8, and its whole length is \(8+2r\) (it crosses a full diameter). Use \(PT^2=\text{near}\cdot\text{whole}\).

    \(12^2=8(8+2r)\).

    \(144=64+16r\Rightarrow16r=80\Rightarrow r=5\).

    Answer: \(r=5\).

    Check: distance \(OP=8+5=13\), and \(\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=12\) ✓

  10. Find the point \(P\) that divides the segment from \(A(1,2)\) to \(B(11,7)\) in the ratio \(2:3\). Explain the formula with Thales' theorem.

    Show solution

    Idea. Ratio \(2:3\) means \(P\) is \(\tfrac25\) of the way from \(A\) to \(B\). Move that fraction of the change in \(x\) and in \(y\).

    \(B-A=(10,5)\).

    \(\tfrac25\cdot(10,5)=(4,2)\).

    \(P=(1+4,\ 2+2)=(5,4)\).

    Why: lines through \(A\), \(P\), \(B\) parallel to an axis are parallel to each other, so by Thales they cut the other axis in the same ratio \(2:3\).

    Answer: \(P(5,4)\).
