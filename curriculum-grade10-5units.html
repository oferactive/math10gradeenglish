01

Analytic geometry

Goal: describe lines and circles with equations, and link them to the idea of a function.

01The straight line and its equation

  • Review of the line and the parabola, linked to the idea of a function.
  • Telling a function graph from a set of points that is not a graph, for example \(x=k\).
  • Finding the equation from two points, or from a slope and a point.
  • Parallel and perpendicular lines, intersection points, midpoint of a segment, distance between two points.
  • Explicit form \(y=mx+b\) compared with general form \(Ax+By+C=0\).

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

02Systems of equations and the circle centred at the origin

  • Systems of linear equations and what they look like on a graph, including systems with parameters.
  • A system of one quadratic and one linear equation (a line meeting a parabola).
  • The circle \(x^2+y^2=R^2\), built from the distance formula. It prepares for trigonometry and is an example of a locus that is not a function.
  • Shifting the circle: \((x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=R^2\) as a transformation. The general form of the circle equation is not part of the syllabus.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

02

Introduction to functions

Goal: build a "feel for functions" that prepares students for calculus, both intuitively and formally.

03Polynomial and power functions

  • Review of linear and quadratic functions. Power functions \(f(x)=x^n\) as their generalization.
  • Even and odd powers. Roots of even and odd multiplicity and how they affect the graph.
  • A polynomial as a product of linear and quadratic factors: where it is positive or negative, behaviour at infinity, number of extreme points.
  • A first look at rate of change.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

04Non-polynomial functions

  • Absolute value \(|x|\), square root \(\sqrt{x}\), \(\dfrac{1}{x}\) and \(\dfrac{1}{x^2}\).
  • Even and odd functions. Describing behaviour in terms of rate of change.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

05Transformations and operations on functions

  • Reflection in the x-axis \((f\to -f)\) and in the y-axis \((f(x)\to f(-x))\).
  • Shifts and stretches: \(a(x-p)^n+k\), \(a\sqrt{x-p}+k\), \(\dfrac{a}{x-p}+k\).
  • Sum, difference, product and quotient of functions.
  • The function \(g=\dfrac{1}{f}\): domain, zeros, asymptotes and how its graph relates to the graph of \(f\).

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

06Composition and inverse functions

  • Composition: the absolute value, square and square root of a function.
  • Inverse function: when it exists, and symmetry about the line \(y=x\).
  • \(\sqrt{x}\) as the inverse of \(x^2\) (notation \(x^{1/2}\)), the cube root as the inverse of \(x^3\), and the fact that \(\sqrt{x^2}=|x|\).

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

03

Differential calculus

Goal: understand the derivative as a rate of change and use it to investigate functions and solve problems. This is the largest area of the year.

07The concept of the derivative

  • Constant and changing rates of change in real situations (fuel, speed, temperature).
  • "Steps" as a picture of rate of change: \(\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\), and making the step narrower.
  • The tangent line as a linear approximation ("zoom in"); its slope is the value of the derivative.
  • Definition: \(f'(x_0)=\lim\limits_{\Delta x\to 0}\dfrac{f(x_0+\Delta x)-f(x_0)}{\Delta x}\), with limits computed intuitively and numerically.
  • A tangent can exist where the derivative does not, e.g. \(\sqrt{x}\) at \(x=0\).

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

08Rules of differentiation

  • \((af)'=af'\), \((f\pm g)'=f'\pm g'\), the product rule \((fg)'\) and the chain rule \((f(g))'=f'(g)\cdot g'\), proved from the definition.
  • How the graph of a function relates to the graph of its derivative.
  • The quotient rule is taught later, with rational functions (unit 11).

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

09Investigating polynomial functions

  • Full investigation: domain, intercepts, extreme points, intervals of increase and decrease, even/odd, sketch.
  • Investigation on a closed interval (maximum and minimum at the endpoints).
  • Qualitative study of how many solutions an equation or inequality has.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

10Tangent lines and antiderivatives of polynomials

  • The equation of a tangent at a point on the graph, and tangents with a given slope.
  • Antiderivative of a polynomial (without the integral sign): finding \(f\) from \(f'\), and asking whether it is unique.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

11Rational functions

  • The derivative of \(\dfrac{1}{x}\); investigating \(\dfrac{1}{f}\) as a composite function.
  • Points where the function is undefined: vertical asymptote or removable discontinuity ("hole" in the graph).
  • Behaviour as \(x\to\pm\infty\) and horizontal asymptotes (intuitive limits, no heavy algebra).
  • The quotient rule.
  • Sketch from the domain, asymptotes, intercepts and signs of numerator and denominator, then full investigation with the derivative.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

12Square root functions

  • The derivative of \(\sqrt{x}\), and \(\sqrt{x}\) as the reflection of \(x^2\) in \(y=x\).
  • How the derivatives of inverse functions are related.
  • Simple composite roots: a linear expression under the root, e.g. \(\sqrt{2x+3}\) (optionally a quadratic).
  • Investigation with the derivative, tangent lines. Harder root functions continue in Grade 11.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

13Optimization (max/min) problems

  • Problems on open and closed intervals.
  • Settings: plane and solid geometry, analytic geometry, functions, trigonometry, physics and economics.
  • Focus on what the extreme value means, not only on the calculation. Some problems solved without a derivative.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

04

Trigonometric functions

Goal: define sine, cosine and tangent on the unit circle and use them to model periodic behaviour. The teacher may teach this area before or after trigonometry in the plane.

14The unit circle and the trigonometric functions

  • Matching a real number to a point on the unit circle by arc length (circumference \(2\pi\)); radians.
  • \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\) as the y- and x-coordinates of the point; \(\tan x\) through the tangent line at \((1,0)\).
  • Periodicity and symmetry, derived from the definitions. Graphs, shifts and stretches.
  • Identities: \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\), \(\sin\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-x\right)=\cos x\), \(\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\).
  • Special values at \(\tfrac{\pi}{6}\), \(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\), \(\tfrac{\pi}{3}\), \(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\). Link to ratios in a right triangle.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

15Trigonometric equations

  • Equations of the form \(a\sin(x+b)+c=0\), and the same for cosine and tangent.
  • General solution, and solutions within a given interval.
  • Modelling periodic phenomena (tides, daylight hours, a Ferris wheel).

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

05

Trigonometry in the plane

Goal: calculate sides, angles and areas in any triangle and use trigonometry in proofs.

16Area formula, law of sines, law of cosines

  • Triangle area \(S=\tfrac12 ab\sin C\), proved for acute and obtuse triangles.
  • Law of sines: \(\dfrac{a}{\sin A}=\dfrac{b}{\sin B}=\dfrac{c}{\sin C}=2R\).
  • Law of cosines: \(c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C\).
  • Calculations and proofs in triangles and quadrilaterals; checking units and dimensions.
  • The difference between an identity and an equation, and between an approximate calculation and a general proof.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

06

Euclidean geometry

Goal: reason deductively and write correct proofs. Euclidean geometry is taught in Grade 10 only; in Grade 11 geometry continues through trigonometry.

17Deductive reasoning and proof skills

  • Quantifiers: "for every" and "there exists". Counterexamples.
  • Necessary and sufficient conditions; a theorem and its converse.
  • Proof by contradiction.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

18Loci and special points of the triangle

  • Loci: circle, perpendicular bisector, angle bisector, parallel lines, with proof in both directions.
  • Perpendicular bisectors meet at the circumcentre; angle bisectors meet at the incentre.
  • Medians meet at the centroid and divide each other in the ratio \(2:1\); the altitudes meet at one point.
  • The fourth congruence theorem (SSA with the angle opposite the longer side) and the case with no congruence.
  • The converse of Pythagoras' theorem.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

19The circle

  • Definitions: radius, chord, diameter, arc, central angle, inscribed angle.
  • Theorems on arcs, chords and angles. An inscribed angle is half the central angle on the same arc; an angle on a diameter is a right angle.
  • Cyclic quadrilateral (opposite angles sum to \(180^\circ\)) and its converse.
  • Tangents: perpendicular to the radius; two tangents from a point are equal; the tangent–chord angle equals the inscribed angle on the arc.
  • Constructions: finding the centre of a given circle, drawing a tangent from a point on or outside the circle.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

20Proportion and similar triangles

  • Thales' (intercept) theorem, its converse and the theorems that follow (midsegment).
  • Similarity theorems (AA, SAS, SSS). Identifying similar triangles and proving similarity.
  • In similar triangles: ratio of perimeters, medians, angle bisectors, altitudes and radii; areas in the ratio \(k^2\).
  • The internal angle bisector theorem.
  • Similarity in the circle: intersecting chords, tangent and secant, two secants from one point.
  • Constructions: dividing a segment into \(n\) equal parts and in a given ratio.

Pages: Explanation · Workbook (30 questions)

07

Not taught in Grade 10

These topics come in Grade 11 and are not part of this site's Grade 10 plan:

  • Sequences (arithmetic, geometric, recursive) and mathematical induction.
  • Integral calculus.
  • The second derivative, concavity and points of inflection.
  • Trigonometric identities for sums and double angles, and derivatives of trigonometric functions.
  • Probability.
